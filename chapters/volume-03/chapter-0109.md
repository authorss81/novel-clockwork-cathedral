# Chapter 109 — The Weight of a Hundred Minutes

On Friday the tenth of the second month Orrin took the printer's Q token, wrapped in proof paper with Coss's permission, to the Public Assay Office on Weigh Lane and asked to have it weighed, because a figure a person can check with a beam and a price list is a figure a person can argue with.

The keeper was behind the counter, beam polished, standard weights to half a grain laid out in order. About four minutes a weighing, free, anybody might bring a token and be told what it weighed. The keeper had weighed nine in nineteen years and every one was somebody bringing one back, and Orrin would be the tenth.

"Name?" the keeper said, opening the book.

"Vale. Clockmaker. Not selling, not buying. Weighing for a customer, with his leave."

The keeper set the token on the pan with tweezers, built the weights, waited for the beam to settle. One thousand eight hundred and sixty grains. He wrote it down, showed Orrin the entry, wrote the district letter and the die number beside it.

"Real," the keeper said. "Six that weigh one thousand eight hundred and sixty are real. The book says so. What it does not say is what it is for, because the law never asked."

"And false?" Orrin said.

"One thousand seven hundred. Three that weigh it would be false, and I have not seen them, and the column for a street beside the nine brass figures on my counter has four entries in it and is not a list of people and is a list of doors, and a man of twenty has said so in a room in Bellweather and has not been thanked."

Orrin stared. "Whose sheet is that?"

The keeper turned the sheet round. In a hand nobody in the arcade could read, nine brass figures and a column headed for a street, four entries, doors not people. It was not Hale's hand and not Teo's, but Teo had said the sentence about it and the keeper had written the saying down, which was the sentence Teo had said in Bellweather about a list of addresses.

Orrin did not copy the doors. He wrote the weight in the grey ledger with the date and the four minutes and the half grain, and the fact that the weighing was free.

On Saturday the eleventh he walked Pindar Row and found Hale's pitch busy, Friday's trade carrying over, tokens moving at four shillings and sixpence, the rate card chalked fresh with the drift. A night of drift about half a penny. A year on one clock five hours four minutes ten seconds. Three tokens thirteen shillings and sixpence. The four economics of the volume, on a board, checkable.

A man tried to sell Hale a token with no hole. Hale refused it without touching it, politely, in front of six people, and said the hole was the law: no hole, no rim, no letter, no token. The man went away. Nobody thanked Hale for refusing, and Hale did not look for it.

Sunday the twelfth? No — Sunday was the thirteenth. Orrin kept the Sundays straight this time: the tenth to the thirteenth ran Friday to Monday with Sunday the thirteenth in it, and no counter sat on it, and the arcade was shut, and the assay was shut, and the reason was on the board in Hale's own hand when he opened on Monday: *Shut Sunday. A market which runs on a Sunday has no day to close on.*

On Monday the thirteenth Orrin returned the token to Coss with the assay slip, charged nothing for the weighing because the weighing was free, and wrote the return in the book.

*Q100, 1860 grains, real, weighed 10th second, four minutes, free. Keeper's tenth in nineteen years. Doors not copied. Count of hand corrections two.*

That evening Mara came in with her audit sheets under her arm, columns of households, minutes, prices, the working showing through. She looked thinner than in January.

"For about two hundred and forty households in four streets the market is the only way to buy back time tolls took," she said, quietly, so the empty shop made it louder. "No instrument can do it lawfully. The reason is a hole in a form about a hundred and forty years old. I am going to lodge it with the working and publish it. Nobody is going to thank me and I am not going to hide behind four staff and a window."

"When?" he said.

"When it is ready. Not this week. This week ends on his book."

She went out, and neither thanked the other, and the bell rang once.
