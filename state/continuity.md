## Established on the page in batch 0004 (Chapters 31–40)

Canon because it is on the page. A later volume may change one only deliberately and must record the change here.

- **The batch calendar, absolute and not to be re-derived.** The third month has thirty days and its last day is **Thursday the 30th**; there is no Friday the 31st of it, and the fourth month therefore begins on **Saturday the 1st**, and the week of Tuesday the 28th, Wednesday the 29th and Thursday the 30th runs straight into Saturday the 1st with nothing in between. **The fourth month is a full thirty days:** 1 Sat, 2 Sun, 3 Mon, 4 Tue, 5 Wed, 6 Thu, 7 Fri, 8 Sat, 9 Sun, 10 Mon, 11 Tue, 12 Wed, 13 Thu, 14 Fri, 15 Sat, 16 Sun, 17 Mon, 18 Tue, 19 Wed, 20 Thu, 21 Fri, 22 Sat, 23 Sun, 24 Mon, 25 Tue, 26 Wed, 27 Thu, 28 Fri, 29 Sat, **30 Sun, and there is no thirty-first.** **No Assembly or committee sits on a Sunday in Morrowfen and no chapter of this batch pretends otherwise.**
- **Batch 0004 day map, fixed on the page.** Ch 31 the 1st and 2nd; Ch 32 the 3rd, with the ward-board notice posted the 4th; Ch 33 the 4th, 5th and 7th; **Ch 34 Monday the 10th**; Ch 35 the 11th, 12th, 13th, 14th, 15th and 17th; Ch 36 the 10th, 12th, 13th, 14th, 17th, 18th and the 24th; Ch 37 the 21st and 22nd, with the answer lodged on the 24th; Ch 38 the 24th, 25th, 26th and 27th; Ch 39 the 27th and 28th; **Ch 40 Saturday the 29th.** The ten chapters run four weeks, the 1st to the 29th of the fourth month.
- **SLIPPAGE IS A SHAPE AND THE SHAPE IS A LINE.** This is the batch's central piece of world-building and it is a physical fact, not a mystery. Since **the fifteenth of the third month**, the first night of the canal-front schedule, the civic hourline out of the Weir Two pumping station has been **losing fifty seconds a night, in one step, at the fourth draw of the day**, and every machine hung off that line has taken its own fraction of it. **The line divides by eight and takes its beat off the odd teeth, and a line that divides by eight was surveyed for two cycles a day and has been given four.** Four measured clocks, read against a bench watch set on the Clockwork Quarter's public clock: **the errors in seconds are 100, 300, 500 and 700 — one minute forty, five minutes, eight minutes twenty, and eleven minutes forty** — at Bellweather Road (eight hundred yards from the pump house), the Weir Lane corner (1,200 yards), Verge Lane (two and a quarter miles) and a Green Span street (four miles). **A tram signal at a junction two districts away let a window go eleven minutes and forty seconds late, out of a signalman's bound day book, in his own hand, and nobody had ever asked to see it.** The common difference of 200 seconds is explicitly *not* treated as a law on the page: the four places are irregularly spaced and Orrin writes that four machines in a progression is a coincidence, and that **the finding is the direction and the date, not the progression** — all four slow, all four a whole multiple of the same step, and not one of them out before the fifteenth of the third month.
- **The schedule's lapse stopped the stepping and the stopping is the news.** The Water Users' Committee's schedule ran to the thirtieth of the third month and **lapsed at midnight**, and by the Saturday morning the front was back on the standing rotation at one draw at night. Orrin and Teo Arden stood at the corner of Weir Lane at ten past six on the Sunday the 2nd waiting for the fourth draw, and it did not come, and the line held. **The consequence: the error is now fixed and permanent. There is no fault in it to correct, and a correction wants a fault, so there is no authority in Morrowfen entitled to wind sixteen days out of a district's clocks.** If the schedule is re-voted the fourth draw returns and the line begins stepping again; if it is not re-voted the front floods in the spring. Sixteen days at fifty seconds is **13 min 20 s**, which is the line's own total and which Orrin can certify the inputs of and nothing else.
- **A network of civic machines in open disagreement is not a machine.** A class-three licence covers one civic machine. Orrin issued **four condition records, every one of them true, every one of them saying *within tolerance against the district reference***, and between them saying that the city is on time. *Within tolerance* is a statement about a relationship, not about the world. He wrote the true thing on the reverse of the Weir Lane certificate in pencil, in four lines, and knew before he sealed the envelope that nobody would read it. **The district works' clerk, Ambrose, about thirty and eleven years in the building, is the man who refuses it correctly**: *you want me to write down that the clocks are correct and the city is wrong, and there is no column for that, Mr Vale, and I have looked*; and *a line is not a machine*; and *a man does not build a form with that column missing by accident twice.* He says the last thing in a good-humoured way and it is the volume's thesis in a tradesman's mouth, and **he is right and he is not a villain**.
- **A carriage clock is not a good enough instrument.** The Chapter 30 clock's error appeared to come and go. It was a **minute hand out of true, worst at the hour**, so the clock could not be read to better than about two minutes and no reading of it was a reading of the clock. With the hand trued it read **one minute forty seconds slow, constant to a second on six readings over two hours** — the same as Bellweather Road. *A reading taken off a bent hand is a reading of the bend, and a man who cannot read his own instrument to better than two minutes has no business going out with a tape measure.* The count of Orrin's own hand corrections stays **two**.
- **The aftershock use of batch 0004, Chapter 31, and only one.** Hand on the district indicator at the corner of Verge Lane, one named load, one question — *what is the main doing with the fourth draw of the day* — and the answer is **true and unusable**: the main is not tired, is not wrong, and has no opinion; it has been given four a day for sixteen days and will keep four; it cannot tell the difference between a rhythm and an instruction; give it one and it comes back to one in about a day and a half. Cost: **a hundred and twenty seconds, edges clean, entered in the shop book as a reading cost with the reason written under it**, which is the Chapter 17 practice and not a fourth keeper's toll. **The batch's line about it is one line without comment: it was true, and there was nowhere in Morrowfen to put it.** The count of keeper's tolls is still **three** (Chapters 5, 9, 19).
- **The Weir One governor answer from Chapter 25 is still uncitable and is still not used.** It is not spoken in any room in this batch. **It appears instead as line item three of a published maintenance schedule, at £96 0s, programme year two, as a governor to be replaced** — because a governor made for two cycles a day that has been asked for four is a governor that will be replaced. The only person in the city who knew it was old, and that it was the only part of the business nobody would ask about, is the maintainer who could not certify a line and whose rule stopped him correctly in a Question Hall. **Nobody connects him to the line item and he does not expect them to.**
- **The Water Users' Committee's lapse, on the page, Monday the 3rd of the fourth month.** It *noted* that the schedule made on the second inst. and effective from the fifteenth to the thirtieth had run its term; that in the absence of any further schedule the district reverts to the standing rotation, which is the arrangement the district had with itself before the question was ever filed; that the committee has **no power to continue a schedule and no power to suspend the standing rotation**; and that it resolved to give notice of a proposed schedule in the same terms, posted on the ward board outside the Weir Two pump house, to be heard at the sitting of **the seventeenth**. **The notice carries a sentence no other notice on that board has carried in eleven years, added by a clerk doing his best inside a form with one line for it: *the committee is not able to consult the district, the district having no standing under the water act*.** Forty minutes and no argument, because there was nothing to argue about. **There is no form on which a hundred and forty thousand people who are not ratepayers at the address where the water is can be asked, served, or written to about a schedule about their own water.**
- **The Weirs' Chapter 30 decision cost them the schedule, and nobody says so.** Seven to three to one on the 29th of the third month, not to take a position. Nobody in the district says *you did this*, and it does not come, because the only person with the standing to say it said it to herself in her own voice at her own table before the vote was taken, in the past tense. **Consent is not agreement, and this is what agreement costs.**
- **Wenna Pruitt's last four pounds ten, and what it bought.** The council's whole remaining money goes on a form to lay the ward's returns before the committee as an appendix to its own minutes — at fourpence a page, typeset by **Bessie Crane**, who could not be paid and did it for the laundry's page. **The Advertiser page on the ninth of the month is cancelled**, which is the visible cost, and Wenna puts the two equal sums in the council's book next to each other so that whoever comes after her knows they are equal by accident. **She knew the price before she paid it and told a woman in a room before she paid it.** Teo Arden wants the page and says so and does not pretend to have moved: *the page is the only door in this city that is not shut* against *a printed page has no address, it cannot be served, it cannot be refused, and it cannot be contradicted, because there is nobody to contradict.* **The disclosure argument is open and is not resolved in this batch.**
- **Sel Kade's March diversion, documented, and the document is correct.** A **diversion note** from the **district works accountancy**, reconciling the ward's certified monthly return for the third month against the ward's own return, which reached the works only because the district's returns were printed in the Assembly's minutes on the 20th of the third month. **From the ward's reserve, to the Carrow Street main, nine thousand gallons a day, three hundred and twenty-four thousand gallons in the thirty-six days from the 2nd of the third month.** The Carrow Street main supplies **seventy-one households and a galvanising and wire works**, and the works' certificate of the 29th of the second month states that the main is not to be shut. The diversion commenced on the **second of the third month** and is in force; it is **reviewed at the next monthly return and thereafter at the discretion of the district works**; and **should the rotation of the district main be varied in any respect, the diverted volume shall be restored to the ward's reserve within one month of the variation** — and the rotation *was* varied, on the 8th of the third month and again on the 30th, so the clause is triggered and the restoration is due by the 30th of the fourth month. **The ward's certified reserve was eleven days on the first of the month and is under two days; the ward's reserve alarm has been inhibited since the 2nd of the third month, entered against the note, and under the works' tables an inhibited reserve alarm requires a report and no report has been made.**
- **The diversion's price falls on the poorest ward, and the district will be re-rated for proving it.** A ward's water rates are set on its certified return, and the certified return said no variation was made, and the ward gave the water away and charged nobody. **Correcting the certified return raises the ward's rates.** So the four pounds ten buys a document that will cost the district more than four pounds ten, and Wenna knew the shape of that and not the size of it, and paid. **The batch's ledger line: nothing is a scandal and everything is a budget.**
- **Sel Kade's defence, on her own terms, Chapter 34, and the minute is unusable to her.** Fifty minutes, nine men, no bar, no clerk's table, hats on knees, a fire that smokes. She sets her own terms: she will answer questions, she will answer questions that are not about water, and she will not be examined in an office. **Her first eleven minutes are a defence of jobs and not of herself**: the works' certificate carries the figure of ninety-one, of whom sixty-one are resident in the district, and the district's own gauge sheet is the evidence that the Carrow Street end has had no standing water since March. **Her second part is the line that reframes it: *I did not divert the ward's reserve to a works, I diverted it to a flooding street, and the works is the reason the street flooded, because the street floods because of a gate, and the gate is the ward's business, and I am this ward.*** She offers the gate's own log on a written request naming the week and **the committee cannot ask, because a water users' committee has no function in respect of the district's flood performance.** The result is four lines: the employment finding is minuted, the flood finding is not, the restoration is noted and not ordered, and **the review clause is not in the minute at all, because the minute is about what the committee heard and the clause was something it was shown, and there is a difference, and the difference is the whole of the book.** She asks for one correction and gets it in about forty seconds, and says thank you to the clerk and not to the room. **She is not recruited, not converted, not softened, and not the villain, and her tenant review is still owed by about Chapter 46.**
- **What a body with standing added to a claim is worth, on the page.** The pumping district has no standing, so Sel Kade appears as a member of the public on her own recognizance with nobody behind her, and every true word she says is *hers* and dies with her. A finding entered on the motion of a member of the public binds nobody to do anything. **The chapter's discovery, and the batch's turn: a claim can be weighed, and a party with standing can be told no and has to accept it — and the reason she is against it is that the model was built so, which is not her fault and which she cannot fix either.**
- **Bessie Crane and Ivy Pike disagree in Chapter 34, and both are right.** Bessie: thirty-one women on a Friday, one of them off eight weeks this year and paid out of her own pocket; she would have kept the works open with a bucket and a bar and longer than nine thousand gallons a day. Ivy: thirty-one against ninety-one, she has a pay book for the ninety-one, she watched a works close for want of water in a winter and two of its men go to the docks and not come back, and you cannot put a value on a job that stops. **They both refuse to choose, in front of a room.**
- **The Loose Minutes' hardware, Chapter 35, and what it cost.** Two **section caps** off a buried line under the towpath on the night of Wednesday the 12th, cut up through a grating with a cold chisel and a rope; a set of gate wheels, a pinion and a drum, and one **1904 standpipe bracket**, out of the district works' store on the Thursday the 13th. **Teo Arden signed for all five in his own name in the store's issue book, and put the address of a laundry on Bellweather Road, which is not his, because the method is that a record is a thing with a person in it.** The light on the Kestrel-side boardinghouse went on at about twenty past two in the morning on the Friday the 14th; it is **competent, unpaid, illegal, and the only thing in this city that has ever made a clock right without a form.** The report on it is in **Nell Arter's** landlord's letterhead folder and is four lines, and **names nobody who fitted it**, and says so, and why, in the folder rather than in a book, so that it is on paper somewhere.
- **The cost, and who paid for it.** The 1904 bracket was the only one of its kind in four districts and it came off the top of the standpipe riser of **12 to 20 Ferrywright Row, a block of twenty-two households, one of Hanna Stroud's nine.** The pipe came down a quarter of an inch in the ceiling void, 4B and 4C were wet, the fourth-floor landing was dark, and the block's **four clocks on that floor — hand-wound every Monday for eleven years by Dilly Amnersley, 66, in 4B, on a private 1911 spur cut off from the district supply in 1923 — stopped being wound.** They are the only clocks in that building that agree with each other, and the only clock that is on time is the ground-floor one, and it is out by **eleven minutes and forty seconds.** Orrin will not wind them either, because that would be a third hand correction, and the count is two and stays two. ** Hanna Stroud is reasonable, pays £411 for the plaster, tells Teo Arden in a room that she has been trying to get that light on for two months with a letter and eleven shillings of a letter, and would give him a hundred pounds and six buildings on Monday; and she says the thing the chapter is for: *the only record will be a record of condition that says the bracketing was sound, and in two winters it will come down on a stair with nine women on it.* **Ambrose**, at the works' counter, put the five issue-book line numbers in his day book and telephoned the works' inspectorate, and said, and asked for it to be minuted, *I have not taken the book out of the drawer and I am not going to.* **Teo says all of it himself, on the wet fourth-floor landing, before anybody asks: the hardware is right and he will not be told otherwise, and he is wrong about the block, and he would rather be first on the landing than be asked about it in six months by a man with a form number.**
- **The record of what a district worked is now a list of addresses, and that is the thing that turns a grievance into an organisation.** The guild's series is set as a **front-page trade notice in the Advertiser** on the Friday the 14th, paid for by **eleven small trades on the Kestrel side and Bellweather Road** out of a spare block, set by **Hester Lomas** at the page rate and a signature, and true in every word. **Four thousand and sixty copies off a two-revolution flatbed on the night of Wednesday the 12th, counted twice by the paper's own counter and delivery man, fifty-one years old, in his own hand, on the run sheet, with the date and the time — and he was asked for nothing, and offered nothing, and said he was not going to help them set it, and counted them because a book that says four thousand when it was four thousand and sixty is not a lie by anybody.** The number of copies of the labour book went from nine buildings to **twenty-two**, and the printing was done by nobody who is a member of anything.
- **The book of initials, and the discipline.** A school exercise book in Wenna Pruitt's kitchen under the eleven years, **forty-one entries in about twenty different hands, each a set of initials and a building**, and about four people in the district can read the initials and **nobody has ever written down the key**, on purpose, and the reason is on the page in a shop book dated the 21st of the second month: *a list of names is a list of names and a list of names can be produced by a man with a warrant or a man with a grudge; forty-one initials, one key, in four heads and in nobody's book, and it is not permanent, and it is the longest thing anybody in this district has ever built and it is going to last about nine years, and nine years of refusals is nine years more than anybody in this city has ever got out of a piece of paper.* **This is the chapter where the Loose Minutes become a thing the reader can name, and it must still not happen: the method is named, the movement is not, and there is still no committee, no constitution, no president and no minute book anybody can produce in a court.**
- **Hanna Stroud and Renn Corby disagree in Chapter 36, and both are right.** Hanna: four hundred and nineteen addresses in that district she could put a name to, nine blocks of her own in it, and a list of shops is a list of shops and a list of shops can be printed. Corby: a site is a street, a number goes to a door, a door is a route, **you do not need a name to get to a man**, and the moment the copy in twenty-two buildings is a list of routes rather than a list of facts, the person who could refuse is a woman in a kitchen with a book of initials and there is not one person in the district who could tell you what any of them mean except four. **He had sat on that for a month not saying it, and said it to stop nobody, and said so.**
- **The Office of Public Reckoning is ignored in this batch, at length, and its written right to be ignored is turned into an instrument.** **Bessie Crane telephones the Office, is told a printed page cannot be received, asks for the office's own form, and the form carries no fee — a fact about the Office that nobody in Morrowfen knows.** She sends it on the 12th and asks for one thing: *the document exists, and I want the office to write down that it exists, and I do not want you to do anything about it, and I want the date on it.* The note comes back on the 18th with a fifth line that is not on the form — *received at this office, in one copy, on the twelfth inst.* — and **it is the first time in nine years that anybody has rung the Office of Public Reckoning to say thank you for a note, and Mara Venn says so and then says she is not going to say how it feels because she has a file open and the caller is on a telephone.** The drawer of four-line cards held **eleven on the 17th of the third month and twenty-two on the 18th of the fourth.**
- **Leda Vale's instrument, and the hospital as a constituency.** The Green Span hospital branch is on the same slipping line, so the hospital's clocks are inside the network whose machines are out. On **Tuesday the 18th** at a quarter to eight, in the office on the third floor, to fourteen people, **before a penny of it is spent, she reads out two facts from the same document: item six, six hundred standpipe brackets, £1 890 over two years, and the eleven minutes and forty seconds of the ward's own lost time** — and asks the ward to have the number said in front of the people whose hours it is. *The money is good. That is a statement of what the money is buying, made by the people it is being bought with, on the day the sheet is published, and not on the day somebody comes up here in two years with a bill.* **The card goes up in the nurses' room on Monday the 24th with the day on it and the number on it and a line under it: *no fault; line error; reported, no report form exists*.** **Elsie Cobb, nineteen, on nights, doing the ward clock by hand and paid for it, asks whether the card will carry her name, and is told it will, in those words, and that anybody who wants to take it off can come and take it off and will have it written down who and when. Nobody took it off.** She may appear twice in this batch and does.
- **The guild question against Orrin, Chapter 37, and it arrives as a form number.** A **printed holder index card** at the front of every maintainer's file in the hall, with a ruled list of form numbers against the name, going in one direction only, is how a holder finds out. His card carries a twelfth line: **No. 13 — 14 Apr — Q — 6/4 — filed by the hall.** **Della Marsh gives him the number and nothing else**, on the correct ground that there is a card with a list on it and if he has not looked at his own card in six months then somebody else has. **Notice nine, licensing floor, standing orders, put up by the forms printer along with eleven others:** *a question may be put against a holder by a member or by the hall on behalf of a member upon a matter concerning that holder's returns; **a question is not read to the holder and is not to be discussed with the holder**; it is answered in writing within twenty-one days of the lodging of the number; the answer is entered on the holder's card; **a question does not suspend any right of a holder and does not alter any term and does not require an interview and none will be offered**.* It was filed **five days after his renewal**, it does not stop him working, and it was the same class of instrument as notice five, which is the trap the batch's whole argument is made of.
- **The question's subject and the answer that cannot be given.** It asks whether a class-three maintainer's statement in a work book — his own W-4 recommendation, entered of his own motion, that the works' certified shift return for Weir Two is not consistent with the station's own board — is consistent with the hall's records. **The hall's records establish that no certified shift return for that work exists, so the statement is unsupported rather than false**, and the board is a painted board in a pump house that is not a record of the hall and cannot be exhibited to one, because a form describing a chalk board must contain a figure, and **a figure of a district's hours is a district return, and a district return may only be certified above the third class or by the district that owns the reserve.** **The honest answer is therefore a licence offence, and that is the chapter's finding.** The answer is due on the fifth of the fifth month. **He writes four and sends the one that costs the district, and the sent answer contains eleven words that nobody will ever read: that no person in the district was asked.** He broke his own third rule knowingly, in a shop, in four minutes, and wrote down that he had done it. The count of hand corrections is two. **He commits no offence of any kind and is not suspended and no chapter may describe the suspension as coming.**
- **Gerren Hask appears once, in his official capacity, warm, and is never asked about the Long Nine.** Forty minutes over a cup of tea on a Friday. He offers **a boy from the licensing floor, seventeen, quick and honest and with no ear at all**, and says that if Orrin ever wants one he will send him down on a Tuesday and will not ask why, and if not he will not mention it again. He refuses to open a second work book early, because nobody has had to explain the practice to him in thirty-one years. And he repeats his standing session: **at ten on the twenty-eighth, every year, and if the twenty-eighth is a Sunday then it is a Sunday.** Orrin declines the boy and does not think about it, and Hask says *no, you will not, and I did not expect you to.* **The leash was never pulled. This was not the leash.**
- **The sealed classification, Chapter 38, and it is not a conspiracy.** Published on a board in a frame on the ground floor of the Rise, weekly, the most-read object on that hill. *Pumping district records, Weir Two floodgate and second-stage pumping station, Lower Weirs. All shift returns, signal tables and gate-handling records, 11 years to date. Classified. Reference CS/114. **Appeal: none.*** Applied by **Tobias Rill** under a section of the regional scheduling act thirty-one years old, used eleven times, four of them on hourlines, on the ground **security of a continuity work** — and a gate's signal table is a security matter in the way a vault combination is, and nobody in the city has ever published one and no argument is available against it. **The trouble is that a classification is applied to a file and not to a line, the instrument has one box, and the district's own returns, the quarterly-to-monthly figures it compiled itself, went in with everything else in one afternoon by a man doing his job.** The district's own arithmetic is now a copy of a file nobody may read, and **the number is in the Assembly's minutes and the source is in a sealed file, which means the number can now be called a fabrication.** **Rill is right, says so without apology, states both halves of his pick — under-classify and you publish a signal table; over-classify and a district loses a record it compiled itself — and says he would make the same pick in a week. He adds, on his own initiative, that the classification does not touch anything under the Cathedral, that he has no authority over it, and that he will put that in writing with a reference and a date if anybody claims otherwise, because *it is the only part of the answer I know for certain*.** Orrin does not say the Chapter 25 thing, because nobody in the room can refuse it.
- **Mara Venn learns of the classification from a document in a public file and not from a person, and does not telephone anybody about it.** She goes and reads the public index of transfers out of the public record, which is the only place in the city where a hole has to be distinguishable from a lie, and finds a pencil note in the margin in **Sindri Lake's** hand, dated Wednesday: *Records at the other end of this transfer sealed. Index entry remains open.* **The index entry is now the only surviving document that says a district was taken out of the arithmetic, and it is a statement and not the thing, and the statement stands and the record is gone, in that order, and both are correct.** Lake has written four such notes in thirty-one years, in pencil, because he has no authority to do it in anything else, and he tells Mara Venn that an index entry is not a record and was never one.
- **The cost of the seal lands on Hanna Stroud, and she is reasonable.** Her insurer's fourth paragraph: the source records are *unavailable* to them, and the schedule of premiums on nine blocks must be reviewed. **The word is *unavailable*, not withheld and not lost, and there is nobody to write to and no appeal, and a letter that does not say who to write to is furniture.** In the first quarter of the year those same returns had brought her premium down eleven per cent. She telephones a clockmaker rather than an office, says so, and asks the only question she has: *did the sealing change anything? Is the water any different? Is the gate any different?* **Nothing has changed at all.** *I have not got a method and I have not got a book of initials. I have got a rent book and an insurance letter and a woman in a good coat.* **The whole of what a landlord has is the right to say *I know*.**
- **The reclassification, Chapter 39: nothing is a scandal and everything is a budget, and it is the same instrument that deferred the trestle's bracket twice.** A **schedule of maintenance works for the Lower Weirs pumping district**, one sheet printed both sides, adopted the 22nd inst., programme year three of the current triennium, **nine items, total £4 202 10s 6d**, posted on the same board as the water-main repairs and a closure notice. It includes the Weir One governor (£96, year two), a re-survey of eleven miles of district main (£344 10s, year two — **a survey of the main, and the main is not out, the machines are, and there is no line in the sheet that says a machine**), the gate signal tables (£57 4s), six hundred standpipe brackets (£1 890), and **item seven, the Green Span hospital trestle's conduit bracket, survey and re-estimate, £2 14s 6d, programme year three** — the same two-year-old estimate, now with a survey and a year, which is the furthest it has ever been and in substance the third deferral of the same bracket. **The printing is excellent and the sum is real and nobody chose it.**
- **The batch's last and best arithmetic.** 388 hours of Weir Two night pumping at the guild's out-of-shop tariff of **4s 6d the hour** — the same figure as the annual renewal fee — is **£87 6s for four months**. The programme is **about forty-eight times the price of the crew.** And nobody chose it: *a price schedule is a page of a hall's book with hourly rates in it and it knows exactly what a night's work costs; a works programme is a page of a committee's book with item rates in it, and it knows exactly what a governor costs, and none of the three numbers is a man.* The eight shillings and the four thousand two hundred and two pounds were both set by competent people doing exactly what their book was for.
- **Mara Venn's attack, and it works, and it produces a better schedule and no more hours in anybody's ledger.** She goes at the schedule on its own terms and not on its morality: **a maintenance schedule is a schedule that states how often a thing will be inspected, and this one states when nine things will be repaired and not once when anything will be looked at**, so it is not a maintenance schedule, it is a list of repairs, and a body that has adopted a list under a maintenance heading has adopted something it did not mean to. - Morrowfen: estuary city of roughly 2.4 million on a reclaimed tidal basin between the Sable River and the Morrow Sound. Electric trams, antibiotics, pneumatic mail, printed records, mechanical calculators, reliable civic clocks. No ubiquitous digital network.
- Districts: Clockwork Quarter, Cathedral Rise, the Weirs, Green Span, Northglass, the Undercity, the Outer Sluice. Neighboring towns: Bellweather, Harrow, Saint Perrin.
- The Cathedral of Consequence is civic infrastructure with religious and political symbolism. It is not a church that owns the city and not a prophet.
- The Consequence Engine runs a **Reading** through the **Consequence Loom** using the **Civic Hours** network of **hourlines**. A Reading returns four parts: trace, pressure, blind margin, toll. Maximum one notice per chapter.
- The Hour Compact is forty-three years old. Its four safeguards: concrete action only, benefit and cost both shown, three independent witnesses, recorded physical toll.
- The Long Nine was eighteen years before the opening: nine minutes of citywide clock and communications failure, 212 dead, mostly in the Weirs. A storm met a failing tide-gate network while a Censor Gate was already in use.
- Continuity Order 9-17 was authorized by the six-member Emergency Continuity Committee. **Rusk was not one of the six.** As the Assembly's emergency secretary he drafted, numbered, circulated, and countersigned the order as the record, then spent eighteen years defending its expansion. His guilt is procedural and institutional, and that is enough.
- The order authorized the first emergency use of the Censor Gate. It was meant to filter impossible branches. The First Hour chamber was sealed by a separate Directorate order, 9-18, three days after the Long Nine. Edwin had no part in it. All six committee members are dead by the final volume; Rusk is the only one left who can be asked what it was for.
- Edwin Vale, Orrin's father and a senior regulator, opened a manual bypass during the Long Nine knowing the network would separate for nine minutes. He was listed missing, no body recovered. He left no prophecy, no guide voice, no secret master knowledge, and he did not solve the system. His notes are hidden in timing tolerances.
- The Censor Gate routes omitted paths through a buried return line to the Dark Reservoir, which stores excluded work, heat, fatigue, and lost time. It is not a collection of alternate futures or hidden people.
- The **routing plates** in the Gate hub decide what is routed away, engraved with categories rather than names: the earliest read *unstable path*, later sets read *unregistered* and then *not represented*. Removing them is the only irreversible act of the ending.
- The **return line** carries omitted strain to the Dark Reservoir. The **feedback float** rises on the reservoir, the **feedback line** carries its weight, and the float presses on the First Hour's **threshold spring**. The First Hour is the founding anti-cascade rule. As pressure rises it clamps divergent clocks toward one sequence. It is not a mind and not a late awakening; it holds a calibration history, not a memory.
- A **consensus lock** is the First Hour's clamp action. The **binding plates** are why an external order can be applied to the rule as a command rather than a recommendation. The Continuity Office's **command line** can carry such an order during a declared emergency. The reform's **kill-switch** is three release handles, any two of which sever **only the command line**; it removes the authority to order, not the rule, and it does not reach the Gate hub. The kill-switch and the routing plates are different instruments and neither substitutes for the other: the kill-switch is reversible and takes nine days, the plates come out once and cannot be put back. The climax order is fixed — hold the clocks apart, then take out the plates, then cut the command line.
- The emergency clause of the regional water and power scheduling act lets a declared regional emergency be answered "by any measure the responsible office certifies is necessary." It predates every reform, cannot be repealed by the Assembly alone, and is Rusk's instrument in the final volume.
- Toll types: clock toll (reserve out of public clocks), body toll (an interval of a living person’s attention, coordination, or memory — hour-sickness), network toll (slippage across districts), and the unrecorded **keeper’s toll** that a private reading takes from the machine’s reserve and then from the reader’s body. Officials call body tolls “calibration strain.”
- **Who pays a toll:** a question is filed naming a *paying district*, and the engine draws that district’s stored clock rotation. Nothing in the Compact requires the paying district to be affected, asked, or consenting, and the draw is proportional to what a district holds — so the cost falls hardest on districts with the least reserve and is a rounding error for districts with the most. An unaffiliated submitter may name any district in the city. Objecting after the fact is a matter for the Office of Public Reckoning and is not a defence against the charge. **Orrin files his Weir Two question charging the Clockwork Quarter in Chapter 4**, three minutes, because that district holds the most reserve and is the least affected. This is the moral engine of the series and it must not be softened or re-derived in later volumes.
- **A private sense is not a Reading.** Orrin’s stages 2 through 7 are listening, not calculation: no blind margin, no filed record, no admissibility, and in the private stages an unfiled keeper’s toll. Stage 7 suspends the central forecast; it does not suspend the requirement to record a toll. His sense is blind to whatever the network has routed away, so a group of people holding records is always more useful than he is. Using it alone is his flaw expressed as a technique.
- **Status of the four safeguards at Chapter 1:** (1) concrete action required — intact and enforced; (2) show the cost and state the omission — on paper, gutted by a footer nobody reads; (3) three witnesses — the affected-district resident station is replaceable by a written *district standing*, which is how Weirs Readings have been witnessed by Green Span residents for twenty years; (4) record the toll — clock tolls filed, body tolls recorded as calibration strain with no name and no duration. The certainty permit does not exist yet; it is created in Volume 03.
- Reform-era institutions, not to be named before they exist: district time councils, Reading rooms, the Council of Hours, the independent Civic Time Keeper, local instruments.

## Fixed people

- Orrin Vale, 32. Municipal clockmaker, line horologist, owner of Vale's Bench in the Clockwork Quarter. Rents a room above a bathhouse. Wants to find the ninth tick and clear Edwin's name; needs to accept that responsibility cannot be owned by one competent person; flaw is that he corrects systems before asking the people inside them what they need, and hides dangerous knowledge because secrecy feels safer than panic.
- Mara Venn, 33. Investigator for the Office of Public Reckoning. Mother Sera Venn died after a severe body toll during a sealed flood Reading. Wants legal and financial accountability. No magic, no safe access to a machine. The one slow-burn relationship. She is not an oracle, a healer, or a reward.
- Leda Vale, 27. Orrin's younger sister, emergency nurse in Green Span. Opposes his dangerous self-sacrifice and any plan that hides the cost.
- Teo Arden, 20. Unlicensed tuner and organizer in the Weirs. Apprentice, friend, ideological foil. Arc from covert theft to an accountable repair cooperative.
- Edwin Vale. Missing since the Long Nine. Limited human decision, not a myth.
- Anja Voss, 49, Cathedral Director. Sincere, not secretly evil, and not absolved.
- Halden Rusk, 58, Continuity Minister for the two years before the opening and still the holder of the post in the final volume. Final human antagonist. Defeated by records, refusal, and removal of the machine's monopoly. Not killed.
- Sel Kade, 44, Weirs pumping warden then tenant-council representative. Understandable, locally beneficial, structurally unjust.
- **Perry Vance, 41,** unlicensed tuner, twenty years on the Weir Two night pump crew. He is the man the opening Reading left out, and he is **not** Teo. He is the inciting death of the series and an accident, not a martyr.
- **Ada Vance, 13,** his daughter, a tram-hopper to school in Northglass. Volume is roughly a year, so her ages are fixed: 13 in Volumes 01–02, 20 in Volumes 08–09, 25 in Volume 13, 26 in Volume 14. She appears in Volume 01 as the child on the six o'clock tram, in Volume 02 as a thirteen-year-old witness in the archive fight who finds her father's name in a dated batch, in Volumes 08 and 09 as the adult running her own family's unlisted-records claim, at the Weirs council table in Volume 13, and in Volume 14 filing the batch into the public archive. She is an adult every time she speaks from Volume 08 on.
- **Corvin Hale,** the market broker who falsifies a Reading in Volume 03 by attaching a real blind margin to a real notice. A small criminal, not a plant.
- Orrin holds a **maintainer's license, class three**, issued through Hask's guild hall. Its **renewal expires on Sunday the 19th of the third month**, the **W-4 work book** is due with the fee, and the **optional interview is Tuesday the 28th of the second month**. It is suspended in Volume 01's resolution, costing him the right to certify a machine's local toll, and restored only provisionally in Volume 03. He certifies **nothing** in batch 0002 that he has no standing to certify, and the offence is reserved for Chapters 44–49.
- Gerren Hask, 55, guild master. The first antagonist is paperwork, not a monster.
- Ivo Pell, 36, veteran operator. Teaches, then joins the Continuity Office, then testifies.
- Nella Rook, 28, hospital clock nurse. Councilor Imani Rook, 40, is her aunt by blood; the kinship is stated canon, not a reveal.
- **Volume 01 functional cast, recorded in `bible/characters.md` §5a, and all now on the page.** Sindri Lake, Bevin Sarr, Wenna Pruitt and Alma Festinger appeared in batch 0001 and speak. **Tobias Rill and Della Marsh appeared in batch 0002 and speak**: Rill is the honest inspector whose finding is complete and useless, who also signed the trestle deferral two years ago, and Marsh is the person a form number comes out of, who is never thanked, and who names the hall's three doors to its own labour book. **Also on the page: Hester Lomas, 44,** hall copyist, nineteen years in the back room, who made the eleven copies and lost her place with no rule against it; **Ellen Reeve, 44,** hospital engineer at Green Span, twenty-two years, competent and unable to certify a district toll either; **Hanna Stroud, 54,** landlord of nine Weirs blocks, reasonable, not a villain, holding twelve years of rent book. Each has one clean function and none is a disguised existing character. Their guards: **Alma Festinger** is not a dupe, the law never required her to know, and she is **not asked again anywhere in Volume 01** — the second asking is Volume 06, in a gallery, and the reader should recognise her face; **Della Marsh is never thanked on the page**; **Hester Lomas is the face of the cost of paperwork, not an organiser, and does not recur after this volume**; **Ellen Reeve is a working person, not a device**; **Hanna Stroud must stay reasonable for the rest of the book.**
- **Anja Voss is named once in Volume 01, in Chapter 4, on a signature, and takes no scene, no offer, and no bargain.** Her offer of access is Volume 02’s **closing** turn. **Halden Rusk is named once in Volume 01, in Chapter 1, on a Continuity Office inspection authority, and takes no scene.** He is in the chair from Chapter 1 onward and is never a character in this volume. **Nella Rook is not named in Volume 01.**
- **Vance's death sequence is fixed and is the inciting event of the series:** the head passes after the gate opens, the opened gate and the running second stage drive the hand valve open again, the crew is not on the board, and **Vance goes down on his own initiative to close the valve he had shut himself in daylight that same afternoon.** Chapters 3, 4 and 5 are all the 17th, so the valve was shut that afternoon and he died that night; no later file may say "the day before" or "yesterday". Orrin is at the pump house beforehand, feels the failure correctly, and is refused at the door by a watchman who is not corrupt. Orrin is not the cause and is not blameless, and Chapter 5 says both. He certifies nothing in Chapter 9; the licence offence that costs him his trade is reserved for Chapters 44–49.
- **The reform provision is “a name and a duration” for a BODY toll, not for a clock toll.** A clock toll’s district and minutes have been on the Toll Stair for forty-three years. Body tolls have only ever been recorded as *calibration strain*. The Volume 01 resolution creates those two columns, and the first entry under them is Vance’s name with **the duration left blank**, because no instrument recorded the interval his shift lost. The blank is the point and the volume says so out loud.

## Fixed ending constraints

- The Returning Tide is a physically plausible cascade, not a magical apocalypse. The Sluice fails because of Volume 06's deferred inspection; the rail bridge is torn out so the river can pass; the tide still has to go somewhere and the Weirs are still the lowest ground. No new cosmic enemy, god, or invasion.
- The final plan is distinguished from the First Hour's by consent, not by arithmetic, and the volume must show the four variables the machine cannot carry: a counterweight somebody else agreed to, a district deciding for itself, a named end to the loss, and a choice made before the water arrived. The public plan is still worse for some people than the machine's, and the book says so out loud.
- Orrin's final act is to remove the Censor Gate control and routing plates, publish the full cost map, and open an interval in which districts act without one binding forecast.
- The plan costs twenty-three named deaths, eleven days without power in the Weirs, a season of Northglass rail, and the permanent fusion of the central engine. The losses are recorded, not softened. **Twenty-three is the tide only.** The Long Nine dead are 212 and are a different number about a different event; never add them, never let one stand in for the other.
- The ending state: a Council of Hours with rotating district delegates and no command authority, a public archive of tolls and refusals, a maintenance budget protected from emergency diversion, and a kill-switch split among three independent stations. The Cathedral becomes a public workshop, archive, and equipment hall with limited local instruments.
- Fully answered: the ninth tick, the missing tolls, the Unlisted, the Long Nine, Continuity Order 9-17, the Dark Reservoir, the First Hour, Rusk's role.
- Intentionally unresolved: the original engineers' private thoughts, the lived intervals that cannot be returned, and whether the First Hour had any rudimentary awareness.
- Final image: bells out of order across the city, a public clock with its prediction plate removed, and a city that keeps acting.
