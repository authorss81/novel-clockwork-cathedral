# Chapter 56 — What the Protection Is Worth

A process that can only find against its author is a trial. She wrote two of the four clauses of the one they had in the pumping ward in the fifth month and she has said in public, more than once, that the third clause is the one she cares about, because a review that may find against and may find for and shall enter both is the only kind there is.

On the Friday the twenty-fourth of the sixth month it was used for the second time, by somebody else, on a ground she did not write, and it found against the office, and the office is her.

---

The ratepayer is called in the notice and a ratepayer who calls a review is named, and her name went on the notice in a copperplate hand on a piece of card the size of a playing card, and the card went up on the ward board outside the Weir Two pump house on the Tuesday the twenty-first at about ten in the morning, and it stayed up for the three days the process requires.

> **REVIEW CALLED. Under clause two of the warden's variation process, at the request of a ratepayer of the ward, who is named.**
> **Ratepayer: Martha Bray, 63, of 6B, Ferrywright Row.**
> **Ground named: that a variation of the canal front's daytime draw, entered at this office on the ninth day of the second month and operative from that day, was not posted on the ward board within one working day of its being made, and was not posted at all until the fifteenth, six days later, and that six days is the whole of the complaint and it is not about the draw.**
> **Review: Friday, the twenty-fourth instant. Eleven o'clock. The room over the shop.**

"I have written it on the card myself," Martha Bray said, on the Friday morning, in the room, "because a card is a thing you can take back to a man who says you did not write it. I did not ask anybody to write it for me and nobody did, and I am sixty-three and I can write a card."

She was asked, by the clerk, whether she had been put up to it, which is a question the process does not require and which somebody in the room asked anyway, and she answered it in one sentence.

"I have lived in this row thirty-one years and the water has come up my steps twice and I have not seen anybody from this office since nineteen fifty-one, and if I was put up to anything it was by the rain."

---

**The variation was entered on the ninth of the second month and posted on the fifteenth.** It was a deputy who entered it. The deputy's name is on the return and the deputy was in the room, and she is twenty-nine and has been in the warden's office for three years, and she was asked nothing by anybody because nobody thought to ask her anything, and then Wenna Pruitt read the return out, and then everybody looked at her.

"I entered it," she said. "I entered it in the book and I put it on the return and I put the quantity and the beneficiary and the date on it, and I did not take it to the board, and I did not forget it, because I have written it on the return twice since. I did not take it to the board because there was a queue at the counter on the ninth and a queue on the tenth and a queue on the eleventh, and because on the ninth the office was the office and there was a man from the works in it asking for a return."

"You should have taken it to the board," said Wenna Pruitt.

"Yes," said the deputy.

"Clauses," said Wenna Pruitt, to nobody in particular. "I am going to say what the clause says and then I am going to stop. Clause one: a variation is entered in the ward's book with quantity, beneficiary and date, and is posted on the ward board within one working day of its being made. Both halves. The half about the book is not the half that matters. The half about the board is."

Nobody argued with that and nobody needed to.

---

**And then Sel Kade, who had not spoken for an hour and a half, said the thing that the finding was going to be anyway and made the room put it in the right place.**

"I am not going to let this be about her," said Sel Kade. "I have been in this office four months in this year and she has been in it three years, and the clause is mine, all four of them, and I wrote the first and the third and I brought the second in and I said then, in this room, that the second was not mine, and the fourth is Mrs Pruitt's and forbids thanking anybody in the finding and I have never once read it out.

"This is a review of a variation. Variations in this district are entered by this office. There are two people in this office. So the finding is against this office and I am this office, and I am asking the clerk to write it that way, and I am asking her to write *this office* and not *the deputy*, and I am telling you why I am asking, and the reason is not modesty and it is not that she is twenty-nine.

"The reason is that the whole point of a variation being posted within a working day is that a person who is affected has a day to object before it starts. And the reason we could not post it on the ninth is that I sent her to the counter instead, because there was a queue and I am the one who sent her, and I have done that eleven times this year, and I have a clerk's card with the queue times on it and I have never shown it to anybody because I did not think anybody would ever want to see it.

"So the finding is against this office. And I am this office. And I would like it minuted that I said so before the finding was read, because in about four years there will be somebody who wants to make this about a girl of twenty-nine and I would like the minute to say that a man who is the office asked to be named first."

The clerk wrote it down. Nobody thanked the clerk, either, and the fourth clause forbids thanking anybody in the finding and does not forbid thanking the clerk outside it, and nobody did.

---

The finding went on the ward board in chalk on the afternoon of the Friday the twenty-fourth, in a copyist's hand, and it is four paragraphs, of which the four numbered sentences are the operative ones, and **it may be read in full on any working day between ten and four in the outer room of the pumping ward**, and the third paragraph is the sentence the district will still be arguing about in three years.

> **1.** A variation of the daytime draw on the canal front was entered at this office on the ninth day of the second month and was operative from that day and was posted on the fifteenth. That is six days.
> **2.** Clause one requires posting within one working day. The clause was not complied with. **The review finds against this office.**
> **3.** The review also finds, and is required to find, and the finding is entered without thanks to anybody: that the practice which caused the failure is lawful, and that the queue at the counter on the ninth was lawful, and that a deputy of this office doing the work of this office at the counter of this office is lawful, and that **the only thing in this business that was not lawful is that the variation was not posted, and it was not posted because there is no rule that says the person who writes a thing down must also carry it to a board.**
> **4.** This review binds nobody to do anything. It is not an order. It is a finding, entered in two hands, and it will be read again in whatever year the next one is called.

Martha Bray read it twice and then asked the only question she had wanted to ask, which she had written on the back of the playing card.

"Is this the right?" said Martha Bray. "Will it stop the next one?"

"No," said Wenna Pruitt. "It stops nothing. It enters both halves in a book that exists, and in about four years somebody in an office will open this book, and will either find that the practice is normal or find that it is not, and either way it is written down and it was not written down before. That is all a review does. It is not a court and it is not a punishment and it binds nobody, and it is the only instrument this district owns."

"Then it is not worth the card," said Martha Bray.

"It is worth exactly the card," said Wenna Pruitt, "and the card is the point, and I am not going to explain that to you because you wrote the card."

---

**The rest of the room was the arithmetic, and the arithmetic was all anybody wanted, and it is as follows and it is on the board in the pumping ward's outer room and it is going to be on a board in a committee room in the ninth month.**

The standing protection the committee gave on the fourth of the sixth is worth about three months. It runs until the guild's labour-ledger report is received by the Assembly's public-health committee and it expires at the first sitting after the report and not before, and the report is due at that committee's sitting in the ninth month, and the audit behind the report takes about fourteen months. **The committee has not undertaken to renew the protection and has no power to promise that it will.**

The maintenance-deferred class is to be abolished by the hall within twelve months of the first of the sixth, and any site in it is to be entered in the ordinary class or not at all, and the ordinary class is **two shillings the head for a year and one shilling where a man is the occupier.**

And the ward's water rate is made on the return, and correcting the return puts the standing back out, and the rate goes up whether the class is abolished or not, at **one shilling and tenpence in the pound against nine pence, on forty-eight thousand pounds, which is two thousand six hundred pounds on the ward, which is thirteen shillings a year on a rated house of twelve pounds.**

Bessie Crane put her number down and Ivy Pike put hers down, and they were in the room at the same time, and one of them said:

"If the hours go in the return, then the pump ward is a place the model can see, and the model can be asked about it, and there is a form on which a station on the witness rail takes a person of the district and there is nobody in the district to put there, and if the hours go in the return then there is somebody to put there. I have wanted that since January. I have wanted it more than I have wanted my own back."

And the other one said:

"Then thirty-one women are in the return at the ward rate, and the ward goes up a shilling and a penny in the pound, and I have a laundry eleven years old that is a penny a sheet under the trade price, and I am not saying she should not. I am saying that nobody in this room is going to say it to her face in October, and if I go to that room in October with a return in my hand then I am the one who did it, and I would rather be in here in June being the woman who did not."

"And there it is," said Bevin Sarr, from the wall, where she was writing in a night book that is not a record of the guild and not a record of the works. "That is the eleventh time I have watched two people in this district refuse to choose and it is the eleventh time both of them have been right, and I have stopped saying anything about it, and the reason I have stopped is that it is not a habit. It is the method. **A district is a place. That is the eleventh time and it is going to be the hundredth and it does not go away.**"

---

Orrin Vale was in that room from eleven o'clock to half past one and he did not speak once, and there is exactly one sentence anybody says about why, and it is not Orrin's, it is Bevin Sarr's, and she said it at the door on the way out and said it to him and not to the room.

"You have got a number about that branch," she said, "that nobody in this room has asked for and everybody in this room wants, and if you had said it in there at about twelve o'clock this afternoon then everything that happened in that room after twelve o'clock would have been a thing that happened because a man from the Quarter stood up in a room over a shop and said a number. And you would have been right. And they would have done it. And in four years it would have been a thing that happened because of a number, and there would be no way in the world of telling anybody the room had decided it by itself before he got there.

"So you sat there for two hours and a half and you said nothing, and I have watched a great many men in this city who could not do that, and I have watched about four who could, and one of them is standing in front of me, and I am not going to say his name in a room, and you would not want me to, and that is the whole of it and there is no charge."

---

**And at ten past one, on the Saturday the twenty-fifth, with nine people in the room, Sel Kade said the other thing, in advance, out loud, and asked for it to be written down and read back, which is a thing that a person does when they mean it.**

"I am going to stand down from the appointed post at the next quarter," she said. "The date is the first of the ninth month and it is printed on a return in an office and I have not told the district and I am telling it now because the district should have just under ten weeks.

"And I am going to say why, and I want it in the book with my name against it, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would like the fourth clause enforced.

"Clause three. A review may find against and may find for and shall enter both, because a review that can only find against somebody is not a review, it is a trial. I wrote that on the back of a warden's return at eleven o'clock on a Tuesday night in May and I was proud of it and I still am.

"And it has been in operation four and a half weeks. It has found against a deputy, and it found against me in the twenty-fifth of May, and both of those findings were correct, and **both of them cost somebody money, and neither of them cost me anything, because I am the one who answers.** I have been the person who answers for nineteen years and I have never once been the person who pays, and a district where one person answers and nobody pays is not a district, it is a queue with a woman at the front of it.

"**There is no form in this city by which a district may be asked what it wants.** That is not a complaint about a hall or a committee or the nine men with a property qualification. It is the actual finding of this process, and it took four and a half weeks and a woman of sixty-three with a playing card, and I am going to write it into a return and hand it in and then go and be a person in a room that is allowed to be asked."

Nobody said anything. Martha Bray asked whether she was to put it on the card.

"No," said Sel Kade. "If it goes on a card it is a complaint and I will be a woman who complained. Put it in the return, and put the date on it, and do not thank anybody, including the person who types it, and go home."
