# Chapter 68 — The Same Four Lines

The Office of Public Reckoning audits tolls. It has audited the Toll Stair for nine years, which is a decision about a stair and therefore a decision about nothing, and since the third of the sixth month it has audited a second set of columns in a glass case in a pumping ward on the canal road, and a Reading's notice carries a toll on it, and a toll is the one thing in this city that office has a lawful function in.

That is the whole reason he was allowed to see it, and it is not a good enough reason, and everybody in the room knew it.

---

She had it out of a box on the Wednesday the tenth of the eighth month at about half past eleven, and it took four minutes to find and about two seconds to read, and it is a printed card, cream, about five inches by three, with a hairline rule at the top and four lines under it and a fifth line in type half the size of the others.

> **QUESTION RECEIVED**
> *Trace: On any night the fourth window is worked, the Weir Two branch takes a second step at the far end, and the Weir One gate opens late.*
> *Pressure: The canal front is short between the second window and the fourth window on any night the fourth window is worked. The loss is largest at the far end of the branch and increases with the distance from the pumping station.*
> *Blind margin: Unregistered district labour is not represented.*
> *Toll: Nine district minutes and one witness interval.*

And under the rule, in half-size type, which is the same size as a witness's signature and smaller than a clerk's:

> *Dated the ninth day of the third month, nineteen years ago. Witnessed at the rail by three, of whom the third was a person of the affected district, that person being of Green Span and of the second-Wednesday rota, and being at the time twenty-six years of age. Paid district: Lower Weirs. Entered, asked for, and read.*

**It is a lawful Reading. Every one of the four parts is true, all four were true on the night it was made, and the third witness was a person of the affected district, which is the whole requirement, and there was no standing in that district and it did not matter, because a person is a person.**

---

The first thing he said, and it is the sentence the whole morning is built on, and he said it standing up in a window that has one chair in it and both of them in it.

"It is my sentence."

"It is your sentence," said Mara Venn, "written by a machine in about four seconds, and it is nineteen years old, and it was on a board in the Question Hall for the period the ordinance requires, and nobody in this city has ever mentioned it to me, including the man who wrote the words down that night and including me."

She put it flat on the sill between them.

"Now compare it. Slowly, and out loud, and if you cannot say the differences in words then you have not compared it."

He did it, and it took about four minutes, and it is the whole of the argument in three parts.

**The trace.** *On any night the fourth window is worked, the branch takes a second step at the far end, and the Weir One gate opens late.* That is the ninth of June, six readings, in daylight, with two watches, on a wall in a pump house in chalk: **it is a ramp and not an offset, and the far end is the worst end.** Nine minutes and twenty seconds, then eleven, then forty minutes and fifty seconds, then twenty-two and thirty, then seventeen and forty. The shape is not a straight line and he has never known why, and the machine's line says the loss increases with distance from the pumping station and stops there, because the machine is not guessing either.

**The pressure.** *The canal front is short between the second window and the fourth window.* That is the warden's own gauge sheet, and it is what Marta Dey said at about half past three on the Saturday morning of the twenty-third of the seventh when she read a gauge at thirty-four instead of forty-one and rang a room, and it is the four dates in the night book in the pump house, and it is the reason a gate opened at about ten to seven instead of about five for two hours and ten minutes on the second of the seventh, and a standpipe at the second court ran dry for four hours on the fifth.

**The blind margin.** *Unregistered district labour is not represented.* That is a footer half the size of a witness's signature, and it is the same four words that have come back on every answer this district has ever received since the first month of the year, and it is the sentence a man of twenty said in a room over a shop in the fifteenth of the sixth, standing up, to about eleven people, and he was right, and he was not the first person to be right and he never will be.

"The toll," said Mara Venn. "Go on. That is the fourth one and you have been avoiding it."

"**Nine district minutes, paid by the Lower Weirs, nineteen years ago, in a month when nobody in this district knew there was a question and the district could not have been told and had no form on which to object.** And one witness interval, and I do not know whose, and the Office has no authority to obtain a health file and no power to certify an interval it has not observed, and you told a branch that in the sixth month and it came back received and noted, and it is the best answer anybody in this city has given anybody all year."

"Yes," said Mara Venn. "Now say the other thing, which is the thing this office has, and which I have been sitting here for two days deciding whether to say."

"The machine did not know one thing I did not already know."

"**Not one thing.** And it took four days and a witness rail to say it in a form that can be used in a room, and I am the room, and I have four staff and a written right to be ignored and I cannot put it in a return, and the only difference between the two of them — between you and it — is that the four days and the rail are what it cost, and it cost them out of a district that was not asked. **You knew it on the ninth of June with two watches. It knew it in four seconds and could not have told you until a man stood on a rail, and by then it was nineteen years old and a man of twenty-six from Green Span was the only person in the district who was in the room, and she was standing in for forty thousand people who were not, and she was twenty-six, and it is lawful, and it has been lawful for nineteen years.**"

Nobody put a number on that and neither of them tried. There is no column in this city for the size of a thing nobody has counted, and the one instrument in it that could count is sitting in a box upstairs with a fee of three shillings, and its application is received and not produced and not refused, and is set for the twenty-first of the first month of the year after next.

---

**And the part of it that is about the rail, and Mara Venn says it once and does not repeat it, and it is not in any form.**

"The third signature is a person of Green Span," she said. "She is in a rota book in a drawer in this office with a date against her name and her age, and she is forty-five now, and the Office may not publish the name of a person who stood on a rail, and the Question Hall may not either, and there is no form on which to ask her what she was told, and there never has been one, and if there were one she would be the wrong person to ask, because she was twenty-six and in a room with three men and a brass frame and a thing nobody had ever explained to her.

"And she stood in the one chair in this city that is filled by somebody from anywhere, and the chair is a chair because in about 1920 somebody wrote a rule to say that a district standing may be substituted for a resident, and the rule is excellent, and it works exactly as intended, and **the only thing wrong with it is that there has never been a district standing to substitute, and the chair has been empty in the sense that matters for nineteen years and full in the sense that can be seen.**"

She shut the box.

"There are thirty-eight cards in the drawer," she said. "Thirty-four on the sixth of the seventh. Eleven on the seventeenth of the third. **That is about six a month across the three and a half months, and since the sixth of the seventh it has gone up four in five weeks, which is not a different rate, it is a smaller number.** It has never once been emptied, and the thing you are going to want to do with what is on that card is to write to her, and you are not going to, because you would have to tell her why and she would have to answer and neither of you would know what the answer was for."

---

**On the Saturday the thirteenth of the eighth month at about ten in the morning he went up Cathedral Rise with the card in a pocket and put three questions into a spindle at a public counter, and the spindle refused all three, and the refusal was the best thing that happened to him that month.**

The counter is public and takes a question in about four minutes. The clerk of the Question Hall is the one of twenty-six who gave him the three requirements on the eleventh of the seventh and who told him his form was a return, and she has a counter card with a printed form number on it and she took all three without looking up and she read them back the way she reads everything, which is as though they were somebody else's and she is only the voice.

**One.** *That the Lower Weirs pumping district be brought within the civic model.* Refused.

**Two.** *That the return of the Lower Weirs pumping district be entered in the assessment.* Refused.

**Three.** *That the district of the Lower Weirs be entered as an affected district in respect of all questions.* Refused.

"That is three," said the clerk, "and I am going to tell you why in one sentence, and then I am going to say the other thing, and the other thing is the reason I have not got out of my chair."

"The spindle takes a thing somebody could physically do."

"Yes. And there is no instrument in this city by which a district is brought within the model. **It is not out of the model because of a form. It is out of the model because of what is in it.** A pumping district is eleven miles of main with every joint entered as a point, two stations and their gates, four stopcocks, six clocks and one hospital branch as a draw, and a man is not an instrument and a man's shift is not an instrument, and the thing that puts a man in there is not a hydraulic return and is not a form anywhere in this city at all.

"**So you are asking the spindle to take a question about a rate return, and a rate return is not a physical act, it is an account of about eight thousand properties, and if it were corrected — and it should be, and the auditor says so in writing, in four pages, on two boards, with the figure left blank because the figure is the ward's and not hers — then about nine thousand people in that district are in the model, and the ward's water rate is a shilling and tenpence in the pound instead of nine, and the notice goes up in the autumn with a number on it and no name on it, and a laundry on Bellweather Road with thirty-one women in it is a large part of why the number is what it is.**

"The spindle does not know that. **Neither do I. And I am not going to work out whether I would vote for it.**"

Nobody said anything for a while, and the counter behind her went on with its ordinary Wednesday.

"That is the safeguard," said Orrin Vale.

"It is a rule about what a question has to be, and it has never been wrong, and nobody in this city has ever complained about it, and **you have been in this building since January and I have watched you come in here with six numbers and no form and a woman of twenty-six and a chalkboard, and you have never once been refused anything, and you have begun, in about six months, to think of this counter as an inconvenience.** That is not a criticism, it is the reason I have said it. **The only thing that has kept this city out of a very large amount of trouble is that a machine will not be asked a question it cannot be told the answer to, and in six months of being right you had almost stopped noticing that the reason it would not answer you was that it could not.**"

He thanked her. She said it was nothing, in the way a person says that at a counter, and he went down the step and stood on it for about four minutes, which he has done before.

---

**And then he did the last thing, which was the thing the four pages in the grey book had been leading to, and it is a question and not an answer.**

The card went into the inside pocket of a coat that has no licence in it. Nineteen years, four parts, a toll paid by a district that could not be asked, and a woman of twenty-six standing in a chair in place of everybody.

He got the grey book out on the step, which is the third time in this year that he has got a book out on a step outside the Question Hall, and wrote four lines, and the fourth one is the one that matters and it is the last thing on the page.

> *Tenth to the thirteenth of the eighth. Three things, and none of them is a number.*
> *One: the machine did not know one thing I did not already know, and it took four days and a witness rail to say it in a form that can be used in a room, and the rail was filled by a person from Green Span who was twenty-six, and there is no form on which to ask her what she was told, and the Office may not publish her name and the Hall may not, and she is forty-five and is in a rota book in a drawer with a date against it.*
> *Two: the spindle will not take a question about my district, three times, and the reason is not a rule against me. The reason is that a district is not in the model because of a form. It is in or out according to what is in it, and what is in it is pipes and gates and stopcocks and six clocks, and the only thing that would put a man in it is a measurement of a man's hours, and nobody has ever taken one, and if they did it would be a rateable figure and the rate would go up in the autumn and a woman of about sixty with thirty-one women in a laundry would be a large part of the reason.*
> *Three, and it is the only one of the three that I did not know on the ninth of June, and I am putting it on this page because a page in a shop is worth nothing in any other room in this city and I have got into the habit of putting the worst things where nobody will find them:*
> ***A safeguard is a rule about what a question has to be. A shortcut is a way of asking it without the rule. I have spent this year building a case that the rules are too narrow, and on Saturday morning a woman at a counter told me that the narrowness is the only reason the machine has never once been wrong, and I have not got an answer, and the answer is not going to come from anybody who wants me to be right.***
> *And on the eleventh of the seventh she told me a form is not a body. And on the twenty-seventh of the seventh I wrote four pages and got to a wall. And on the first of the eighth nine men with a property qualification took forty-four minutes and ten seconds out of my best number, lawfully, on a Monday. And on the sixth of the eighth I got a thing I have wanted for seven months and it cost forty-one seconds and gave me a thing I use every day of my life and cannot get back.*
> *A body is what lodges the instrument. I still do not know what a body that is not a body signs with. Nobody in this city does. **And the only office in this country that will tell you what you may not ask is the counter I have been going to for eight months, and it is run by a woman of twenty-six who is very good at her job, and on Saturday she made a speech, and there was nobody to minute it.***
