# Chapter 52 — Outside the Reliable Model

On the Sunday morning of the twelfth of the sixth month about forty people read six numbers chalked on a wall in a pump house, and by the afternoon there were three versions of what the numbers meant, and all three of them were wrong, and none of the three was anybody's fault.

---

What the front actually said, over about six hours, standing in a queue for the water:

That the clocks on the hill were out by three-quarters of an hour. That was Mrs Dench's arithmetic, and it is out by rather more than that, and the reason it is out by rather more than that is that the two clocks on the hill are not out by the same amount, and there is no way of arriving at that from a street, and Mrs Dench is fifty-four and has kept a coal book for thirty years and does not do division in her head.

That the pumping station had gone wrong. Nobody knows who said this one. It is the version people want, because a station can be reported and a branch cannot.

That somebody was going to be sacked. This one was Ivy Pike's, and she is a woman who signs for the Weir One gate and has signed for it for eleven years, and she said it looking at the four figures on the board the whole time, and then she took it back herself, in front of about nine people, and said: *No. There is nobody to sack, because the two things this is about are a water act and a schedule and a clock, and a water act is Parliament's and a schedule is nine men's and a clock is a thing in a case, and I have signed for the gate for eleven years and I have not once been asked a question about the timing of it and there is not a form on which I could answer one if I was asked.*

---

Ruth Ashby came into the pumping ward at about ten o'clock on the Monday the thirteenth of the sixth month, and she had a folding rule of her own and a brass weight and a book with a rubber band round it, and she asked for the board and read it twice, and then she asked whether the man who wrote it was in the building.

Orrin Vale said that he was.

"Then I will say to you first, in front of the warden's clerk, because I do this for a living and you should not hear anything from me that I have not written down in front of another person," said Ruth Ashby. "My name is Ruth Ashby. I am an auditor of district reliability in the returns and scheduling office of the Directorate, which is a title nobody has ever been able to say quickly, and I have held it for nine years, and I am not a person who comes down here to catch anybody, and I want that on the record before I read you anything."

"There is nobody here to catch," said Orrin Vale. "I have no licence. I cannot certify a machine and I cannot certify a toll and I wrote six numbers on that wall with two watches in my coat."

"I know you have no licence," said Ruth Ashby. "Your class is suspended. It is on a board in a hall in the Quarter and the hall publishes it, and I have read it, and I want to say that I have read it and that it has nothing whatever to do with me and that I have not given it a thought this morning. Mr Vale, I am not here about your licence. I am here about the number on that wall, and the number on that wall is the most useful thing anybody has put up in this district since I came into this office, and I want to tell you that before I tell you the other thing, because the other thing is going to sound as though it is about you and it is not."

She opened the book and squared it on the counter, the way a person does with a book they intend to be exact in.

---

Her return is four pages and it is dated the thirteenth of the sixth month and it is called a **statement of district reliability, Lower Weirs pumping district, pumping year to the thirtieth of the sixth**, and it is public, and it goes on a board in a public street the same afternoon, and three things in it are true and one of them is the sentence the whole of this is about.

The first true thing is the return itself. The district's **certified return for the pumping year is nil.** No hours, no shifts, no men, nothing in it. The water authority's return for that district has been certified nil for eighteen years, and the last year in which it carried anything was the year before the storm month, and the reason is in a copybook in a building on the other side of the hill and in the margin of a public index: **forty-one heads were taken out of the district's standing on form GH-9 at two shillings the head for a year, in the storm month eighteen years ago, and the form series takes a district out by removing its people from the return, and nobody had to do anything else.**

The second true thing is the alarm. **The pumping ward's low-level alarm on its reserve has been inhibited since the second of the third month.** That is in the district's own record and it was on a gauge sheet in a corridor in a hospital, and the district works has a line item for it at two pounds fourteen shillings and sixpence in programme year three, because a joint is a joint.

The third true thing is her own arithmetic, and it is the only part of the four pages that is difficult to argue with, and she read it out.

"The Cathedral maintains a hydraulic return for every district drawing on a common main, and the return is what the model is built out of. It is not a description of a district. It is the set of numbers the model runs on. And a hydraulic return is only as good as the instruments that feed it.

"Your district's instruments are a reserve with an inhibited alarm and a branch whose far end is one hour and forty-five minutes out of the district reference. I have not come down here to check your arithmetic, Mr Vale, because your arithmetic is right — I have had it looked at by a man who does this for a living and he says it is right, and I have told him that if he says anything else I will put it in this return and he does not want that.

"What your arithmetic does is tell me that the numbers this return is built on are eleven years old, and that a reserve which should read forty to forty-two pounds reads thirty-four at the far end of a mile and a quarter of conduit, and that a machine at the end of your branch is out by more time than most of the wards in this city can afford. So the hydraulic return for the Lower Weirs does not describe the Lower Weirs. It describes the Lower Weirs as it was in the year before the storm, and the difference between those two districts is a set of changes that never reached a form."

She turned the page over.

"And here is the sentence, and I have written it exactly as I am going to read it, and I would like you to let me read it twice.

"A district whose own instruments are not reporting cannot be a district the model is wrong about."

---

She read it twice. She read it the way a clerk reads a required line, flatly, without any weight on it at all, and then she said:

"That is not a sentence I wrote to be cruel and it is not a sentence I have any pleasure in, and I have had it in drafts since the second of the third month and I have taken three months and a bit to be sure of it, and I will tell you why I took four months, because it is the only interesting thing in this business.

"I tried it the other way round for about six weeks. *A district whose instruments are out of report is a district the model must be corrected for.* And it is a true sentence too, and it is much more useful, and I could have put it in the return, and if I had put it in the return then in about nine months somebody in this city would have had to go and look at the instruments in this district and would have found the alarm and the branch and the joint and the two pounds fourteen shillings and sixpence, and one of those four would have been repaired because a return had to be corrected.

"I did not write that sentence because I checked what it would cost, and I checked it in your own district's figures, and I checked it in about half an hour. I am required to say what a return means. I am not required to say what a district may afford, and I looked, and there is no column in my form for what a district may afford, and I did not go and find one, and I am not going to pretend to you that I did not look."

Orrin Vale stood at the counter of the pumping ward's outer room and listened to all of it, and the thing he was thinking about, and could not have said out loud without saying more than he had come to say, was a form.

There is a form in this city. It is a standing withdrawal and reinstatement, series GH-9, and it costs two shillings the head for a year and one shilling where a man is the occupier, and he filled one in for forty-one heads on the first of the fifth month, alone, with his own money, at a counter, in four minutes, with a wet stamp, and told nobody, and the four-line card that came back says the crew is part of a certified shift for that year.

**He knows that a district's hours are the price of the ward's rates, and that he said so to himself on the first of the fifth month before he said it to anybody, and that he has never in his life paid a rate.**

---

"What I am here to offer you is in the second half of the return," said Ruth Ashby, "and I want to be careful, because I know what a document with an offer in the second half of it sounds like, and I have been in this office nine years and I have seen what the city does with offers.

"The district can be brought inside the model. It is not difficult and it is not expensive in the sense of money. A pumping district acquires a standing under the water act by entering its hours. The hours go in the return, the return becomes current, the model is corrected against instruments it can rely on, and from that day the district is inside every hydraulic answer this city gives, in the correct position, as a district, and not as a list of properties.

"The hours can be entered by a ratepayer of the district, or by the district's own office, or by a person holding a standing withdrawal and reinstatement in the series. The cost of the standing is two shillings the head for a year and one shilling where a man is the occupier. The re-rate follows the return, because a ward's water rate is made on its return, and the return is made on the hours.

"I am not authorized to raise your rate and I am not authorized to promise you that it will not rise, and I have looked, and it will rise, and it will rise on the same people the hours belong to. I have written that in the return. I have written the rate line in the return and I have left the column for the figure blank, because the figure is the ward's and not mine, and I would not put a number in a column I have not been asked for and cannot defend."

She closed the book.

"That is the whole of what I have. It is not a threat and it is not a favour and there is nothing in it for me. I get a return either way and I would rather have a return I can defend than one I cannot."

---

He asked her the question in the corridor outside, at about half past twelve, and it is the only question of the day that was really his.

"Miss Ashby. If the district came in tomorrow and the hours went in and the rate went up and you had it in the model — what would be in the model?"

She did not answer it in the corridor. She got the book out again, on the windowsill, and found a page, and read it out flatly.

> **INSTRUMENTS ADMITTED TO THE HYDRAULIC RETURN, LOWER WEIRS PUMPING DISTRICT, on admission.**
> Weir One, station clock and gate. Weir Two, station clock and second-stage. Two pumping stations.
> Four stopcocks. Eleven miles of district main, with the position of every joint entered as a point.
> The canal front, as a draw, with the four draws timed.
> Four district clocks, being Weir Lane, Bellweather Road, the second court and the coal wharf.
> One hospital branch, being the Green Span feed, as a draw and not as a supply.
> *The above are the instruments. A district does not enter the model. A district's clocks and its gates and its pipes enter the model, and a district is inside it when its instruments are in it, and there is no other way in and there is no other test.*

"There," she said. "That is the list and it is the whole of what would be in there. Eleven miles of pipe. Two pumping stations and their gates. Four stopcocks. Five clocks on the district, and one branch. Six clocks, and she who wrote them out counted."

"And the men," said Orrin Vale.

"The men are not on that list," said Ruth Ashby, "and they are not on any list in the Cathedral, and that is not an insult to the men and it is not a judgement about the men. A man is not an instrument. A man's shift is not an instrument. What is in the model is the machine and the load and the time, and if you want a man in there then the thing that puts a man in there is not a hydraulic return and it is not a form in this city at all, and I have looked for that as well, and I would tell you if I had found it."

---

The return went on the board outside the Weir Two pump house at about four o'clock on the Monday the thirteenth and on the board outside the district works' yard on the canal road the same afternoon, because a statement of district reliability goes on two boards and the ordinance does not offer anybody a third place to put it.

Four hundred people walk past a ward board in a day in a district of a hundred and forty thousand. About ninety of them can read the whole of a page of small type. On the Tuesday morning the number that had been read off the board and repeated in the front and in two courts and in the queue for the water was not the hour and three-quarters at the far end of the branch.

It was the third line of the second page.

> **FINDING.** That the Lower Weirs pumping district is not, at the date of this statement, within the reliable model; and that the district is not to be treated as an affected district in respect of any question put to the Consequence Engine; and that no answer returned in respect of this district is to be cited as a forecast of conditions in it.
>
> *The auditor has not found any person in fault. The auditor has not found any person in fault.*

That second line is the reason a person who read the whole thing would sit down, and it is in the return because the office's own form requires the phrase, and it is the first sentence in four pages that is a kindness with a number on it.

---

At about eleven o'clock that night Orrin Vale sat down on the end of a bed in a rented room above a bathhouse and wrote in the grey ledger, and the entry is four paragraphs and the fourth one is the reason any of this is still going on.

> *Ninth of the sixth. Six readings on a board in a pump house.*
> *Thirteenth of the sixth. A statement of district reliability, four pages, two boards, and the finding is that the Lower Weirs is not within the reliable model and that no answer returned in respect of this district may be cited as a forecast of conditions in it.*
> *And the statement is true, and every figure in it is true, and the woman who wrote it told me in advance that she had looked for a cheaper sentence and had not found one on her form, and she put a rate line in it with the figure left blank because the figure is the ward's and not hers.*
> *Sixth of the fifth. Eighty-two shillings, form GH-9, forty-one heads, a year, my own money, a wet stamp, and I told nobody. The hall's card came back in four hours and said the crew is part of a certified shift for that year.*
> *She has just offered the same thing to a hundred and forty thousand people as a matter of policy, and it will cost them a rate, and she has written the rate line in the return with the figure left blank, and she is not hiding anything and she is not being kind to me.*
> *It is the same document. It is the same document at the scale of one counter and at the scale of a ward, and I am the only person in Morrowfen who can see both at once, and I have been the only person in Morrowfen who can see both at once since the first of the fifth month, and on the first of the fifth month I thought that made me useful.*
> *It does not make me useful. It makes me the only person in the city who is in a position to know that a thing which was reasonable at a counter was reasonable for a reason that does not scale, and there is no form on which I can put that, and there is no column for it, and I have told nobody and I am not going to and I do not know what I am going to do instead.*
