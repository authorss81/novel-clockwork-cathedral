## Batch goal

Open Volume 02 on a number instead of a bang, and establish the volume's antagonist, which is a **nil return** and not a villain.

The line out of the Weir Two pumping station has been given four pumping cycles a day since the fifteenth of the third month, lawfully, on a schedule the Water Users' Committee re-voted twice. It takes its beat off the odd teeth, so every machine in the chain is a further step from the root, and after eighty-five nights the far end of the line is out by more than an hour and forty-five minutes. The water is failing in the Weirs because a gate that should have opened at four o'clock opened at a quarter to six. **Nothing is broken. The city is simply being wrong faster than it can be told.**

And a district that is out of time cannot be shown to be failing, because the district's own return is nil, and a nil return is a *good* return, because there is nothing in it to go wrong. A Directorate auditor arrives with a table and a true sentence: *a district whose own instruments are not reporting cannot be a district the model is wrong about.* The reader's anger should be entirely reasonable and entirely useless, exactly as in Chapter 25.

The batch's genre promise: **a scale is a different kind of horror from a man.** Volume 01 argued about one column and one empty cell, and the reader was right to be satisfied. This batch finds out that the empty cell is on a line, and that the line is the ward, and that the ward is not in the model.

## The Weir Two branch — the numbers, fixed

The line runs out of the Weir Two pumping station, under the canal road, up the Quarter hill, out along Weir Lane, and on to the branch at Weir One gate. **Every machine in the chain is a further step from the root, and the further out it is the more of the step is left in it.** The step is **fifty seconds a night, in one step, at the fourth draw**, and it has run every night since the fifteenth of the third month.

**Chapter 51's table. All six readings are slow of the district reference, taken on Thursday the ninth of the sixth month in daylight with two watches and a notebook, and every one of them can be checked again by anybody with a watch and a day.**

| Machine | Slow of the district reference |
| --- | --- |
| Weir Two pump house, the station clock (the root of the branch) | 3 min 50 s |
| Pumping ward, the canal road, outer room | 13 min 10 s |
| Quarter hill, the north public clock | 24 min 10 s |
| Quarter hill, the south public clock, four hundred yards away | 1 h 05 min 00 s |
| Weir Lane, the three tenement clocks, in agreement | 1 h 27 min 30 s |
| Weir One gate, the gate clock | 1 h 45 min 10 s |

**Three checks the reader may perform, and the chapter must perform all three.**

1. **The Quarter hill pair's mutual difference is 40 min 50 s.** 1 h 05 min 00 s less 24 min 10 s = 40 min 50 s. And: the pair were 8 min 20 s apart on Monday the first of the fifth month (Volume 01, on the page), and **thirty-nine nights at fifty seconds is thirty-two minutes thirty seconds, and 8 min 20 s plus 32 min 30 s is 40 min 50 s.**
2. **The projection was right.** Volume 01 recorded that ten more nights at fifty seconds would take the pair to **sixteen minutes forty seconds apart on the eleventh of the fifth month**, and Orrin said in terms that it was a projection and not a delivered reading. **On the eleventh of the fifth month it was sixteen minutes and forty seconds.** He checked it himself, alone, and being right about it is worth nothing, and the chapter says so.
3. **The ramp, not an offset.** The differences between neighbours are 9 min 20 s, 11 min 00 s, 40 min 50 s, 22 min 30 s and 17 min 40 s, and they are all different, which is why it is not one fault and why nobody found it: **every machine on the line is individually within tolerance, of its own local beat, and a fault does not do that.** He has the shape and not the division and he says on the page that he has not got the division and that it would take a night with a man on the root end.

**One hour forty-five minutes and ten seconds** is the volume's number. Say it once, plainly, and do not say it again for ten chapters.
