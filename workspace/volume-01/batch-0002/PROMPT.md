## The batch in one paragraph

A private discovery becomes a public fight, and the fight is won on paper and lost in fact. Orrin has a form number, a price schedule, a countersignature and a private sense that has already cost a man; what he lacks is the machinery that turns a document into a consequence. This batch gives him that machinery — an honest inspection that finds a discrepancy nobody can act on, the renewal's paperwork, a councilor filing her own demand on arithmetic, a form number looked up behind a closed door, a copybook showing the practice is not local, and a deferral of a bridge bracket that puts the argument on money — and then takes it away in Chapter 19, where he reads a failing hospital pump correctly and cannot certify the repair, so the warning he was right about does nothing at all. The batch's genre promise: **a copy of the ledger is not the same thing as a right to be believed, and the difference is a room, a witness, and somebody's signature.**

## Batch goal, shape, and turns

- **Chapters 11–12, the beginning:** an honest Continuity Office inspection that finds nothing actionable because the district has no document for the officer to check; then the licence renewal's paperwork, which is a form and a conversation and needs no threat. The slack goes out of the leash in Chapter 12.
- **Chapters 13–14, escalation:** Councilor Imani Rook files the toll-ledger demand herself, three chapters after blocking it, on arithmetic and in her own name; Della Marsh is made to look up a number behind a closed door for a fee, and the copybook shows the practice in at least four districts.
- **Chapters 15–16, the midpoint:** Chapter 15 is the human centre — supper with Leda, the rule read aloud and priced by somebody who has to live in it, and one strictly bounded beat about the Long Nine that reveals nothing about Edwin. Chapter 16 is the discovery that the trestle's conduit bracket was deferred for cost two years ago on the same form and the same price schedule, signed by an inspector. After Chapter 16 the argument moves from blame to arithmetic.
- **Chapters 17–19, escalation to climax:** Chapter 17 is practice, on a dead pump, a hospital timer and a bridge joint, with a third keeper's toll and a boundary Orrin imposes on himself; Chapter 18 is the copied ledger in circulation and the counter-record costing a tenant block something real; Chapter 19 is the volume's failure — right, unprovable, useless.
- **Chapter 20, aftermath:** recovery and a reading-back, a second rule worse than the first, and Teo Arden in the doorway with a page and a question.

## Chapter cards

Authoritative, expanded cards are in `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0002.md`. One line each, to keep the shape in your head:

1. **11 — "The Honest Man With a Form."** Tobias Rill's routine Weirs hourline inspection. Nobody can write down who was in the building; Bevin Sarr's board is offered and refused as a document because no office is obliged to receive it. His finding is honest, complete, and useless, and he is right that a record needs a person who can sign one. Discovery as a dead end.
2. **12 — "What the Form Wants."** The renewal notice, the fee, the endorsement, and the hall's standing request for the holder's work book — a request for the grey ledger, which is not the hall's property. Orrin does not lie and does not hand it over, and learns the price of the next six weeks without a threat being made. The renewal itself must not arrive or be called imminent.
3. **13 — "Arithmetic."** Imani Rook files the demand herself, on four months of the Weirs' own uncorrected hours, and says in the room that she was shown a number and not a feeling. She pays for the record out of her own ward's clerical allocation. She is not softened. Assembly procedure used correctly: debate, division, referred to committee, read twice, entered in the minutes, discharged.
4. **14 — "Behind a Closed Door."** Della Marsh looks up a number for a fee with the door shut, frightened of an inquiry and loyal to nobody, and the copybook behind the form shows *non-certified standby* in four districts, not one. The labour ledger is copied; be explicit about who reached the book and who paid.
5. **15 — "Supper, and the Rule."** Leda on nights, the kitchen for an hour. She makes him say the ledger rule out loud and tells him what it is worth when the person who needs it is a patient and not a witness. He gives her the copybook's existence in a form she can hold. One bounded Long Nine beat: she was nine, she was in a shelter, she remembers the bells stopping, and she does not know what their father did. No note, no message, no hidden timing tolerance — that is Volume 08's.
6. **16 — "Deferred, for Cost."** The Continuity Office files are public on petition. The trestle's bracket was noted, priced, signed by an inspector and deferred two years ago. The same form removed a night crew from a model and put a bridge bracket on a shelf. Nobody accuses Rill. Mara and Orrin disagree about whether to use it, in a corridor, once, and do not resolve it.
7. **17 — "A Dead Pump, a Timer, and a Joint."** Practice, with the cost visible: a dead pump in Northglass, a hospital timer somebody is waiting for, a bridge joint with no consequence. He learns to ask narrow questions and to stop before curiosity becomes compulsion. Ivo Pell comes to the bench about an unrelated repair, is right and contemptuous once, and offers no lesson. This is not a mentor scene.
8. **18 — "Rumour With Page Numbers."** The copied ledger circulates in the Weirs. A landlord and an employer use the district's own hours against it and a tenant block loses something real in an afternoon. Teo was right to send it and has to stand in the room where it does damage. He does not apologise to Orrin, because Orrin did not send it.
9. **19 — "The Pump He Could Not Certify." The batch climax.** Orrin reads a failing Green Span hospital pump correctly, out loud, in a room, having learned in Chapter 9 that a private correct answer is a rumour with his name on it. Then: certification of a district toll is not in a class-three licence, the hospital's district cannot ask for a standing it does not have, a Reading is charged to the reserve that has failed, and the rail is booked a day ahead. He obeys his own rule and the room objects and nothing happens. He certifies nothing and commits no offence, and the pump is shimmed by an unlicensed man on a Sunday, and it holds, and two medicines arrive four minutes apart. A third keeper's toll, the largest, taken because he wanted to act and did not.
10. **20 — "What the Ledger Says About Him."** Recovery and reading-back. Four small jobs and a body that is not cooperating. He writes a second rule under the first: *no result of mine goes out of this room without a person named who can refuse it.* He goes to work. Teo arrives with a page of the copybook and a question about a Northglass boardinghouse, which is the first of the work they will do together and is not an alliance.

## Hard guardrails

- **Keep the two kinds of toll apart in every line.** A clock toll is minutes and a paying district. A body toll is an interval of a living person's attention, coordination or memory and has no column anywhere. *Calibration strain* is the Directorate's euphemism for a body toll; *self-inflicted occupational strain* is the guild's printed phrase for an unfiled private reading. Both entered the reader's vocabulary in Chapter 6. Never use one for the other.
- **The renewal does not fall due.** It is about six weeks after this batch starts, which is outside this batch and outside Volume 01. What arrives is the notice, the form, the work-book requirement, the fee and an interview nobody is obliged to hold. Never describe it as imminent. Never suspend him.
- **Orrin commits no licence offence.** He certifies nothing at a district toll. The offence that costs him his trade is Chapters 44–49 and it is for certifying a local toll he had no standing to certify. In Chapter 19 he is right, he cannot certify, and he deliberately does nothing.
- **A private sense is not a Reading.** Nothing he hears can be filed, cited or defended, it has no blind margin, and it always costs. In Chapter 19 the whole chapter turns on that rule, not against it.
- **No certainty permit, no Council of Hours, no district time council, no Reading room, no local instrument, no Civic Time Keeper, no reform-era term at all.** Volume 01 is a book about paper.
- **The Censor Gate, the return line, the Dark Reservoir, the First Hour, the binding plates, the routing plates, the Gate hub and the command line are not named.** The batch may show a practice has a form and a price. It may not name the machinery that benefits.
- **The Archive of Unlisted stays shut.** Named, sealed, not open to petition, not empty. No one in this batch gets closer to it than a signature on a routing card.
- **Anja Voss and Halden Rusk do not appear.** Voss had her one appearance as a signature in Chapter 4; Rusk his as an appointing authority in Chapter 1. Neither may appear again.
- **Nella Rook is not named.** Imani Rook's kinship is canon and stays unrevealed in this volume.
- **Do not stage, preview or file the Chapter 25 Reading.** That is batch 0003's midpoint and it must arrive cold, with the same people omitted a second time and larger. Nothing in Chapters 11–20 files a Reading about the Weirs' water schedule.
- **One Reading notice maximum per chapter, and this batch should need none.** If one is used, four lines, that order, plain declarative voice, a flat pressure line, and never a paying district.
- **Twenty-three must not appear in any form.** Two hundred and twelve is the Long Nine, eighteen years back, and belongs to the guild's liability annex, which is a Volume 01 resolution item rather than this batch's work.
- **No chapter ends mid-sentence or mid-action.** Endings are decisions, costs, discoveries or refusals. Chapter 19's failure must be quiet and complete; Chapter 20 is a recovery that is also a decision.
- **The Office of Public Reckoning has a written right to be ignored and is ignored at least twice in this batch.**

## Style

Rain-lit, tactile, grounded in repair work. Orrin's competence must be visible in the doing: he can diagnose a failing governor by sound, isolate a false bearing, name the screw he replaced two winters ago. He is not a scholar of politics and can misread a room. His interiority is procedural — he counts, he measures, he works out what a thing will do — and it should sound like a craftsman thinking, not a novelist describing. Show information through work, conversation and consequence; do not deliver lore. Let the important moments breathe. Keep paragraphs to two to six sentences, with a one-line paragraph only as a deliberate beat. Vary rhythm; do not lean on *held, steady, plain, counted, breathed, wrote, nodded*. Keep the prose accessible and free of inflated diction and technical-sounding language. The System is the Consequence Engine: one sparse physical notice in plain declarative language, at most one per chapter, and in this batch probably none.
