# Chapter 87 — The Neighbourhood That Pays

A three-clock rehearsal, worked out on the back of a works' counter sheet by four people in one afternoon, says that the only place in this district where a pressure can be taken out of the Weir Two branch without an order from anybody is the brick cistern under Sluice Row, which was put in in 1889 off the canal and has been out of use since 1911, and the twenty-two houses on that row would be without water for five hours in broad daylight on a Saturday while eleven of their clocks were wound by hand by people whose names nobody has, and the toll would have to name a paying district, and the only paying district available is a district four streets away that has nobody's water in it, and there is no form on which a rehearsal may be put to a vote, and the room in which the arithmetic was read out decided the only thing it was able to decide, which is to go and ask.

---

**Monday the fourteenth of the eleventh, from about nine in the morning, in the outer room of the pumping ward, and the board has six figures on it and a line above them about a rate and a date with no name on it, and under all of that, from this week, there is a new block, and Bevin Sarr ruled it herself and it is the tallest thing on that wall.**

> *THE THIRD NIGHT, or: what it would cost, and who would pay, and it is not a plan and it is not run.*
> *Branch figures on the fourteenth of the eleventh. Nights since the sixth of the tenth: 26 in the tenth month, the sixth to the thirty-first inclusive, and 13 in the eleventh, the first to the thirteenth inclusive. **Thirty-nine nights at fifty seconds is 1,950 seconds, which is 32 min 30 s.** The gate: 2 h 29 min 20 s + 32 min 30 s = **3 h 01 min 50 s.** The hospital branch: 1 h 36 min 50 s + 32 min 30 s = **2 h 09 min 20 s.** The pair on the hill: 40 min 50 s + 32 min 30 s = **1 h 13 min 20 s.***
> *Three clocks on a line and a question that asks nothing. **The rehearsal has not been run. Nobody will sign it. It certifies nothing, it can stop nothing, and it has no seal and no form and there is no form on which it could have one.***
> *WHAT THE THREE FIGURES SAY, worked out on the back of a counter sheet and not by the rehearsal and not by anybody's instrument: the three clocks are 9 min 20 s apart and then 51 min 50 s apart, in three buildings, on one line, and the difference is the length of the main and not a fault. **A pressure on that line cannot be taken out at the head, because the head is a works' asset and a works' man opens it and a works' man is given an engineer's written order and there is no form on which an engineer of this city can be asked for one by a person of this district. It cannot be taken out at the ward, because the ward's tank is a tank and not a reservoir and it has been exactly balanced at fourteen hours since the seventh of October.***
> ***It can be taken out at Sluice Row.*** **Sluice Row, off Weir Lane, twenty-two houses, eleven clocks, two standpipes, fed from a brick cistern under the row itself, put in in 1889 off the canal and not off this main at all. The cistern's inlet is under the row in a brick vault with a handwheel on it and it has been out of use since 1911 and it is the only water in this district that is not the branch's, which is exactly why it is the only place a pressure can be put.**
> *Cost: the cistern drawn down and refilled, **five hours, 300 minutes, seven in the morning until noon on a Saturday.** Eleven clocks on that row go free and are hand-wound throughout. Two standpipes dry. Twenty-two households.*
> *Names of the eleven winders: **NONE. The eleven clocks were counted from the pavement in daylight by two people. The list can only be made by going along and knocking.*
> *Toll, minutes: 300. **Paying district: LOWER WEIRS.** The toll on a clock is a quantity of minutes and a paying district. **The Lower Weirs is four streets from Sluice Row and has none of its water and is not able to be told anything about it. Sluice Row is not in the Lower Weirs pumping district and cannot be consulted and cannot object and has not been told.***
> *And nobody in this city can vote on it, and here is why, and it took an afternoon: **a schedule is made or varied by the Water Users' Committee on a notice with seven days' notice, and that is the only instrument in this city for changing when water comes to a place. A rehearsal is not a schedule. There is no form on which a rehearsal may be put to a vote, and no committee's standing reaches it, and the Water Users' Committee has no notice it could post about it because there is nothing for it to post about. Nobody can vote on it because it is not the kind of thing anybody votes on.***

Bevin Sarr read the whole of it standing up, from about four feet, which is the way she reads.

"**There is a line on here with nothing in it,**" she said.

"Yes."

"That is not the same as the line with the rate on it. That line has a figure in it and a date. **That line has a column in it and the column is for names and the column is empty, and the empty is the only thing on this wall that is not arithmetic.**"

"Yes."

"And you have not gone and got the names."

"No."

"**Then write under it that you have not gone and got the names,**" said Bevin Sarr, "and do not write under it why, because you have written down why four times this year in four books and every one of the four books is in this room, and a wall is not a book, and a thing a person has to look up is a thing a person does not look up."

He wrote it under.

> *The names of the eleven winders have not been got. **Nobody has been knocked on.** The list can only be made by going along and knocking and that has not been done and I am not going to do it in a room with the price on the table, because the whole of this year is that asking is the only instrument there is and I have not asked.*

---

**On the Tuesday the fifteenth and the Wednesday the sixteenth it was in the two rooms over a chandler's shop, and the room is the Weirs tenant council's, and it is not the Water Users' Committee, and it is not the pump ward, and it is not the Bellweather and Weir Lane sick and burial mutual, and nobody in it has ever confused the four and the confusion is a thing the four of them have each corrected the others about at least once this year.**

The sheet went on the table face up. Eleven people. There is a stove and a chair and a bell that has not worked since 1949 and a shelf of minute books in which the most recent volume is 1948, and there is a clerk's pencil and no clerk, and everything that has been decided in that room this year was decided by a show of hands.

Nobody spoke for about four minutes, which is long in that room.

Then **Wenna Pruitt** said:

"**I have been in eleven rooms this year and I have got a range, a form, a register office, a counter, a printer and a price, and every one of the six of them is still open and two of them take money, and I have been trying all year to get somebody to tell me the price of this.** And here it is. **Three hundred minutes, twenty-two households, eleven clocks, a Saturday morning in daylight, and a paying district that is not the street it happens on.** And I am not going to say that is wrong, because it is not wrong, and if it were wrong we would be able to refuse it and I have spent this year waiting for something I could refuse."

"That is not the half of it," said **Hanna Stroud**, who is fifty-four and has nine blocks and about two hundred and eleven households, and who has not been in this room since August and came on the Tuesday. "**The half of it is the line with nothing in it.**"

"I know what the line with nothing in it is."

"**Then say what it is, because nobody in this room has said it out loud and it has been on that wall since Monday morning.**" She put her hand flat on the table, which is a thing three people in this book do. "**It is the twenty-two houses. It is not the arithmetic and it is not the price. The arithmetic is finished. There is nothing left to argue about and every person in this room can check the whole of it in about four minutes and two of us have already done it twice.** And the thing that is not finished is that nobody on this earth knows the name of one of the eleven people who is going to wind eleven clocks on the morning this happens, and the only way to find out is to go along that row and knock on twenty-two doors, and **nobody in this district has ever done that, and it is not that nobody could.**

"I have let tenants go for twenty-two years without ever going to their doors to ask them a question about water. **I know why I have not done it and I am not going to stand in this room and pretend it is because I could not.** I could go tomorrow. I have a key ring with about two hundred and eleven keys on it and I have never used it to ask anybody anything."

Somebody said something about the mutual and about the undertaking and about the four lines on the sheet signed on the fourteenth of the tenth.

"**That is twenty-six addresses on two streets and it is not this street and none of the twenty-two households on that row is one of the twenty-six, and I have checked the four lines and I have checked it against a thing I have not seen and did not ask to see,**" said Hanna Stroud. "**And I am not going to pretend that that is a different kind of act. It is the same act. Somebody standing on a doorstep asking a person who lives there a question about a thing that is going to happen to them. There is no form for it in either case and in one case we have written down four lines saying we will not do it and in the other case we have not written anything down at all, and I have been waiting all year to be asked which of those two is worse.**"

"You are not going to choose," said Teo Arden, from the back, and he was not smiling and it was not a joke and it was the third time he had said it.

"**I am not going to choose,**" said Hanna Stroud, "and Wenna has not chosen since the nineteenth of the eighth and I have not chosen since the nineteenth of the eighth, and the reason we have not chosen is that we are both right, and it is not going to be a thing that gets settled by a better speech this month, and Mr Vale is not going to settle it by putting a number on a wall, and if anybody in this room thinks a number on a wall settles it then he has not read the line with nothing in it."

---

**And then on the Wednesday the sixteenth the room decided, and the room is eleven people and a show of hands and a bell that has not worked since 1949, and the decision was not his and he did not get to move it and did not want to.**

It went like this, and it took about six minutes, and it was not unanimous and nobody said so.

> *That the sheet be put where the people who live on that row will see it, in daylight, before anything is done and not after: **on the wall board of the Bellweather Road washing-yard, which is a public board, and on the notice board of the galvanising and wire works' gate, which is not ours and which the works' clerk at that counter has agreed to let up for one week and has not been thanked for and does not want to be.***
> *That no person be asked to put their name to anything, in the town or out of it.*
> *That the twenty-two households on Sluice Row be told the whole of it — the five hours, the three hundred minutes, the eleven clocks, the paying district, and the line with nothing in it — **by somebody going along that row in daylight and speaking to the people who live in it, in person, and not through a paper.***
> *And that this be done on **Saturday the tenth of the twelfth**, at about ten in the morning, in daylight, in weather, by whoever in this room is willing to walk, and **that the asking be done by more than one person, because a single person standing on twenty-two doorsteps is a thing that can be refused and two people from two streets is a thing that can be disagreed with.***
> *Carried on a show of hands, nine to two. **The two are a landlord and a wire-drawer of twenty-four and they did not say why and were not asked, and Wenna Pruitt wrote *two against, reasons not taken* on the back of a minute book and did not read it out.*

"And I want one thing said out loud," said Wenna Pruitt, "**and I want it said by him and not by me, and then I am finished.**

"**A result of mine does not go out of this room without a person named who can refuse it.** I have given him that line nineteen times this year and I have watched him break it twice and I have watched him satisfy it once, and on the thirtieth of May he satisfied it with a document on four boards and it cost him a licence, and that was the right way round. **And he has satisfied it again this afternoon and he has not noticed and he did not do it.**"

"I noticed," he said.

"Then say what you did, because nobody in this room knows it."

"**I have not produced a result this afternoon,**" he said. "**I have produced an arithmetic, on the back of a counter sheet, with four people's hands on it, and it has nobody's name at the bottom of it, and the name of the person who can refuse it is written on a wall in chalk and it is a street, and there are twenty-two people on it, and I cannot refuse any of them and they cannot be asked by me, and that is the whole of the nineteenth time and it is the first time the person has been a neighbourhood.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. The landlord and the wire-drawer did not give their reasons. The works' clerk at the counter did not want to be thanked and said so. Hanna Stroud put her hand flat on the table one more time and said the only sentence she said all afternoon, and she said it to nobody:

"**Twenty-two houses. I have never once been to their doors about water and I am going to be on that row at ten o'clock on the tenth of the twelfth and that is the first thing anybody in this district has done that I cannot undo, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be told afterwards that it was the right thing, because nobody who has not stood on that row knows whether it was.**"

**And the rehearsal is set for the twenty-fourth of the eleventh, which is ten days after this room met, and nobody has signed it, and the cost of it is on a wall in a pumping ward four miles away, and the line with nothing in it is on that wall under a price.**
