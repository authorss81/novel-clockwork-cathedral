# Chapter 61 — The Sheet Nobody Voted On

The valve at the corner of the second court on Weir Lane opened at about twenty to six in the morning of Tuesday the twelfth of the seventh month, and the paper it was obeying says it should open at a quarter to four, and the difference between a quarter to four and twenty to six is one hour fifty-five minutes exactly, and that is the figure the branch was out by that morning, and the branch was measured on the ninth of the sixth.

There is no fault anywhere in that sentence. That is the whole of the chapter and it takes four days to say.

---

He did not go out to the second court. He went to the top of the branch at about ten on the Tuesday morning with two watches and a notebook and did the arithmetic on the back page where nobody can see it, because it is a piece of arithmetic a man should not do in a street.

**Weir Lane, the ninth of the sixth: one hour twenty-seven minutes and thirty seconds, off the district reference.** That figure is in a notebook, in six lines of chalk on a board in a pump house, and nowhere else in this city.

**Fifty seconds a night.** That is the step, it is on the same page, and it is the only number in this business he would stake a bench on.

**Nights.** From the ninth of the sixth to the twelfth of the seventh, counting the night of the ninth and the night of the eleventh, is thirty-three. Thirty-three times fifty seconds is sixteen hundred and fifty seconds, which is twenty-seven minutes and thirty seconds.

**One hour twenty-seven minutes and thirty seconds, plus twenty-seven minutes and thirty seconds, is one hour fifty-five minutes.**

He checked it twice. Then he added the valve's own figure underneath it, because that is the part that is about a person and not about a clock.

**A quarter to four, plus one hour fifty-five minutes, is twenty to six. Which is the time the tap started running into a bucket on Tuesday the twelfth of the seventh month, and the bucket is the same bucket.**

The woman is about fifty-five and she has a shawl over her head and she is on the council's list of people the council sends a card to, and she sent a card this time too, and on the card she had written *it is the same as last time and I have told the office twice and the office sends a man in about nine days.* The man in about nine days is him. He has been in the district four times since the ninth of the sixth and this is the first time anybody has told him that the office is sent a man in nine days, and that the office has a known interval, and that she has written it down twice.

"You are early," she said.

"I am early," he agreed, "and I am sorry about that, and I am not going to tell you a time, because I do not have one, and the last man told you four o'clock."

That was a lie. He did have a time. He had worked it out on the back page of a notebook on the top of a hill an hour ago, and it was right to the second on Tuesday morning, and he had spent eleven years learning that a number you can carry in your head and a number you can put in a person's hand are not the same object, and he had told her once in June that he could not tell her when the water would come back and he was not going to say it twice. **A figure that is right to the second on a Tuesday is a figure that is a minute and forty seconds wrong by Thursday, and this one is growing, and that is the whole of the reason and it took him a summer to learn it.**

He wrote the two figures on the back of the council's card instead, and gave it back.

**Branch, this morning: 1 h 55 min 00 s out of the district reference. This is a figure I have had since June and it is growing fifty seconds a night. Your valve is on this branch. I have no time for it and I am not going to invent one.**

---

**The sheet is a piece of paper about eleven feet from his shoulder and it took him two days to find it.**

The search is the boring half of this and it is worth setting down, because everything afterwards depends on the fact that it was difficult.

The Water Users' Committee does not keep the district's rotation. It keeps *variations* of it, and it has kept eleven variations in four years, and it is allowed to vary a schedule and to do nothing else, and the clerk of it — Nadia Erridge, about thirty-five, who has posted the ward board's notices in this district for years and who wrote the sentence about the district having no standing into a notice that did not ask for it — will tell a man at a counter what a committee may do and what it may not, in about four minutes, and did.

"Mr Vale. I can tell you exactly what this committee is, because you have asked the right question twice this month and nobody asks it twice.

"The committee is nine members with a property qualification. It sits eleven times in four years. **It may vary the schedule. It may not make one.** There is no form on which that committee makes a rotation, and there has not been one since the water act, and if somebody stood up at a meeting of that committee and asked them to approve a rotation they would have to look it up on the table and they would find nothing to look up, and I have watched a man do that in about 1929 and I have never forgotten the silence."

"And the schedule it varies," said Orrin Vale. "Where is that?"

"That is the other thing," said Nadia Erridge, "and I am going to say it slowly because it is the whole of what you have come for and nobody has ever asked me for it in a way I could answer.

"The rotation is not ours. The rotation is *underneath* ours. It is a standing document of the district. When the committee does nothing at all, the standing rotation applies, and the standing rotation is the district's, and it is not in this building, and it is not in the Assembly, and it is not in the works, and it is not in the cathedral, and I do not know where it is and I have been three years at that counter."

---

It was not in the works either, and the second day of the search is the part that tells you what kind of document it is.

"Ambrose. A printed sheet, about eighteen inches by twelve, a water rotation, four draws in it, on a board or in a file, and I am not asking to take it. I am asking where it is."

Ambrose put his pen down, which he does about four times a year, and said: "There is one on the wall of the yard. Behind the ladder, by the weighbridge. It is a print and it is not a copy, and it is the same print as the one in the warden's office and it has been in the yard since before I came, and I have read it and I can tell you what is on it."

"Tell me what is on it."

"Six lines. It says the district, and it says the four windows, and it says the hours, and it says *revised 1951*, and it says *printed 1919* at the foot in small type, and it says the printer." Ambrose picked his pen up again. "That is the whole of it. It is a piece of paper, it is nailed to a wall behind a ladder, and nobody in this yard has needed to look at it since I came here eleven years ago, and if you had asked me in my first month I would have said I did not know there was one."

"How much is it to keep it there," said Orrin Vale.

"That is the question I have been waiting nine years for," said Ambrose. "It is a pound and two shillings a year for the paper and the printing and the frame. I have it in the maintenance schedule. **Paper and printing, one pound two shillings, against four thousand six hundred and thirteen pounds eighteen shillings and sixpence for the whole three-year programme.** You will notice that nobody has ever put those two figures in the same sentence and I would like it kept that way and I am not going to ask you why."

---

**The warden's copy is in the warden's office, and it is a copy of a copy, and the 1951 addition is in a different typeface.**

The office is a room over a shop on Weir Lane and it has a counter, a bench, a barometer, and a return that goes up to the works on the first working day of the month. Sel Kade is the warden and she is forty-four and she has held the post nineteen years and she announced on the twenty-fifth of the sixth that she will stand down on the first of the ninth, and the announcement is on the return in her own hand, and there are just under five weeks of it left.

She let him look at it. She did not ask him why, and she did not ask him not to, and she said one thing about it.

"It is the only one in the district. I have asked. There is a print in the works' yard and there is this, and this came out of a hall in about 1951 when they reprinted it, and it is a **copy of a copy**, and the reason the ward holds it and the works holds a print is that the works is a body and this is not. The water authority could ask a works. It could not ask me, because a pumping district is not a body and a document cannot be a party to anything, and a warden is a person, and a person is not an institution.

"**So when I go there is nobody in the district holding it, except a woman in an office who is not an institution either.** Take a copy if you want one. It is a sheet of paper; nobody is going to stop you."

He took a copy. It took four minutes and it cost him a sheet of his own paper and a shilling at the copyist off Church Street and a bus fare, and Hester Lomas set it for fourpence a page with her own signature at the foot, because she does outwork for the hall and does not mind other work, and she was not thanked, and did not ask to be.

**And then he read the whole thing in a pump house at about half past four on the Wednesday afternoon, with a lamp, standing up, because the office copy is in a room and a document read in a room is a document somebody is watching him read.**

Six lines. The district's name. Four windows: **a first, a second, a third, and a fourth.** The hours, which are **02.00, 03.00, 04.00, 05.00** in the warden's copy. At the foot, *printed 1919*. Above that, in a different typeface and a different size and set tighter, four words: **fourth window added 1951**.

The rest of the sheet is dated. The head of it says the rotation was **agreed the eleventh day of the first month, 1911**, and nobody has signed it and nobody voted on it and there is no minute of it anywhere, and it has been reprinted eleven times since and every one of the eleven reprints is dated 1919 at the foot because the imprint on the sheet is the year the *form* was set, and a form set in 1919 can carry an addition made in 1951 without anybody noticing, because the date a man reads is at the bottom of the paper and the change is in the middle of it.

**Nobody writes the name of the meeting.**

That is the whole discovery and he came at it sideways and did not see it for about a day. He went looking for the 1951 addition as an event — a meeting, a minute, a committee, a signature, a name — and there is nothing. There is a line of type. Somebody in 1951 decided that this district needed a fourth window, and the decision is a line of type on a sheet of paper, and there is no minute because no meeting sat, and there is no name because nobody writes the name of the meeting, and there is no form because there has never been a form on which a person may add a window to a district's water.

The Water Users' Committee cannot make a rotation. The warden's office cannot make a rotation. The works cannot make a rotation. **Nobody in this city can make a rotation and everybody in this city can live under one.**

---

**On the Thursday morning of the fourteenth he went back and stood at the same valve with a watch in his hand, because a time reported to him by a woman with a shawl on her head is a time he has not measured, and it opened at about twenty to six again, and that is the number that arbitrates the argument.**

The two copies disagree. The works' print says the fourth window is at **03.45**. The warden's copy says **04.00**. Fifteen minutes. The branch was out by one hour fifty-five minutes and fifty seconds on Tuesday, and a night later, being thirty-five nights at fifty seconds, it was out by **one hour fifty-six minutes and forty seconds**.

**03.45 plus 1:56:40 is 05:41:40. 04.00 plus 1:56:40 is 05:56:40.**

The valve opened at about twenty to six. Twenty to six is 05.40. He wrote the minute and forty seconds of drift on the back page as well, because a figure that moves fifty seconds a night is worth exactly as much as the observation that it does.

He wrote both sums on the back page and the arithmetic in front of a standpipe with a bucket under it, and a woman and two children watched him do it, and he did it slowly, because he has never once in his life been able to make a person watch a sum go right and has never once been able to stop himself.

**The branch is following the print in the works' yard. The copy in the warden's office is fifteen minutes out. Both are lawful. There is no third copy, and no version of the document, and no office in this city that can say which of the two is the district's water.**

He stood there afterwards for a while with the notebook shut, and the thing he was thinking about was not the fifteen minutes. It was that he had been in a pump house at half past four the previous evening reading a document that says the fourth window is at four o'clock, and the water had gone at twenty to six, and both of those were true, and **the difference between them is a document nobody voted on in 1951 and reprinted eleven times and cannot be identified.**

A document nobody voted on outlasted a document a committee re-votes. The committee's schedule had run out at midnight on the thirtieth of the fifth and the line had not stopped stepping, and now the committee was going to re-vote again and the step would stop, and the sheet would still be on the wall behind the ladder with four windows in it, and nobody would ever be able to say that the four windows were the committee's.

**A schedule is a piece of paper. A district's life is a schedule. And there is no form on which a district can object to a thing nobody did.**

---

**And there is a thing he could have done at about a quarter past six on the Thursday morning that he did not do, and it is on the page because the count of it is a thing he keeps.**

The valve on Weir Lane is a two-inch riser with a clock-driven valve at the bottom of it, and the clock that drives it is in a cast-iron box about eight inches above the pavement with a lid on it, and the lid has four screws in it and two of the screws are bright and two are not.

He has fixed clocks in boxes like that for eleven years. He has had one of those boxes open on a knee in the rain. It is a five-minute job with a screwdriver and it would have brought that valve back to the district reference by the following morning, and he would have had to put it back before six, and nobody would have known, and there is a rule he has never broken and it is not a rule of any hall in this city.

He knelt down and looked at the box and put his hand flat on the lid, and he asked himself the four words, out loud, in a street, at about a quarter past six in the morning, to a standpipe.

Then he did not ask them, because the box is four feet from a bucket somebody had carried out of a cellar in June, and because the answer would have been true, and because a true answer he had paid for is the only currency he has ever had, and because **he is not standing on that front. His rates are made in the Clockwork Quarter. He has never drawn a bucket on that street and he does not know any of the fourteen houses in it**, and he said that sentence out loud on the canal front in the fifth month of this year in front of fourteen people and signed under it, and the sentence has not got any less true in two months.

> *My own hand corrections on this city's machines: two. Eleven minutes on a lane in the Weirs and eight on a bracket at the Ferrywright corner, both in the second month of the year, both now inside an institutional form. Two, and it is two, and a clock-box four feet from a bucket was not a third, and I did not open it, and the not-opening was the only thing in four days that was actually difficult.*

---

**He wrote the fourth window on Bevin Sarr's board on the Friday afternoon, and it is four lines, and there is no name on it, and he did not ask her to initial it.**

> *The rotation this district runs on is a printed sheet. Nailed behind the ladder in the works' yard. Printed 1919. Reprinted eleven times. FOURTH WINDOW ADDED 1951. There is no minute of the adding and no name and no form. The sheet says the windows are 02.00, 03.00, 04.00, 05.00.*
> *The works' print says the fourth window is 03.45. The warden's copy says 04.00. The branch followed the print. The valve on Weir Lane opened at about 05.40 on Thursday morning, being 03.45 on a branch that was 1 h 56 min 40 s out, and it opened at 05.40 on the Tuesday before it, being the same print on a branch 1 h 55 min out.*
> *Paper and printing for that sheet: £1 2s a year. Nobody has voted on it. Nobody can.*
> *My figures, and my hand, and I have no licence, and this is not a certificate, and it is not in anybody's office, and the only reason it is on a wall is that Bevin Sarr's wall is the one place in this city nobody has to answer for.*

She read it standing, the way a person reads a thing they will have to answer for later, which is how she has read everything on that board for eleven years.

"1951," she said.

"1951."

"In this district in 1951 the water was short at the laundry for about six weeks and a woman on Bellweather Road went to a meeting about it and came back and said it would be looked at." She put the chalk down. "I do not know that. My aunt told me that in about 1931 and she could have made it up and I have never checked it and I am not going to put it on a wall in a pump house."

"That is exactly right," said Orrin Vale.

"I know it is exactly right," said Bevin Sarr. "Go and find the person who was at the meeting."

---

He did not go and find the person who was at the meeting, and the reason is in the grey ledger and the reason took him one afternoon to arrive at and it is the restraint of the whole batch and it is not a moral pose.

**The meeting, if there was a meeting, was in a room in this district in 1951, and the minutes of it, if there were minutes, are in the Variation book in the warden's office on the shelf above the barometer.**

He has been in that office four times since the first of the sixth. He has a copy of the rotation. He has seen the shelf above the barometer from the doorway, and there are eleven books on it, and one of them is bound in a different colour from the rest, and it is about two inches thick, and if anybody in this city has ever read it, it has been a woman who has been the person who answers for nineteen years.

He could have asked. He has a foot on the stair and she is a person and not an institution and she has never once refused him anything, and there is no form on which a man with no licence may ask a warden for a book in her office, and the reason there is no form is that nothing in that building is his business.

So he wrote the reason down instead.

> *Fifteenth of the seventh. There is a Variation book in the warden's office on the shelf above the barometer and it is probably in it and I have not asked and I am not going to.*
> *Two reasons and the second one is the real one. The first is that I have no standing and she has no duty to a man with a notebook. The second is that if I ask her for a book in her own office in her last five weeks, then whatever is in that book becomes a thing a man from the Quarter asked a woman to produce, and everything either of us says in that room afterwards is a thing that happened because I asked. Bevin Sarr said that to me at the door of a room over a shop in June and I wrote it down and I did not understand it for a month.*
> *And there is a third reason and I am not going to pretend it is not the first one. It is that if the book is in there, then the answer is in a woman's hand, and if the answer is in a woman's hand then it dies with her office on the first of the ninth, and I would rather it died in a woman's hand than be copied out of it by a man with a shilling at a copyist's.*

---

**And on the Saturday afternoon of the sixteenth of the seventh month he found the one thing nobody in Morrowfen has looked for, which is not a fact at all, and it is the only thing in four days that anybody could have called a discovery.**

The works' print is nailed behind the ladder. The warden's copy is on a shelf. Neither of them is in a *file*, because there is nothing to file them in.

**There is no index of them. There is no accession number. There is no print run recorded anywhere. The printer's imprint at the foot of the sheet is the name of a firm on Church Street and the firm is a printer of handbills and a printer of a weekly in Bellweather, and the impression is that the sheet is the only thing that firm has ever printed at that size, and that is a guess, and he wrote down that it is a guess.**

Which means: eleven reprints, and no count, and no list, and no way of establishing which of the eleven is the one behind the ladder, because they are all the same and the additions were typed onto each of them as it came off the press.

He stood in the yard and read the imprint and then read his own notebook and then wrote one line and put the book away.

> *Sixteenth of the seventh. I have found the thing there is to find and it is not a fact. It is that eleven reprints of a sheet, spread over forty years, have no run list, no index, and no way of being told apart, so that the document which governs the water in this district cannot be identified, cannot be dated, and cannot be cited, and there are two of them and they disagree, and both are in this city right now, and I could not tell anybody in a room which one is the district's because **the district is not a thing that can be told things to.***
> *And the sheet is dated 1911 in its head and 1919 at its foot and 1951 in the middle, and those three years are the only three people in it, and I have found none of them.*

He walked back up the hill at about five with a copy of a sheet in his coat and a figure in a notebook and a rate of fifty seconds a night that he cannot stop, and the question he was carrying at the end of the fourth day was the one that has no door in it.

**Who do I ask about a sheet from 1911.**
