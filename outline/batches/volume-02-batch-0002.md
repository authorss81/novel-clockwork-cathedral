## Batch goal

Convert a rate into a fact and then watch the fact do damage. Chapters 51–60 established that a district's clock error is a **ramp** produced by four pumping cycles a day, lawfully voted, on a schedule that lapsed into a standing rotation nobody has read since 1919. This batch does the three things that turn that into a volume.

1. **It finds the document.** A pumping district's water is not governed by the Water Users' Committee, which can only *vary* a schedule. It is governed by a printed standing rotation dated 1919, reprinted eleven times, with a fourth draw typed onto it in 1951, with no minute and no name; two copies of it exist in the district and they disagree by fifteen minutes, and there is no way to say which is the district's.
2. **It takes the instrument away and gives it back as a shape.** Pell's three practices, the fourth keeper's toll, the first choice trace, and the discovery that half of what Orrin calls a gift is instruments. The toll costs him, permanently, the ability to hold the order of assembly of a movement in his head without writing it down.
3. **It shows the cost arriving in other people's institutions.** A tram signal out by an hour and thirty-six, a true certificate, a correct ordinance, a fare table, a school, a market, a works' shift, eleven minutes at a junction. Then the omission used on purpose for the first time, by a works manager with a pencil, and a stoppage called for the day the pump warden stands down.

**The batch's engine, in one sentence: a city's real constitution is its schedules, the schedules are older than the people under them, and a committee of nine men with a property qualification can change the physics of a district by voting, lawfully, and can also improve it, and neither is a conspiracy.**
