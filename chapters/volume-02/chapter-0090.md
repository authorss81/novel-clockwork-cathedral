# Chapter 90 — He Agrees to Try the Only Instrument Left

On Saturday the third of the twelfth month, in a room over a chandler's shop in Bellweather, with the cost of it chalked on a wall four miles away and brought into the room on a sheet of foolscap and read out loud in full, a man of thirty-two agreed to try the only instrument he has left, which is three clocks, two operators who will not sign his name, a written question with nothing in it, and a street of twenty-two houses that has to be asked, and the instrument cannot stop one single thing in this city, cannot certify anything, and has no seal and no form; and the woman who refused to have her name on a piece of paper about her own street was in the room, and she came of her own accord because nobody knocked, and she is the reason the cost is a number and not a feeling; and the room decided, and he did not.

---

**Tuesday the twenty-ninth of the eleventh and Wednesday the thirtieth, and it is the recovery and it is on the page because a man who has not slept properly since the first of October is not to be trusted with the end of a book.**

He was in bed until ten on the Tuesday and read. He did the Wednesday and the Thursday and the Friday and the Saturday: **six hours on a long-case of 1898 for a woman in Bellweather at a price agreed before the case was opened, being two pounds five shillings and sixpence, of which six hours at the hall's out-of-shop rate of four shillings and sixpence is twenty-seven shillings, and eighteen shillings and sixpence of material, and the addition is exact and the invoice is the estimate.** He slept nine hours on the Wednesday night and eight on the Thursday and had not slept nine hours since the first of the fifth.

And on the Thursday morning the long-case came off the bench with a third wheel that can be corrected by hand in about eleven minutes, and he looked at it, and he wrote in the narrow book with the other one, on the shelf behind the bench, in pencil, one line: *Could have. Did not. Two is two.* **That is the fifth time this year the chance has come and gone and the count is two and it is going to stay two, and a chapter of somebody else's life is going to say that he is a man who does not correct things by hand, and he is not, and the reason he is not is in a book he bought for a shilling in the sixth month and will be on a shelf in the fourteenth.**

---

**And on the Wednesday the thirtieth of the eleventh, at about eleven in the morning, the date moved, and it moved because of the asking, and nobody thanked anybody for the delay.**

The rehearsal was set, on the Sunday the thirteenth, for **the twenty-fourth of the eleventh**. Pell had not chosen the date and had said so on the sheet so that it would be written down. Sabine Kaye had not chosen it and said the same.

On the Wednesday the thirtieth, in a room over a chandler's shop, with seven people in it, it was moved.

"**The tenth of the twelfth is when people on that row are going to be told what it costs,**" said Wenna Pruitt. "**You cannot run a thing on the twenty-fourth of the eleventh and ask a street on the tenth of the twelfth. The asking is not a courtesy before it. The asking is the only part of this that has a person in it, and if the asking happens after then the whole thing is a plan that was announced, and we have spent four months establishing that we are not that.**"

Nobody argued. The date was fixed in about four minutes and the new one is **the seventeenth of the twelfth**, which is a Saturday, and the minute of the three clocks is half past seven in the morning because that is the minute Ivo Pell chose in September and it was written down in advance before anybody knew which day the thing would happen on.

**And here is the whole of the instrument, and it is five lines, and it was read out in that room on the Saturday and it does not grow.**

> *Three clocks: the pumping ward, canal road, outer room. Weir Two pump house, station clock. Quarter hill, south public clock.*
> *One written question, in the same words, written down before the day: **at the minute stated, read each clock named below and write down, in figures, what it says, against the district reference of the Cathedral public clock; and state the time of reading, and state who read it.***
> *Two operators. **I. Pell**, who has countersigned four certificates this month and has a fortnightly arrangement with a man in a shop which he does not describe as instruction. **S. Kaye**, who has four certificates a month and two watches. **Neither of them will sign anything, and neither of them has moved, and one of them lost nine minutes on the telephone in the middle of this and neither of them mentioned it.***
> *No toll is taken and none is owed. Nothing is entered on any rail. Nothing is filed, nothing is certified, nothing is admissible. There is no seal and there is no form and there is no instrument in this city that could put a seal on this.*
> *And the cost: **three hundred minutes, twenty-two households, eleven clocks hand-wound, two standpipes, a paying district of the Lower Weirs, and the column of names on that wall with nothing in it, and the names can only be got by going along and knocking.***

---

**And on the Thursday the first and the Friday the second of the twelfth, the two of them were in the district, and they were told the date and the cost, and neither of them said anything about it for about four minutes, and then Pell asked the only question anybody asked in the whole of those two days.**

"You have moved it three weeks and two days."

"We have moved it three weeks and two days. **Twenty-fourth of the eleventh to seventeenth of the twelfth, and a reader can do that on a calendar, and we did not choose either date.**"

"**Why.**"

"Because a street is going to be told on the tenth," said Wenna Pruitt, "and a thing that costs somebody their water for five hours on a Saturday morning ought to be said to them before anybody has a date, and not after."

"**That is the correct answer and it is the first time in about four years that anybody has given me one,**" said Pell, "and I am not going to say what I would have said, and you know what I would have said."

"He would have said the asking does not change the arithmetic," said Sabine Kaye.

"He would have said the asking is a cost and it has no column and it goes on the same side of the ledger as the three hundred minutes and is therefore not free."

"**It is not free and it is not in the three hundred minutes and I am not going to let anybody put it in,**" said Kaye. "**Three hundred minutes is water off a cistern and eleven clocks hand-wound. Going along a row of twenty-two houses in daylight is about four days of somebody's week and it belongs to whoever does it and it is not a minute of a clock and it will never be admissible to anybody.**"

---

**And on Saturday the third of the twelfth, at about half past seven in the evening, there were eleven people in the room over the chandler's shop and the sheet was on the table face up and a woman of twenty-four came in at about a quarter to eight, up the outside stair of a building in Bellweather, and nobody had sent for her and nobody knocked, and there is a reason she came and it is not the one anybody would write down.**

**Nell Sayer is twenty-four. She is a wire-drawer at the galvanising and wire works, which is at fifty-eight of ninety-one, and she has lived at number nine, Weir Lane, for six years, and that is the ninth of the twenty-six addresses, and there is a service pipe against her name in a book in a kitchen with a date of the fourteenth of March 1948 in a hand that is not Adela Marrow's, and there are four lines on a signed sheet saying that nobody is to go to that door, and that the twenty-six households are not to be told, and nobody has broken any of the four lines and nobody is going to.**

"Nobody knocked," she said, before she had sat down.

"Nobody knocked."

"**I have not come about the twenty-six and I want that said first, because everybody in this room has been extremely careful about the twenty-six for a month and I am not the twenty-six today, and if I start talking about the twenty-six I will be able to stop talking about what I came about.**"

She put her hand on the table.

"**I came about the three hundred minutes.** And I want to say something about them and I have been saying it to myself since Wednesday and I am going to say it once and then I am not going to say it again, and you are not going to thank me for it, and if anybody in this room starts thanking me I will leave.

"**You have made it a number. That is the whole of what has happened and nobody has noticed it happening.** Twenty-two households. Five hours. Eleven clocks. Three hundred minutes. A paying district of the Lower Weirs, four streets away. **And it is on a wall in chalk and it is on a sheet and it is in a room, and it has a figure on it, and a figure is a thing a person can be angry at.** I have been in a works for six years and I have watched men be argued out of a thing in about nine minutes because somebody put a number on a table, and I have watched the same men walk out of a room for a month over a thing that was true and felt, **because a feeling is a thing that gets carried away by whoever is loudest, and I have been the loudest person in about four rooms a year for most of my life and I know exactly what I sound like from the other side of it.**

"So that is why the cost is a number and not a feeling, and it is not because anybody here is clever. **It is because I have spent six years being the feeling, and I know what a feeling does in a room.** And I have still not signed anything, and I am still not going to, and there is a form that says I am a head on a return and it cost two shillings and got me nothing anybody could refuse me for, **and on the tenth of December I am going to be standing on a street that is not my street, because I was one of the nine who voted against, and I said on the Wednesday that I would be on that row at ten o'clock, and a wire-drawer of twenty-four is not a body and does not get to do this, and I have not joined anything, and if anybody writes the word *committee* on anything about this I will stop.**"

Nobody said anything for about twenty seconds.

"That," said Teo Arden, from the back, "is the best thing anybody has said in this building since the eleventh of October, and she has not joined anything, and neither have I, and neither of us is going to be thanked, and I am going to say the only thing I have got and then I am going to sit down.

"**A number with a working on it is the only instrument anybody in this district has ever been handed, and it is handed to the loudest person in the room, and the loudest person in the room is almost always the person who is going to be hurt, and that has been true all year and nobody has wanted it to be.**"

---

**And then the sheet was read out, in full, in about four minutes, by Wenna Pruitt, standing, with eleven people in the room, and it included the line with nothing in it, and nobody was permitted to read past it and nobody tried.**

And then Orrin Vale said the thing, and it took about ninety seconds, and it is the last decision anybody took that evening and he took it in a room where the cost was on the table and readable and he was the only person in the room who was not going to be asked whether he wanted it.

"**I have spent five months looking for a way to ask the city's machine a question about a district it has not got, and I have got a rehearsal, and it is three clocks and a pencil and a piece of paper with three times on it and nothing else, and it has no seal, and it cannot certify anything, and it cannot stop one single thing in this city, and the rate is a rate in the twelfth month as well as the tenth and the four lines on the wall in that pumping ward are still four lines on a wall.**

"**And I am going to try it, and that is all it is, and I am not going to be able to tell you on the seventeenth that it worked, because on the seventeenth it will have read three clocks and written down three figures and that will be the whole of it.**

"**And here is the reason I am going to try it, and it is the reason and it is not noble and I have been circling it since the fourteenth of the tenth.** In the fifth month of last year, at about four minutes to ten past one in the morning, in a pump house on this canal road, in the dark, in front of about twenty people, I put my name under three hundred and sixty minutes, and I had no standing to sign it, and I knew I had no standing to sign it, and there was nowhere else in this city to put my name. That is what I did, and it cost me a licence, and the licence was suspended on the first of the sixth in four true lines by a man who had told me exactly what would happen about a quarter of an hour beforehand, and he was right, and I would do the hour again at six in the morning in front of the people it is done to.

"**There is a room.** There is a room in Bellweather with a stove in it and there are eleven people in it and there is a number on the table and there is a cost on a wall in chalk four miles away and a woman of twenty-four in the room who has just told me the whole of what a number is for, and a landlord of nine blocks who voted yes and a landlord of nine blocks who voted no, **and the number is in the room, and I do not get to decide.**

"So I am going to say the only sentence I have left, and then I am going to stop, and then this meeting is over and I am going to go and do four hours of somebody else's clock on Monday and the price is written out and I am not going to come back here in a fortnight with a different version of it, and if I do, you will all know that somebody taught me to, and I will not have it.

"**The room has decided, and the room is not me, and the only instrument this district has is the one we cannot pay for, and I am the man who is going to have to go and stand on a street and say a number out loud, and I cannot be refused, and nobody in this room can refuse me either, and that is not a gift and it is the reason nobody is going to help me, and I have been told that by a man of thirty-six on a Thursday and I did not sleep for it.**"

> *Twenty-ninth of the eleventh to the third of the twelfth. Nine hours' sleep, eight hours' sleep, a long-case of 1898 at two pounds five shillings and sixpence with the price written out before the case was opened and the invoice the same, and a third wheel that can be corrected by hand in about eleven minutes, and **the count of his own hand corrections is two and it is two this morning and it is two for ever if he is careful and it is not going to be three because a woman at a counter once told me a different figure about a different thing and I believed her.***
> *The date moved **three weeks and two days**, from the twenty-fourth of the eleventh to **the seventeenth of the twelfth**, which is three weeks and two days on a calendar, because a street is going to be told on the tenth The minute is half past seven in the morning. Two operators. Three clocks. A question that asks nothing. No seal, no form, no rail, no toll, nothing admissible and nothing that could be made admissible.*
> *The cost is on a wall in chalk in the pumping ward and it was read out in full in a room with eleven people in it, including the line with the names in it, which is empty, and the only way to fill it is to go along a row of twenty-two houses and knock, **and that has not been done and it is not going to be done in a room with the price on the table.***
> *A woman of twenty-four came up an outside stair at a quarter to eight because nobody knocked, and said the thing nobody else in this district can say, which is that a number on a table is a thing a person can be angry at and a feeling is a thing that gets carried away by whoever is loudest, and that she has been the loudest person in about four rooms a year for six years and knows what she sounds like from the other side of it. **She has still not signed anything. She voted against. She is going to be on the row at ten o'clock on the tenth and she has joined nothing and she does not want the word committee used about it.*
> *And in the fifth month of last year a man of thirty-two put his name under a number he had no standing to sign because there was nowhere else to put it, and it cost him a licence, and he would do the hour again. **And there is a room, and the number is in it, and he does not get to decide, and the last thing that happened in the eleventh month of this year is not a discovery and not a refusal and not a door. It is a room deciding something, and the room is not him, and nobody thanked anybody, and he has said so and he will be asked to say so again on the tenth of the twelfth on a street that is not his.*
