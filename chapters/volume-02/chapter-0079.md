# Chapter 79 — The Fifteenth of the Tenth, and Who Was Asked

The last ninety men went in at the change of shift on Wednesday the fifth of the tenth month and nobody in this district could have told you on the Monday which day it would be, and the room in which it was decided was a manager's office on the first floor over a gate with a letter book open on it and four hundred men who were not in it, and on the Thursday a committee of nine men with a property qualification put four pumping cycles back into a schedule that nobody in a hundred and forty thousand people had been asked about, in about eleven minutes, and the far end of the branch began to move again at fifty seconds a night, and on the Sunday morning a clockmaker chalked a new figure on a wall with a date on it and no name on it, and that is the argument of this district coming back as a rate.

---

**What the five weeks cost, and it is not what anybody thought it was going to cost.**

The stoppage was never one thing and there was no day anybody could have named. It was on for about nine days in the first ten, and quiet for most of the middle of the month with a hundred and forty men in and out at the change of shift and nobody recording which, and the last of it was the week of the twenty-fifth, and it ended on the Wednesday.

**Nobody was dismissed. Nobody was locked out. Nobody was fined. There was no strike, no fund, no arbitration, no return date, and no two sides, and the man of twenty who said all of that in a room over a chandler's shop in the last week of September was right about every word of it and was not thanked for saying it and had not asked.**

**The cost, in the order it arrived:**

**Water.** The ward's reserve tank went under a day on the Sunday the twenty-fifth of the ninth. There was no alarm, because the low-level alarm has been inhibited since the second of the third month and no report of the inhibition was ever made and no form requires one. The make-up across the works' boundary was made by hand, out of a gearbox, by one man of forty-one with a bar in an override, on and off, for five weeks, at a time of his own choosing, and **there is no column in this city for those hours, and he is the man at the boundary and that is the job, and nobody asked him and nobody has since and he is not a hero and does not think about it.**

**A hospital.** Two rounds, by hand, off a bracket clock eleven inches high on a landing, twenty-two intervals and then twenty-four, in two nights in September, and four hundred and ninety-two minutes of a living person's attention taken off a night sister and two others and put into a column with a date on it, which is the only place in this book where that has ever been done.

**A tram.** The route came off the public timetable on **four Wednesdays** during the five weeks, at a private hire of **four pounds eight shillings against one pound three on the public table**, the difference three pounds five shillings, carried out of a gate fund in a tin at the front office of a works with four hundred men in it. **Perpetua Nye, fifty-one, twenty-six years on that line, held a bus at the Green Span junction on four afternoons, for eleven minutes, eleven minutes, nineteen minutes and eleven minutes.** She is not thanked and has said she will not be, and the reason is the one she gave in August and it has not changed: stopping is her hands and not anybody's number.

**A school.** About two hundred and ten children in two sessions, whose bus is not on a timetable and therefore is not in the box on the post, and the box governs the road anyway, and it is the same box and the same figure.

**A market.** Nine stalls out of forty did not open on four Wednesdays in a row, which is a thing that ends a market, and the traders from the far side worked out about halfway through that the second session's bus and the first tram were the same afternoon, and nobody had told them and nobody had asked them.

**A maintenance programme.** The district works' own men were on the road for five weeks, and the canal front's standpipes were not looked at once, and the trestle brackets' four-year interval was not due for another year and eleven months and were not looked at, and the crossing brackets' twelve-month interval was inspected in June and is not due again until next June, and the six hundred standpipe brackets on the works' own sheet at one thousand eight hundred and ninety pounds were not looked at by anybody and are on a wall in chalk in a pumping ward with the price on it.

**And then on Wednesday the fifth of the tenth, at about half past six in the morning, at the change of shift, the last ninety men went in, and Idris Vane was not at the gate, and the reason he was not at the gate is that he does not stand at gates.**

He decided it on the Tuesday, alone, in his own office, with a letter book, and he has nineteen years in the works and four hundred men and he is not a villain and he is not a fool and he has never once in his life pretended that a thing was anybody else's decision.

> *The order book carries to the fourth week of this month. Two of the four have gone to Sloughside and one has been put to March. **A furnace that goes cold costs a fortnight and I have got one furnace left and about seventy men in the fettling shop and four of them are the only four in this city.***
> *Nobody has asked me to end it. **Nobody is going to end it. It is going to end because there is no money in it and the money is in the order book, and that is not a reason anybody can be angry with, and I have found out in five weeks that the whole of what I have got in this world is an order book and a gate.***
> *I am not going to tell anybody it was a decision. I have made no decision this month. I have watched a thing run out of money and I have written the date on it.*

He told nobody that either. He said it once, on the Wednesday morning, to a man from a firm in Bellweather who was not a reporter and was not anybody, and it is in no minute, and there is no form in this city on which a works manager may be asked what he decided.

---

**And on Thursday the sixth of the tenth, at about four in the afternoon, nine men with a property qualification sat in a room above a shop in Bellweather and put four pumping cycles back into the canal front's daytime draw, and every word of the notice that went up on the ward board on the Friday morning at about ten is true.**

> **VARIATION. Canal front, Bellweather Road to the Weir One gate. Daytime draw.**
> *The schedule of the first day of the eighth month is further varied as follows: the daytime draw shall be worked in four cycles in place of two. Effective from the sixth day of the tenth month.*
> *The committee finds that the demand upon the front in the present month, the season being changed, requires the full rotation, and that the standpipes at the second court and at the Bellweather washing-yard cannot be maintained in two cycles. The committee is not able to consult the district, the district having no standing under the water act.*
> *Clerk of the committee.*

It took about eleven minutes. It was lawful, it was correct, and the reasons in it are as good as the reasons were in August, and the reasons in August were also correct.

**Nobody can object to a schedule they were not asked about. That is not a defect in the objection. There is no other kind in this city.** The district was not consulted because it cannot be, and it cannot be because a pumping district is a list of properties and not a body, and a list of properties has no mouth, and the clause that says so is in the ordinance, and somebody in a building had to go and look whether it was, and nobody has written down who.

And the fourth week of the five is the week the tank went under a day in, and in the week it ended the front is back at four cycles in a district that has not had a hand-wound night since the second of the ninth.

---

**And the notice for the next sitting went up on the ward board on Saturday the eighth at about eleven in the morning, seven days' notice, and it is one sheet, and the date on it is the fifteenth of the tenth, and the whole of the question it is named for is on it and cannot be answered by it.**

The next business is the winter rotation, and the winter rotation is a printed sheet agreed in 1911, printed in 1919, reprinted eleven times, with a fourth window typed onto it in 1951 in a different typeface, with no minute of the addition and no signature and no name, because nobody writes the name of the meeting, and **there has never been a form on which it could.**

The only copy in the district is in a box in a store at the pumping ward, on its edge, with the rest of it, and the store's form says where a thing is going to be kept and what it says is THE STORE, and a warden is a person and a person is not an institution and the person left on the first of the ninth.

**Who was asked about the fifteenth of the tenth.**

Nobody. There is no form on which a member of the Water Users' Committee may ask a district anything, and there has never been one, and a pumping district has no standing under the water act, and cannot be consulted, and cannot object, and cannot be told what it is about to be done to it in advance, and the third line of the notice says so in Nadia Erridge's hand, which is the same sentence she wrote into a notice in April that did not require it, and which is in the ordinance now, and nobody has written down who went and looked.

And the one person in this district who could be asked is a man of thirty-two with two benches on a lease to Michaelmas, no licence, and a rateable value in the Clockwork Quarter, and he is not a ratepayer of this district and could not stand for it at a meeting even if the meeting would hear him, which it would not, because a member of that committee has a property qualification and he has none.

---

**And the recovery, which is a bed, a bench, and an ordinary paid week, and it is here because a man who has not slept properly since the twelfth of the ninth is not to be trusted with anything else this week.**

He was in bed until nine on the Thursday morning and read. He did four and a half hours on the Thursday and a full day on the Friday and a full day on the Saturday, and on the Friday the seventh he did a long-case of 1902 for a man in the Clockwork Quarter at a price agreed before the case was opened, **one pound fourteen shillings and sixpence**, being four hours at the out-of-shop rate of four shillings and sixpence, which is eighteen shillings, and sixteen shillings and sixpence of material, and the addition is exact and the invoice is the estimate. He slept nine hours on the Friday night and eight on the Saturday and he had not slept nine hours since the first of the fifth, and he wrote that down and wrote down that he wrote it down, and the reason he wrote it down is that a grey book is worth nothing in any room that is not the one it is in and this room is his and he is the only person who will ever read that line and he has decided that is allowed.

**And on the Friday morning he read the sheet.**

It is one sheet of foolscap in three hands with a minute on it and a date on it and three figures on it, and it was done on Monday the third of the tenth at a quarter past four in the afternoon by three people who were not paid and who were not asked to be paid:

> *Pumping ward, canal road, outer room — read by B. Sarr — **57 min 20 s** slow of the district reference.*
> *Weir Two pump house, station clock — read by I. Pike — **48 min 00 s**.*
> *Quarter hill, south public clock — read by C. Tole — **1 h 49 min 10 s.** Read from the pavement. Wet.*

Three clocks. Three buildings. Three hands. Three figures that do not agree with each other by nine minutes and twenty seconds and by fifty-one minutes and fifty seconds, and **all three of them are the figures on the wall in chalk, to the second, and they have been the figures on the wall since the second of the eighth.**

He read it twice. Then he put it down and did not go and check any of the three.

**That is the thing that happened in that fortnight and it is not a small thing and there was nobody there to see it. He has been checking other people's figures against his own since the ninth of June, six readings, two watches, a folding rule and a good level, and he has stopped, in the space of about four minutes on a Friday morning, from checking three figures written in three hands by a woman of thirty-three, a woman of about sixty, and a man of forty-one who was standing in the wet.**

He wrote one line about it. *I have not checked them and I am not going to, and the reason I am not going to is that if I check them then the only thing that has ever been produced in this district by three ordinary people is a thing that had to be produced by me, and that was the whole of the argument in June and I was on the wrong side of it in June.*

---

**And Elsie Cobb's card came back to him on the Saturday the eighth, in an envelope, through a nurse on the ward, and she had written four words on the outside of it, which were *you may read it*, and her aunt had not sent it and she had sent it herself, which is the first thing she has done in this district that anybody has had to ask her about twice.**

He read it in about four minutes. It is a folded card with about seventy columns in pencil in a small hand and two of the columns are marked *days* and left blank, and the new column has three lines in it now and not two.

> *New col. INTERVAL WORKED BY HAND. Night sister E. Cobb, ward rate.*
> *2nd Sept. Line lost 2.08 a.m. Restored 4.00 a.m. 22 intervals. Worst 6 min 40 s. **Total 1 h 52 min.***
> *26th Sept. Line lost 9.10 p.m. Restored 3.30 a.m. 24 intervals. Worst 7 min 05 s. **Total 6 h 20 min.***
> *3rd Oct. No loss. Line held. Free v line, worst gap, **unchanged at 1 h 03 min**. Who was waiting: none.*

**One hour and three minutes.** The ward's worst gap, on the third of the tenth, with the branch frozen since the second of the eighth, is **1 h 03 min**, and it is the same figure as the gap on the second of the ninth and on the twenty-sixth, and it has not moved in three weeks while the line has not moved, and **that is the first time since the twenty-fourth of the fourth that that column on that card has read the same on three lines.** The branch is 1 h 36 min 50 s out and the ward's gap is 1 h 03 min and the half hour between those two numbers has been there for four years and belongs to a bracket clock on a landing that nobody has looked at since 1949, and it is not a defect, and it is the only figure in this district that has not got worse in four months.

The third line is the first line on that card in five months in which the column reads *unchanged*, and it was written in a different pencil, and there is a note under it in the same hand, and it says: *Free master in 1 s a day on the 20th of July and has been since. Line has not moved. **It is the first fortnight I have had where the answer did not get worse and I am putting it down because nobody would believe it and I would like it in my own hand on the third of October.***

He read that four times.

**Nobody is going to be thanked and nobody is going to be told, and the empty column in a glass case in a pumping ward is still empty, and the ward clock book in a store in Green Span still has four dashes in it from the twelfth of March 1951, and the two of those things are not comparable and the girl of nineteen has never once been asked whether her father is in either of them and she has not offered and she would not.**

---

**And on Sunday the ninth of the tenth month at about half past seven in the morning he was on the Quarter hill with two watches and a notebook because the hill can see it, and the pair had been forty minutes and fifty seconds apart every single morning since the second of the eighth, and on the Sunday the ninth of the tenth month they were forty-three minutes and twenty seconds apart.**

Three nights. The sixth, the seventh and the eighth. Three nights at fifty seconds is one hundred and fifty seconds, which is two minutes and thirty seconds, and forty minutes and fifty seconds plus two minutes and thirty seconds is forty-three minutes and twenty seconds.

He read it twice with a watch in each hand. Then he went down the hill and got the chalk and wrote on Bevin Sarr's board, in the middle height, in the small upright hand, under the six figures that have not moved in ten weeks:

> *Sunday the ninth of the tenth. The pair on the hill are 43 min 20 s apart. It was 40 min 50 s on the second of the eighth and every morning since.*
> *Four cycles again from the sixth. **A step is a displacement and a displacement is permanent, and a rate is a thing a committee can vote on, and the rate is back, and that line is the whole of what has happened in this district since the ninth of June and it has taken me two months to write it under my own figures.***
> *The gate is 2 h 31 min 50 s. The hospital branch is 1 h 39 min 20 s.*

Bevin Sarr read it standing up, from about four feet, which is the way she reads.

"No name on it," she said.

"There is a date on it."

"**There is a date on it and no name on it, and in about a month somebody in a good coat is going to come down those steps and ask me what a rate is, and I am going to tell them it is a number, and then they are going to ask me who wrote that line, and I am going to say that the line has got a date on it and the wall has not got a name on it, and that is the tradition of this stair and it is nineteen years old and it started with a man of fifty-four and a piece of chalk and it has not got a name on it since.**"

"That is right."

"It is right and it is not me being proud of it. **It is right because the moment a figure on this wall has a name against it, the name is a person somebody can write to and say sorry to, and I have been at the bottom of that and I am not going back.**"

> *Fifth to the ninth of the tenth. An ordinary week, four jobs, nine hours' sleep, one long-case at one pound fourteen and sixpence with the price written out before the case was opened and the invoice the same.*
> *Ninety men went in on the Wednesday at the change of shift because an order book ran out and a man of fifty-one wrote the date on it in a letter book and told nobody and there is no form on which he can be asked.*
> *Nine men put four cycles back on Thursday in about eleven minutes, lawfully, on a notice with a third line about a district that cannot be consulted, and the third line has been in the ordinance since April and nobody has written down who went and looked.*
> *I did not check three figures. **Three people, three clocks, three hands, a minute written down in advance, and I believed them, and it is the first thing I have done this year without an instrument, and I am not going to be able to explain to anybody what it is, and I have not tried, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I have noticed that not being thanked is the entire content of what I have learned this year.**
> *The pair on the hill are 43 min 20 s apart and the rate is a rate again and the step is permanent and the far end is 2 h 31 min 50 s at a water gate with nineteen households drawing off the main under it, and it is going to be an hour further out by the spring.*
> *And a girl of nineteen has written the word unchanged in a column in her own hand on the third of October and put a sentence under it because nobody would believe it, and that is in this book and it is the only good thing in it and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be the man who goes and tells her she did well.*
