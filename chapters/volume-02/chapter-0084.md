# Chapter 84 — What a District Learns From Being Willing

Nothing happened in the four weeks after the fifth of the tenth month, and that is the finding, and it took a month to establish and a fortnight to be believed, and what a district learns from having been willing is that being willing is a thing that costs and a thing that arrives, and the two are not the same size, and only one of them has a form.

He was in bed until half past nine on the Sunday the thirtieth and read, and he was on the bench from the Tuesday, and by the Thursday he had done three days, and the district around him was the ordinary district, and the ordinariness of it is the whole of it.

On the Tuesday the first of the eleventh he did a long-case of 1904 for a man in Bellweather at a price agreed before the case was opened, and the price was **two pounds one shilling and sixpence**, being five hours at the hall's out-of-shop rate of four shillings and sixpence, which is twenty-two shillings and sixpence, and nineteen shillings of material, and the addition is exact and the invoice is the estimate. He slept ten hours on the Sunday night and eight on the Tuesday and he wrote both figures down in the grey book and wrote down that he had written them down, and the reason he wrote them down is the reason he wrote the two hundred and twenty-one days down and it is the same reason every time, and it is that **a grey book is worth nothing in any room that is not the one it is in, and this room is his, and he is the only person who will ever read that line, and he has decided that this is allowed.**

---

**The men went back in. That is the sentence and it is the whole of the finding and it took four weeks to be true of everybody.**

The foundry is back to four hundred and eight on a Tuesday morning, which is eight more than it was on the first of September, because two men who had gone to the yards in August came back in October and one man was born. Nobody recorded either event. **The gate is a gate.** It is two leaves of steel and a guard's hut and a rail that is there to stop a cart and not a man, and it is open at half past six and at two, and Idris Vane is on it at two most days and is not on it at all on the days when there is nothing to be on it about, and **there is no longer a single sentence anywhere in this district about that gate and there has not been for a fortnight, and the absence is not being discussed by anybody, and a thing that stops being discussed is not the same as a thing that has been settled.**

And the thing that has not been settled, and cannot be, is in a pay book in an office on the first floor over that gate, and it is this: **there are three columns in it. Rate, hours, standing deduction. There is no column for the reason a man was not at work between the first and the fifth of September, and there never was, and the men who were out for the whole of the first ten days have a line in that book identical to the line of a man who was ill, and identical to the line of a man who was in a second place, and a clerk cannot enter a reason that has no column, and a clerk is not going to invent one.**

**Three weeks of a pay book with no column for the reason.** He read it, standing up, on the Tuesday afternoon, in the company of a works clerk of about thirty who took the minute of the first document in this district in a year with a body at the bottom of it and has never been thanked for it. It is fifteen lines. Four of them have an odd figure in the hours column and nobody can say why, and the odd figure is eleven minutes short in one week and fifty minutes long in another, and both of those are a man's own arithmetic, and both of those are that man's business, and neither of them is a record.

And the district's other works is the one the whole argument of this book started in, and it is a different works and a smaller one. **The galvanising and wire works is at fifty-eight of ninety-one.** Thirty-three men have gone since the first of the ninth, to the yards, to the docks, and to two districts, and the two districts are not named in any return and the yards did not ask them why and the docks took them because a man who draws wire can stand on a boat.

**That is the half that did not come back, and there is no half that did not come back at Northglass, and the two facts are the same size and only one of them is a decision.**

---

**The tram came back to the public timetable on Tuesday the first of the eleventh at one pound three shillings, and four of the nine traders came back, and five of them did not.**

The route had been off the public timetable on four Wednesdays during the five weeks, on a private hire at four pounds eight against one pound three on the public table, the difference three pounds five, out of a gate fund in a tin. It is on the public timetable now and it is at the public price and the tin has not been asked for a second time and there is no column anywhere for the three pounds five either.

**Perpetua Nye, fifty-one, twenty-six years on that line, held a bus at the Green Span junction on four afternoons in September, for eleven minutes, eleven minutes, nineteen minutes and eleven minutes, and she has not been thanked and has said she will not be, and stopping is her hands and not anybody's number.** She is on the public timetable. She stands at the stop. The stop is a post.

The market on Bellweather Road is a market of forty stalls and **nine of the forty did not open on four Wednesdays in a row, and five of the nine have not come back, and the market is now thirty-five stalls on a good Saturday and it does not know that it is five stalls smaller**, and a market does not have a column either. A trader who lost four Wednesdays because a bus was hired privately for a works' shift-change is not a person who has been harmed by a decision. He is a man who has found out that he is a stall.

---

**And the man at the boundary did what the instruction said, because the instruction had a date in it that nobody had read, and that is the whole of it.**

The works' engineer's eleven lines, in his own hand, in a file, are true:

> *District make-up is not to be made across the boundary during the stoppage. The pumping is a works' asset and the works is not able to supply water across its boundary to a district having no standing under the water act, there being no form on which the supply could be entered and no person in the district who could refuse or accept it. Manual operation of the boundary sluice is a works' operation and is not to be undertaken without the engineer's written order.*

**It is the fourth sentence and not the first that has been governing for five weeks.** *Manual operation* is a works' operation. The boundary sluice is a 1911 clock-driven valve the size of a small table with a reach of about four feet, a pointer and a plate, and it opens on a clock, and the clock has been opening it and shutting it for forty-six years, and the reason a man of forty-one has been standing at it with a bar in an override for five weeks is that the gate is a wedge and the seat has a great deal of iron in the way and the clock-drive will not lift a seat that has been shut since the second of the third month, and it is not a fault and it is not a step and nobody in this city has ever written it down.

On the **Thursday the sixth of the tenth**, at the change of shift, at about half past six, Corvin Tole opened the boundary by hand for the last time. He was in it for fifty-five minutes. He had been in it for fifty-five minutes on the Friday morning of the second of the ninth, and for two hours in a night in May, and for fifty-five minutes at a time every working day since the Thursday of the eighth of September, which is **twenty-one openings and about nineteen hours with a bar in an override at a gearbox, on his own initiative, with no order and no column.**

On the **Friday the seventh** he did not touch it. The clock-drive did it, because the stoppage was over on the Wednesday the fifth and the instruction said *during the stoppage*, and nobody extended it and nobody renewed it and nobody asked him anything.

**And there is no form on which to ask him.** There is no form on which a works' man may be asked why he did a thing for five weeks; there is a form on which a works' man may be instructed, and the instruction was correct, and the forty-one of them worked. He is not a hero and does not think about it and has never been thanked and has never asked. What he has done since the seventh is the only thing anybody in this district did on purpose out of all of it, and it is this: **he has been writing the ward's tank level on a sheet of paper and folding it into his coat, every afternoon at about four, since the second of the ninth, because a man can wash his hands.** A number a man has in his head from looking at a dial is not a record and he has known that for nineteen years and could never have explained why, and now he can, and the explanation is one sheet of paper in a coat.

Nobody has asked him for it. There is no form on which to ask him for it.

**And the tank is at about fourteen hours, and it is not falling, and that is the discovery and it is the end of the four weeks.**

It is not falling because the clock-drive is now putting in exactly what the front and the hospital branch take out, and it has been doing that since the second of the third month and there was a margin of about two days in it until a man put a bar in an override, and now there is none. **The reserve is a tank doing two jobs and it is exactly balanced at fourteen hours and it will be exactly balanced at fourteen hours tomorrow, and it will not come back above two days, and nothing in it is a fault.** And there is no alarm, because the low-level alarm has been inhibited since the second of the third month and no report of the inhibition was made and **no form in this city requires one**; and the warden's day book with the four figures in it and the figures after it is in a box in the store with the store's form on the lid, and the person who kept it stood down on the first of the ninth; and the only person in this city who can see that number every afternoon is a man at a gate who has a piece of paper in his coat and has been asked nothing.

> *And here is what a district learns from having been willing, and it took four weeks to learn and it is not the thing anybody in September expected, and no two of the people who have worked it out have the same sentence.*
>
> **Four hundred and eight men went through a gate at half past six on a Thursday morning because a man of twenty read a works' whistle and did not go through it. Nothing in this city is under any obligation because they did. The rate came back on four cycles on the sixth of the tenth on a nine-line notice with a third line about a district that cannot be consulted. The men who came back went back because a furnace that goes cold costs a fortnight and four of them are the only four in this city. Nobody was dismissed, nobody was locked out, there is no return date, no fund, no arbitration and no two sides, and a man of fifty-one decided in a letter book that he had decided nothing, and there is no form on which a works manager may be asked what he decided.**
> **A district can stop a works. A district cannot stop a schedule. The first took five weeks and four hundred men and one man with a bar in an override. The second takes eleven minutes and nine men with a property qualification and nobody at all, and it is on a printed sheet from 1911 with a window typed on it in 1951, and the only copy of it is on its edge in a box in a store with a form on the lid that has a woman's name on it in capitals.**
> **They are the same size of thing. That is what four weeks of quiet has established and it is the only thing in it. One of the two is real. The other one is a thing that happened, and things that happen are not a thing a district has, and nobody in this district has ever yet been able to put the two on the same line and get them to stay there.**
