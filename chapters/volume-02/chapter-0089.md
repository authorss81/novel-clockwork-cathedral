# Chapter 89 — Where Does a Person Go to Refuse

Ivy Pike signs for the Weir One gate, has kept a book of her own for eleven years and has never shown it to anybody, and on Thursday the twenty-fourth of the eleventh month she went and found out what a person in this city has to do in order to refuse a thing that is being done to the street she lives on, and it is five places and a sixth link that is not a place at all, and it costs fifteen shillings and sixpence, and it takes fifty-one days, and at the end of it there is no counter.

---

**The street is Green Row. The thing is the fourth window.**

Not the fourth window as it is on the copy in Bellweather. **The window that the water actually comes through.** Green Row is off Weir Lane and it has nine houses and it has been getting its water in the small hours since before she was born, and the standing rotation and the winter rotation both put that row on the fourth window, and the print at the works' counter says the fourth is at a quarter to four, and the copy with the fifteen minutes on the face of it says four, **and the difference between those two is the whole of the difference between the two prints, and Green Row is on the fourth window, and there is nothing in this district that says which of the two the machine is following tonight.**

She has read the gauge on the district side of the Weir One gate for eleven years, in a book of her own, in her own hand, on a Sunday, about four times a year, and she has never once been asked why.

"You have a book," he said, on the Thursday morning, in the outer room of the pumping ward, when she put it on the table and then would not leave it.

"I have a book. **And the first thing you are going to do is tell me not to bring it, and I am going to do the thing you are going to tell me not to do, and I have decided that this morning, so save it.**"

"I was not going to say that."

"**Then say what you were going to say, because I have had a week to think about this and I have not slept and I have decided that I am going to do it properly, and I would like the man who has spent six months telling me that asking is the only instrument there is to be in the room while I do it.**"

---

**So she did it properly, and it took four days, and here is the whole of it in the order she did it, and every line of it is a fact and every one of them was true, and not one of the five places did anything to anybody.**

> **1. THE COUNTER OF THE WATER USERS' COMMITTEE, above a chandler's shop in Bellweather. Thursday the twenty-fourth, about eleven o'clock.**
> A letter, in her own hand, four lines long, asking which of the two prints of the winter rotation the machine is following in the fourth window.
> **Nadia Erridge, clerk.** She read it standing up, which is the way that room reads everything, and she said: *I can take this. I can minute that it was received. I cannot act on it and I cannot put it to the committee, and there is no form on which a member of this committee may ask this district anything, and the district has no standing under the water act, and I wrote that sentence on a notice in April because the ordinance has no line for it.*
> **Cost: nothing. Time: about four minutes.**
> *And she said one more thing, at the door, which is not in the letter:* **"You have asked the wrong room first and I am telling you so that you do not waste the second one. Ask the works. The pumping is a works' asset at the boundary and they have to be told."**

> **2. THE DISTRICT WORKS' COUNTER, the yard on the canal road. Thursday the twenty-fourth, about half past two.**
> The clerk is a man of about thirty with eleven years at a counter and he did not sit down.
> *I will receipt this. **A ratepayer may complain about a works' asset and I am a ratepayer, and you are one, and here is the receipt and the date and the reference.***
> **Cost: nothing. Time: twenty-one days, which is what the works' office calls a reply and which is a question and not a determination and cannot stop anything and does not alter any term.**
> *And Ambrose said, standing up, in about forty seconds:* **"The district works does not print the rotation. It prints it and it is the Water Users' Committee's sheet and I have told you that on a board in chalk in a pumping ward. What I will do is find out which print the district got, and I have a way of doing that and I am not going to tell you what it is, because the last time I told a man in this district a method he did it himself and got it wrong and it is in a file."**

> **3. THE WARD. Friday the twenty-fifth and Saturday the twenty-sixth, and this is where the chain stops being a chain and becomes a wall.**
> There is a process. It was designed on the twenty-third of the fifth month in the back room over a chandler's shop and it is four clauses and **it belongs to a woman of forty-four who stood down on the first of the ninth and is not on that room's committee and has not asked to be and has said in four rooms in four weeks that she is the person who answers and has never once been the person who is asked**, and it is the **only instrument in this city by which a person may put a thing in front of a body.**
> *Clause one: a variation entered in a ward book, with quantity, beneficiary and date, posted within one working day.*
> *Clause two: a review on a ground named in the notice, at the request of the council, the works, or any ratepayer of the ward, who is then named.*
> *Clause three: a review that may find against and may find for and shall enter both, because a review that can only find against somebody is not a review, it is a trial.*
> *Clause four: the finding posted in full, with no person thanked in it.*
> **Ivy Pike wrote out the twenty-sixth on the back of an envelope and read it to a woman of twenty-four in a room over a chandler's shop and the woman of twenty-four read it back to her twice and then said the sentence that ends it.**
> *There is no variation. **Nobody varied anything. The winter rotation was agreed in 1911 and printed in 1919 and reprinted eleven times and a window was typed on in 1951 by somebody who wrote nothing down, and the thing it is going to do to me on Tuesday morning is going to happen to me on Tuesday morning and there is no event, no book, no quantity, no beneficiary and no date on which anybody could post a notice about it, and clause one of the only instrument in this city is a notice.***
> **Cost: nothing. Time: it does not start.**
> *And nobody in that room said the obvious thing for about a minute, and then Teo Arden, from the back, said it, because he is the only person in it who enjoys being the one who says it:* **"You cannot put a sheet in front of anybody. You can only put an act in front of somebody, and it has to be an act somebody did, and if nobody did it then there is nothing to review, and that is not a gap in the process. That is what the process is."**

> **4. THE ASSEMBLY'S PUBLIC-HEALTH COMMITTEE. A letter posted on the Friday and a demand entered on the Monday the twenty-eighth.**
> **Councilor Imani Rook, 40, chairs it, and she filed the toll-ledger demand herself on the fourth of the sixth on arithmetic, and she is right, and nobody thanked her.**
> She will take a demand. **A demand is a standing thing: read twice, entered on the minutes, discharged, read again next year, and it is the only instrument in that building which survives a change of clerk.** A question takes twenty-one days and cannot suspend anything. A demand does not bind the works and does not bind the committee and does not alter a schedule.
> **Cost: nothing, and a stamp. Time: nineteen days to the next sitting, which was the earliest she could fix in a telephone call, and she fixed it in about ninety seconds and did not soften it.**
> *And she said the one sentence she has said all year and has never softened, standing, before anybody asked her for anything:* **"I am not asking this committee to feel anything about a sheet of paper from 1911 with a window typed on it in 1951. I am asking it to build the thing that is missing, which is a column, and a column exists for about forty years and a feeling does not."**

> **5. THE OFFICE OF PUBLIC RECKONING. Monday the twenty-eighth, at about ten past eleven, at the one window, in a building with a court outside it.**
> Four staff. One window. A written right to be ignored, and a drawer of printed four-line cards.
> *And what the Office can do about it is put it on a card, and the card goes in a drawer, and a drawer holds a card about nine hundred times, and the Office has never in nine years had a second person in a room with it, and it added up four hundred and eleven head counts in three sittings in October and published the sum of them with five sentences of margin under it, and it is the only instrument in this city that can count anything, **and it cannot make a pumping district's water come at a quarter to four.***
> **Cost: nothing. Time: about four minutes and then it is in a drawer, and the drawer is not a place anybody has ever been held to account.**

> **6. And there is no sixth office, and there is no sixth counter, and this is not because this city is careless. It is because the last link is not an office.**
> A question can be put to a machine. The spindle in the Question Hall takes a named act — a thing somebody could physically do, to a named thing, with a number and a day on it — and it takes it or it does not take it.
> ***This is not a named act. It is a refusal.***
> **There is no form anywhere in this city on which the question *may I refuse this* may be put to anybody, because a form requires a thing to be done and a refusal is a thing not done, and every form in this city is a form for doing something, and the whole of this district's condition in one sentence is that **the only thing a person may put in writing in this city is an instruction to somebody else to act, and there is no paper on which a person may say no and be heard.***
> **Cost of the whole chain: three shillings for a box at the Archive that Ivy Pike did not need; one shilling to inspect the register of companies, which was a mistake and which she made on purpose to find out what a shilling buys; two shillings and sixpence a bus, four times; and one shilling and sixpence in stamps. Fifteen shillings and sixpence.**
> **Time: eleven days to a review that does not start, twenty-one days to a reply that cannot stop anything, and nineteen days to the next sitting of a committee that cannot bind either of them. Fifty-one days. And at the end of the fifty-one days there is no counter.**

---

**And the two of them were in the room on the Saturday afternoon, and they disagreed, and both of them were right, and nobody in the room is going to make them choose.**

**Wenna Pruitt, 38, tenant-council organiser.** "The chain is a gift and I am not going to be the woman who says it is not. **Eleven days, twenty-one days, nineteen days, four minutes and five places, and every one of the five is a place where a person was received, and being received is not nothing — in this city being received is most of what there is.** I have spent this year saying that a page has no address, and a chain has five addresses, and I would like somebody in this room to sit with that for a minute."

**Hanna Stroud, 54, nine blocks, about two hundred and eleven households.** "**And it is worth nothing, and I am not going to be the woman who says it is worth nothing, and here is the difference between us and it is not a difference of arithmetic.**

"You have been in eleven rooms this year and every one of them received you. I have been in about two hundred and eleven rooms this year, four a week, on a rent dispute, and in every one of them I was received, and **in none of them was I refused anything, because I never asked to be.** That is the discovery. **There is no counter in this city that has ever refused anybody anything, because there is nothing to refuse. A refusal needs a thing to be offered. Nobody in this city offers a pumping district its water; the water is a works' asset at a boundary, and the works' office received my complaint and it is a question and a question cannot stop anything, and a body that receives a question and cannot stop anything is a counter that has never once said no in four years.**

"So I am not going to stand here and be grateful for five addresses. **I would rather have one counter that has said no to me.**"

**Wenna Pruitt:** "That is the best thing anybody has said in this district since September and you are not going to be thanked for it by me, and I am not going to be thanked for it by anybody, and I am going to say the half of it and then I am going to stop, and I have said since June that neither of us is going to choose.

"**If a counter never refuses then a district can be walked. A refusal needs a thing on the table to be refused, and there is nothing on the table, and the nothing on the table is the schedule, and the schedule is a sheet from 1911 and it is in force and it is correct, and there is no act, and there is no notice, and there is no ground to name in it.**"

**Hanna Stroud:** "Then we are where we were on the nineteenth of the eighth."

"**We are exactly where we were on the nineteenth of the eighth,**" said Wenna Pruitt, "and I am not going to choose, and neither are you, and it is not going to be settled this month, and Mr Vale is not going to settle it with a number on a wall, and I would like it minuted that both of those were said out loud in a room in November and neither of us moved."

The minute book in that room is 1948 and nobody wrote it down.

---

**And the finding is Ivy Pike's and she made it on the Monday morning at about half past six at the Weir One gate, standing, with a book of her own in her hand, and she has never shown it to anybody and she showed it to four people on the Monday because the argument was over whether it counted and she wanted it to be seen that it did not count.**

"I have got eleven years of Sundays in this," she said, "and four readings a year, and every one of them in my own hand on a Sunday, and it is the most careful record of the water at that gate in this district and it is worth **nothing whatever.** It has no form. It has no countersignature. It cannot be produced in a room because I would not produce it, and I would not produce it because I am not going to be the woman who did not go and knock. **It is eleven years of the only evidence in this district that anybody keeps, and it is a woman with a book, and the reason it is not evidence is that nobody has standing to ask her for it and she has not offered.**

"So that is the answer to your question and I have been walking about this city for four days looking for it and it is the one thing I already knew and would not write down."

"Which is."

"**A person who knows the chain can be walked.** That is the one honest use of it and it is not nothing and it is not a comfort. **I can go into that works' office and I can say to a clerk: you have received this, and the answer you have given me is a question, and a question cannot stop a rotation, and you know that, and so do I, and there is a fifth place further along and it cannot bind you either, and there is no sixth counter at all, and I have been in four rooms this week and every one of them has been extremely polite to me.** And a polite clerk is a person who is going to do nothing and is not going to be blamed, and I can say all of that in about ninety seconds, in a yard, in daylight, to a man at a counter.

"**And being walked is not the same as being refused. Being refused needs a man who has to say no and be able to say what happens if he does. There is no such man in this chain. And this city has never once had to make the second thing work, which is why nobody in it has noticed that the second thing is the whole of it.**"

She put the book in her coat.

> *Twenty-fourth to the twenty-eighth of the eleventh. **A chain, traced from the other end by a woman who signs for a gate, and every link of it true, and there are six links, and the sixth is not an office.***
> *Three shillings for a box that was not needed. One shilling for a register, spent on purpose to find out what a shilling buys. Two shillings and sixpence a bus, four times. One shilling and sixpence in stamps. **Fifteen shillings and sixpence.***
> *Eleven days to a review that cannot start, because there is no act. Twenty-one days to a reply that is a question and cannot stop anything. Nineteen days to the next sitting of a committee that cannot bind the works or itself. **Fifty-one days.***
> *And at the end of the fifty-one days there is no counter, and the reason is not that this city is careless. **The reason is that every form in this city is a form for doing something, and a refusal is a thing not done, and there is no paper on which a person may say no and be heard.***
> *And the chain has one honest use and Ivy Pike named it at the gate on the Monday at half past six, standing up, and it is this: **a person who knows the chain can be walked. And being walked is not the same as being refused, and this city has never had to make the second thing work.***
> *Two working women disagreed on the Saturday and both of them were right and neither of them chose, and it is the fourth time he has said it and the third month of it, and it is not going to be settled here, and it is not going to be settled by a number on a wall.*
> *Nobody thanked anybody. A woman of thirty-eight and a woman of fifty-four have been refusing to choose since the nineteenth of the eighth. A woman of sixty-one who reads a gauge four times a year has a book nobody may ask her for. And a man of thirty-two was in the room the whole time and was not asked a question and did not put one.*
