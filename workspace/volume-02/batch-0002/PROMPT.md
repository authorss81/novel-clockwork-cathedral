## The batch in one paragraph

The batch begins with the document that has been running the district for four years and that nobody has voted on. **A pumping district's water is not governed by the Water Users' Committee, which can only vary a schedule; it is governed by the standing rotation, which is a printed sheet dated 1919, reprinted eleven times, with a fourth draw added to it in 1951, and which nobody in Morrowfen has read since.** A man with no licence walks four and a half miles of branch to find out why a valve opened at twenty to six in the morning, and the answer is a piece of paper about eleven feet from his shoulder, and the piece of paper is in a warden's office because a pumping district is not a body and a document cannot be a party to anything. He practises on two retired hospital timers and on a bridge joint, and finds out that half of what he has been calling his gift is instruments, and that the instruments cost eleven pounds and eleven minutes and a sounding board. A veteran operator puts him on the engine and he gets a **choice trace** — three pressure paths, a blind margin he has read a hundred times, a toll — and it takes forty-one seconds out of a sequence he cannot get back, and after it he cannot hold the order of assembly of a movement in his head without writing it down, for ever, and starts a second, narrow book. The machine's answer about his own district is **the same four lines he could have got from his own hand in June**, and it took four days and a witness rail to say, and when he tries to ask it whether the district should come inside the model the spindle refuses it, because it is not a thing anybody could physically do. The Water Users' Committee re-votes the daytime draw from four to two because the front needs it in August, and **the ramp stops on the second of the eighth month and his best number goes out of date because nine men met**, and both are correct. A tram route comes off the public timetable for six hours on a certificate of condition that is **true**, because a condition is certified against a machine's own local beat and not against the district reference, and a public timetable is what a fare table is based on. And on the Northglass freight line a works manager of fifty-one has done his sums and found out that **a nil return is worth three shillings in the pound on sixty-one thousand pounds a year, and he is right, and there is no column in this city for what his four hundred men are not**, and at the end of the chapter a young man with a list of eleven starts to organise a stoppage at the only works in the district that can be stopped, and the reader can see immediately that the hospital's supply runs through it.
