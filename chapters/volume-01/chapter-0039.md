# Chapter 39 — Deferred, With a Schedule and a Budget Line

The schedule of maintenance works for the Lower Weirs pumping district is a single sheet, printed on both sides, with a rule at the head and a table beneath it, and it went up on a board outside the district works' yard on the Thursday morning of the twenty-seventh of the fourth month, and it went up on the same board as the water-main repairs and the hydrant renewals and a notice about a closure.

It is a good sheet. That has to be said first and it has to be said without anything else, because the whole of what follows turns on the sheet being excellent and correct and lawful and published, and because none of those four words is a defence of anything at all.

> *DISTRICT WORKS PROGRAMME. SCHEDULE OF MAINTENANCE WORKS, LOWER WEIRS PUMPING DISTRICT. Adopted the twenty-second inst. Programme year three of the current triennium.*

> | Item | Work | Estimate | Programme year |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | 1 | Weir Two, second stage: bearing renewal and re-alignment of the gate-handling line | £412 6s | Three |
> | 2 | Weir Two, first stage: sluice gearing, complete | £268 14s | Three |
> | 3 | Weir One, escapement governor: replacement of the governor and its bed | £96 0s | Two |
> | 4 | District main, Bellweather Road to the Weir One gate: re-survey of timing and regulation, eleven miles | £344 10s | Two |
> | 5 | Gate signal tables, all four gates: renewal of the printed tables and of the lamp boxes | £57 4s | Two |
> | 6 | Standpipes, district: bracket replacement, six hundred brackets | £1 890 0s | Two and Three |
> | 7 | Green Span hospital trestle, conduit bracket: survey and re-estimate, with new pattern from the original drawing | £2 14s 6d | Three |
> | 8 | Weir One gate, indicator lamp and contact | £4 2s | One |
> | 9 | Anticorrosion, pump house roofs, Weir Two and Weir Four | £1 127 0s | One |
>
> *Total, district works programme, Lower Weirs pumping district, three years: **£4 202 10s 6d**.*

Item three is the Weir One gate's escapement governor. It is on the sheet because a governor made for two cycles a day that has been asked for four is a governor that will be replaced, and it is on the sheet with a figure and a programme year and no note of any kind about the fact that the only person in this city who knew it was old, and that it was the only part of the business that would not be asked about, was a licensed maintainer in the Clockwork Quarter who could not certify a line and could not say the thing out loud in a room of a hundred and thirty people, and whose rule stopped him, correctly, in a question hall on the eighth of the third month, and who has not been able to use it since and did not try.

Item four is the line. Eleven miles, re-survey of timing and regulation, £344 10s, in programme year two. The line is not named. The line is described as *the district main, Bellweather Road to the Weir One gate*, and it is the main, and there is nothing in the sheet that says the main has been running eleven minutes and forty seconds out for a month, because the main has not been running out. The main is correct. The machines on the main are out, and a survey of a main is a survey of the main, and nobody has asked for a survey of the machines.

And the trestle. Item seven. The conduit bracket on the Green Span hospital trestle that was surveyed two years and two months before the sixteenth of the second month, estimated at £2 14s 6d including a new pattern from the original drawing, deferred twice in a lighter hand to *the district works programme, next triennium*, and carried forward each time, on a line under a bracket that had gone out of service from a tired cast fixing, with a span dropped three-eighths of an inch at the third bracket, of a pattern not made in thirty years.

It is on the sheet. It has been carried forward twice and it is now in a programme with a year against it, and the year is the year the survey falls, and a survey is not a repair and the estimate on the line is two years old and the number under it is a number Orrin wrote in a corridor two months ago and read the reverse of twice and pressed.

It is the furthest forward it has ever been. It is also, in substance, the third deferral of the same bracket, in a different type and with a survey attached, and a survey is the thing a bracket needs before a bracket can be paid for, and he is the man in this city who knows that the survey is the only good news on that sheet, and there is no column on the sheet for the good news.

---

Leda Vale did it on the Thursday morning, in the office on the third floor, at about a quarter to eight, to fourteen people, before anybody had spent a penny of it.

She had asked for the room and had been given it, and she had asked for a copy of the programme sheet, and the ward's clerk had got it off a board in a yard because the hospital's engineering department keeps one pinned up, and Leda had it in her hand.

"I am going to read you a number and then I am going to read you a paragraph out of a works programme, and then I am going to ask the two ward sisters a question, and the question is going to be about your hours and not about the ward's budget, and you are allowed to tell me to get out."

Nobody told her to get out.

"Item six is standpipes, six hundred brackets, eighteen hundred and ninety pounds, over two years. That is a great deal of money and it is real money and it is in a programme and it will be spent, and I have read it and I am glad of it, because a standpipe bracket is a fire thing and this hospital has one riser on the third-floor landing that has been weeping since the second month of the year.

"Here is the other number. The Green Span hospital branch of the district main is eleven minutes and forty seconds out. It has been out since the fifteenth of last month. It was out on the Thursday before that. It is out now. Three clocks in this building disagree with each other, and the pharmacy timer loses four and a half minutes a round, and the public clock in the porter's hall is correct to a tenth of a second on four readings and is the only one anybody should be using and is the only one nobody uses.

"Those two facts are in the same document. One of them is being paid eighteen hundred and ninety pounds over two years. The other one is in a programme at item four as a re-survey of a main, at three hundred and forty-four pounds, in programme year two, and it is a survey of the main, and the main is not out. The machines are out. And there is no line in that sheet that says a machine, because a machine is a maintenance item and a machine is fine.

"So here is what I want said in this room, out loud, before any of that money is spent, in front of the people whose hours it is. Our hours are eleven minutes and forty seconds shorter than the hour we are being given. That is not a complaint about the money. The money is good. That is a statement of what the money is buying, made by the people it is being bought with, on the day the sheet is published, and not on the day somebody comes up here in two years with a bill."

"And if the eleven minutes and forty seconds is still there in two years?" said the senior sister.

"Then it will still be here in two years and I shall say it again at this hour on a Thursday morning, and it will be on the card on the board in the nurses' room, and the card will be dated, because that is what I have been doing since the twenty-fourth of last month and I am not stopping."

The senior sister said, "Say the next bit."

"The next bit is that nobody is going to do anything about it and I have not come here to be disappointed. I came here to have said it in a room with fourteen people in it before a thousand pounds is spent on a standpipe, because I have worked in two buildings where the money arrived and the number was said afterwards, and in both of them the number was smaller when it was said afterwards." She put the sheet down. "That is all. I have a shift at two."

---

The works committee of the Assembly sat on the Friday morning of the twenty-eighth of the fourth month and it is a body of eleven members and it does not meet often and its minutes are printed, and item six of the Lower Weirs schedule was in front of it as a queried item because a Member had asked, and the Member was the chair of the Assembly's public-health committee, and the chair of the Assembly's public-health committee is Councilor Imani Rook of Green Span, and she was in the third bench from the back and she did not speak for forty minutes.

Mara Venn had eleven minutes and she had asked for them as a member of the public, which is the same instrument as Sel Kade's and it is the only instrument there is, and she was not able to ask for them and get them and so she had asked the Member who had asked, and the Member who had asked was Imani Rook, and Mara Venn had never in nine years been on the same side of a table as Councilor Rook and had not expected to be.

"I am going to take one minute on the health of everybody in this room and then I am going to do the thing I came to do, and Mr Chairman, I would be obliged if you let me do it without the minute."

"Proceed."

"Thank you." She put a sheet on the table. "This is a schedule of maintenance works. It is presented under a maintenance heading. It is adopted under a maintenance heading. And under the works rules a maintenance schedule is a schedule that states how often a work is to be inspected, and this one states, over nine items and three years, when nine things are going to be repaired, and it does not say once when anything is going to be looked at.

"Which means it is not a maintenance schedule. It is a list of repairs. And a body that adopts a list of repairs under a maintenance heading has adopted something it did not mean to adopt, and the reason it did not mean to adopt it is that the reason anybody in this room wanted a maintenance schedule is that the district's gear is old, and there is no way on God's earth to know how a thing is doing from a list of when you are going to mend it.

"I am not attacking the sum. The sum is honest, the sum is well got out, and I have checked the arithmetic of two of the items against the district's own returns and I am not going to say one word against the sum, and that is the first time in nine years I have stood in a room and said that about anything.

"I am asking the committee to add an inspection interval. Every item in this schedule to be inspected at a stated frequency, from the day of adoption, and the first inspection to be in programme year one, not two, and the interval to be on the sheet and not in a file. If the committee does that, then in a year's time somebody in the district works will have to walk eleven miles of main with a watch, on a date, and put something on paper, and that something will be a number, and the number will be in the district's hands, because a number that comes off an inspection is a number a district can be given.

"If the committee does not do that, then this document is a list of nine things somebody intends to mend in about two years, and the word *maintenance* on the head of it is doing a piece of work that the contents of it have not done, and I do not think anybody in this room is dishonest and I think every one of you would find that embarrassing if you were asked about it."

Mr Chairman said, "Is there a section?"

"There is a section and I have brought it and I am not going to read it out, because the clerk can find it in four minutes and because a woman reading a section aloud in this room sounds like a woman with a point, and I would rather it sounded like a form."

"Will the chair take a motion?"

Imani Rook stood up and it was the first time she had spoken and the room noticed it.

"I have a question and then I have a motion and the two are the same thing and I am going to be very short." She put her hand flat on the table, which she did not do, and had never done. "Mr Chairman, I have chaired a public-health committee for three years and I have blocked a demand in this city that a toll ledger be published, and I did that on arithmetic, and I have said so, and I would like somebody to write in the minutes today that I am still saying it.

"Item six is a standpipe programme. Item seven is a hospital trestle bracket that has been carried forward twice, and the estimate on it is two years old, and a survey is what a bracket needs before a bracket can be paid for, and I have been waiting since the second month of the year for somebody to put a survey on a sheet with a year against it, and it is on this sheet, and I have read it twice, and I did not know until this morning that it was there.

"Mr Venn's motion is right. And the reason it is right is not sentiment and it is not the Weirs and it is not anything anybody said at me in a room. It is that a schedule with no interval is a schedule I cannot hold anybody to, and I have spent three years trying to hold people to things and I have never once succeeded with a document that had no date in it for the next look.

"I move the interval, at the frequency the works' engineer states, from the day of adoption, and the first inspection in programme year one. And I will tell the committee why I am moving it, because I am not going to pretend to a motive I do not have: **I have been shown arithmetic again, and I have never yet been shown anything else, and I do not intend to start now.**"

The division was carried by eight to three, and the third was not the clerk, and no amendment was offered, and the new sheet was printed the same afternoon.

It went up on the same board, over the top of the old one, and the type was the same type.

> *As adopted, with the addition at item 10:*
> *10. Inspection interval. Every item in this schedule to be inspected at the interval stated by the works' engineer and not exceeding twelve months, from the date of adoption. The first inspection falls in programme year one. The interval and the results of inspection to be published in the district's own sheet and not filed only.*

And the total on the new sheet was **£4 613 18s 6d**, because item 10 put eleven inspections a year on the schedule for three years at the district works' rate of **twelve pounds nine shillings and fourpence** the inspection, which is thirty-three of them, and the sum had gone up by **four hundred and eleven pounds and eight shillings** in the space of about six hours, and nobody in that room thought that was important, and Orrin Vale did, standing at a board in the rain at half past five in the evening with a sheet of his own arithmetic in his pocket.

---

Here is the arithmetic, and he did it at the bench that night with the grey book open and the hand shut, because it took him about two hours and it is the only arithmetic in this book that anybody will want in a year's time.

*The published total for three years of maintenance in the pumping district: £4 613 18s 6d. The figure without the inspection interval: £4 202 10s 6d. The difference is thirty-three inspections at twelve pounds nine shillings and fourpence. Both are real and both are on a board and both are in type that is well set.*

*The hall's price schedule: two shillings the head for a year, maintenance-deferred class, standing withdrawal, form GH-9. Four men. Eight shillings. For a year.*

*The hall's out-of-shop tariff: four shillings and sixpence the hour. That is the figure I charge a customer for one hour of my own time away from the bench, and it is the same figure as the annual renewal fee, which is a coincidence of institutional life that has never meant anything.*

*At that rate, the three hundred and eighty-eight hours of night pumping at Weir Two that the district counted off a board and a kitchen book, and that the Assembly's public-health committee ordered printed in full with the minutes on the twentieth of last month, at four pounds ten out of a Green Span ward's allowance, would have come to eighty-seven pounds and six shillings.*

*Eighty-seven pounds and six shillings for four months. Four thousand two hundred and two pounds over three years for the whole district. The programme is about forty-eight times the price of the crew.*

*And nobody chose that. That is the sentence. A price schedule is a page of a hall's book with hourly rates in it and it knows exactly what a night's work costs, because it is a page of hourly rates. A works programme is a page of a committee's book with item rates in it, and it knows exactly what a governor costs and exactly what a bearing costs and exactly what a survey costs, and none of the three numbers is a man. The arithmetic is not a mistake. The arithmetic is the correct use of a well-run system, and the system is not corrupt, and nobody in it is a villain, and the eight shillings and the four thousand two hundred and two pounds were both set by competent people who were each doing exactly what their book was for.*

*And that is what all of this has been about since the fifteenth of the first month, and I have been writing it down in this book since the nineteenth of last month as though it were an event, and it is not an event. It is a budget. Nothing in this business has ever been a scandal. Everything in this business is a budget.*

He read that last line twice and it did not improve, and he left it.

And the two things that had happened in the eight hours since the morning were these, and he wrote them side by side on the same page because the page had room and because the page was the only place either of them was going to be.

*A woman in a hospital said the number out loud, in a room, in front of the people whose hours it is, before a penny of it was spent. That is the second instrument in this book. I have watched it work twice now and both times it produced a line on a sheet and not an hour, and both times the people who did it were right, and both times the line was worth having.*

*An investigator in a room on the Rise defeated a document on its own terms in eleven minutes and got an inspection interval and a publication clause into a published schedule, and it cost four hundred and eleven pounds and four days, and in eleven months somebody is going to have to walk eleven miles of main with a watch on a date, and put a number on a sheet, and that number is going to be a number the district can be given.*

*Neither of those two things would have happened this morning. Both of them happened because two people knew how to make a room read a form, and one of them has said out loud in a hall of a hundred and thirty people that she will not be able to do it again, and the other of them said it into a corridor in a hospital four months ago and has been saying it to a ward ever since.*

*And the district still cannot be on a rail. And the ward still compares three clocks by hand every four hours. And the trestle bracket has a survey and a year and a figure that is two years old, and that is the furthest it has ever been, and it is the only line on that sheet that anybody in this city is glad of, and I am glad of it, and I cannot say why I am glad of it in any language that the committee would accept.*

He shut the book, and the hand closed, mostly, and the little finger was still late, and outside the rain was coming up the street in the way it does when the tide is making, and it was a Thursday night, and the sheet was on a board in a yard with an interval on it and a sum that had gone up by four hundred and eleven pounds because a woman had stood up in a room on the Rise and said *I have been shown arithmetic again*, and the price of the crew was eighty-seven pounds and six shillings, and the whole of it was on a board, and nobody was going to be angry about it in the morning, and that was correct, and there was not one thing in it that was anybody's fault.
