# Chapter 75 — The Scale, and a Girl Reading a Card

Between forty thousand and sixty thousand residents of this city have been taken out of its civic model in eighteen years, and more than half of them lived in the Weirs, and they were taken out in dated batches of which four hundred and eleven are entered in a register, and every one of them is a form with a series, a head count, a price, and a countersignature, and every one of them was lawful, and it is not a scandal, and it is not a rounding error, and the reason that a man of twenty in a room full of people cannot get angry about it is that there is nothing in it to be angry at, and the reason a girl of thirteen can find her father's name in it in about eleven minutes is that it is a printed book with a margin.

---

**Thursday the fifteenth of the ninth month, at about eleven in the morning, on the two boards outside the public Archive on Cathedral Rise, and there was a queue by two o'clock.**

> **PUBLIC STATEMENT OF SCALE. ARCHIVE OF UNLISTED — INDEX OF BATCHES.**
> *Published by the Registry of Transfers and Access at the public counter, Cathedral Rise, and open to inspection on the day of publication and on every day thereafter until withdrawn by the Commissioner. It is a statement of scale. It is not a copy of records, and no record may be taken away, and no person may be given one.*

**Sindri Lake put it up himself, and he has been the keeper of that public room for nineteen years, and he did it in about four minutes with his own hands, and nobody asked him to.**

He is sixty-one. He is a keeper and not an archivist and he says so. He has four things: a card index of names with the date each was taken out, which is the public one; a batch register, being the batches; an accession book; and a shelf list, which is public and is eleven lines long and has not changed in nineteen years. **Three of those four are Directorate records, are not in the public room, cost three shillings to ask for upstairs, and the fee is not the obstacle.** The obstacle is the other three of the four, and it has always been the other three of the four, and the price on the door is a courtesy and everybody in this city has believed it for a hundred years.

And what was on the second board, in the Registry's own type, was this, and it is the whole of the document:

> *Minute of the Registry of Transfers and Access, the fifteenth day of the ninth month. **Received and noted. Published with the minutes.***
>
> *The Registry records that the number of residents of this city removed from the civic model in the operation of the transfer and access series, from the eleventh day of the first month, 1911, to the date above, is stated in the accompanying index to be **not less than forty thousand and not more than sixty thousand persons**; that **more than one half** of that number are resident in the pumping district of the Lower Weirs and in the wards adjoining it; and that the removals were made in dated batches, of which **four hundred and eleven** are entered in the index, each batch being a form carrying a series, a head count, a price, and a countersignature.*
>
> *Source of this minute: the batch register, being a Directorate record in a box. The reference is not stated to the public.*
>
> *This minute is published. A number in a set of minutes whose source is a box may be called a fabrication. The Registry does not publish numbers that may be called fabrications and will not do so again.*

---

**And the man who wrote that last paragraph came to the counter at about ten past two on the Thursday afternoon and said the reason out loud before anybody asked him for it, which is the first thing that has ever happened in this building.**

He is **Perry Shaw, forty-seven, Commissioner of the Registry of Transfers and Access**, and he is not a villain and he is not a plant and he is a public servant who has been extremely careful.

"**That is the reason and it is a good reason and I will not stand in this city and let it be said to be a dirty one.**" He had his hat off. He had it off in a room with about eleven people in it and nobody had asked him to. "A figure in a minute that is not published is a figure anybody may call invented, and in this city that is not a slander, it is a technique, and I have watched it used four times in six years against people who were telling the truth. I have been asked for that figure four times in six years. Three of those times the request came from a man who wanted to win an argument in a room and one of those times it came from a man in a good coat who wanted to be the first man in England to have it.

"**I am giving it to everybody at once, on two boards, at a public counter, with my office's name on it and the source stated as a box.** If somebody is going to call forty to sixty thousand invented, they may come to this counter and look at the index, which is the thing itself and is not a copy, and they may put their name in the book of visitors, which is public and has not had a full page in nineteen years and will have four pages by the end of this month."

"And it is not the reason anybody in this room wanted it," said Orrin Vale.

"**No. It is not.**" He put his hat back on. "The reason the man in the good coat wanted it is that he wanted to be first. The reason a man of twenty in your district wanted it is that he wanted a number to put in front of somebody. And the reason the Assembly wants it is that a committee cannot discuss a thing it has not counted, and every one of those three is a good enough reason and I would have published on any of them and I am telling you the real one because you would have found it out in a fortnight and I would rather you heard it from me on a Thursday."

He looked at the second board for a moment.

"And I will say one more thing, since I have this counter and there is nobody here to stop me. **I have not put the head counts on those boards and I will not, and the reason is not concealment. Four hundred and eleven head counts is a figure, and a figure wants an audit, and there is one office in this city that audits anything and it has four staff and one window and it has never audited this.** The number on that board is a range because a range is what a Registry can stand behind in a morning. **The moment somebody adds up those four hundred and eleven numbers properly, with a working on it, in a document, the range is over, and it will be a figure with a working on it, and I want that done by somebody with a ledger and not by somebody with a pencil.**"

Nobody had a ledger.

---

**Friday the sixteenth, from ten in the morning until about seven in the evening, in the reading room, and the room is a public room and about twenty-six people were in it and nobody was asked anything.**

There are nine people who came to do what Orrin Vale did, which is read.

The index is a foolscap book, about four hundred pages, and it is a single column, and every line is a batch, and the printing is the Registry's and it has been printing it since about 1920 and the type is the same type.

> *BATCH 21 OF 4 — SERIES GH-9 — lodged the seventeenth day of the first month — **head count 41** — price: standing withdrawal, maintenance-deferred class, two shillings the head for a year — **countersigned**, in accordance with the standing orders of the hall, on behalf of a district having no standing under the water act.*

There are four hundred and eleven of them. Every line has all five things on it. **The series letter in the second column, the date in the third, a head count in the fourth, a price in the fifth, and a countersignature in the sixth.** Every price on four hundred and eleven lines is a price in shillings per head per year, and the prices in the book run from a shilling and fourpence to two shillings, and there is not one of them that is not a price.

And in the left-hand margin, against every executed batch, **a diagonal stroke through the number.**

**There is no name on any of the four hundred and eleven lines.** There is a countersignature. There is a class of holder and a hall. There is no name, and that is not an omission and it is not a redaction, and the reason is a rule one man made for himself in about 1919 and has never written down, because a written rule is a thing somebody can repeal in a morning and his own practice cannot.

Sindri Lake said it out loud in the reading room at about half past eleven on the Friday, to about nine people, and it is the sentence the whole book has been standing behind without knowing it.

"**An index entry is a statement that something was taken out. It is not the thing that was taken out, and it never was, and it never will be.** The day an index entry becomes a copy of what it indexes is the day somebody changes a head count in a room with no light in it, and I have never permitted it in this room and I am not going to now, and the reason I have not permitted it is not integrity. **It is that the day you permit it, the index is worth exactly as much as the box, and the box is upstairs and the box has a fee on it, and the index is on a public counter for nothing.**"

"So there is no name," said Teo Arden, from the back, "on any of the four hundred and eleven."

"No."

"**Then nobody can be traced and nothing can be denied, and those two facts are the same fact, and they are yours, and you have had them since 1919, and you have never written them down.**"

"That is the first accurate thing anybody has said in this room in about nine years and I am not going to thank you for it and I am not going to tell you that you are the only person who has worked it out, because a woman of twenty-six worked out a version of it at a counter on the eleventh of the seventh about a form and a body, and she is not in this room and she is better at it than you are."

---

**And the scale was said out loud in that room four times on the Friday and twice on the Saturday, and every time it was said somebody else said it back in a different mouth, and none of the six was wrong.**

**The first mouth was the keeper's**, and it is in official capacity, and it is the oldest thing in the room.

"**Lawful at every step.** I want that said by somebody who has been in this room nineteen years and who can see the whole of it, and I am the only person who can, and I am going to say it plainly and I am not going to be thanked for it because it is not a thing anybody should be thanked for.

"Every one of those four hundred and eleven is a form. Every form has a series and a date and a head count and a price and a countersignature. A price is a decision about a person, and a decision with a price on it was taken by somebody who knew exactly what they were doing and had a number in front of them when they took it. **There is no warrant in the law of this city for taking a person out of a district. There never has been one. Nobody in this city has ever been marched out of a district, and I would know, because this is the room where they would have been brought.**"

"Then how—" said somebody at the back.

"**They were not taken out of a district. A thing was withdrawn. A class, a series, a return, a standing.** The class goes; everybody in the class goes with it, the way a number goes with a line when a clerk strikes a line out of a schedule. **A man is not removed from this city. A man is removed from a series, and a series is a piece of paper, and the piece of paper is priced, and the price is two shillings the head for a year, and it was correct, and the clerks did their work.**" He put his hand flat on the open book. "And I have kept the accession book for nineteen years and I will tell you what is in it, because nobody has ever asked me: **four hundred and eleven in, and four hundred and eleven out, and the two numbers agree, and the out column has a date against every line.** It has never disagreed. Not once. Not in nineteen years. **And that is the most frightening thing I know about this city and I have never said it in this room before and I am saying it because the man in the good coat has been dead for a year and the queue is outside the door.**"

**The second mouth was a man of twenty's**, and it is the argument of the whole business, and it took about four minutes and nobody interrupted him.

"**It is not a scandal.** I have been waiting since the fifteenth of the sixth for somebody to walk into a room and tell me it is a monster, so that I could be angry at a building, and I could go and put a stone through a window of a building and be *right*, and I have been in eleven rooms this year and I have got the number and there is no building.

"Every one of those four hundred and eleven was done by a man who knew exactly what he was doing. At a price. On a form. In daylight. With his own name at the bottom and somebody else's at the top and a countersignature against it, and every fourth one of them countersigned on behalf of a district that had no standing, **which means there was no person in that district who could have said no, and the man who wrote that clause on the form knew it, and the man who countersigned it read it.**" He had his hands at his sides and he was not performing. "**It is not a rounding error. Forty to sixty thousand is not a number that got out of hand. It is a number that was planned, in batches, in a series, over eighteen years, and the batches got smaller and the price got steadier and that is what a *programme* looks like, and a programme is not a monster, a programme is a thing with a schedule.**"

"Then what am I angry at," said Wenna Pruitt.

"**You cannot be, and that is what I have found out, and I have fought for five months and I have won every single fight and I have got a bill.**" He turned round. "I have got a range and a form and a hall and a register office and a counter and a printer and a price, and every one of the six of them is still there and open, and two of them take money still. **I came into this district in the fourth month to find out why a man's name was in a column with nothing beside it, and I have got this, and it is the answer, and the answer is that the city removed forty to sixty thousand people at two shillings the head and wrote down the price of every one of them, and then it went on doing the next one, and it never had to lie to anybody once.**"

Nobody in that room had anything to say to that, and he was twenty years old, and there is nothing on that table to be angry with, and there is nothing on it to do either, and both of those are the same sentence and it is the sentence this district has been living inside since the fourth of the fourth month.

---

**And at about four o'clock on the Friday afternoon a man of about thirty with a pencil started to do a sum, and a woman of thirty-three stopped him, and she gave a different reason than the one anybody in this district has heard before, and the reason was different because the argument had moved.**

Ambrose had it in about nine seconds, because he is a man who does sums in rooms and has done two of them this year and both of them were correct.

"More than one half of forty to sixty thousand, in a district of a hundred and forty thousand. **If the half is at the low end of the range and it is all in the Weirs, then—"**

"**Stop.**" Mara Venn is thirty-three and she is an investigator of the Office of Public Reckoning and the Office audits tolls and a batch of two shillings the head is a toll in the oldest sense there is, and she has four staff, one window, and a written right to be ignored, and she came to a public Archive on a Friday morning because nobody else in that building audits anything.

"What?"

"**You are about to turn a range into a figure and the range is the only honest thing on that board.**" She had her hand flat on the table, which is a thing two women in this book do. "The reason it says forty to sixty and not a number is that the Registry produced a range and not the list. **The list is four hundred and eleven head counts and it is in that book and it is not added up, and it is not added up because adding it up is an audit, and an audit is a thing that is done, and an office closes an audit and the drawer holds a card about nine hundred times.**"

"That is not a reason, that is a procedure—"

"It is the only reason there is and I have given it to better men than you." She was not angry. "**If you do that sum in this room, then in about four years there is a figure in a newspaper, and a figure in a newspaper has a source, and the source will be a man who did a sum in a room at the Archive of Unlisted, and the four hundred and eleven head counts will never be added up properly by anybody for ever.** And the four hundred and eleven head counts are the whole of it. **Forty to sixty thousand is a number somebody estimated. The four hundred and eleven is a list of what happened, and it is four hundred and eleven forms, and the arithmetic is four hundred and eleven additions, and it is a fortnight's work for two of us.**"

"And you will do it."

"**I am going to ask my office to do it and I am going to be refused, because I have no standing and the Archive is a public room and the box is a Directorate record and there is no form on which an office of four people may require a box out of a Registry, and I shall apply, and the fee is three shillings, and the fee is not the obstacle.**" She picked up her bag. "And when it comes I shall enter it as an audit of the toll series and it shall be *received and noted* and it shall be published with the working on it, and it will be the first figure anybody in this city has published about this, and **it will be mine, and I will not be thanked for it, and if you use it, use the audit and not the board.**"

The sum was not performed. Nobody performed it, then or afterwards, and there is a line in a book in a shop that says so.

---

**Saturday the seventeenth and Sunday the eighteenth, and the queue, and this is what the scale is when it is not a number on a board.**

Twenty-six people on the Saturday. Four on the Sunday. **The counter is shut on a Sunday and was shut, and the door is not unlocked and there is no counter sitting and no clerk, and Sindri Lake opened the door at about ten in the morning because there were four people on the step and the door is his to open and it is not a policy and it is his own practice and he will not write it down.**

They read. That is all. They read for between four minutes and two hours and nobody asked them a question and nobody was asked a question on their behalf.

A woman of sixty-one who has lived at the bottom of a flood since 1901 stood at the table for about forty minutes and then said, to nobody, *that's a grandmother of mine*, and Lake said *yes*, and she said *there is no more*, and he said *no*.

A man of about thirty read all of it and then asked one question, which was the only question asked on the Saturday, and he asked it of Lake, and it was: **"Whose signature is on the countersignatures? Not the name. Whose office."** And Lake said: the office of a holder of the first class of the Clockwork Quarter hall, in accordance with the standing orders of the hall, in every one of the four hundred and eleven, without exception. And a certificate of condition of that same hall, in that same form, under those same standing orders, with that same five-second tolerance, dated the third of the seventh of this year, is in a works' file four miles from here **with an hour and a half in the inspector's column of it and *within tolerance* on the face of it.**

**And the man of thirty said *oh* and read the rest of it and came back on the Monday, and he is the man in the good coat and he is nobody and he is not a journalist, and he will not be back.**

---

**And on Monday the nineteenth of the ninth month, at about half past three in the afternoon, in the reading room, a girl of thirteen was brought in to be listened to, and she was not asked a question.**

Sindri Lake sent for her on the Friday morning and he sent for her before Orrin Vale thought of it, and the reason is a sentence that is in a book in his own hand, written in the sixth month, and it is a keeper's day book and it is not a public record and he has never shown it to anybody and he showed that page to exactly one person in nineteen years and that person is the girl.

> *Twenty-first of the sixth. A girl of thirteen, brought by an aunt. Asked three questions and no more, and I asked them and not anybody else.*
> *1. Her father brought a tally home every night from the works and put it on the dresser, and his wife said put it away, and he said it's the only thing I've got, and he put it away in a drawer under a plate cloth.*
> *2. She was nine when it happened and asked twice and was told it was a form.*
> *3. Her answer, and she gave it before I asked the third question: **it had a letter on it and then a slash, and I know because I used to draw it.***
> *That was enough. I have written it down and I have done nothing with it since and I did nothing with it for three months because there was nothing I could do with it and I have not slept particularly well and that is not a reason.*

He has a card index of forty-one other dates in a drawer under the counter and he will not show it, and the reason he gave for that, when it was asked of him in the fourth month, is *in about four years somebody would be able to tell from a card index which applications to sit on*, and the reason is correct and it is the same reason as the countersignature with no name, and he is one man and he has been doing it since 1919.

**On the Monday he handed her a card and a pencil and he asked her nothing.**

There were eleven people in the room and most of them had been in it since the Friday. There was no agenda. Nobody had a page. **The card was a printed index card, the same size as the ones in his own drawer, and the pencil was a stub with a piece of rubber on it, and he put them on the table in front of her and stepped back about two feet, which is the distance at which a keeper stands when he has handed a person something.**

She is thirteen. She is Ada Vance. Her father was Perry Vance, of Weir Lane, who died on the night of the seventeenth of the first month at forty-one, and the drawer under the plate cloth is gone because they moved in the third month, and the card is in a box of bills in her aunt's house, and there is a number on the box.

She did not read the minute on the board. She did not read the first page. **She read the left-hand margin**, because she had been reading margins since she was nine, and she went down the four hundred and eleven lines with a stub of pencil held above the page and not touching it, and the room watched, and nobody said anything, and it took her about nine minutes.

And on the three hundred and ninth line, in the margin, there is a diagonal stroke through the number, and in the second column, at the top of the line, there is a letter.

**The register is in accession order and not in date order, because that is what a register is, and nobody has ever asked to have it otherwise, and nobody ever will: a person cannot look up a year in that book and find what happened in it, and the only way through it is to read it.**

"**G,**" she said. "**It was a G. And it was on the left and the slash was on the right, and he used to do the letters in the evening at the table when he came in, and I could do a G before I could do an A.**" She looked up. "**He never said it was a thing you could go and look at.**"

And that was the only thing she said in that room, and it was about a letter and a slash, and it was not about the Cathedral or the model or a series or a district, and she has not asked a question in four years and she was not asked one.

**And nobody in that room comforted her, and nobody tried, and the reason is that the room is not wrong.**

Lake did not say he was sorry. He said, in his own voice, from about two feet away, the only sentence that is his office and no more:

"**A person may be present at the reading of an index and may speak and may not be represented, there being no party to a record. You are a person. That line in that book is the whole of what I have, and I have been its keeper for nineteen years, and I have not altered one head count, and I could not tell you one name on it, and I have not been asked for one by the Registry and I would not give it to them if I were.**"

Orrin Vale started to say something on the Wednesday and did not say it, and started again on the Thursday and did not say it, and has written in a book on a hill that he is the only person in this city who could have said the one useful thing, and that the one useful thing was that he had put eighty-two shillings of his own money into a form in the same series on the first of the fifth of this year and has told nobody, and that a girl of thirteen read the margin of a public register on a Monday afternoon and found the right one of four hundred and eleven out of a mark she had been copying onto the back of her hand with a biro on a Sunday when she was nine.

**And the batch is lawful. It was priced at two shillings the head for a year, it carries a series, a date, a head count of forty-one, and a countersignature, and the countersignature is a man in a hall, and the hall cannot be asked to count anything, and the removal was correct, and it is not a rounding error, and it is not a scandal, and every one of those five sentences is true, and none of the six people in that room is wrong, and a girl of thirteen came out of it at about ten past four and went down Cathedral Rise on her own.**

> *Fifteenth to the nineteenth of the ninth.*
> *Forty to sixty thousand. More than half in the Weirs. Dated batches. Eighteen years. A series, a head count, a price and a countersignature on every one, and the prices run from one and four to two shillings the head and there is not one of them that is not a price.*
> *Four hundred and eleven forms. Every one lawful. Not one concealed that was not concealed by a rule that still stands. The index carries a countersignature and no name, by one man's unwritten practice since 1919, and that is why nothing in it can be traced and nothing in it can be denied, and the two are the same fact, and it is his, and he is sixty-one, and he has not been thanked for it and is not going to be.*
> *Nobody did a sum. I started one in my head on the Friday and I have written here that I did not do it and I did not do it and the reason I did not do it is the reason a woman of thirty-three gave in a room and it is a better reason than the one I have been using since the nineteenth of the eighth and I have been wrong about for a week.*
> *And a girl of thirteen read the margin of a public register for nine minutes and found a G and a slash, and nobody in that room could say one word to her, and the room is not wrong, and that is the first time this year I have wanted to be somewhere else and been somewhere else.*
