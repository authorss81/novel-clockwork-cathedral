## Orrin Vale, 32 — municipal clockmaker, line horologist

- **Wants, in the order he would say them if asked:** to find out what his father did in the storm eighteen years ago and whether the street photograph ever becomes a name; to keep a district's night hours on a page that an office is obliged to receive; and, since Chapter 27, to understand why the correct thing and the useful thing are two different documents. He no longer wants to prove a district can produce its own hours — the district proved that in the first month and it cost them in the third.
- **Has, at the end of batch 0003:** a **class-three licence renewed on Sunday the 19th of the third month**, on the expiry date itself, in a two-hour window, endorsed by Hask in four seconds, **valid for a year from the date of lodgement**, and **not suspended**; a W-4 lodged with forty lines out of two thousand pages, one of which has put eleven years of the Weirs' night work into the hall's own form of his own motion; the copybook's contents; the priced deferral; the copper wedge; eleven copies in nine buildings; a **fair copy of the Weir Two line in the hall, perfect, signed, and missing the only evidence there was**; a district that will not be a single thing; a grey ledger; and a footnote on page nine of a printer's return that names him in one clause about as long as a sentence.
- **His leash, paid and still slack.** It never had to be pulled. A two-hour Sunday window on a notice that is not on his card, a form with three columns and no fourth, a printed card the hall sends out four times a year, a gift of a second sheet nobody asks for, and a conversation that cost **nineteen days** of margin and told him nothing. **He has understood, and it is written in the book, that the whole of the slack the notice removed cost him nothing because the constraint was never the days: the constraint was the book.** He is not angry about it. He is a man who paid a fee rather than a price, which is the difference between a building charging you to keep what is yours and a building charging you to be allowed to do it, and he says so on the last page of Chapter 27.
- **Power:** Stage 2, Aftershock, **practised with a price he can quote before he pays.** One use in this batch, in the Question Hall, with a true answer about a governor that will hold and is the only part of the business nobody will ask about, costing a hundred and forty seconds entered as a *reading cost* with the reason written under it. **He knows the price now and the knowledge is worth nothing, because the problem is not a machine.** No Choice Trace, no Loom access, no new filed Reading, no certification of a toll, no licence offence, no suspension. **A private sense is still not a Reading, and Chapter 25 is the chapter that proves it, because his answer was right and there was no form under it.**
- **The ninth beat is not a stage and not part of this line.** Two hearings, in Chapters 1 and 3, and **in Chapter 15, on a tram at ten past five, he notices he was listening for it in the middle of the Quarter's ordinary perfect hour and it does not come.** It is a sound with a count in it that leans on a place he cannot name or locate: no picture, no word, no cost, no prophecy. He must never treat it as a free Aftershock. It is unexplained at the end of Chapter 20 and stays unexplained through Chapter 50.
- **Cost of the readings, in the order the page fixes them, and it escalates.** First, Chapter 5: the minute, with clean edges, and two incompatible accounts. Second, Chapter 9: two days of fine motor control in the right hand and the permanent ring. **Third, Chapter 19: thirty-eight minutes, and the permanent loss of the ability to judge a movement by ear** — he can still hear a watch at thirty feet in a still room and has tested it twice since the Tuesday; what he cannot any longer do is tell by the sound alone whether a movement is running true, which is the whole of a clockmaker's trade, and nothing had been strained, so it is not coming back — because he wanted to act and did not. **This is a precision loss and deliberately not a range loss, recorded so that Volume 08's long-range injury and Volume 14's clean single-machine hearing are both still available.**
- **His three rules, all on the page and all at the top of the grey ledger, and all three now tested.** First, Chapter 10: *nothing of mine is used again without its blind margin and its toll named in public, in a room, to somebody who can object.* Kept in Chapter 19; **did nothing.** Second, Chapter 17: *a toll is what happens when you ask something you cannot pay for. Ask one thing, about a named load, and know before you ask what it will cost.* **Working: in Chapter 25 he quotes the price before he pays it.** Third, Chapter 20: *no result of mine goes out of this room without a person named who can refuse it* — **the only one that has ever done anything, and it has twice done the opposite of what he wanted.** In Chapter 25 it stopped him with a true answer and no gain. In Chapter 27 he found its hole, on the page: a form is not a room, and a fault observed on a civic machine is a job, so filling in the W-4 put eleven years of the district's night work into an institution with nobody able to refuse it. In Chapter 30 he offered it to the district for nothing and was told the district had outgrown it, because nine people who can refuse is a room and a room has no address.
- **Flaw, in action rather than in summary:** he said *keep it* about the one book he could not replace; he did not tell his sister about the lamp; he let Hask's question about work off the board pass with two words unsaid; in Chapter 16 he decided not to put a document in front of the person it was about and gave himself a good reason on a stair; and in Chapter 24 he sat in a warm room and did not tell a master that he has been at the governor again, because he could not construct the sentence in which a man refuses a master and then adds *by the way*.
- **What the batch costs him that is not a number:** he handed eleven years of the Weirs' night work to a guild in the guild's own form with nobody able to refuse it, and he knows exactly what he did and why he did it, and the reason was that the alternative was a book in a shop with a lease. He fills a form of forty lines out of two thousand pages and the selection is a judgement nobody in the building will ever audit. And he is named in a footnote and is glad, and works out that this is the only place in the city where he has ever been useful, and that is not modesty.

### Batch 0004 (Chapters 31–40) — Orrin at the end of the batch

- **His competence, on the page, doing rather than explaining.** He trues a bent minute hand in ninety seconds with callipers and a straight edge and works out that **a reading taken off a bent hand is a reading of the bend**; he times four machines on one hourline against a bench watch set on the Quarter's public clock, gets 100/300/500/700 seconds, notices the common difference and then **explicitly refuses to build on it** because four machines is a coincidence; he finds a signalman's bound day book nobody had asked to see, and the step size, and the onset date, in about a day. He writes down that he was nearly wrong three times: a carriage clock's variation, a hand correction he declined, and a reading he had taken with an instrument he could not trust.
- **What he now knows, and it is the volume's new vocabulary.** Slippage is a shape and the shape is a line. A line surveyed for two cycles a day, given four, divides by eight, takes its beat off the odd teeth, and steps fifty seconds a night at the fourth draw; **the four machines he can get to are each a whole multiple of a hundred seconds out, 1:40, 5:00, 8:20 and 11:40, and the common difference of 200 seconds is on the page as something he refuses to build on.** **He is the only man in Morrowfen who can say that, and the batch's last image is that by Chapter 40 he no longer needs to be: two clocks four hundred yards apart can be compared by eye from a window, the lit one six minutes and forty seconds behind the dark one.** The distribution of sight arrives far too early and it is not a stage, and must not be written as one.
- **Power.** One Aftershock in the batch, in Chapter 31, at a price he quoted before he paid: **a hundred and twenty seconds, entered in the shop book as a reading cost with the reason written under it**, which is the Chapter 17 practice and is not a fourth keeper's toll. **The count of keeper's tolls is still three** (Chapters 5, 9, 19), the judgement and not the hearing is still gone, the right hand still closes mostly, and the little finger is still late. He hears a watch at thirty feet in a still room. **Still a precision loss and not a range loss: Volume 08's long-range injury and Volume 14's clean single-machine hearing both remain available.**
- **What the licence has done to him in this batch, which is new and is not the leash.** His own hall has **filed a question against him, question 6/4, lodged the 14th of the fourth month, five days after his renewal, answerable in writing by the fifth of the fifth month**, and he learns of it as a line on a holder index card and **is not told, is not read the question, and cannot see the file.** It does not suspend anything, alters no term, and requires no interview, and there is no interview to be had. He writes four answers and sends the one that costs the district, and the sentence that will survive him is eleven words: *that no person in the district was asked.* **He broke his own third rule knowingly, in about an hour on a bus on Cathedral Rise, on the morning he was told the price, and wrote it down that night in the grey book in one line, and said he would do it again.** He commits no licence offence, nothing is suspended, and nothing is coming.
- **The decision nobody knows about, and the flaw it is made of.** On Monday the first of the fifth month he is going to put **£4 2s of his own money** into form GH-9, the same series, the same box, the same price — eighty-two shillings, forty-one heads at two shillings the head for a year — to put the crew's standing back for a year. It will not bring back eleven years or one lost afternoon, and the hall's four-line card will come back with it, and the district's own hours will be a form in a drawer in a series nobody reads. **He is going because he has run out of every other instrument, which is the flaw and not the method, and he is going alone, and he is not going to tell the district, because a district asked to fund a reinstatement is a district asked to be a party, and he has spent four months watching what that does.** He has said in the book that he is not going to pretend about it. **Chapters 41-50 must not let this be cheap: it is the wrong thing done for the right reason by a competent man, and the volume has to be able to say so.**
- **What he cost the district in this batch, besides the answer.** He declined to wind four clocks on a dark fourth floor, which meant a woman of sixty-six carries a lamp up a stair, and the only clocks in that block that agree with each other have stopped agreeing. He wrote a four-line report on a stair light that **names nobody who fitted it**, on purpose, and said so on the face of the folder. He let Ambrose refuse him correctly. He did not say the Chapter 25 governor thing in a room because nobody in the room could refuse it, which is the rule working.
- **His three rules, all still at the top of the grey ledger, and all three now exhausted.** Rule one, name the margin and the toll in a room where somebody can object, was worth nothing in Chapter 19 and is not tried again. Rule two, price the question before you ask it, **worked in Chapter 31** — he knew the price, paid it, and got a true answer he could not spend, and he says that in one line without comment. Rule three, no result of mine goes out without a person named who can refuse it, **stopped him in Chapter 25, found a second hole in Chapter 27, was declined by the district in Chapter 30, and he broke it himself in Chapter 40.** It is the only piece of his method that has ever done anything, and it has now stopped him twice, been refused once, and been broken once.
- **His growth, and only as a method offered to a person.** He stops trying to be the man who can see it. In Chapter 39 he is glad of a line item on a published schedule — a survey, a year, a two-year-old estimate — and cannot say why in any language a committee would accept. In Chapter 40 he watches a method he taught a room get used on an office, and in Chapter 36 he watches a form he had no part in get used better than he could have used it. **Volume 06's distributed horizon is not here and must not be named or staged. It arrives as a person learning to see, not as a man acquiring an instrument.**

## Mara Venn, 33 — investigator, Office of Public Reckoning

- **Wants:** the same, and now with a method she has admitted out loud to a corridor and is ashamed of — three years of a committee being bored by a bridge, and then a clerk asking what the form number on it is — **and has now said out loud in a room of a hundred and thirty people at the Cathedral, which is a bigger thing than she intended.** She chose the one ground in that room where a form is the thing being read, and she lost, and she said in advance that she would not be able to use it twice: *you cannot get a city to look at a man; you can get it to read a form, once, and then you have used the form up.*
- **Has:** a copybook's contents, a price schedule, a form number, a four-shilling debt to a woman who is never thanked, a requisition for the guild labour book in a tray for about fourteen months, **eleven identical four-line cards in a drawer**, a seven-day note with *no further correspondence on this matter* in the corner in small type, and one thing nobody has said out loud in a room yet: **a name on the GH-9 countersignature line**, whose first entry would be a woman of twenty-six in the storm month eighteen years ago.
- **Does not have:** any authority over any other office, and any safe way to read a machine. Her mother's name is still not spoken and must not be until a volume the outline names. She said *Mara* to Rook once without thinking and did not take it back.
- **State with Orrin:** professionally allied and privately uninformative, and in Chapter 16 they agree about a bridge bracket and disagree about a whole case and do not resolve it in a corridor. She gave him, in Chapter 19, a page with his own decision on it, witnessed, because she knew he would need it in four months when he decided a rule was a thing that was true in a pump house.
- **Her stake in the argument, unchanged and now sharper:** a name-and-duration column cannot be drawn beside a figure nobody has counted, and her office is ignored three times in a fortnight by people who are not being cruel.

### Batch 0004 — Mara Venn

- **Her office is ignored, at length, and the ignoring turns out to be the method.** A woman in a Weirs laundry telephones her office, is told a printed page cannot be received, asks for the office's own form, discovers that **it carries no fee**, sends it, and asks for one thing: *the document exists, I want the office to write down that it exists, I do not want you to do anything about it, and I want the date on it.* The note comes back with a fifth line that is not on the form. **It is the first time in nine years that anybody has rung the Office of Public Reckoning to thank her for a note, and she says she is not going to say how it feels because she has a file open and the caller is on a telephone.** The drawer holds twenty-two four-line cards, against eleven on the seventeenth of the third month. **Her written right to be ignored is a machine, and somebody has finally found the cog, and it is her own.**
- **She wins, once, and it costs the city £411 and gives nobody an hour.** In a committee room on the Friday she goes at a published maintenance schedule on its own terms: a maintenance schedule states how often a thing will be inspected, and this one never does, so it is a list of repairs wearing a maintenance heading. She refuses to read her section aloud *so that it sounds like a form*, says she is not attacking the sum — the first time in nine years she has said that of anything — and gets item ten: an inspection interval, first inspection in programme year one, results published in the district's own sheet. **She is explicit that this is not the thing she did in the Question Hall; there she got a room to read a required line, which she said in front of a hundred and thirty people she could never do twice, and here she is defeating a document where the document is the agenda.** The reader's anger is reasonable and does nothing, and the second time it is quieter.
- **She finds out about the seal from a board and tells nobody.** She does not telephone the works, the ward, the guild or her own office. She goes and reads the public index of transfers, and finds a pencil note in the margin in **Sindri Lake's** hand, and the index entry for batch 21/4 — forty-one entries, a form number, a price, four routing copies and a woman's name — is now the only surviving document saying a district was taken out of the arithmetic. **She wrote a note she did not send.** Her office cannot require a sealed file and has no function in one, and a number in the Assembly's minutes whose source is in a box can now be called a fabrication by anybody with a grudge and a letterhead.
- **She has still not told Orrin about her mother and must not.** The one slow-burn line in this batch is that she telephones him only when she wants something from the Office and not when she has something for him, and he telephones her not at all, and neither of them has done anything about that.

## Leda Vale, 27 — emergency nurse, Green Span

- **From method to consequence, and she is the one who says it.** In Chapter 15 she prices his rule. In Chapter 19 she tells him in a corridor that the rule failed afterwards, in a file, the next day. **In Chapter 26 she is the one who reads the blind margin aloud in a room that can write it down** — the Water Users' Committee of the pumping district, Monday the thirteenth, a member of the public, granted without objection, entered in the minute — because she works in a building where that sentence is the whole of her trade, and she tells the room what it means: *we are the cost, and the cost is not wrong, and the cost is not a lie, it is simply not in.* The committee does not re-open the schedule.
- **And then, in the rain, the hardest thing in the batch:** her ward is on the wrong side of the Chapter 25 bill for a decision taken in a room she was not in, the water is worth more than the bridge was and she has been arguing against that for four hours and has nothing, and **there is no answer.** She has looked. She is the one who has to say so, and she says it to a man with a bar in the street, and she tells him not to write it down as a lesson, and she says that what she wants is to have been in the street when it happened, which is the whole of what she has ever wanted from him. **The beat is in Weir Lane and not in a hospital corridor, which is a deliberate relocation from the batch card and is recorded as a card-versus-prose divergence in `state/open-threads.md`: a corridor is where institutions put their women, and this one is standing in the weather outside a building that has already decided.**
- **What she has put on the page:** the four districts on the back of a ward roster, unsigned and undated, on the duty board in the nurses' room where families read. She is the one who gets asked about it and she has a shift pattern that lets her say *I am not on until Friday*.
- **The lamp, named at last:** she was nine, in the chapel school on Weir Lane, about sixty people, the bells stopped, she counted, her aunt came at four with a basket, their mother three years dead. **She does not know what their father did and has never once pretended.** She has told Orrin that his not-telling is not a kindness, that she would have come, and that she cannot hold a lamp for something she does not know is happening. This is the reason his third rule exists.
- **Not a reward, and she leaves no hospital.** Her ward still has a morning in which two medicines arrive four minutes apart, and a girl of nineteen on nights is doing the ward clock's work by hand and being paid for it.

- **The hospital is a constituency now, not a bystander.** The Green Span branch is on the same slipping line, so the ward's clocks are inside the network whose machines are out by eleven minutes and forty seconds, and she says the number out loud in a room of fourteen people at a quarter to eight on the Tuesday the eighteenth, **before a penny of the money in the maintenance programme is spent**, and names it as a statement of what the money is buying, made by the people it is being bought with, on the day the sheet is published and not on the day somebody comes up in two years with a bill. ### Batch 0004 — Sel Kade

- **The diversion is documented and the document is correct.** A district works accountancy note: from the ward's reserve to the Carrow Street main, **nine thousand gallons a day, three hundred and twenty-four thousand gallons in thirty-six days from the 2nd of the third month**, to seventy-one households and a galvanising and wire works, on the strength of the works' certificate of the 29th of the second month. **Reviewed at the next monthly return and thereafter at the discretion of the district works. Restored within one month of any variation of the rotation** — and the rotation was varied on the 8th of the third month and again on the 30th, so the clause is triggered and will not be served. The ward's reserve alarm has been inhibited since the second of the third month and no report has been made. **It is the first time anybody has written it down, including her.**
- **She defended it as a defence of jobs and she is right about the jobs, and the minute is unusable to her.** Fifty minutes, nine men, her own terms. Her first eleven minutes are about ninety-one jobs of which sixty-one are resident; her second part reframes the whole thing: **she did not divert the reserve to a works, she diverted it to a flooding street, and the works is the reason the street flooded, because the street floods because of a gate, and the gate is the ward's business, and I am this ward.** She offers the gate's own log on a written request naming the week, and the committee cannot ask, because it has no function in respect of the district's flood performance. **The minute carries the employment finding and not the flood finding, and the review clause is not in the minute at all, because the minute is about what the committee heard.** She asked for one correction and got it in forty seconds and thanked the clerk and not the room.
- **She is not recruited, not converted, not softened, and not the villain, and the chapter says so four times.** She refuses to have the beneficiary's name struck out. She refuses to be examined in an office. She states plainly that she was not a fool and was not a child and did not need a man to explain the arithmetic, and asks that nobody write otherwise. **The tenant review of the March diversion is still owed by about Chapter 46 and is a different thing from the hearing of Chapter 34 and from the countersignature of Chapter 40.**
- **Her countersignature has now been put in front of her, after eighty-six days, and the promise is paid.** She was twenty-six and a deputy warden, and the batch's mechanism is the one the whole book turns on: **a district cannot sign, a form cannot be given to a place, so the hall got a person**, and no rule says the person had to be asked by the district because there is no body in the district that could ask. She remembers the room in the storm month, remembers being told the class meant the work would be done and not paid, and remembers the one good question she asked once and did not ask twice: *if the work is done and not paid, who is it done by?* — and the true answer she was given, that a district is a list of properties and not a list of persons, and that she was signing the list. **She was never told the price. Two shillings a head is what the district paid and she had no function in the price.**
- **And she has told him the price of putting the crew back.** Forty-one heads at two shillings a year is eighty-two shillings, and the form would be the same series, the same box, the same price. **What makes it impossible is not the money: a ward's rates are set on its return, so putting forty-one heads back into a district's standing makes the ward's hours real, and the ward's rates go up, and the rise falls on the same people the crew stands on.** She said that to him before he said it to himself, and she is right, and it is the reason he is going alone, and she is not on his side and was not asked to be.
- **She kept the one date anybody has ever given her, and it cost the same people the diversion helped.** Wednesday the 19th to Saturday the 22nd, three hundred and twenty-four thousand gallons back into the ward's reserve out of the ordinary rotation at about eighty thousand a day, **bought with an hour a night off the canal front's own draw**, which Bevin Sarr put in the ward's night book, the first line in it about anything a warden has done. **She leaves the reserve alarm inhibited and the report unmade.** The galvanising and wire works are told there will be water on Friday and there is, and by the Saturday the Carrow Street end is under water to the second step of about forty houses again, at the same depth, **and the gauge sheet that proves it is the only document about a flood anybody in this city has ever produced, and she has three copies and one of them is in an office on the canal road and nobody asked her for it.** **And none of it comes off anything**: the certified return is corrected whether the water went back or not, the ward's rates rise in the spring, nobody in the Weirs says one word to her, and the one person who has noticed is Hanna Stroud, a landlord with nine blocks. **The review is on the thirtieth, and after it the diversion is at the discretion of the district works, and discretion has no date on it.** **She is still not recruited, still not softened, and still not on anybody's side, and the tenant review is still owed by about Chapter 46 and is a different thing from all three of these.**

## Gerren Hask, 55 — master of the Clockwork Quarter guild hall

- **His crime is legible to the reader and invisible to every character, and batch 0002 did not change that by a word.** He asked for the work book, explained that the hall cannot take it and would resign before signing that, explained the other half more plainly, offered an optional interview that cannot be refused without giving a reason, and said the words *it is not a threat* in effect without ever using them.
- **What is new is the mechanism, not the crime:** from the renewal card onward, the only record of what a maintainer has found in any office in the city is one the hall writes, on form W-4, with three columns and none for *I am not sure*. The licence **expired on Sunday the 19th of the third month and was renewed in Chapter 27 without incident**, in a two-hour window he never mentions, endorsed in four seconds, with no letter, no threat and no inquiry. **The interview on Tuesday the 28th of the second month was forty minutes and cost nineteen days of margin and produced no information at all.** He gave Orrin the second sheet nobody asks for, told him the counter is open on Sundays, told him **his standing session is at ten on the twenty-eighth every year**, and told him the escapement on his own bench wants oiling — kindly, in front of the only witness, to a man who can no longer hear it. He committed no impropriety: the notice that compressed the renewal is set by a forms printer, the hall's four-line card has been in a drawer since before Orrin was apprenticed, and a copyist made the Weir Two enclosure because she did not know what a station was. Hask is owed losing the right to certify silent shifts and publishing the labour ledger with a liability annex naming the night of the Long Nine as the night the hours stopped. **He is not destroyed; he becomes a repairer under public oversight.**
- **Never asked about the Long Nine on the page.** Della Marsh names the liability annex in Chapter 14 and refuses the figure, which is not Hask and does not touch him.

### Batch 0004 — Gerren Hask

- **He appears once, in his official capacity, warm, for forty minutes over a cup of tea, and he is the good man in the chapter and it costs him nothing.** He offers **a boy from the licensing floor, seventeen, quick and honest and with no ear at all**, and says that if Orrin ever wants one he will send him down on a Tuesday and will not ask him why, and if not he will not mention it again; Orrin declines and does not think about it, and Hask says *no, you will not, and I did not expect you to.* He refuses to open a second work book early, because nobody has had to explain the practice to him in thirty-one years. ## Secondary people established on the page in batch 0002

- **Tobias Rill, 44,** Continuity Office inspector of hourlines. **In Chapter 38 he applies a sealed classification to the Weir Two pump records, reference CS/114, appeal none, and he is right, and the batch makes him say so.** The ground is a section of the regional scheduling act thirty-one years old, on the security of a continuity work, and a gate's signal table is a security matter in the way a vault combination is. He states both halves of his choice — under-classify and you publish a signal table and put a crew's hours on a public file; over-classify and a district loses a record it compiled itself, for ever, with no appeal — and says he would make the same pick in a week and has made it before. **On his own initiative he adds that the classification touches nothing under the Cathedral, that he has no authority over it, and that he will put that in writing with a reference and a date if anybody says otherwise, because it is the only part of the answer he knows for certain.** He also signed the trestle deferral two years ago, and the trestle bracket is now item seven of a new maintenance schedule at £2 14s 6d, and nobody accuses him of it. On the page, in person, and the most reasonable man in the book.
