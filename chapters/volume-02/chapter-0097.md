# Chapter 97 — What a District Keeps

On the twenty-seventh to the thirtieth of the twelfth month a district keeps four things it has never kept before: a printed method with a maintenance schedule on it, six names, a rate that has gone up and a landlord who asked for it in the name of nine blocks, and an item that cannot be a line item for a reason that is not money; and it does not keep a company, and the company is not formed and nobody has been asked to form it and nobody has been asked to stop refusing; and a man of fifty-five, who is a repairer under public oversight and the only living keeper of how a night shift in this district used to be certified, has a line on the maintenance schedule with nothing in it, and the nothing is there on purpose, and the reason is a strip of form pinned inside the front cover of a book he published for a shilling and twopence, and nobody has asked him anything and nobody is going to.

---

**The reading of the twenty-fourth, at half past seven in the morning, seventeen minutes after seven, with the screw of a cistern open under a row of twenty-two houses and twelve clocks on the row going free.**

The method was printed on Monday the twenty-sixth at about eleven in the morning on two sheets, one for the wall and one for the ledge, and it is the first document this district has ever printed about itself, and it is four paragraphs and a table and it does not grow.

> **THREE CLOCKS, ONE QUESTION, FOUR MINUTES, AND WHAT IT IS NOT.**
> **1.** Three clocks: the pumping ward, canal road, outer room; the Weir Two pump house, station clock; the Quarter hill, south public clock. Two are hung in public places and one is in a room with a glass case in it, and anybody may stand at any of the three and read any of them, and nobody needs to know anybody to do it.
> **2.** One question, in the same words, written down before the day: *at the minute stated, read each clock named below and write down, in figures, what it says, against the district reference of the Cathedral public clock; and state the time of reading, and state who read it.* The words are the words. If a copy of this question in any hand in this district says *write* where this one says *write down*, **that is a different question and whoever is holding the two copies has to be told, and the only instrument for it is two people with the two copies in their hands.**
> **3.** The minute is half past seven in the morning, on a Saturday, and it is half past seven because I. P. chose it in the ninth month and did not tell anybody why until the twentieth of the twelfth, and the reason was on a wall at a works' gate and it is that **a rehearsal is run at the hour it is going to be used at, and not at an hour that is convenient, and a rehearsal which requires anybody to get up early is a rehearsal which will not be run, and a rehearsal which requires anybody to stand about watching will not be run twice.**
> **4.** **What it is not.** It is not a reading. It certifies nothing, it has no seal, it has no form, it is not on any rail, nothing is filed, nothing is admissible, and there is no form in this city on which any part of it could be made to be any of those things. The station on the rail takes a person of the affected district; there is none, and there has not been one for nineteen years. **Nothing about this method goes onto that rail by any other road.**

And the maintenance schedule, which is the second thing on the sheet and is the whole of what the district is keeping.

> | | Maintained | How often | Paid |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | 1 | I. Pell, operator | every Saturday, and the question once a month in the same words | **no** |
> | 2 | S. Kaye, operator | as above | **no** |
> | 3 | O. Vale, unlicensed, may not certify anything | as above, and he reads the third clock | **no** |
> | 4 | B. Sarr, ward clerk, canal road | the wall, the night book, the case, the key on a string | her work |
> | 5 | H. Stroud, nine blocks, about two hundred and eleven households | the rate, and the paper | by nobody |
> | 6 | W. Pruitt, tenant council | the room, the minutes, and the putting of them where anybody may read them | her work |
> | 7 | — | — | — |

**Three of the six are the operators, and the other three came from somewhere else, and the reason they are not operators is the whole of the argument a year of this has been making.**

"A method is three clocks and a question and two other people's hands. That is a rehearsal and that is all a rehearsal is, and you can run it in a shed with a gate on it. **What you cannot do in a shed is find out whether the result is going to be refused, and that is the part that has to be maintained and it is not done by an operator.**" He had the four of them in a room over a chandler's shop on the Monday and it took about ninety minutes and nobody enjoyed it.

"**Bevin Sarr keeps the wall and the night book and the case, and the reason she is on this sheet and I am not the only name on it is that the case has a key on a string on it and the key belongs to a room and the room is hers in the way that a ward clerk's room is hers, and if the wall is wrong on a Monday morning then six figures are wrong on a Monday morning and four of the people who read that wall work in that building and two of them are named on it.**"

"**Hanna Stroud keeps the rate,**" said Wenna Pruitt, "and I want it minuted, by which I mean I want it written on the wall, that she is on this sheet because a rate has gone up in this district in the twelfth month of this year for the first time since the storm month, and it went up because she walked into a public room in October in a good hat and asked for it against herself, in the name of nine blocks, and **she is the only person in this district who has ever spent her own money to make herself cost more, and there is no instrument anywhere that describes what that is and it is on a maintenance schedule in a room over a shop.**"

"And Wenna keeps the minutes and the putting of them where anybody may read them," said Hanna Stroud, "and I have not been thanked for October and I am not going to be thanked for December, **and I am on that sheet and I would like it understood that I am on it because a rate is a thing a committee can vote on and there is no committee in this district that can vote on it and a landlord who is on a maintenance schedule is a landlord who has put her own hand on the thing that raised her own bill.**"

"That is the fourth reason," said Teo Arden.

"It is the same reason as the other three and you have known that since the sixteenth of the November and you have been waiting for about five weeks for somebody to say it in that order."

**Nobody is thanked in the minutes and no person is thanked in the sheet and the sheet is the document and not the room.**

---

**And the seventh line is empty, and the reason it is empty is four months old and is a strip of form pinned inside the front cover of part one of a book.**

> | 7 | | | |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |

"There is a line on that schedule for the person who used to certify a night shift in this district, and there has not been a class in this city in which a shift of this district's could be certified since the first of July, and the class was created by a hall in 1931 and abolished by a standing order of that hall on the first of July, and the form is not printed and is not held, and the hall has said *not held, not printed* in four words to a man who asked on the eighth of the ninth, and **that man has been the only living keeper of how a night shift in this district was certified, for nineteen years, and he is a repairer under public oversight and his class is under review and he certifies no shift alone.**"

"Then put him on it."

"**No.**" He said it flatly and he did not enjoy saying it and neither did anybody else. "Because a man of fifty-five published that book in full at the counter of his own hall for one shilling and twopence and pinned a strip of his own form inside the front cover in pencil, and the strip says *I have read all of it. Do not ask me about it. I will not say*, and **nobody in this district is entitled to ask him anything, and I have not asked him anything, and there is no line in this schedule that is going to make me entitled.**"

"So the line is empty."

"**The line is empty and the line is on the schedule and the schedule is on the wall, and anybody who comes into that room from now on is going to see a row with nothing in it and there is nobody in this city who could tell them what it is for except the six of us, and two of us did not read the strip until the twentieth of the tenth.**" He put his hand flat on the table, which means nothing and has always meant nothing except that a hand is going on a surface. "And I am not going to fill it in and I am not going to take it out, and if anybody in this district ever asks me what an empty line on a schedule is, I am going to say that in this city there is a column in a glass case with a name in it and nothing beside the name, and there is a dash in a book in a store in Green Span, and there is a line on a wall in a pumping ward, and **they are all the same sentence and the sentence was written by three offices that have never met and none of them knows the other two exist.**"

Nobody asked him anything else about it. It is the fifth time in a year that a man has been found by reading and never asked, and the count of people in this district who have been found by reading and never asked is now five, and it is a number, and it is a number about a practice and not about a man.

---

**The cost, per week, and who pays it, and the answer being a body the district already has.**

"Four hours a week. Three readers at about an hour each including the bus and the writing up, and twenty minutes of a ward clerk copying into a book, and about ten minutes on a wall. **Four hours at the hall's out-of-shop rate of four shillings and sixpence the hour is eighteen shillings.** Four sheets of foolscap and two of carbon at a penny a sheet is sixpence. **The whole of what a district's own weekly reading costs is eighteen shillings and sixpence.**"

"Who pays the eighteen shillings?"

"**Nobody can.** There is no ratepayer for a rehearsal. A rate is struck on a return and a pumping district is not in a return and has not been since the storm month, and there is no body that can be invoiced, and a body is not a fund, and a fund is not a rate, and I have written all three of those on the board at some point this year and I have never once put them in the same sentence, which is a sentence about this district and not about money."

"And the sixpence."

"**The sixpence comes out of the ward's rate, which went up on the twenty-fourth of the twelfth and is £1,044 a year on a rateable figure of £6,960, of which nothing had ever been collected, and about two hundred and eleven households in nine blocks are in it, and the landlord who has nine blocks asked for it in the name of all nine, in a good hat, in October, and she is on the maintenance schedule and she would like it understood that she put her own hand on the thing that raised her own bill.** The body that spends the sixpence is the Weirs tenant council, which is a body and not a fund, and has no officer and no clerk and a bell that has not worked since 1949 and a minute book whose most recent volume is 1948, and **the money is the ward's and the council spends it and the council cannot be sued and cannot sue and cannot be given a rate and cannot be asked a question by anybody, and the arrangement is lawful because there is nobody in this city with the power to say it is unlawful.**"

**And Teo Arden did the multiplication and then did not do it, and that is the whole of what happened to the company this year.**

"It is forty pounds a year," he said. "Four hundred heads at two shillings the head for a year is eight hundred shillings, which is forty pounds, out of a tin at a front office, in a works, on a shift, and forty pounds is not a sum, it is a signature, and a company can minute things, and the objects clause goes into a public register in a named man's name for ever and reads *to represent the pumping district of the Lower Weirs in all questions concerning the water supply of that district*, and it has not been formed, and the envelope is in the works' file under a minute that binds nobody, and **nobody has been asked to form it and nobody has been asked to stop refusing, and I have said no three times for three different reasons and the third one was that I want a thing that can be lost.**"

"And?"

"**And I am not going to multiply eighteen shillings and sixpence by anything.**" He said it without any pleasure at all. "Because the minute you multiply it, it is a rate, and a rate is a thing a committee can vote on, and the second it is a rate somebody in this district will minute it and in four years there will be a company with a fund of the same size as the ward's paper bill and an objects clause in a public register and it will be the fourth door and it will be a door I opened with a pencil. **A figure without a date is the whole of what this district is, and a figure multiplied is a figure with a date, and I have spent this year refusing to put dates on things and I am not going to start on a Tuesday because it would be a good line.**"

"He is right," said Hanna Stroud, "and I am not going to choose between him and Wenna about it, and neither is she, and this is the fifth month of that and it is a month I would like to be able to put a date on."

**Nobody thanked anybody. A man of twenty did the only arithmetic available in that room and then did not do it, in front of nine people, and did not explain it twice.**

---

**And the item that cannot be a line item, and the reason, and the reason is not money.**

It is the item that has been on a wall in chalk in the pumping ward's outer room since the middle of the June, and it has the price on it and the date on it and the asker on it and the refuser on it and the reason underneath, and it is six hundred standpipe brackets which are fittings on services and are not on Ruth Ashby's map because they are not an instrument, and the price of them is not the reason.

It is **the inhibited low-level alarm on the ward's reserve**, and there is no line item for it, and there never will be one, and the reason has nothing to do with money:

"**A line item is a thing somebody owns.** That is the whole of it and it is on a page in the middle of the sixth month in a yard with forty people in it and a man of forty-four said it and was right. A standpipe bracket is a fitting on a service and a service is on a property and a property is in a return, and the thing that would own six hundred of them is a list of properties that does not exist in any form that can be acted on, and the next adoption is in the fourth month of next year and the notice goes up in the third, and a new item goes in on adoption and not before, and it is not going in this year by anybody's decision. **All of that is about ownership and none of it is about money. A tell-tale that would tell anybody on that towpath whether one of those brackets has moved costs eleven shillings and fourpence, and there are six hundred of the brackets, and both of those figures are on the wall in chalk in the pumping ward's outer room in the same line where a ratepayer of sixty-six put them in the ninth month, and I have not added them up this month and I am not going to, because the number is the part of this that every person in that yard had in about nine minutes and the reason is the part that nobody has.**"

"Then what is the inhibited alarm."

"**The alarm is a report.**" He said it as though he had been waiting eleven weeks to say it to somebody. "**An inhibited alarm on a reserve requires a report, and no form in this city requires one, and that is the whole of why there has not been one for nine months. A line item would be a way of buying a bell. What is missing is not a bell and it is not money and it is not a man to read it. What is missing is a form on which a person may say *the reserve is under a day and the alarm is off and nobody has been told*, and there is no such form, and every form in this city is a form for doing a thing, and a person saying that nothing has been done is a person saying no, and there is no paper on which a person may say no and be heard.**"

"And the tank is at about fourteen hours."

"**The tank is at about fourteen hours and it is exactly balanced and it is not falling, and the man who has been writing it down every afternoon since the second of the ninth in a sheet of paper in his coat is going to be in that room on Saturday morning at six with the sheet in his hand and about nine people in it, and he is not going to be thanked and he does not want to be, and it is the only record of the level of that tank in existence and it is going to be a thing that happened in a room and not a thing anybody has.**"

The six hundred standpipe brackets are not a line item in this chapter or in any other, and the next adoption of the district works' schedule is in the fourth month of next year with the notice in the third, and a brass tell-tale at eleven shillings and fourpence is on a wall in a pumping ward in chalk with the asking on it and the reason underneath, and nobody has to be told about it again.

> *Twenty-seventh to the thirtieth of the twelfth.*
> *A printed method in four paragraphs and a table, on two sheets, one for the wall and one for the ledge, and it does not grow, and the fourth paragraph is what it is not.*
> *Six names on a maintenance schedule and a seventh line that is empty, and the empty line is for the only living keeper of how a night shift in this district used to be certified, and he published a book about it for a shilling and twopence and pinned a strip of form inside the front cover in pencil and nobody is entitled to ask him anything, and he was found by reading in the twentieth of the tenth and has not been asked a question since and is not going to be.*
> *Three of the six are the operators and are unpaid, and the other three are a ward clerk who holds a wall and a glass case and a key on a string, a landlord of nine blocks and about two hundred and eleven households who spent her own money in October on being made to cost more, and a tenant council's organiser who keeps a room and a minute book whose most recent volume is 1948. **And none of the three is an operator and the reason is that a method is three clocks and a question and two other people's hands, and what cannot be done in a shed is finding out whether the result is going to be refused.**
> *The whole cost is eighteen shillings and sixpence a week, of which nobody can pay the eighteen shillings, and the sixpence comes out of a rate that has gone up by £1,044 a year on a rateable figure of £6,960, of which nothing was ever collected, and the body that spends it is the Weirs tenant council, and a body is not a fund, and the arrangement is lawful because nobody in this city has the power to say it is not.*
> *A man of twenty did the multiplication of the second of those figures and then did not do it, and said why, and it is that the minute it is a rate somebody can minute it and in four years there will be a fourth door, **and a figure without a date is the whole of what this district is.**
> *And the item that cannot be a line item is an inhibited alarm on a ward's reserve, and the reason is not money and is not a bell and is that **a line item is a thing somebody owns and there is no owner, and what is missing is a form on which a person may say that nothing has been done, and there is no paper in this city on which a person may say no and be heard.**
> *Nobody was thanked. The company is not formed and nobody has been asked to form it and nobody has been asked to stop refusing.*
