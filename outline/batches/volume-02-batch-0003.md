## Batch goal

Open a volume on the cost of a decision not to tell anybody, pay the midpoint by reading, and end on a question.

1. **It pays the reversal of quantity, once, in Chapter 75, and nowhere else.** Between forty and sixty thousand residents removed from the civic model, over half in the Weirs, in four hundred and eleven dated batches, each a form with a series, a head count, a price and a countersignature. **Lawful at every step. Not a scandal. Not a rounding error. A girl of thirteen finds her father's line in the left-hand margin of a public register in nine minutes because a man at a counter wrote down in June what she said when she was nine, and nobody in the room can comfort her because the room is not wrong.**
2. **It lets a stoppage happen and then refuses to call it a strike**, because there is no employer in it, no return date, no fund, no arbitration and no two sides, and the cost of it arrives as water, and the water arrives at a hospital nobody told, and a nineteen-year-old night sister times a round by hand and writes the first duration this city has ever had a column for.
3. **It converts three open questions into objects with prices on them** — a certificate that says *within tolerance*, a schedule line that cannot exist, a company whose objects clause goes in a public register — **and it resolves one long argument by a deliberate decision and asks the volume's question out loud.**

**The batch's engine, in one sentence: the size of a thing nobody has counted cannot be argued about, so the first thing anybody does with the count is to make it a number in a room where it can be used by people who were not in it.**
