## The batch in one paragraph

The last batch proved that a copy of the ledger is not the same thing as a right to be believed, and it proved it by losing. This batch is the consequence: people who have been proved right get organised, and an organisation turns up to a hearing with a plan, and a plan that works is the most dangerous thing in this book, because it makes a district look like a bloc and lets the machine treat it as one. Underneath it runs a clock the batch must not soften: **Hask's optional interview is Tuesday the 28th of the second month and the class-three licence expires on Sunday the 19th of the third**, and the W-4 work book falls due with the renewal, and from the day the notice landed the only record of what Orrin has found in any office in the city is one the hall writes. And in the middle of the batch, in Chapter 25, the Cathedral does it again — properly, lawfully, in the correct form, at ten times the scale, omitting the same people.

## Batch goal, shape, and turns

- **Chapters 21–22, the beginning:** the Weirs turn a counter-record into something that can be produced in a room, and the cost of producing it is named before it is paid. Bevin Sarr's board, Wenna Pruitt's ledgers and Teo Arden's nine copies become a thing with a method, and the method is ordinary and exhausting and has no name in it.
- **Chapters 23–24, escalation:** the district's own arithmetic goes in front of the body it was built to embarrass, and the guild's answer is the four-line reply that is entirely true. The interview happens on Tuesday the 28th of the second month and Hask does not threaten him once.
- **Chapter 25, the volume's midpoint reversal.** A second and larger public Reading on the Weirs' own water schedule, properly filed, properly witnessed, technically correct, approved by people who have already read the blind margin once, and omitting the same people, who are by then organised, informed and furious, in the room, and still not on the rail. It must arrive cold.
- **Chapters 26–28, escalation after the reversal:** the shape of the volume changes and the batch must change with it. Orrin is no longer a man trying to prove something happened; he is a man whose proof has been published twice in the correct form. The renewal falls due and is not suspended. The W-4 falls due and the contents of the grey ledger are the contents of a hall record.
- **Chapters 29–30, aftermath and settlement-in-part:** recovery, and a decision about what a district is, made by the district, that nobody in the room wanted and everybody can live with.

## Style

Rain-lit, tactile, grounded in repair work and in rooms where paper is read out loud. Orrin's competence must be visible in the doing: he can find a gland in ten minutes with a thumbnail, name the fault in a street clock, and do a fair copy of a passage of a labour book correctly because he does it for his own shop every year. He is not a scholar of politics and can misread a room; in this batch he is in rooms he is not a member of, and the reader should feel the difference between a room he can be in and a room he can only be written about in. His interiority is procedural — he counts, he measures, he works out what a thing will do — and it should sound like a craftsman thinking. Show information through work, conversation and consequence; do not deliver lore. Let the important moments breathe. Keep paragraphs to two to six sentences, with a one-line paragraph only as a deliberate beat. Vary rhythm; do not lean on *held, steady, plain, counted, breathed, wrote, nodded*. The System is the Consequence Engine: one sparse physical notice in plain declarative language, at most one per chapter, and in this batch only Chapter 25 should need one.

**Chapter 25 is the one to get right.** The reader has been given a correct answer once and watched a man die. Everything since has been a fight over paper. If the second Reading is dramatised as a triumph, the volume has no argument; if it is dramatised as a joke, the book has no fear. It is a well-run machine doing exactly what it is built to do, in front of people who now know what it is built to do, and everybody in the room is behaving reasonably inside a rule that was designed to let this happen.
