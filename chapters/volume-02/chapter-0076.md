# Chapter 76 — Two Hundred and Fifty-Five Days

On Tuesday the twentieth of the ninth month a woman of thirty-three told a man of thirty-two the first fact of her life that she had never put in a drawer, and on the same afternoon he told her the one he had been carrying since the first of the fifth, and neither of them thanked the other, and the page that governs what they do has two hands on it and a date and no witness, and what happened to it that afternoon is that it stopped being the only thing in the book either of them was holding.

---

**The Office of Public Reckoning is one window and four staff and a written right to be ignored, and it is in a building with a court outside the window, and three of the four desks in the court are occupied on a Tuesday morning.**

She had the drawer out on the table. It is a flat drawer under a counter and it has been in that office since the office was founded and it is not locked and there is nothing in the world anybody would want from it.

"There are forty-six in it," said Mara Venn. "Thirty-eight on the tenth of the eighth, and I have counted them twice, and it is six in about a month and a half and I have stopped saying a rate out loud in this office because I have been wrong about the rate three times this year."

"Four-line cards."

"Four-line cards. Question, answer, toll, and a fifth line in half-size type. **They are the only instrument in this city that can count anything.** Not the Cathedral, which forecasts. Not the Assembly, which votes. Not the guild, which certifies. The Office of Public Reckoning audits tolls. It is a small dull office that has been counting other people's arithmetic for nine years and it is the only thing in this country that has ever produced a *figure that anybody can check*, and the reason is that an audit is a thing that is done, and an office closes an audit, and the file goes in a drawer, and a drawer holds a card about nine hundred times."

"That is your argument of the fourth of the sixth and it is still the best thing anybody in this city has said to me."

"It is not an argument, it is a job description." She put a card down. "So here is the job. **On Thursday of last week the branch came to me with a request and the request is the first time in nine years anybody has asked this office for anything that was not about a clock.**"

---

**The request is one line and it is in the branch's hand and not in hers.**

> *That the Office of Public Reckoning, being the office to which the second set of columns of the public toll ledger is a standing requirement, be required to state the number of persons in this city whose body toll is recorded as calibration strain, and for whom no duration is recorded, in each of the four wards of Green Span, by the year.*

"And I said yes," said Mara Venn, "before I had finished reading it, and then I sat there for about a minute and a half and understood what I had said yes to."

"Tell me what you understood."

"That they have given me a ward with a number in it instead of a man. **That is what the demand of the fourth of the sixth did and I filed it and I thought I had built a column.** I said in that Assembly that a column exists for about forty years and a feeling does not, and I was right, and I have spent three months finding out what a column is for. **A column is for counting a population.**"

She turned the card over.

"There are about **sixty-one calibration-strain entries a year in the four Green Span wards.** Sixty-one. It is in the ward returns and it has been in them for forty years on a phrase roughly forty years old, and it is the only column in this city that has ever held the people nobody counted, and it holds them as a phrase and not as a name and not as a duration.

"And the request asks me for the number of **persons**, not the number of entries, and those are not the same, and the difference between them is a fight about the size of a population and not about a man, and **it is going to take about four months, and the answer is going to be a number in the fifties, and the number in the fifties is going to be in a published audit, and every person in this city who wants to be angry about the Lower Weirs is going to be handed a figure in the fifties with a working on it and no name in it, and it is going to be the most useful thing anybody in this city has published this year.**"

"And no duration."

"**No duration, and there will be no arguing about it, and that part is easy and it was settled in June and it is in a note on top of this card.**" She held it up. The five lines were there in the Office's type. "Where no instrument recorded the duration, the column is left empty and is not filled by inference, and any person who fills one in by inference commits an offence against this office and against the person whose name is in the column beside it.

"So we are going to produce a figure about how many people there are, and we are not going to produce a single figure about how long it took any of them, **and I have read that note about four times today and it has never once stopped being the correct answer.** And I want you to sit there and understand that I have just been handed the instrument I have wanted for nine years and it is the same instrument as the one that produced a thousand pages and an annex with two hundred and twelve in it and told this city about a night in a way that could be checked. **The count is the only thing anybody in this city has ever been able to give anybody, and I have spent three months wishing I had one about a man and it turns out I wanted one about everybody and there is not one about everybody and there never will be.**"

---

**And then she said the part she had not said to anybody, and she said it flatly, and she said what it was not first, because she is a person who has been in a building for nine years and knows exactly how a sentence is going to be read.**

"**That is what I have been sitting on since Friday and I have not told one person in this office, including the man who is not an inspector, including my own clerk, and I want the reason for that said before the fact and not after, because after the fact it looks like a confidence.**"

"What is the reason."

"Because a drawer holds a card about nine hundred times, and I have been putting this in a drawer since Friday, and it has been in the drawer for four days, and I know exactly what four days does to a thing in a drawer, because I have watched other things go stale in other drawers for nine years. **I have a written right to be ignored and I have used it as a filing system and it has been very comfortable for nine years and it is the reason this office is the only instrument in this city and also the reason it is the only instrument in this city that has never once in nine years had a second person in a room with it.**"

"Which is the arrangement," said Orrin Vale.

"**Which is the arrangement,**" she said, "and I am not going to pretend that I have not noticed that you and I have a version of it, and I am not going to say it out loud in this room, and you are not going to either, and I want it understood that I have noticed."

The court outside the window was doing what it does. Somebody put a return in the tray. Somebody else took one out.

"It is not my mother," she said. "**I want that said first, because you have known me five months and you have never asked and I have never said, and I am not going to let you sit there this afternoon thinking this is the thing I have been keeping.** My mother is where my mother has been for nine years and I have a note about her that is one line and I have not sent it and it is not about a document. **This is about the office. It is about the fact that a small dull office with one window is the only thing in this country that counts, and I have been the only person in it for four years, and on Friday I realised that I would rather be wrong in public than right in a drawer, and that is not a professional judgement, it is a personal one, and it took me four days and a Tuesday morning to say it to a man in a shop.**"

"That is not nothing," he said.

"It is not nothing and it is not a compliment. **It is the price of the two of us, and you cannot have it back, and I am not going to pretend it is a small thing, because two people knowing a fact is not the same as one person knowing a fact and it is very much worse for the fact and better for the fact, and both of those are true at once.**"

---

**And he told her his, and he did it badly, and he did it in about four minutes, and she let him get all the way to the end of it before she said anything at all.**

"I have got a form," he said. "**Form GH-9. A standing withdrawal and reinstatement, series GH-9, which is the guild's series. I filled it in myself on the first of the fifth at a counter, in four minutes, and paid eighty-two shillings in coin out of my own money, and it is for forty-one heads, and it is the Weir Two night crew, and nobody in this city knows that I did it.**"

"Mr Vale."

"**I have known you for five months and I have not told you, and you have told me about a drawer on a Tuesday morning, and that is the whole of the difference between us and it is not a small one and I am not going to dress it up.**"

"How long."

"The first of the fifth. Four months and nineteen days. **A form in that series runs a year from the date of lodgement, so it runs out on the first of the fifth month next year, and on that day forty-one heads fall out of the ward's standing with no hearing and no vote, and the hall's four lines become true again about them, and the hall's four lines have been untrue about them for a year, because a reinstatement in that series makes them part of a certified shift, and nobody in that hall has noticed and nobody ever will.**"

He had the grey ledger out. He had it on his knee with a page turned to it and he did not read from it.

"You are the second person in Morrowfen who could put those two documents together and you are the first I have said it to. Della Marsh is the other one and I have not asked her and she has not offered and I am not going to."

Mara Venn sat quite still for about four seconds. Then she said:

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"There is no rest of it."

"There is. You have not said why you did it and you have not said why you have not told me and one of those is a reason and the other one is a decision, and you are a man who writes down decisions, and I have read four lines of yours and I know what a decision looks like in your hand."

He said it. She was right and it took him about a minute.

"**Because on the eleventh of the seventh a woman of twenty-six at a counter told me the thing I had paid for was a return, and a return is not a party to a question, and I had been going to that counter twice a month for two months and I had not heard it. And a return is made out on the rateable value of properties. I corrected it myself, on a form, in the guild's own series, in the guild's own hand, out of my own pocket, on the first of the fifth, in four minutes, and it cost eighty-two shillings, and the ward's rate went up a shilling and tenpence in the pound on the strength of it, which is about two thousand six hundred pounds a year, and nobody in the ward knows that I am the reason, and Bessie Crane paid sixty-two shillings in coin in the seventh to be counted and did not know that I had been counted first, and I have not been able to make myself go and tell her since July.**"

"That is the reason and it is a good one and it does not get any of it back."

"No."

She stood up and put the drawer shut and did not lock it, because there is nothing in the world anybody would want from it.

"Right," she said. "**Two things, and then I have a branch coming at half past three and it is about eleven miles of main and I have to be in it at four.**

"**One. Your form is a fact about forty-one men and it is a fact about you, and it is not a fact about them.** They do not know. They will not know. On the first of the fifth next year forty-one men will fall out of a standing they have never been told they are in, and they will fall out of it the day after a form you signed runs out, and there is no hearing and there is no vote and there is no form on which they could have refused, and that is not a scandal either, and I am not going to let you make it into one, because a scandal needs somebody to have done something and you did a thing in four minutes with your own money and it worked for a year and it cost the ward two thousand six hundred pounds a year and a woman with a cloth bag sixty-two shillings she did not have to pay. **You did a good thing that does not survive, and you knew it did not survive, and that is the whole of your character and I have known it for about four months.**"

"And the second."

"**The second is that our page is dated, and there are two hands on it, and there is no witness, and you have just used it against me.**" She said it without any edge at all. "There is one clause on that page. *Neither of us uses a result without naming its blind margin.* And in four months I have watched you not use a result four times and I have admired it and I have written it down as evidence and I have never once asked you what the margin of **this** was, because I assumed — and I have not examined the assumption and I have had nine years of practice at not examining things — that a man who does not use a result is a man who has nothing to hide.

"You have had something to hide since the first of the fifth. You have had two things. I know about the form. **You have also had the twenty-six addresses, and the promise you wrote in your own book on the day you were given them, and I have known about that one since the fourth of the fourth month and you told me about it yourself in a room over a shop.**"

"Yes."

"So here is what has happened to that clause this afternoon, and it is the only thing I have learned all day that I did not know at breakfast. **It does not say that we tell each other everything. It says that we do not use a result without naming its margin. It has nothing to say about what we keep, and I have spent four months believing that a man who observes his own blind margins is a man with no blind margins worth naming, and he has had two since the fourth of the fourth month and I have been sitting across a table from him.**"

She sat back down.

"That is the cost and I have decided to pay it and I am not going to be thanked and you are not going to thank me and we are going to go on with the page exactly as it is, **and I am going to tell you now that I would pay it again and I am not going to tell you that I would pay it again, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of what I have got.**"

"That is fair."

"It is not fair. It is a Tuesday afternoon in an office with a drawer in it. **I have not come here to be fair with you, I have come here to hand you a figure about sixty-one people in four wards so that you can do something you have been unable to do for six months, and you have not asked me once what I want out of it, and I notice that, and I am not going to raise it.**"

---

**And then, at about four o'clock, he said the other thing, and it was the reason he had asked for a pencil at the start of the afternoon and had not used it.**

"There is a figure in my grey book and it is wrong and I have had it since the seventh month and I have never once checked it, and I want to check it now because you have just handed me a number you did not compute and I would like to have one of my own that is done properly, for reasons I cannot fully explain to you and which are not noble."

"Go on."

"**It says *two hundred and fifty-five days.*** He turned the book round and put it on the table with the page towards her, which he has never done. "It is in my own hand and it is dated the ninth of the seventh and it is the number of days I had written as remaining to the first of the fifth next year, and it is wrong by thirty-four days and I had it wrong for two months and I never checked it because **a figure in a book in a shop is worth nothing in any room that is not the one it is in, and there has never been a room.**

"**It is two hundred and twenty-one days.** From tonight, the twentieth of the ninth: a hundred and two days to the thirty-first of this year, and a hundred and nineteen from the first of the first month of next year to the first of the fifth, and a hundred and two and a hundred and nineteen is two hundred and twenty-one. And two hundred and twenty-one is not a nicer figure and it is not a worse one. **It is a figure with a working on it.**"

Mara Venn looked at the page for a while. It was a grey ledger of about two thousand pages and there were four marks on the left in four colours and the writing was small and upright.

"Two hundred and fifty-five," she said. "**You wrote that in July and you were out by thirty-four and you did not know for two months, and you checked it the moment I gave you a number I had not computed, and I would like to say something about that and I am not going to say it kindly.**"

"Go on."

"**That is not humility. That is a man who has learned in five months that a number he likes and a number he can check are not the same object, and he has learned it from a stair, and it took him a hundred and forty questions to notice that he had learned it, and it is worth very much more than the thirty-four days and I am not going to pretend it is not worth very much more than the thirty-four days.**"

"I have not made a plan about the first of the fifth and I am going to write that down tonight so that I cannot make one later."

"**Do not make a plan about the first of the fifth.**" She said it fast and it was the only time in about an hour that she raised her voice. "Not a rescue, not a renewal, not a second form, not a fund, not a letter to a clerk. **It is a piece of paper and a date you set yourself and it is going to expire, and a thing that is going to expire is not a deadline, it is a bill, and you do not get to plan a bill, you get to pay it.** And when it falls, forty-one men who have never been asked anything will lose something they cannot name, and you will not be able to help them, and **the only thing you will be able to do is be the man who has it in a book and say so out loud, in a room, to somebody who can object, which is your first rule and the only one of the three you have ever kept without a price.**"

"Neither of us has been thanked," he said, "and the page has no witness on it, and neither of us has ever called this a friendship, and I am not going to start at four o'clock on a Tuesday."

"No," said Mara Venn. "**And I am not going to tell you what I have been calling it, because I have not decided, and because there is no form on which either of us could be said to be anything to anybody, and because the only office in this city that would have a column for it has four staff and a drawer and I am one of the four.**"

> *Twentieth of the ninth. The Office of Public Reckoning, one window, four staff, a court with three desks in it.*
> *She has been asked to count the number of persons in four Green Span wards whose body toll is recorded as calibration strain and for whom no duration is recorded. **About sixty-one entries a year and the answer is going to be a number in the fifties and it is a fight about the size of a population and not about a man.** The drawer holds forty-six. The note on the card is the same note in the same type and no duration will be written beside any of them, and that is settled and it is not going to be argued.*
> *She told me a thing she has not told anybody and it was the office and not her mother, and she said the reason first, and the reason was a written right to be ignored used as a filing system for four days, and she gave it to me and she cannot have it back and she said so and did not say whether she would do it again.*
> *And I told her about the first of the fifth and the eighty-two shillings and the forty-one heads, and she is the second person alive who can put those two documents together and the first I have said it to, and she said it was the only useful thing anybody has said to me all day and I have not been able to get it out of my head and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be thanked.*
> *The page is dated. Two hands. No witness. **Two hundred and fifty-five days was in this book in July and it was wrong by thirty-four and I did not look at it for two months, and I have checked it tonight, and it is two hundred and twenty-one.***
> *I have not made a plan. I have written that I have not made a plan. A thing that is going to expire is a bill and not a deadline and I am not going to be able to do one single thing about it on the day, and the only thing left is to be the man who has it in a book, and that is not a plan, and I have written it down so that I cannot call it one later.*
> *Nobody has been thanked. The page has no witness on it. Neither of us has used the word and neither of us is going to today.*
