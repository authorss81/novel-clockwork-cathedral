# Chapter 94 — What the Street Said and What the Street Did Not Say

The four days from the thirteenth to the sixteenth of the twelfth are the fourth, the fifth, the sixth and the seventh days of a notice that this city has no right to give and that twenty-two households are being given anyway, house by house, in the same four words, and in those four days a woman of thirty-three put a figure on a ward's return that makes two hundred and eleven households pay for water they have been carrying since 1936, and a man of thirty-two told her a second thing he had told nobody in three months because a street has been asked and a district has been told and it would be cowardice not to; and the clause on the page between them — *neither of us uses a result without naming its blind margin* — was used for the last time in this volume, and what it cost them was not a number.

---

Tuesday the thirteenth, at about ten in the morning, in a room with a court outside the window and one window in it, and three of the four staff in the room because the fourth had a drawer.

**The return went to the rate authority on Friday the fifth of the twelfth and the answer came back on Friday the twelfth, and the answer is a figure and the figure is the price of the water, and Mara Venn put it on the table face up.**

"Auditing schedule, entered on the ward's return, with the working and the five lines, before the rate is struck. Received and noted at the authority on the fifth. **The rate is struck on the corrected return and the corrected return carries a rateable figure of £6,960, and the district safety levy is three shillings in the pound on the rateable value as entered in the district return, so £6,960 at three shillings in the pound is £1,044 0s 0d a year, and none of it has been collected since the storm month, because a nil return is a good return.**"

"You have put £1,044 on a ward's return."

"**I have put the water on a ward's return, and the water has been there since 1936, and the rate is not a thing anybody in this city sets out of conscience. It is a thing a rate authority sets out of a return, and the return said nothing, and I have made the return say what it has always said, and the £1,044 is not a punishment and it is not a discovery and it is the bill.**"

"And you are on it."

"**My name is on the schedule and the Office's is not, and the Office is me, and there are four staff and one window and I have not hidden behind any of them and I am not going to.**" She turned the sheet over. "And the two hundred and eleven households in nine blocks of Hanna Stroud's are in it, and the shilling and one penny in the pound that a certain form put there in the fifth month of this year is still on it, and this is a second rise on the same sheet, and **a rate is the one thing in this city that nobody in a room over a chandler's shop can argue with and everybody in that room is going to feel.**"

"She asked for it."

"She asked for it in October, at a counter, in a good coat, and she asked for it in the name of nine blocks and she did it against herself and she has not been thanked for it either, and I want that written down in this room by somebody who is not me." She looked at him. "**And I am not going to be thanked and I have told three people in this building and I am telling you because you are the only other person alive who has to carry a version of it.**"

---

The three of them then did the thing they do, which is that a result goes on the table with its margin under it and not in a drawer, and this time there were two results and not one, and the second one was on a sheet of foolscap in a different hand from the first.

**The street, on Saturday the tenth, in the four words of a notice, at twenty-two doors.**

"Twenty-two houses. Thirteen of them have a clock on that cistern in it, and there are twelve clocks, because two of the thirteen share one in a court. Twelve said yes. One said no at a door in about nine seconds and gave a reason about a washing stand and a son of five, and asked to be asked again on a Friday, and I am not writing his name in this book and Hanna Stroud wrote his number on the back of her hand and did not write his name either."

"**Blind margin of that, and I am going to name it before you do, because I have read it four times since Saturday and I have got it down and I want to know if I have got it down.**"

"Go on."

"**A household said what it wanted to say to a man on a doorstep.** Not to a room. Not to a committee, not to a counter, not to an office, not to a minute — to a man on a step, in daylight, at about ten in the morning, in a street where he has never knocked. **And that is a different thing from a record, and only one of the two is a record, and I do not know which one this is and neither do you and neither does she.**"

"You have got it."

"**I have also got a second one and it is mine and not yours and I am putting it on the same sheet.** *A street that has been asked is not a street that has been told, and the difference is not a matter of accuracy. Being asked changes what a person says, and it changes it in a direction nobody can predict, and a man who has been asked a question has made a decision inside himself about how much of the truth the person asking is entitled to, and he will not have made that decision if he has been handed a notice on a board, and he may make a different one if he has been asked by a woman of fifty-four with a key ring full of keys he has never seen used.* **And the result is twelve names against thirteen houses, and one refusal that was made by a man who worked out the fifth column of the instrument in nine seconds at his own front door, and nobody in a room over a shop would have found it in a year.**"

"That is not a margin. That is an argument for it."

"**It is both and I have written it in both hands and I am not going to be tidy about it in front of three people, because the day I am tidy about it is the day the figure starts being a thing I can hold.**" She put her hand flat on the table, which is a thing she does. "So. The margin of the street is these two sentences, and the second one is in my hand and the first one is yours, and they do not cancel, and **the difference between a doorstep and a room is the only thing in this district that has ever worked and we have not got a name for it and we are not going to have one inside a year.**"

"You are not using the clause."

"**No.**" She said it flatly. "And I want that said out loud and I want it said by both of us, because it is the last time it is used and we both know what it has cost. *Neither of us uses a result without naming its blind margin.* Nine years of that page and this afternoon it has stopped being a good instrument and started being a habit, and a habit you keep for the sake of keeping is the same thing as a permit, and **a safeguard is a rule about what a question has to be and a shortcut is a way of asking it without the rule, and I am not going to be a woman who keeps doing it after she has noticed that doing it has become the easy bit.**"

"Then what is it, from today."

"**It is that neither of us gets to be the one who decides. From today, when there are two results and they do not agree, we put both on the table with both margins and we do not go looking for a third thing that is the answer.**"

He wrote that down. She read it back and made him change *we do not go looking for a third thing* to *neither of us goes looking for a third thing*, and he did, and she was right to.

---

**And then he told her the second thing, and he had been carrying it since the twenty-fifth of the ninth, and it is the reason this chapter exists.**

She did not ask. She had told him in November that she would not ask and had given the reason, and he had taken the arrangement as a permission and it was not a permission, it was a description of what she was prepared to be told, and there is a difference and this afternoon is the difference.

"**The ward's reserve.** The tank at the pumping ward. It has been at about fourteen hours and exactly balanced since the seventh of the October, and the clock-drive has been opening the boundary on the works' own schedule and putting in exactly what the front and the hospital branch take out, and it has been doing that since the second of the third month, and there has been no margin in it since March, and the low-level alarm has been inhibited since the second of the third month, and no report of it has been made, and no form in this city requires one.**"

He put the working on the sheet, because he puts the working on everything and she has never once told him to stop.

> *Fifty-six nights at fifty seconds is 2,800 seconds, which is 46 min 40 s, and that is a rate, and a rate is a thing a committee can vote on, and no committee has voted on this one and none can.*
> *A man at the Northglass gate has been writing the level on a sheet of paper in his coat every afternoon at about four since the second of the ninth, and there are about fourteen weeks of it, and it is the only record of the level of that tank in existence, and it is in a coat.*

"Why," said Mara Venn.

"**Because a street has been asked and a district has been told, and because a cistern is going to be drawn down under a row of twenty-two houses on Saturday morning at seven, and because the only water in this district that is not off the branch is going to be doing something I cannot predict, and there is a tank with no margin in it and an alarm that has been off since March.** And because a man of forty-one has promised to put a sheet of paper on a table at six in the morning on Saturday the seventeenth and take it off it at eight, in a room with nine people in it, and nobody will have a copy, and nobody has a copy, and I was in the room, **and it is cowardice to be the only person in this city who knows a number like that and to have told nobody, and I have been not telling you for three months and you noticed on the twenty-second of November and did not ask, and I have not thanked you for not asking and I have not deserved to be thanked and I am not going to be.**"

She did not say anything for about a minute, and she is a woman who has been in an office for nine years and knows exactly how a sentence will be read, and what she did was write it down. She wrote it on a printed four-line card, which is what the Office's cards are and are in every drawer in that building, and she wrote the date on it, and she wrote the two figures on it and the two dates, and then she turned it over and wrote on the back.

> *Received at this office, in one copy, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth. **M. Venn.**
> *An inhibited alarm on a ward's reserve since the second of the third month, no report made, no form requiring one, and a reserve at about fourteen hours and exactly balanced. **This office has no authority to obtain a water return, no power to certify an interval it has not observed, and no form on which it could be given water and therefore no form on which it could be given nothing.** The figure is entered as received and noted and is not a certification and is not admissible and is not to be used by any person in support of any demand.
> *The reverse: the number is in a drawer. **A drawer holds a card about nine hundred times. The Office of Public Reckoning is the one instrument in this city that counts anything and it is not able to do one single thing with this card, and a number an office cannot act on and cannot refuse is a worse thing than a number in a drawer, and I have put it in the drawer deliberately and I know what I have done and I would like it minuted in a room of three that I said it out loud first.**
> *The blind margin, and it is mine: **the office cannot say whether the tank was the same tank in March and it is the fourteenth of the twelfth. It cannot say what the level was at two in the morning on any night in the fourteen weeks, because a man wrote one number a day at about four in the afternoon and a man can wash his hands and a man is not an instrument.** It cannot say whether any person in this district knew. It cannot say what happens at fourteen hours. It cannot say what the number is a number of.*

"The card is the right instrument and the drawer is the right place and I want to say the second half of that out loud as well," he said, "because I have spent three months holding a number in my own head on the grounds that a woman of thirty-three would put it in a drawer, and she has just told me she would have done it on the thirteenth of December anyway, and my arrangement was not a protection of the number. **It was a protection of me.**"

"I know."

"You do not, but I am going to say it anyway and then I am going to stop, and you are not going to answer it, and if you answer it I will not be able to work on Saturday."

"**I am not going to answer it**," said Mara Venn, "and I am not going to be thanked for not answering it, and there is a difference between those two things that I have had four years to learn and it is the only thing in this office I am proud of."

And then, at the door, with her hand on the frame, she said the other thing, and it is one sentence and it is the only time in this volume that anything of the kind is said, and **it does not contain a name.**

"My mother would have said that a card in a drawer in a building with one window is a card in a drawer, and then she would have asked who keeps the drawer, and nobody has ever been able to answer that question about anything. **You are not going to ask me her name and I am not going to tell you and we are both going to behave as though this afternoon was about a tank.**"

Then she shut the file, because the file was about a tank, and she has been doing that for nine years and she was not going to stop on a Tuesday in December for anybody.

---

**The fourth, the fifth, the sixth and the seventh days, and they are the only part of this week that happened on a street.**

Four people went down the row again on the Tuesday afternoon, the Wednesday morning and the Friday afternoon, with the same four lines and the same fifth question, and the fourth day was for the times and the fifth day was for the passage clock at the second court and the sixth day was for the nine households with no clock in them, whose water is a different subject and which have no column, **and the seventh day was the man at number eight, on a Friday, at about eleven in the morning, at his own door, and he was in.**

"It isn't Saturday."

"It isn't Saturday. It's Friday, and you said a Friday, and I have written it down in this hand and not in a book, and I am not going to knock on a Saturday again because you told me not to and because I have been in this district for eleven years and I know exactly what a Saturday knock is."

"Will you wind yours."

"I'll wind mine. **My washing goes on the Sunday and the clock goes on the Sunday.**" He did not say anything else for about a minute. "You've given me the whole of it four times. That's four times more than anybody's given me anything, including the works, and I don't know what to do with a person who's told me the same true thing four times."

"**I have not told you the same true thing four times. I have told you four different things that are all true, and the difference between them is that I was not finished on Saturday, and I am finished now, and a thing that is not finished is the whole of what people mean when they say nobody told me anything.**"

"Then it is finished."

"It is finished, and on Saturday morning at seven o'clock eleven people will wind eleven clocks and nine households will have no water and one cistern will be empty and five hours, and **that is the whole of it and there is no other fact about Saturday and I have not got a better one, and I would rather you had that than had six things to be frightened of.**"

Nobody was thanked. There is no form on which a person may be thanked for standing on their own step.

And the sixth day, the fifteenth of the twelfth, at about eleven in the morning, at a gate on a works' boundary, a man of forty-one who has not been thanked in nineteen years said four sentences, and they are the terms for Saturday morning and they are the fourth and last statement of them and they are the only ones that were given in advance, and he gave them to a wall with a ladder against it and four people reading.

"Three clocks. One question, in the same words, written down before the day, and the words are the words. **Half past seven, and I chose that minute in September and I have not told anybody why and I am not going to until Monday.** Each of us writes what his own clock says and puts his own name to it and hands it to nobody, and Mr Vale reads the third clock and hands his to nobody, and if anybody hands anything to anybody else then we have built a machine and it will take four years to find out what we built.

"Nothing about Saturday is admissible and nothing about Saturday is a certification and there is no seal and no form and no rail and no filing and the count of keeper's tolls is four and it does not move on Saturday, and the count of Mr Vale's hand corrections is two and it is two on Saturday, and I have said all of this three times and I am saying it a fourth time and I will not say it a fifth.

"**And on Monday morning I will tell him why I chose half past seven, and it is the whole of what I have come down here for, and I came down here on the Tuesday and I am saying it on the Friday because the sixth day is the day a person may be told what he is going to be asked and not before, and there is no form on which a person may be told that and I am doing it against a wall.**"

And then a bus came in at about a quarter to four in the afternoon, and the seventeenth of the twelfth was the day after tomorrow, and a rehearsal could not stop one single thing in this city.
