# Chapter 77 — The Stoppage Is Not a Strike and the Company Is Not a Party

The tank went under a day on Sunday the twenty-fifth of the ninth month and nobody was told, and the reason nobody was told is a switch that was thrown off in the third month by a man who did it for a good reason and entered it in a book, and the reason the report was never made is that no form in this city requires one, and those two sentences are both true and neither of them is anybody's fault, and the consequence of the two of them together is that a hospital with eleven patients and three staff on a branch was about to be out of water and had about four hours' notice of it in a form that nobody was going to read.

---

**Sunday the twenty-fifth, about four in the afternoon, and the gauge.**

The reserve is the tank. The tank is the hospital's clock pressure and the canal front's four draws a night. It is one tank, in the pumping ward, doing two jobs it was never sized for, and it has been under two days since the second of the third month, and the low-level alarm has been inhibited since the same date.

**That is a sentence with four parts and one of them is a man's decision, and the decision was made for the best reason anybody in this district has ever had.** The alarm had gone off eleven times in about four months in 1951 and each time it was a false alarm on a float that has never been quite right, and the sound of it in a warden's office at three in the morning had brought a woman out of a bed eleven times, and it was inhibited on the second of the third month, and it was written in the warden's day book, and no form requires a report of the inhibition and none was made.

The reserve has been under two days ever since, on the warden's gauge sheet, in a column with no heading, and the warder's gauge sheet is in three copies, one in a works' office, one in a cabinet in a house on Ferrywright Row since 1951, and one in a box in a store at the pumping ward.

On the Sunday the twenty-fifth of the ninth month it went under one day, and there is no alarm, and there is no form, and there is no column with a heading, and the only person in Morrowfen who would have seen it is a woman of forty-four who stood down on the first of the ninth and is at this moment on a bus going nowhere in particular because she had nowhere to be on the Thursday, which she had arranged in June.

**At about a quarter past four on the Sunday afternoon a low-level float on the district main at the Northglass boundary reads a quarter of an inch under the make-up, and the man who reads it is Corvin Tole, forty-one, nineteen years at that gate, and he reads it every afternoon at about four because it is on his round, and nobody has ever asked him what he does with the number.**

He did not telephone anybody. There was nothing to telephone anybody *with*. He wrote the figure on the back of his hand in grease pencil because that is what the round is done with, and he opened the boundary from about half past four to about ten past five to put six hours back into the tank, and the tank read just over a day at six o'clock, and he wrote that on the back of his hand too, and he washed his hands at ten and the number went with the grease.

**On the Monday morning the tank was at about nineteen hours and the branch was at thirty-one pounds, and at about ten o'clock on Monday night it was at about fifteen hours and the branch was at about twenty-eight, and on Tuesday morning it was at about nine hours.**

Nobody in this city has to be told any of this and nobody was.

---

**Monday the twenty-sixth, from about two in the afternoon, at Green Span, and the second round of a night nobody had been told about.**

The first time, on the second of the ninth, the branch lost pressure at ten past two in the morning and came back at four o'clock, and Elsie Cobb timed a round of twenty-two intervals by hand and wrote the duration in a new column on her own card.

The second time, on the evening of Monday the twenty-sixth, it lost pressure at about ten past nine in the evening, and nobody was in the ward who had not been in the ward the first time, and the ward ran off the free master from about ten past nine in the evening until about half past three in the morning, which is six hours and twenty minutes, and **Leda Vale said in the nurses' room, out loud, in front of eleven people, that six hours and twenty minutes is about ten times the ward's slack of forty minutes, and the number forty minutes has been on a card in this room since June.**

There was no alarm on the ward either. A hospital's water is not a ward's water, and the branch has no reserve of its own because a branch is a draw and not a supply, and that has been on a form somewhere since 1951 and the form has been in a store.

**Elsie Cobb did the round again. Two watches, eleven patients, three staff, the free master, the bedside, the docket, and the new column with a second line in it, dated the twenty-sixth, and she did not ask anybody's permission and nobody thought to offer any.**

> *2nd col. 26th Sept. INTERVAL WORKED BY HAND. Night sister E. Cobb, ward rate.*
> *Line pressure lost 9.10 p.m. Restored 3.30 a.m.*
> *11 pts, 3 staff, 24 intervals worked off the free master at the bedside, each checked with two watches. Worst single interval 7 min 05 s.*
> *Total hand time for the round, 9.10 p.m. to 3.30 a.m. — **6 h 20 min.***

And she said that one out loud too, in the room, in the same voice, and Leda Vale wrote it under hers and put her own name against it, which is now the second time in eleven years that two names have been under a figure in that ward.

**And that is the whole of the discovery of the second of the ninth and the twenty-sixth of the ninth: the ward's free master, which is inside a second a day, is the reason a hospital with eleven patients is not a catastrophe, and it is a bracket clock of 1949, eleven inches high, on a third-floor landing in a building that was up before either of them came to it, and it is kept in a job by nobody in particular, and it is wound by whoever is on, and it is the only reason this book has two numbers in it and not none.**

---

**Tuesday the twenty-seventh, at about eight in the evening, in the two rooms over a chandler's shop in Bellweather, and there were about fourteen people in them and it was a Tuesday.**

Two sentences were said in that room on the Tuesday evening and both of them were said by a man of twenty and both of them were about names, and he gave the reason for each of them, and the reason for each of them is the whole of this district.

"**It is not a strike.** There is no employer in it. Nobody has been dismissed and nobody can be, and there is no return date and no arbitration and no fund and no two sides, and a strike is a thing that two sides go to arbitration about. **I have said that once in this district already and I am not going to say it again, and I have watched four people this week say it and one of them was a reporter, and he has gone away and written it down wrong.**"

"And the company."

"**And the company is not a party.** It is in an envelope in a works' file with a minute on top of it that binds nobody, and it has not been formed, and nobody has signed anything, and it will not be formed this month, and I have said why and I am not going to say it again either. **A party is somebody who can be asked a question and refuse it. There is nobody in this district who can be asked a question and refuse it. That has been true since the fourth of the fourth month and it is the reason I cannot make this into a thing that anybody can win.**"

He sat down. Hanna Stroud said, "And the ninety men still out?"

"**Ones going back in on Wednesday. Some of them. Not because anybody has agreed anything. Because a fettler is nine years in a trade and there are four of them and a furnace that goes cold costs a fortnight.**"

---

**And then a man of about thirty at a counter said a word that is not a word in this city yet, and a man of thirty-six corrected it, and the correction is the reason the rest of the week happens.**

It was Ambrose. He had a sheet of paper on his knee and he had been working on it for about forty minutes, and what he had on it was a thing four ordinary people could do.

"It is a reading room," he said. "**That is what this is. Three clocks, a common question, a written minute, three people with watches. It is a reading room. I have read the ordinance and there is nothing in it that says three people may not do it and the Question Hall does it with a machine and a brass frame and we are doing it with three clocks and a pencil.**"

**Ivo Pell had come at about half past seven, at a time of his own choosing, and had been standing at the back with his hands behind his back for about an hour.**

"That is not a reading room," he said.

"It is four of them—"

"**It is a Tuesday evening with three clocks and a piece of paper, and I am going to tell you what it is not, and I am going to tell you in front of fourteen people, and I am not going to do it twice.**" He came forward. "A reading room has a spindle in it. A spindle takes a named act — a thing somebody could physically do, to a named thing, with a number and a day on it — and it takes it or it does not, and the not-taking is a decision made by a machine that was built so that it would make it, and the reason it is built that way is that a man who cannot be stopped from asking a question is a man who will ask a question he cannot answer.

"Your three clocks and your written minute have none of that. **They have no rule about what a question has to be. They will agree with each other or they will not and you have no way of telling which, and a room that cannot tell you whether your question was a proper question is not a room, it is three men with watches.**"

"Then what is it," said Ambrose.

"**It is a rehearsal.** That is the word and it is the only one I know and you are not going to get a better one out of me in a fortnight. Three independent hourlines. A common question, written out, in advance, in words, so that nobody is answering a different one. And a map of what you are asking about, because you cannot map a district you have not got a district for." He looked round the room. "And you are going to need three things you have not got and I am going to name all three, and then I am going to go to my train.

"**One: two other trained operators. Not two other men. Two other people who can do a thing to a machine and say honestly what came back, and there are about eleven of those in this city and none of them has been asked, and I know because I am one of them and I have the list.**

"**Two: a person of the affected district.** Not a stand-in and not a district. That is the requirement, and it is the only requirement in this business that has never once been bent, and the reason it has never been bent is that it is the whole of the safeguard. **A safeguard is a rule about what a question has to be, and a shortcut is a way of asking it without the rule, and there is no permit in this city and there is not going to be one out of this.** And the station on the rail takes a person of the affected district, or a person named in a district standing, and the Lower Weirs pumping district has no standing under the water act, and has not had for eighteen years, and the form that would make one is two shillings the head for a year, **and two shillings the head buys a return, and a return is not a party to anything, and a girl of twenty-six told a man in a shop so at a counter on the eleventh of the seventh and he wrote it down and has not been able to get round it since.**"

The room did not say anything.

"**Three: nobody who will put their name to it.** A rehearsal with a common question and a written minute and three clocks produces a piece of paper. **A piece of paper with nobody's name at the bottom of it is worth exactly what you would expect and I have seen about two hundred of them in twelve years and I will not countersign one.**"

He got his coat. "And Ambrose — the thing you have written on your knee is not nothing. **Three people who do not agree, at a minute that was written down before the day, in three hands, with a date on it, is a thing this city has not got and cannot vote on and cannot minute, and that is not the same as being right.** Do it. Do it without my name on it and do not thank me and I will come and look at what you have written about in a fortnight because I said I would."

He went.

---

**Wednesday the twenty-eighth and Thursday the twenty-ninth, and the three clocks, and the cost of them, and it is on the table and it can be read.**

The room over the chandler's shop has two tables pushed together and eleven chairs and a stove and a shelf of minute books in which the most recent volume is 1948.

**The three clocks are these, and the choice of them is the whole of the argument and it was argued for about two hours.**

> *1. The pumping ward, canal road, outer room. A bracket clock, on the district line, **57 min 20 s** out of the district reference since the second of the eighth.*
> *2. The Weir Two pump house, station clock. On the same line, **48 min 00 s** out. Nine minutes and twenty seconds nearer than the first.*
> *3. The Quarter hill, south public clock, on a bracket over a door at the corner where four roads meet, **1 h 49 min 10 s** out, and about nine hundred people a day walk under it without looking up.*

"**That is the point of the three and there is no fourth thing to point at,**" said Ivy Pike. "Two clocks nine minutes and twenty seconds apart, in two buildings, with a man who has measured it twice. And one clock out by an hour and forty-nine minutes in a street where anybody can stand under it. **You do not need anybody to agree. You need three people to be in three places at a minute somebody wrote down in advance, and to write down what they see, and to have the three figures that do not agree in three hands.**"

"And a written question," said Ambrose. "**What is the question.**"

"The same one, in the same words, and written before the day."

"Written by whom?"

"By whoever is going to be worst embarrassed by it. **That is the only rule there is and it is not mine and Ivo Pell did not say it, I have been saying it since the fifteenth of the sixth and nobody wrote it down.**"

The written question is this, and it is on one sheet and it is signed by nobody and it is dated the twenty-eighth of the ninth, and it is for the third of the tenth, at a minute to be fixed:

> *At the minute stated, read each clock named below and write down, in figures, what it says, against the district reference of the Cathedral public clock; and state the time of reading, and state who read it.*
> *Pumping ward, canal road, outer room. Weir Two pump house, station clock. Quarter hill, south public clock.*

And there is no question in it at all, and that is the point, and Orrin Vale said so and said why.

"**It asks no question. It is the nearest thing to a question that I have ever been able to write down and it is the first thing I have ever put in front of three people that has no rule about what it has to be in it at all, and that is not a virtue, and I have spent a fortnight in a room with a man of thirty-six being told that a safeguard is a rule about what a question has to be, and I have written a paper with no question on it, and I would like somebody to explain to me why the safest thing in this business turns out to be a piece of paper with three times on it.**"

Nobody could.

---

**And the cost went on the table on the Thursday morning and it is the reason there is an ending to it, and it is this, and every line of it is arithmetic and none of it is opinion.**

> **What it costs, and who pays it, and there is no fund.**
>
> *The three readings take about twenty minutes each including walking, and the third one is a clock over a door at the corner of the Quarter hill, and on the third of the tenth it is raining or it is not.*
>
> *Nobody can be paid for any of it. **The district's maintenance is entered in the ordinary class or not at all, since the first of the seventh. The standing protection that might have covered an unregistered person in this district was carried in June and expired at the first sitting of the committee after the report was received, which was Tuesday the thirteenth, and it was not extended and it cannot be.** The ordinary class is two shillings the head for a year and there are three people, which is six shillings, and six shillings is not the problem.*
>
> ***The problem is that there is no ratepayer. There is no district to be in the schedule of. A person who is paid to stand under a clock in Bellweather Road is being paid by somebody, and the somebody has to be named, and there are four candidates on that list and every one of them is a person who will be asked about it:***
> *— the tenant council, which has a stove and a bell that has not worked since 1949;*
> *— the mutual, which said no in four minutes on the twenty-ninth of the seventh and is not to be asked again this month;*
> *— the works, which will pay for a works' man and not for a person of a district it cannot consult;*
> *— and a company, which is in an envelope in a works' file with a minute on top of it and has not been formed.*
>
> *So the three readings are done for nothing, by three people, on their own time, and two of them are on a gate and one of them signs for a gate, and a woman of fifty-four with nine blocks will find out that one of her tenants stood in the rain for nothing because somebody mentioned it in a shop.*

And that is the cost, and it is on the table in a room with a stove in it, and it can be read, and it is arithmetic.

**And the decision that was taken in that room on Thursday the twenty-ninth of the ninth month at about eleven in the morning, and it was taken by a show of hands, and there is no minute book newer than 1948, and there is a chair and a stove and eleven people, and it went like this:**

It was **not** to have a fund, because a fund is a thing that arrives with a rate and a rate arrives in the autumn.

It was **not** to use the company, because the company has not been formed and one man in the room has said no twice for two different reasons and a second reason is a reason and not a decoration.

It was to do the three readings on the third of the tenth, at a minute fixed on the Monday, for nothing, by three named people, and to write the three figures down in three hands on one sheet, and to put the sheet in a folder on the ledge in that room, under the glass case and **beside** the ledger and not in it, because the ledger is a ledger of body tolls and it has one entry in it and nothing beside the entry, and there is no column in it for a clock.

And Bevin Sarr said, at the end, the thing that is the end of it, and she said it standing up with her hand on the door:

"**Three figures that do not agree, on one sheet, in three hands, with a minute on it that was written down before the day. That is the whole of it and it is worth about eleven minutes a week for three people for as long as they feel like it, and there is no form for it, and there is no seal on it, and nobody can be ordered to do it, and it cannot stop one single thing in this city.** And I have been in this district eleven years and I have never had that, and we are going to do it on the third of October for nothing in the rain, and **I am not going to be thanked and neither are they and there is nowhere to put it.**"
