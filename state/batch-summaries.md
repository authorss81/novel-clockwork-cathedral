# Batch Summaries and Rolling Volume Summary

One compact section per completed batch, newest last. The rolling volume summary at the end is what a writer should read first when picking a volume up mid-run. Older chapter detail lives in `state/chapter-summaries.md`; the fourteen-volume index lives in `state/continuity.md`.

## Volume 01, Batch 0001 — Chapters 1–10 (The Ninth Tick, part one)

**What happened.** Orrin Vale certified the Cathedral's crown-case public clock on Monday the 15th, heard a ninth pulse arrive from below in a clock that was correct twice on his gauge, and told nobody. The next day Gerren Hask talked him out of a defect note, showed him the hall's labour book with a line in a copying clerk's hand reading *Weir Two, night pumping, non-certified standby, 1 crew, 3 nights*, and handed him an eighteen-year-old Long Nine governor that was stopped mid-travel on purpose with a bar, to be reduced to unrecorded scrap; Orrin refused to scrap a regulator with a cut pin and took it home. On the 17th he met Perry Vance alive at Weir Two, told him the second stage would run past midnight, watched him shut the hand valve in daylight and chalk *hand shut*, and heard the ninth beat a second and last time. He then filed one question as a private citizen, paying three district minutes from the Clockwork Quarter's reserve — the district with the most reserve and the least standing to object — and watched the Loom hold Ferrywright Bridge through the night tide. Vance went down at about ten to shut a valve the model said was shut, and the pump that should have stopped did not stop. Two more were injured; the Fenn boy is fourteen. The Directorate's notice pinned to the pump house door called the day a success and was not lying.

**What it cost.** Three minutes charged to a district that was neither affected nor consenting; Alma Festinger's witness interval on a Wednesday rota; Orrin's first Aftershock, unrecorded; and a lost minute of his own attention with clean edges that he can never account for. The Weirs paid the rest in hours nobody was paid for, as they had for twenty years.

**Where the batch ends.** The omission is not a machine failure but a **guild form number** — GH-9, standing withdrawal, maintenance-deferred class — with a price schedule (two shillings the head for a year) and a batch of forty-one entries transferred eighteen years ago to the Archive of Unlisted, countersigned for the affected district by a then-twenty-six-year-old deputy pumping warden, **Sel Kade**. A routing copy went to a Green Span public-health rota and the attendance line reads *FESTINGER, A.*, and nobody has ever connected it. The first repair done in company — a bearing block, a shim and a named sacrificial plate on the hospital trestle's conduit bracket — held the clocks, was paid for out of a ward's forty minutes of slack and nine men's Tuesday morning, and left Orrin with two days of lost fine motor control in his right hand and a permanent ring above a given pitch in his right ear. The first institutional gain is an objection on the toll receipt, accepted for filing. The batch's defeat is the committee: the demand for a durable toll record and for a name and a duration in the body-toll columns was referred to committee until the spring, by Councilor Imani Rook, on arithmetic rather than sentiment.

**State at the end of the batch.** Orrin: Stage 2 Aftershock, deliberately used once and named aloud; two keeper's tolls taken, neither filed; no licence offence committed; class-three renewal about eight weeks out and slack; private ledger open at the front with three entries that matter. Mara Venn: an objection on file, a measured blind margin, a professional fear for Orrin's hearing, no personal disclosure. Teo Arden: correct, unsoftened suspicion, calls it an audit. Leda Vale: arguing about method, not character, and on the page in the climax. Hask: his crime is legible to the reader and invisible to every character.

**One deliberate repetition, do not fix it.** Orrin's rule — *nothing of mine is used again without its blind margin and its toll named in public, in a room, to somebody who can object* — appears as a standalone line at the end of the Chapter 9 ledger entry and again in Chapter 10, where he turns back to it, reads it twice, and copies it out at the top of a clean page because a rule that lives at the back of a book is a note and not a rule. A duplicate-paragraph audit will flag it. It is motivated and it is the beat the batch's rule requirement turns on.

**Carried into batch 0002:** the class-three renewal's paperwork, not the renewal; the guild labour ledger and Della Marsh's drawer; the Weirs' counter-record and Bevin Sarr's board; Imani Rook's own filing due about Chapter 13; the Green Span hospital pump Orrin reads correctly and cannot certify; the hospital trestle; Sel Kade's reserve as a public fact; the two uncharged minutes; and the fact that the Archive of Unlisted stays shut until Volume 02.
