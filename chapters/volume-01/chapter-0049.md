# Chapter 49 — Standing Protection, for a Stated Period

The public-health committee of the Assembly is a room over a printer's on Cathedral Rise with a window that does not shut and a table that four of the members have turned the wrong way up at different times, and it meets on Thursdays, and on the Saturday the fourth of the sixth month it met on a Saturday, which it may do, and which it does about twice a year, and which is a form of the reason it is the only committee in this building that is frightened of being asked a question twice.

Councilor Imani Rook chairs it. She is forty. She is the Green Span delegate and she is the chair of the public-health committee and she has been for three years, and in the first month of this year she blocked, in this room, a demand that a toll ledger be published, and she did it on arithmetic, and she has said so in this room twice since, and she has not softened, and nobody in this room is being gentle with her, because she has never once been gentle with them.

The thing about the room is that the Lower Weirs pumping district has no delegate to it. A district is not a ward and a pumping district is not a ward and there is no form by which one could be put on this committee or on any other, and what there is instead is a member of the public, who may be seated at the back with a paper and may not speak to a motion, and who must leave when the committee goes to a division.

There were two members of the public on the Saturday. One of them was Teo Arden, twenty years old, with a paper. The other one was a man of about thirty from the works on the canal road who had a variation at item one in his coat and about nine minutes' notice of what was going to be said about it.

He did not have to speak. Item two came up at four minutes past eleven and it was moved by the chair and it was the chair's motion and it was in her own hand, and it was the fourth thing she had moved in two years and the first one she had moved on the toll side, and the two men at the back of that room heard it being read out.

> *That no shift in the Lower Weirs pumping district be certified in the maintenance-deferred class from this date until the guild's labour ledger, eighteen years, and its annex, be published in full and received by this committee; that the protection is temporary; that it expires at the first sitting of this committee after the report is received and not before; and that this committee has not undertaken to renew it and has no power to promise that it will.*

Carried, on a show of hands, without a division, in eleven minutes from the chair's moving it.

The protection is temporary. It was temporary in the first sentence of the motion, in the ninth word, and it was temporary in the ninth minute when a member asked whether the committee intended to renew it and the chair said no, and it was temporary in the eleventh minute when the chair said that the protection ran from the fourth of this month to the first sitting of this committee after the guild's report was received, and that the guild's report was due at this committee's sitting in the ninth month next, and that if the report was late then the protection would be before the committee in the twelfth month and the committee would decide what to do about it, and that she could not tell anybody what the committee would do, and that she was telling them anyway so that nobody in that room could go away and tell a district that it was safe.

That is three months. The audit of nine series and eighteen years of a guild's labour book takes about fourteen months when it is begun with a requisition and does not have a committee sitting on it, and every person in that room knew the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is the reason the motion is not worth what it looks like, and it was carried anyway, because three months of a night crew being on a published sheet with their names against it is three months more than nobody at all, and because a district four months and an inspectorate nine years had got exactly this far, and eleven minutes was what the rest of it cost.

"That is a good motion and it is nearly nothing," said a member who had been in the Assembly for nineteen years, not unkindly. "Madam Chair, may I ask what eleven minutes of this room has been worth?"

"Three months of a maintenance-deferred class not being certified in a district of a hundred and forty thousand people, against nothing on the fourteenth of the first month," said Councilor Rook. "I would like to be permitted to add one fact to that figure and the fact is not sentiment and it is not the Weirs and it is not anything anybody has said to me in a room. It is that a maintenance-deferred class is a class in which a night crew's hours are not hours, and for eleven years the reason that was true of that district is a form at two shillings the head and a batch number, and that form and that batch number have been in the public index on the hill since the second of the second month of this year and I have read it, and I have read the countersignature line, and I have known about it since the ninth of the fourth month and I have not said anything in this room about it, and I am saying it now because in about nine years the annex will be published and this room will want to know who had it when."

Nobody in that room said anything at all, and a clerk took it down, and the chair did not look round.

"Item five," said Councilor Rook, "and this one is mine, and I am going to say what it is before I move it, because I moved the other one against it three years ago and I would like the minutes to show that I know that.

"The demand of the thirteenth of the fourth month, of which I was the mover, asked this Assembly to receive a district's own returns as evidence, to direct the Office of Public Reckoning to establish the means by which a district may enter a record of its own hours, and to print the returns in full with the minutes. It cost four pounds ten out of a Green Span ward's clerical allocation and it contains no name column and no duration column, and I left both out on purpose, and I have said publicly in this city for three years that I left them out because I did not know the size of the population a name-and-duration column would describe, and that the two numbers would be read together. That was true and it was the reason and I am not withdrawing the reason.

"What I am moving today is the clause I left out, and I am moving it on arithmetic, and I have been shown arithmetic and I have not been persuaded of anything, and the arithmetic is the reason.

"An audit is a thing that is done. The Office of Public Reckoning requires, in the first line of its own instrument of the third of this month, that every body toll recorded by any body in this city be recorded with a name and a duration. That is one line in one office's own practice and it took a determination of a licensing floor to bring about, in four true lines, at a counter, in front of thirty people, in about nine minutes, and I was in that room.

"An office does that once. An office has a right to be ignored, in writing, in its own hand, and it is written on a form in a drawer in a building on this hill, and I have got a copy of that form in a binder with the district's returns in it, and the form is nine years old. An office closes an audit and the file goes in a drawer and a drawer holds a card about nine hundred times.

"A demand is not that. A demand is a standing thing. It is read twice, it is entered on the minutes, it is discharged, and it is read again next year, and it is the only instrument in this building that survives a change of clerk, and this building changes its clerks."

She put her hand flat on the table, which she had done once in this calendar year, in a different room, over an item on a schedule.

"I move that the Assembly receive as evidence, and publish in full with the minutes, the toll ledger of the city, in the two sets of columns, and that the second set of columns be a standing requirement of the Office of Public Reckoning's audit and not a matter of the Office's practice; and that where a duration was not recorded, that fact be printed in the ledger, in the column, and not elsewhere. And I am not asking this Assembly to feel anything about a man whose name is in a column with nothing beside it. I am asking it to build the column, because a column exists for about forty years and a feeling does not."

The division was carried by nine to four. The four were not the clerk. No amendment was offered. The motion was read twice and entered on the minutes, and the chair did not thank the four and did not thank anybody.

Nobody in that room was satisfied and that is not a remark; it is what the twenty-eight minutes were. The protection is temporary and is three months long against an audit of fourteen, and the demand publishes a ledger with one name in it and an empty space beside the name, and the district is not represented in the room where both were decided, and two men at the back of it heard both and one of them is twenty years old and has not been in a room like that in his life and is not a member of anything and could not speak to a motion if he tried, and the other one had a variation in his coat and was gone before the division.

Teo Arden said the one thing he said, and he said it to the chair and not to the room, at the door, in about four minutes.

"Councillor, you have just carried a ledger with a name in it and a column beside the name with nothing in it, and I have spent three months trying to get a printed page about a number of mine, and I have said the whole of my half of that argument twice in a room over a chandler's shop and been right both times and it has not been printed, and Mrs Pruitt has the other half and she is not in this building and I am not speaking to her, and you are the first person in a room in a year who has had to decide something, so I am going to say her half out loud, and neither of us is here."

"You are neither of you here," said Councilor Rook, "and that is correct, and I am not going to pretend to you that the committee can do anything about a page, and I am going to say the other half because nobody else in this building is going to. It costs eleven shillings and the tin is empty and the tin being empty is the reason the process exists, and a district that will not spend eleven shillings is not going to be represented in nine years, and I have been shown arithmetic and I have never been shown anything else and I do not intend to start now."

---

The Office of Public Reckoning is on the second floor of a building on Cathedral Rise and it has four staff, one window, a written right to be ignored, and a drawer of printed cards, and on the Monday the sixth of the sixth month at about eleven in the morning Mara Venn took out a form and read it twice and then wrote four lines and a half, which is the most she has written on a form in nine years.

The form had come in on the Friday from the Directorate's health records branch, and it is a form, and it is correct, and it asks the Office of Public Reckoning to supply the duration of the body toll recorded against the name of Vance, P., in the toll ledger of the city, and it says that the Directorate's health files hold entries of calibration strain, undated and without duration, from which a duration might be derived by the attending officer, and it asks whether the Office will supply the figure for the ledger's fifth column so that the ledger may be complete.

She wrote back:

> *Received and noted. This office has audited the Toll Stair for nine years and has, from the first of the sixth month, required that every body toll recorded by any body be recorded with a name and a duration, and that where no instrument recorded the duration the column be left empty and be not filled by inference.*
>
> *The Office has no authority to obtain a health file, has no power to certify an interval it has not observed, and is of opinion that an interval derived from a health record after the fact is not a duration but an estimate, and that the column of a public ledger does not take an estimate.*
>
> *Any person who fills that column by inference commits an offence against this office and against the person whose name is in the column beside it. The Office would rather have an empty column than a comfortable one, and it says so in a printed note at the head of the ledger in five lines, and it would be obliged if the branch would read it.*
>
> *Received at this office, in one copy, on the fourth inst.*

*No further correspondence on this matter*, in the corner, in small type, the way it has said it since the first month of the year and three hundred times before that, and this is the first time in nine years that anybody in this city has got a *received and noted* that was the right answer to the question that was asked.

The drawer of four-line cards held thirty-one on the Monday the sixth of the sixth month, against eleven on the seventeenth of the third month and twenty-two on the eighteenth of the fourth, and she put this one in on top, and she was rung up on the telephone on the Tuesday by a woman in a laundry who said thank you twice and who was told by an investigator of the Office of Public Reckoning, in nine words, that the office had received the ledger in one copy on the fourth, that the fifth column would be empty, and that the office had said so in a note, and that the office could not do anything else about it and would not try to, and that the woman was welcome.

She put the telephone down and sat with a file open for about a minute, and then she wrote a note she did not send, and the note is one line, and it is the only thing in nine years of that office that is not about a document.

Then she closed the file, because the file was about a document.

On the Sunday the fifth of the sixth month the galvanising and wire works on the Carrow Street main started again on a reduced draw, about eight in the morning, and Ivy Pike was there at ten with her pay book and could say exactly what the number was, which was fifty-eight of ninety-one, and the other thirty-three were not at that gate and had gone to the yards and the docks and two districts, and the rate on nine of those men's houses in this ward had gone up a shilling and a penny in the pound on the first of the rate year, and the finding about the flood was still in chalk on a board outside a pump house, and the review that had produced it could not have kept the water on and had not been asked to and did not claim to have.

Wenna Pruitt did the September arithmetic on the Sunday. It is the same September. It is always the same September. She has done it in the first month and the second month and the fourth month and the fifth month of this year and she will do it in September for the spring, and the two numbers at the front of her book are still the four pounds ten that bought an appendix and the eleven shillings that bought nothing, and she has still got them side by side, and she still cannot tell anybody why they are equal by accident.

Orrin Vale cleaned a barrel on the Monday and beat a clock to a rate on the Monday afternoon and wrote a certificate of neither, and could not, and the two benches had four days' work in front of them on the book and a lease to Michaelmas, and on the Monday morning a man came into the shop off Verge Lane with a carriage clock under his arm, the same one he had brought in at the end of the third month, and asked whether Orrin could tell him what was wrong with it, and could not certify that the repair was right.

"You can have it back on Saturday," said Orrin Vale, "and I will tell you what is wrong with it, and I will write you what I did, and you can take it to a man with a licence if you want the paper. I have a lease and a bench and I have never once not had a bench."

That is the whole of the punishment. Nobody has taken his lease and nobody has taken his bench and nobody in this city has asked him to leave either, and the price of an evening's public arithmetic in the first month of the year was a plate on the bench in the shop window that said, in the same type it has said for eleven years, **HOROLOGER. REPAIRS TO ANY DESCRIPTION.**
