# Chapter 66 — The Step Stops, and So Does His Number

The Water Users' Committee met on Monday the first of the eighth month at about four in the afternoon, in a room above a shop in Bellweather, and **re-voted the canal front's daytime draw from four cycles to two, effective to the thirty-first**, and they were right, and the reason they were right is that the front needs the water in August, and the district was not told, could not be told, and had no form on which to be asked.

The step stopped on the second of the eighth month. Fifty seconds a night, in one step, at the fourth draw, since the night of the twenty-first of the fourth — and the whole of the last ten weeks of Orrin Vale's working life came out of that vote, and it took him a day to work out how.

---

**The notice went up on the ward board outside the Weir Two pump house on the Monday morning at about ten, in the committee's own type, in Nadia Erridge's hand, and it is one sheet and it is nine lines and every word of it is true.**

> **VARIATION. Canal front, Bellweather Road to the Weir One gate. Daytime draw.**
> *The schedule of the seventeenth day of the fourth month is further varied as follows: the daytime draw shall be worked in two cycles in place of four. Effective from this day to the thirty-first day of this month.*
> *The committee finds that the demand upon the front in the present month exceeds the supply available in four cycles and that the standpipes at the second court and at the Bellweather washing-yard cannot be maintained in four cycles. The committee is not able to consult the district, the district having no standing under the water act.*
> *Clerk of the committee.*

The third line is in the ordinance. It was not in the ordinance in April, when she wrote the same sentence into a notice that did not require it because a clerk does her best inside a form with one line for it, and it is in the ordinance now, and **somebody in that building had to go and find out whether it was, and nobody has written down who.**

He read it standing up at about ten past ten on the Monday morning with two other men who had come to look at it because it was on a board, and he did not say anything, and one of the two men said *that's them lot again* and the other one said nothing at all.

**There was a room above a shop in Bellweather with nine men in it at four o'clock that afternoon and there is no form on which a man with a notebook may be in that room, and the nine are not the district and do not know that they are not the district and are not to blame for it, and one of them is a chandler, and two of the other eight are landlords, and every one of them draws more than a family of five on that main and would have been on that main in 1911.**

---

**Tuesday the second, about ten in the morning, at the pump house, with two watches and a notebook and a good folding rule, and he wrote nothing at all for about four minutes because he was waiting for a number to move.**

It did not move. He had the pair on the Quarter hill in front of him, four hundred yards apart, in daylight, and the difference was what it had been on the fourth of August's predecessor, the ninth of June, and he read it twice with a watch in each hand and then a third time on the Friday morning and again on the Saturday, and it did not move on any of them.

**Fifty seconds a night is the step, and the step was at the fourth draw, and there were four draws, and on Monday the first of the eighth a committee of nine men voted that there would be two, and the branch has taken its beat off the even teeth since Tuesday morning instead of the odd ones, and the step was on the odd ones.**

He did not say it that way to anybody. He wrote it that way in a book, with the sentence under it that the other half of it is a thing he cannot do:

> *The branch takes its beat off one set of teeth and with four draws a night it was the odd ones, and the step was on the odd ones. With two it is the even ones, and the step is not there.*
> *I have the beat. I have not got the division and I did not have it in June and I have not got it now, and I have not looked for it this week and I am not going to, because a man who has just been shown that his number was beaten by a vote has got exactly one week of not looking for things.*

**Then he did the arithmetic, and it took him the rest of the morning, and it is the only arithmetic in this business that a man can do with a pencil, so it is set out whole.**

> *The nine of June, taken in daylight with two watches, in the order they are connected, slow of the district reference:*
> *1 Weir Two pump house, station clock — 3 min 50 s*
> *2 Pumping ward, canal road, outer room — 13 min 10 s*
> *3 Quarter hill, north public clock — 24 min 10 s*
> *4 Quarter hill, south public clock — 1 h 05 min 00 s*
> *5 Weir Lane, three tenement clocks — 1 h 27 min 30 s*
> *6 Weir One gate, gate clock — 1 h 45 min 10 s*
> *Fifty seconds a night. The last night of the four-draw week was the night of the thirty-first of the seventh. From the ninth of the sixth to the thirty-first of the seventh, counting the night of the ninth, is fifty-three nights. Fifty-three times fifty seconds is two thousand six hundred and fifty seconds, which is forty-four minutes and ten seconds.*
> *Therefore, on the morning of the first of the eighth, and on every morning since, because it does not step:*
> *1 — 48 min 00 s*
> *2 — 57 min 20 s*
> *3 — 1 h 08 min 20 s*
> *4 — 1 h 49 min 10 s*
> *5 — 2 h 11 min 40 s*
> *6 — 2 h 29 min 20 s*
> *And the five differences between them are 9 min 20 s, 11 min 00 s, 40 min 50 s, 22 min 30 s and 17 min 40 s, and those are the same five figures, the same five figures to the second, that they were on the ninth of the sixth, and I have checked them twice and I have had them in front of me and I have written out the whole of it on the board in chalk where a man of sixty-eight can read it.*

---

**And here is the thing he did not expect, and it is the finding, and it took him until about two in the afternoon on the Tuesday to arrive at it standing at the board.**

He had expected two things and both were wrong.

**He expected the line to come back.** A man who has watched a line walk away from a reference for ten weeks, and who has then seen the thing that is doing it stop, expects a clock to go back. It does not go back. **A step is a displacement and it is permanent and there is nothing in this city that has ever put a machine back, and the far end of the Weir Two branch is now two hours and twenty-nine minutes and twenty seconds out of the district reference and it is going to be that figure on the last day of August and on the last day of the year, and it is a gate, and nineteen households draw on it.**

**And he expected the shape to change, and the shape did not change at all.** Five differences, identical, to the second, seven weeks apart. There is no kink in it and no lump and no place where it doubles. It is exactly the same shape it was on the ninth of June, standing still.

Which means the finding is not about the six clocks. It is this:

> *On the ninth of June, what was on that wall was a **rate.** Fifty seconds a night, and a rate is a thing nine men with a property qualification can vote on, and I have spent ten weeks of my life and about six months of my thinking establishing that.*
> *On the fourth of August, what is on that wall is a **figure.** Two hours and twenty-nine minutes and twenty seconds at a water gate. And a figure is not a thing anybody in this city can vote on, and there is no form for it, and nobody can be asked about it, and **the committee that took the step out of it has just proved, lawfully, on a Monday, in about eleven minutes, that the whole of what is wrong with this district is a matter of committee procedure, and that if the procedure went the other way tomorrow the district would be an hour further out and nobody anywhere would be able to point at the moment it happened.**

And then the entry, and it is four lines and it is the hardest thing he wrote that month, and he wrote it in the shop on the Tuesday evening.

> *And here is what I cannot get round, and I have been at it since two o'clock.*
> *On the ninth of June I projected the pair on the hill for the first of August. Fifty-three nights at fifty seconds added to forty minutes and fifty seconds is **one hour twenty-five minutes exactly.** I wrote it in the ledger in June with the arithmetic under it and it was right in every part.*
> *The pair were forty minutes and fifty seconds apart on the fourth of August, and on the first, and on the second.*
> *I was out by forty-four minutes and ten seconds, and I was out by exactly the amount that nine men removed from a schedule on a Monday afternoon, and **there is not a fault in that anywhere and there is nobody to argue with.***
> *A district's error is a thing a committee can vote on. And so is mine. And the only person in this city who has said that sentence out loud before the eleventh of the seventh month is a man of twenty in a room over a shop, about a form, and he did not mean it to be about me.*
> *I do not think this is a scandal. I have gone at that for two days and this is the whole of why: a district that cannot be asked cannot object to a thing nobody did, and I have spent five months proving it, and a man who proves it and then gets voted at is not a martyr. He is a man who found out where the edge of the thing he was standing on was, and it moved the same week.*

---

**Bevin Sarr read the new set of figures standing, on the Tuesday afternoon, and then she picked up the chalk, and what she said took about four seconds and it is the sentence the whole of this volume turns on.**

"Chalk the high," she said. "There is a man of sixty-eight on this side who cannot read at the height you have written it."

He chalked it high. And then he wrote one more line above the six, in the same chalk, in the same hand, and it is the only line on that board that is not a figure.

> ***RATE. 50 s a night.*** ***Figure. 2 h 29 min 20 s at the gate. A rate is a thing a committee can vote on.***

"That is the best thing that has been on this wall in eleven years," said Bevin Sarr, "and I am not going to say any more about it, and I want it understood that I am not saying no more about it because I have run out, and I am going to be asked in about a month by somebody in a good coat what a rate is, and I am going to tell them it is a number, and that is the last time anybody in this district will be able to stop the answer."

---

**The board in the ward's outer room, on the Wednesday and the Thursday, had four other things on it, and they are on it because a man who has a wall will use it, and Bevin Sarr does not stop him.**

> *The standing protection, the Assembly's, the fourth of the sixth: no shift in this district certified in the maintenance-deferred class until the ledger and annex are published and received. **Worth about two months now. The audit behind it takes about fourteen. The report is due at the ninth month's sitting and is not in and is not promised, and the committee has not undertaken to renew it and cannot.***
> *The maintenance schedule, item ten: eleven inspections a year for three years at twelve pounds nine shillings and fourpence, being four hundred and eleven pounds eight shillings, first inspection in programme year one. **There is no first inspection in it yet and it is August.***
> *The 1931 sheet in the works' file: a pressure interlock on a station of that class, with its wiring, estimated at thirty-one pounds eight shillings. **Never fitted.** The line against it is in pencil in another hand and reads: pr. intlk. no — trips main prot. Do not fit. Sixty-one years, and the man was right, and nobody has replaced him.*
> *And the front's daytime draw, two cycles, to the thirty-first, posted, lawful, and the reason for it is in the third line of a nine-line notice and the third line is a sentence about a district that cannot be consulted.*

---

**And Ivo Pell came on the Tuesday the third, because he said he would come on a Tuesday, and he stood in the outer room of the pumping ward for about forty minutes and did not sit down.**

He read the six figures and the line about a rate. He read it the way a man reads a figure off a return, standing up, from about four feet, which he does not say he can do.

"Two cycles," he said. "So the beat has moved to the other set of teeth."

"Yes."

"And the step was on the set of teeth the beat was not on."

"Apparently."

"**Then it is not a fault and it is not a ramp and it never was, and you have had it right since June and wrong since June, and both of those are the same sentence.**" He put his hand flat on the glass of the toll case, on the ledge, not on the book, which is the nearest a man may get to a public ledger without asking. "And now nobody can vote it back up except the same nine men, and they will be able to vote it back up in October, and they will have a reason in October, because October is a month."

"Yes," said Orrin Vale.

"So say the rest of it, because I am not going to be here in October and you will be, and you will need to have said it before then." Pell turned round. "**When there is a party in this district — and there will be one, and it will not be a warden's party and it will not be yours and it will not be nine men with a chandler in it — it will be somebody's. It will be a body, and a body decides what it wants, and a body can want the water back at four o'clock, and it will be right about the water and it will be right about the hours, and it will be *right*, Mr Vale, and there will be nothing in that room to argue with.**

"That is the thing you are building towards and you do not know that you are building towards it, because a thing that is being built towards is not visible from inside the thing that is being built. **And I have been the right man in a building nobody elects for twelve years, and the only reason I say that out loud to you and not to a room is that a room would put it in a minute.**"

"You could be in the room," said Orrin Vale.

"I have four certificates to countersign this month and two of them are in the Sloughs and one of them is a bus," said Ivo Pell. "Give me the book."

He read the shop book off the shelf in the outer room, with his hands behind his back, and gave it back, and said, "If you write down that I came you have had the day wrong, because I did not come, I was on a platform and a man in a good coat was standing next to it," and went out to Northglass.

---

**And on the Thursday the fourth, at about eleven in the morning, Councilor Imani Rook came to the pumping ward with a chair.**

She had it under her arm. There is one chair in that room and it is by the ledger on the ledge and it has a book stand on the back of it, and she came up the two steps, set her own chair down about four feet from the board, and would not let Marta Dey move it, and then she read the board standing up, from the beginning, twice, which took her about nine minutes and which nobody interrupted.

She is forty. She has been chair of the Assembly's public-health committee for three years. She filed the toll-ledger demand herself in the sixth month on arithmetic and not out of feeling, and she said in that room that she had been shown arithmetic and never anything else and did not intend to start now, and she was out-argued in the first month of this year and said so herself and has never once pretended otherwise.

**She is in that room once. She is right, and she is not thanked, and she is not softened, and she is not going to be argued with, and she is not a woman who was shown a number and felt something about a man in a column.**

"Right," she said, when she had read it twice. "I have two things and the first one is against my own motion in June and I am going to say it in this order on purpose.

"On the fourth of the sixth I moved a standing protection for this district, and the protection was that no shift in it should be certified in the maintenance-deferred class until the guild's ledger and annex were published and received. It was temporary. It expires at the first sitting of this committee after the report, and not before, and we have not undertaken to renew it and I cannot promise that we will.

"**And the class was abolished on the first of July, by a standing order of a guild, and I did not notice the date.** I have been chair of this committee for three years. The protection is a protection of a *certificate*, and there is no class left to certify anything in, and what it does not protect — what it has not protected since the day it was carried — is six men whose names are in the district works' own maintenance schedule in four-inch capitals with *licensed: no* against two of them, and who are not in a certified shift, and never were, and were not on the thirtieth of May when I moved it either.

"That is the finding and it is mine and it is four months late and I would like it minuted that I said it before I asked for anything, which is the only procedure in this building I have ever been sure of."

Nobody thanked her. She had not asked to be thanked and she had said so in June and she did not say so again.

"Second thing." She took a folded sheet out of her bag. "I want the six figures on that board entered in the minutes of this committee, as an observed condition of a civic hourline, with the date, and **published with the minutes.** Not adopted. Not proposed. Observed, dated, and published.

"And I will tell you the reason and it is not a largeness. **A minute that is not published is a minute that a clerk can lose.** I have been chair for three years and I have found two of mine that were not, and one of them was the more important one, and I am not going to tell you which and I am not going to pretend to you that I know what I am doing. I am putting a number about a ward into a published minute because the number is true and the ward has no other door, and if somebody in that building thinks a number in a set of minutes whose source is a chalkboard is a fabrication then he is entitled to write to me and he will get a straight answer and I will say where it came from, which is a man in a shop on a hill with no licence, and I will give his name, and I have asked him whether I may."

"You have not asked me," said Orrin Vale.

"You are not going to object," said Councilor Rook, "and if you are going to object, do it now, in this room, and not in a letter to me, because I have four of those already this year and I have answered all of them and none of them came from a man who had ever been to this district."

---

**And that was the last hour of the committee business, and she went, and at about half past six that evening a clockmaker from the Clockwork Quarter did four and a half hours of ordinary paid work in a shop on Verge Lane and went to bed at ten and slept nine hours.**

It was a bracket clock off a jeweller's wall in the Quarter, and it was eleven minutes a day slow and had a cracked backplate and a worn worm, and the jeweller was told the price before the work and the price was **one pound eighteen shillings**, being four and a half hours at the hall's out-of-shop rate and nineteen shillings of material, and it was done in a day, and it was the fourth paid job that month, and it is not in any of the four places a district keeps anything.

> *Fourth of the eighth. Tuesday of the third: a woman of forty carried a chair up two steps into a pumping ward and would not let anybody move it, and read a wall twice, and found out that the standing protection she won for this district in June protects a certificate in a class that had been abolished five weeks before she noticed, and said so before she asked for anything.*
> *Tuesday of the second: the step stopped on a Monday's vote and the far end of that branch is two hours and twenty-nine minutes and twenty seconds out and it is a gate and it is going to be that figure for ever.*
> *I have been out by forty-four minutes and ten seconds on the only number of mine that was ever going to be true in advance, and the thing that beat it was a vote, and a vote is what I spent five months proving is the whole of the difficulty, and I would like it noticed that a man who proves a thing and is then proved at is not the same as a man who is wrong, and that I have not been able to explain the difference to anybody including myself, and that the only person who would have explained it to me is on a train to Northglass.*
> *And I did four and a half hours of somebody else's clock for one pound eighteen shillings and went to bed at ten and slept nine hours, and I have not slept nine hours since the first of the fifth, and I am putting that in the book because a grey book is worth nothing in any room that is not the one it is in, and this room is mine, and I am the only person who will ever read this line, and I have decided that is allowed.*
