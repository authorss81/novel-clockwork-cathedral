## 1. The ending as delivered

On Monday the ninth of the first month a room over a chandler's shop in Bellweather, with a stove in it and a bell that has not worked since 1949 and a shelf of minute books in which the most recent volume is 1948, gave this district a right over what may be asked of the clocks of a street of twenty-two houses off Weir Lane. **The right is a right to refuse. It is held by the Weirs tenant council, which is a body the district already had, on terms the district can be held to, which means the council may be refused by the same people it may refuse, and there is nowhere in this city or any other to which either refusal may be carried.** Five clauses. The chair is a woman of sixty-one who signs for a gate, who does not want the chair, and who took it because *a body that cannot be refused is not a body, it is a room, and I have been in this district for nineteen years and I would like there to be one thing in it that can be.* A warden of forty-four is on it and does not chair it. A landlord of nine blocks is not on it and gave the reason in one sentence before anybody asked. The council's organiser of thirty-eight is not a member and has never been one, because if she is a member she is inside the decision. **A clockmaker of thirty-two is not on it and did not ask, and the reason is one sentence: *a person who can be refused is the whole of the instrument, and I am the man who cannot be refused, and that is not a gift and it is the reason nobody is going to help me, and it is also the reason I am not on that body and it is the only thing about this week I am proud of.***

The fourth clause is the one that makes it a right and not a permit, and nobody argued about it: **the council may not put a question to the row either; the row gets the same seven days' notice a schedule gets; the row may refuse the council in the same words in the same book; and there is no office, court, committee, form or appeal to which either refusal may be carried, and both of them stand.** A works' clerk of about fifty said *licence*, then *permit*, then *certainty instrument*, then asked whether there is a Council of Hours, and was corrected four times, and put his pencil down and did not pick it up again, and the chapter says **the words are not forbidden, they are only not true, and a district that bans a word has stopped being able to say what it is frightened of.**

In the pumping ward's outer room there are **two books on one ledge** — the Office's toll ledger in a glass case with a key on a string, carrying MINUTES and DISTRICT for forty-three years and NAME and DURATION since the third of June, with `VANCE, P.` in the first row of the new columns and **nothing at all in the duration column beside it** — and beside the case and not in it, **the row book**, about a hundred and forty pages, bound in sacking, in a hand nobody at the Office can read, with the street's figures in it and the Office's figure nowhere. At the head of the row book are five lines in the same shape as the five lines on the inside cover of the ledger a foot away, about a column with nothing in it. **Two offices have never heard of each other and neither book cites the other, and the shape is the only part of a rule anybody in a bad situation can copy.** A chair, a pencil and a jar of ink are on that ledge and nobody in this city is asked to pay anything to sit down and read either book.

A woman of fifty-one of Bellweather Road, who has kept a box of bills for nineteen years, bought a printed copy of four hundred and eleven lines at a penny on the Wednesday the fourth of January, and told nine people in a room with one window in it that she is her niece's responsible adult and **that there is no form in this city on which responsible adult is a thing anybody can be, and she has been one for four years by accident and is going to go on being one by accident, and the accident is a card in a box of bills.** The girl is thirteen. **She has not been asked a question since the twenty-first of the June and she is not going to be asked one by anybody**, and that is not a promise, it is the answer the keeper of the Archive gives: *nobody in this building is entitled to, and the price of a printed line is a penny and she is thirteen and she may buy one the same as anybody and nobody in this building has any business knowing whether she does.*

**And the last line of Volume 02 is a letter of four lines, on the Cathedral's own paper, dated the eleventh of the first month, signed by Anja Voss, Director of the Cathedral, forty-nine, who was named once in this district on a signature in the fifth month of the first year and has taken no scene since.** It offers him the rooms under the building that are sealed, and the records in them, and the unit on the floor below the north gallery that is not described in any paper she has signed, and the machine itself — any one, or all three, on a day of his choosing, with no condition and no form and nothing asked of him and nothing asked of him again. It names the difference itself: *a room is a form and a form is a way of asking without the rule, and this is a door, and I cannot make anybody walk through it and neither can anybody in this building, and that is the whole of the difference between a door and a permit and it took me four years to be able to write that sentence down.* **He did not answer it. The last line of the volume is: *If you would come on a Tuesday I will have the north door open.*** The Continuity Minister is not named in Volume 02 and his door is not opened.

## 6. The fixed calendar, and the one thing in it that does not add up

**The first month is thirty days, 1 Sun to 30 Mon. The second month is twenty-eight days, 1 Tue to 28 Mon. The third month is thirty-one days, 1 Wed to 31 Fri. The fourth month is thirty days, 1 Sat to 30 Sun. The fifth month is thirty-one days, 1 Mon to 31 Wed. The sixth month is thirty days, 1 Thu to 30 Fri. The twelfth month is thirty-one days, 1 Thu to 31 Sat, which is where Volume 02 ended.**

**Do not print a year length on the page and do not derive an interval from one.** The twelve month lengths sum to three hundred and sixty-three and some prompts assert three hundred and thirty-three, which is true of nothing in this book; **every checkable day count in the series is a count on the month map** — the canonical 221, the 251 and 670, Chapter 91's 150 and 221, every ramp figure in the last twenty chapters of Volume 02.

**The one thing that does not add up at the turn of the year, and it is a one-day slip, and it is not to be fixed in prose.** The second month is given as twenty-eight days beginning Tuesday the 1st, which makes the first of the third month a Tuesday; the map and the canon both require **Sunday the nineteenth of the third month** for the expiry of a licence, which requires the first of the third month to be a Wednesday. **A Volume 03 batch may not print any interval that crosses from the second month into the third month**, and must take the weekday of any stated anchor date from the map's own weekday column rather than from a month length. The nineteenth of the third month is a Sunday and the licensing counter takes lodgements on Sundays, and a porter unlocks it, and a clerk is behind it, and nobody else is in the building.

**No Assembly, committee, guild counter, water committee, market or mutual sits on a Sunday in Morrowfen.** A chapter may run through a Sunday; a meeting may not, and a chapter that runs through one may not put a counter, a board, a rail, a review or a pitch on it. A committee may sit on a Saturday and does about twice a year.

## 7. The four figures Volume 03's first batch must not re-derive, and may re-use

## 9. The two conflicts the volume-close phase was asked to decide

**Both are decided here, in writing, and neither was fixed in prose, and no closed chapter was edited.**

**One — `outline/volume-02.md` §Escalation step four dates the guild's report into the tenth month; the page puts its receipt on Monday the twelfth of the ninth and the lapsing of the standing protection on Tuesday the thirteenth of the ninth.** Three consecutive prompts forbade moving it and four batches carried it. **DECIDED: the page governs, without exception and without a repair.** The report was received on the twelfth of the ninth, the protection lapsed on the thirteenth, nobody extended it, and the only thing that stopped was a piece of paper that had been protecting nothing for eleven weeks. `outline/volume-02.md` is a routing document and its step-four sentence is a summary of a scene; the four-line summary is wrong and the scenes are right. **A Volume 03 writer may say *the report was received on the twelfth of the ninth and the protection lapsed on the thirteenth and was not extended* in those terms, once, and may not re-open the date, and may not say that the report was late.** The outline file is not edited by this phase and the sentence stands as a known summary error, because a volume outline is a plan and a plan that is corrected after the prose is a different kind of document from a chapter.

**Two — `chapter-0078.md` says four times, including in a closing ledger, that six hundred tell-tales at eleven shillings and fourpence is thirty-four pounds. Six hundred times 136 pence is 81,600 pence, which is £340.** The same figure is carried in `bible/terminology.md`, in `state/batch-summaries.md` and in `state/current.md`, and the batch 0003 review computed it wrongly in the same way and certified the result. **DECIDED: the page is wrong, the true figure is £340, and the correction is not made, for three reasons that are the house's own.** A closed chapter is not edited by a later phase. A figure that is true on the page and false in the world is a live inconsistency, and the house rule is that a writer who finds one logs it and does not edit across a batch boundary. And **Chapter 97 already refused the multiplication in the only terms that matter: the paragraph names the two quantities and declines to multiply them, and says why on the page.** **Therefore: the volume's guardrail is that the figure does not appear in Volume 03 at all.** `bible/terminology.md` is not edited by this phase and its entry is known-wrong; **a Volume 03 writer who needs the six hundred brackets uses £1,890 for the six hundred brackets and eleven shillings and fourpence for one pointer, in different sentences, and never multiplies them, and the narration may not notice that he did not.** A maintainer may correct the bible and the four state files in one pass; that is a different kind of job from writing a hundred and ten chapters.

**Neither decision changes a single fact on a page, and both are recorded in `state/open-threads.md` and `state/continuity.md` §Volume 02 closed.**
