# Chapter 81 — The Only Copy in the District

On Saturday the fifteenth of the tenth month a committee of nine men with a property qualification sat in a room above a chandler's shop in Bellweather and did the only business it had, which was the winter rotation, and it could not produce the winter rotation, and what it did instead was order a copy of it at fourpence a page, made in four hands, with the fifteen minutes' disagreement between the two prints written on the face of it; and the reason it could not produce it is that a pumping district is not a body, a document cannot be a party to anything, and a woman of forty-four put the only copy in a box in a store on the first of the ninth and filled in the store's own form in her own hand, and nobody has been in charge of it since, and the one name attached to the only copy of the document that governs the water of this district is the name of the person who has left.

---

**Saturday the fifteenth, ten in the morning, in a room above a shop, and it is a Saturday, and a committee may do that about twice a year.**

There were seven of the nine. One is in hospital and one is at sea and neither sent anything, because **a member of the Water Users' Committee who is not present at a sitting has not been asked anything and there is no form on which a member of this committee may be polled.** The chair is **Josiah Meak, 61**, a builder, six years on it, and he draws more than a family of five on that main and has three houses on it and eleven on it, and he is not a villain and is right about the water.

**Nadia Erridge is the clerk** and she put the notice up herself on the ward board on Saturday the eighth with seven days' notice, and the business was named on it, and the date on it was the fifteenth, and the notice is a lawful notice and the seven days is a lawful seven days.

The second item on the notice was the ordinary business and took nine minutes. The third item was the winter rotation and it took the rest of the morning.

"Clerk."

"Mr Meak."

"Where is it."

That is the whole of the exchange and it took about four seconds and four people heard it.

> *I have the shelf copy in the store at the pumping ward, Mr Meak. It went in on the first of the ninth in the first box, on its edge, against the wall, and the store's form was filled in by hand and the line on the form that says where a thing is to be kept says the store.*
> *I cannot produce it. **I cannot produce it because a pumping district is not a body, the copy is not mine, the post is vacant, and there is no person in this city who is in charge of it.** I have asked. I was told on the nineteenth of the fifth that I would get four words and a signature and I got four words and a signature and they were that it was received in this district.*

Meak did not look up.

"Open the store," he said.

"There is a key and it is on the board in the pump house and about nine men have a key to that pump house and none of them has any business with a box in the store and the store's form is the only authority anybody in this district has ever been able to point at for where that box is. **I could go and get it. It would take me until about eleven and I would be doing it as a person and not as a clerk, and there is a difference and I know exactly what it is because I wrote it on a notice in April and the ordinance had no line for it and I put it in anyway.**"

"Go and get it," said the chandler, who is a member and has been a member for eleven years.

"I have four minutes' notice of the ninth item," said Meak. "**Clerk, put it at the foot of the minutes in these words: that the committee was unable to produce the winter rotation, the only copy being held in a store, and that the copy is not produced.**"

"That is not a thing minutes do."

"**It is the only thing minutes do in this building and you have been in it four years.**"

---

**And so the copy, and it is worth setting out in full because it is the first document this district has produced all year that is about itself.**

It was made in a room over a chandler's shop on the Saturday afternoon and on the Monday morning, and it cost **fourpence a page**, and there were four pages, and **one shilling and fourpence** was paid out of a tin, and it was made in four hands because there are four people in this district who will do it.

**Bevin Sarr**, ward clerk, canal front, thirty-three. **Ivy Pike**, who signs for the Weir One gate and has kept a book of her own for eleven years and has never shown it to anybody. **Ambrose**, a works clerk of about thirty with eleven years at a counter. And **Orrin Vale**, who has two benches on a lease to Michaelmas, no licence, and a rateable value in the Clockwork Quarter, and who is not a ratepayer of this district and could not stand for it at a meeting even if the meeting would hear him.

**There are four copies of the winter rotation in this city and there are four hands, and that is not a coincidence. The other three are the works' print, the copy at the Water Users' Committee's own counter, and the one at the register office that came with the ordinance in 1883 and has not been opened since about 1948 because the drawer sticks.** He had been at all three of the other rooms. He had been in them since the twelfth of the seventh month.

And on the face of the fourth copy, in Bevin Sarr's upright hand, is the sentence that makes it a document and not a transcript:

> **THE WINTER ROTATION, Lower Weirs pumping district.**
> Agreed 1911. Printed 1919. Reprinted eleven times. A fourth window added 1951, typewritten, in a different face. No minute of the addition and no name.
> Two prints of this sheet in this city disagree by fifteen minutes in the **fourth** window and agree in the first, the second and the third.
> **The copy made at the pumping ward and the copy made at the works' counter disagree by fifteen minutes, and the difference is in the fourth window, and the fourth window is the one that was typed on.**
> *The windows are 02.00, 03.00, 04.00 and 05.00 in the district's copy. The works' print says the fourth is at 03.45. Fifteen minutes. **The branch followed the print, on the fourteenth of the seventh, and the branch is the only one of the three things in this city that has ever answered the question.** The other two are two sheets of paper and a bucket, and we do not know which one the machine is following tonight, and a watch and a bucket at a standpipe are the only arbitrators this city has.*
> Cost of this copy: 1s 4d, fourpence a page, four pages. Paid out of a tin at a room over a chandler's shop.
> Made in four hands because there are four people in this district who will do it. **This copy is not the winter rotation. A copy made at fourpence a page is not the sheet.**

And then, because the committee had sent for it and the committee is a body, there is a fifth hand on it, and it is the clerk's, and it is a line at the foot:

> *Received at the counter of this committee, the seventeenth day of the tenth month. **N. Erridge, clerk.** Entered: that this is a copy made in four hands and not the sheet, and that the sheet was not produced and could not be produced, and that the committee has not directed that any person be asked what the difference of fifteen minutes is.*

Nobody in that room thanked anybody and nobody said the word *rotation* in the way the word is said when a person is making a speech.

---

**And this is the discovery, and it is not the schedule, and the schedule is on three boards in nine places.**

On the Monday morning, at about eleven, he was in the store at the pumping ward with a lamp and Bevin Sarr, and the box is a wooden box about two feet by one, and the copy of the winter rotation is against the wall on its edge with the day book and the gauge sheets, and it is eleven sheets of a printed form stapled at the corner, and the top sheet is the sheet.

**The store's form is on the lid.** It is a printed form with a line for a thing and a line for a date and a line for where it is to be kept, and the two first lines are filled in in a hand that he knows, and the third line is filled in in the same hand, and the third line says THE STORE, in capitals, and the capitals have been done by a person who was not writing a form for anybody.

> *Winter rotation, standing, Lower Weirs. One sheet, eleven copies stapled. **S. Kade, warden. 1st of the ninth.***
> *WHERE IT IS TO BE KEPT — **THE STORE***

That is the whole of the discovery and it took him about four minutes and Bevin Sarr stood at the door and did not come in, because it is not her box.

**The only name attached to the only copy of the document that governs the water of a hundred and forty thousand people in this district is the name of a woman who put it there herself, on the day she gave up the post, in a form filled in by hand, and the form says a store and not a shelf, and a store is a room and a shelf is a piece of furniture and the difference between them is the whole of the difference between a document and an institution.**

"You could take it out," said Bevin Sarr, from the door.

"I could."

"You could put it in your coat and go up to the works' counter this afternoon and leave it there and the works would receipt it."

"I could."

"**And then there would be a receipt, and a receipt is a thing with a date on it, and you have spent four months of this year finding out that the only instrument anybody in this district has is a piece of paper with a date on it.**"

"Yes," he said. "And the receipt would say that it was received at the works' counter, and it would be received in about four days, and the sheet would be in a drawer in a building four miles from here with a lock on it and no person in charge of it, and the next time somebody needs to know whether the fourth window is at a quarter to four or at four they will have to write to a works' office, and the works' office will write to another office, and in about four years the answer will come back on a form and the form will be right and the fifteen minutes will still be in it."

He put his hand flat on the lid of the box and then took it off again, which is a thing he does about once a year.

"**I am not going to do that, and I want it written down that I did not do it and why, and the why is not noble. If the only copy of the governing sheet of a district is in a lock-up, then a person cannot ask to see it, and if a person cannot ask to see it then a person cannot object to it, and I have spent this entire year finding out that a district can stop a works and cannot stop a schedule. The lock-up is not the reason. The lock-up is just where the schedule happens to be. If I take it out of the lock-up I have made a copy of a bad habit into a worse one, because in about four years there will be a copy of the winter rotation in four places and no name on any of them and no person in charge of any of them, and the one place I could still get into with a lamp is a room with a lock on it and a form on the lid.**"

---

**And on the Monday the seventeenth, the Tuesday the eighteenth and the Wednesday the nineteenth of the tenth month he went and asked three people the only question anybody in this city can be asked about a sheet of paper dated 1911, and he did not go to the Water Users' Committee, and here is the whole of what he got: the hall on the Tuesday, the works on the Wednesday morning, and the register office on the Wednesday at twenty past twelve.**

> **The Clockwork Quarter hall, the licensing floor, about eleven in the morning on the Tuesday.** The clerk of the counter is a woman of about twenty-six who does not raise her voice and has not looked up from the book for a moment in four years.
> "The certification clause was made by the Assembly in 1883 and it is not ours. The maintenance-deferred class was created by this hall in 1931 and abolished by a standing order of this hall on the first of July and the standing order is minuted and you may read the minute, and the form is a form of this hall's and the form is not in this hall's hands and has not been in this hall's hands since about 1948. **The hall does not make the forms.** Nobody has ever asked us and I have said it four times this year to four different men and twice to a woman."
> "Who does?"
> "That is the whole of the answer and you have had it."

> **The district works, the counter in the yard on the canal road, the Wednesday morning.** Ambrose, in about ninety seconds, standing up, because he is a man who says things to rooms and not to people.
> "**We print it. We have printed it since 1919 and we reprinted it eleven times and the last reprint was in 1946 and we did the fourth window in 1951 on a typewriter, and I know it was a typewriter because the office did not have one and it came up from the works office on a hand truck.** The fifteen minutes is ours and it is in the fourth window, which is the one we typed on, and **I can tell you which one the machine follows: it is ours, and it followed ours on the fourteenth of the seventh with a valve on Weir Lane and a standpipe at the second court, and I have never in nineteen years told anybody that and I have just told two men in my own yard.**"

> **The register office, the third floor, the same Wednesday, about twenty past twelve, and the man there is about fifty and has been in the room nine years.**
> "The copy that came with the ordinance in 1883 is in the bottom drawer of the third press and the drawer sticks and it has stuck since about 1948 and the key is in the drawer with it. **Nobody has asked for it in nine years. You are the fifth person to ask me and four of them were asking for something else.**"

**So the answer is the hall's, and it is the answer to a question nobody in this city can be asked, and it is two words long, and it is true: the hall does not make the forms.** Nobody in Morrowfen can be asked who typed a fourth window onto a printed sheet in 1951, because the typing was done on a hand truck in a works office and there is no form on which a person records that they have typed a window onto a district's governing water document, and because the document has no owner to ask, and because the person who owns it is a room.

He had been trying to find that out since the twelfth of the seventh month. He had been to eleven rooms.

**And on the Wednesday evening he wrote it down in the grey book, and what he wrote was not a question and was not a resolution, and it is the only decision there is in it:**

> *Wednesday the nineteenth of the tenth. I have stopped.*
> *I have stopped trying to find out who typed the fourth window on in 1951 and I have written here that I have stopped and I have written down the reason so that I cannot go back to it in a fortnight with a better version of it.*
> *The reason is that the sheet is now the only copy in this district. It is not a document anybody holds; it is a document a room holds. If I find out who typed it, I learn a name, and the name goes in a book in a shop, and in about four years a man in a good coat will ask me for the name and I will not have it, because a name is not the sheet. **A copy made at fourpence a page is not the sheet, and neither is a name.** The sheet is eleven sheets stapled at the corner, on its edge, against a wall, in a box in a store, and there is a form on the lid and the form has a woman's name on it and the woman's name is the only name in this district that is attached to anything, and she is on a bus going nowhere in particular because she arranged in June to have nowhere to be on the Thursday, and I am not going to go and ask her what a window in 1951 was for, because **she has said four times in four weeks that she has been the person who answers and has never once been the person who is asked, and I am not going to be the man who makes her the fifth.***
> *So the sheet stays in the store. And the copy is on the counter of this committee with my committee's clerk's name at the foot of it and fifteen minutes written on the face of it. And a question that a district cannot be asked about has been asked of four printed sheets in four rooms, and the four rooms said four different true things, and not one of them said the word.*

> *Fifteenth to the nineteenth of the tenth. A committee of nine men with a property qualification sat on a Saturday, which it may do about twice a year, and could not produce the winter rotation, and had the fact minuted in the only words minutes in that building have for it.*
> *The rotation is a printed sheet agreed in 1911, printed in 1919, reprinted eleven times, with a fourth window typed on in 1951 on a hand truck, with no minute and no name. **It is a worse schedule than the standing one. It is lawful. It is correct. It is the reason the front is short all winter and has been since 1911.** The third line of the notice says the district cannot be consulted, and the third line of the notice is the reason the sheet is in a box and the reason the copy is a copy and the reason fifteen minutes is written on the face of it in four hands.*
> *One shilling and fourpence, fourpence a page, four pages, out of a tin in a room over a chandler's shop. Four hands and there are four people in this district who will do it.*
> *I have stopped looking for the name. **The only name attached to the only copy is on the lid of the box, in her hand, in capitals, and the capitals are a woman telling a form that a shelf is not a store.** And a sheet nobody can see is a sheet nobody can object to, and that has been true of this district since 1911, and it is true of it tonight, and it is the same size of thing as four hundred men at a gate and it is the only one of the two that is real.*
> *Nobody has been thanked. Not the committee, not the chandler, not the clerk, not the four hands, and not the woman on the bus.*
