## 1. The ending as delivered

On the morning of the eighth of the sixth month a clockmaker in Verge Lane has a lease on two benches to Michaelmas, a sign that has said **HOROLOGER. REPAIRS TO ANY DESCRIPTION.** in the same type for eleven years, and no licence. He can clean a barrel, bush a wheel and beat a clock to a rate; he cannot certify a civic machine, and **nobody shortened his clock** — his licence runs to the nineteenth of the third month next year, and a master moved nine months and a bit ahead of his own expiry, on his own signature, lawfully, in four true lines, at a counter, in front of thirty people. What is on the counter is a piece of paper with his own name under a number he had no standing to sign.

His repair is one anybody can check. About forty people stood in a works' yard and watched a cast-iron conduit bracket come out of a canal crossing **whole**, with a quarter-inch ring of iron round the two fixings that had been holding, and the number went on a board in chalk at eye height against a datum cut in the coping in 1911, and **the division is written longhand on the stone.** Thirty feet of conduit came out of the span in daylight, and **four people in this city have a piece of it.** It had to be daylight because three weeks earlier a casting of a pattern not made in thirty years let go at eleven at night in a flood, and he got within about eleven minutes of clamping it privately, at night, unwatched, and **stopped, and telephoned a pump house at ten past three and asked for a room**, and said out loud in the middle of that floor that he did not know, and that in four months the fixing would take the clamp and the conduit and the line with it, and there would be no plate and no name and no number and no room. He still has a **bearing block off a dead tram bogie** and **a plate with a boy's name on the back of it** from the flood of the first month, when nine men lost an hour of a Sunday morning and his right hand lost two days.

A district with a **protected maintenance line**, won in eleven minutes on a show of hands: no shift certified in the maintenance-deferred class until the guild's eighteen-year labour ledger and its annex are published and received. A **public ledger** in a glass case in the pumping ward, key on a string, a chair and pencil and ink on the ledge, **two sets of columns, one entry, and nothing at all in the column beside it.** A **four-paragraph finding in chalk** outside a pump house, in which **the review finds against the warden in respect of the flood and expressly not in respect of employment, employment not being a ground before it**, her own gauge sheet records standing water on eleven of thirty-six days, and the reserve alarm was inhibited eleven weeks with no report made and no form requiring one. The warden holds her office. The process stands. No person is thanked. And a **chandler** who chairs a committee that can be run without would like it minuted that he is a chandler and this is not his room in any sense that matters, because in about four years somebody will tell a council that the Weirs hold reviews and will be asked what the review is *for.*

A **master who is a repairer under public oversight and did not confess**, having declined a certification the ordinance permitted, on a good reason, with no rule against him, and would not be thanked, because a man who has been thanked for coming at two in the morning never comes twice. A **nurse with a card and a date on it and four names on it**, one of them a girl of nineteen who has asked twice to be named, and on the card the ward's whole slack: **forty minutes, across eleven patients and three staff, on the best night of the year.** A **woman of sixty-six winding four clocks with a lamp** on a private spur cut off from the district supply in 1923, four minutes ten out of the district reference and in agreement with one another, unpaid for eleven years, and whose reason for that is on his certificate anyway because she gave it. A **copyist owed eleven shillings**, who has asked for a lease and a wage a month and has not been given either.

And a **stair**. A chalker of fifty-four, nineteen years on it, put four columns on a stone stair in the Cathedral where four hundred people a day go up, because it is the one place in Morrowfen where people stop to read things: **3** and **Clockwork Quarter** in a district's own hand, **26** and **Clockwork Quarter**, and then **VANCE, P.**, and **nothing in the column beside it.** He put the chamois against the wall below it and took it away again and left it. A stranger in a good coat stopped, because that is what this stair is, and asked what the chalk meant. *Three minutes.* Out of the Quarter's clocks. And then the two new ones: a name and a duration, for every toll taken out of a person's attention, coordination or memory. The column beside Vance's is empty because no instrument in this city timed how much of that shift the reading took, and he was in the building, and he is not going to put a number there that he made up. And then the stranger asked the question nobody has asked him since the fifteenth of the first month. *How many more?* He could have said a labour book with nine series and no name on any of them, or a box under the Cathedral with a keeper, not open to petition, not empty, with a name on it and no date on it. **Every one of those is a document and not a number, and there is no column for the size of a thing nobody has counted.** *I don't know.* And the man will not be back.

On the Wednesday morning, at a quarter to twelve, on the second step of the Cathedral, in front of four hundred people and a tram, he counted, because a clockmaker who stands under a public clock and does not count is doing something else. **Nine of them, arriving with eight and then one over**, round and low and not the sound of a wheel, out of the ground under the pavement the way water comes back into a pipe it has been out of, and it went on past the tick with a weight on it somewhere he could not name and could not go and find. **No night in it. No face. Not one word. It cost him nothing, and it is not a warning, and it is not an answer, and it is not Aftershock.** It is the ninth thing in a machine that does eight. **The eight is the time. The ninth is not.** Three times in five months, and the next one will be the fourth, and it is still, exactly, unexplained.

## 2. The seven resolution items, with chapter and numbers

All seven are on the page. Cite them from here; the verbatim documents are in `state/continuity.md` §batch 0005 (a), (b), (c).

1. **A repaired pump and a public toll ledger with two kinds of column — Ch 48, the second and third of the sixth month.** Weir Two's second stage: **item one** of the published schedule, brought forward into the current programme year **in about nine minutes, on a variation, at a counter, by a man of about thirty with eleven years at that counter, who refused correctly in Ch 31, to whom nothing was done, and who was thanked by nobody including the person it was for.** The bearing came out of a box in the pump house and was **fitted on the Thursday the second and the Friday the third, two days, £412 6s** — the first work done out of that schedule. **Maintained by name, in the works' own hand, in a four-inch column that is the first time anything under it has been a person:** *B. Sarr. A. Dench, known as Amos, to be entered as a full name and not as an initial. T. Arden, from the 30th ult., licensed: no. R. Morrow, night porter, Green Row, hours as returned.* And: *Vance, P. — 1st month, the seventeenth. Night shift 4. Carried on the line because the line is a line and the line does not begin where a man begins.* The ledger: **a glass case in the pumping ward's outer room, key on a string, a chair in the room, pencil and ink on the ledge; MINUTES and DISTRICT forty-three years old, NAME and DURATION new; the first entry under the new columns is `VANCE, P.` and the duration column has no figure at all** — no dash, no word, no mark, no blank rule; the recorded figure for it is *none*.
2. **A standing protection from silent shifts, temporary, and the reader told so in the same sentence — Ch 49, the Saturday the fourth of the sixth month.** Moved by the chair, carried on a show of hands without a division in **eleven minutes**: no shift in the district certified in the maintenance-deferred class until the labour ledger, eighteen years, and its annex are published in full and received; **the protection is temporary** (first sentence, ninth word); **it expires at the first sitting of the committee after the report is received and not before**; **the committee has not undertaken to renew it and has no power to promise that it will**; the report is due at the ninth month's sitting, and if late before the committee in the twelfth. **About three months against an audit of about fourteen.**
3. **Hask loses the right to certify silent shifts and publishes the labour ledger with a liability annex naming the night of the Long Nine — Ch 48, the first of the sixth month.** Three standing orders read out of the guild's own book: his class is under review from this date and pending it he certifies no shift alone, every certificate countersigned by a first-class holder, and a list published monthly at the counter; **his right to certify a shift in the maintenance-deferred class is withdrawn, is not restored by the review, and the class itself is abolished by the hall within twelve months, any site in it to be entered in the ordinary class or not at all**; and the **labour book, series 1 to 9, eighteen years**, published in full at the counter and for sale at a price that covers paper, with an annex drawn by the hall stating the date of the night of the Long Nine, **the figure of two hundred and twelve**, and the hall's finding as to the hours of that night. **212 is the figure of that annex and is never an entry in a shift book and is never added to any other figure.**
4. **Sel Kade keeps her post and submits to a tenant review — Ch 46, the twenty-third to the twenty-sixth of the fifth month.** A process she did not have, in four clauses: a variation entered in a ward book with quantity, beneficiary and date and posted within one working day (hers); a review on a ground named in the notice, at the request of the council, the works, **or any ratepayer of the ward, who is then named** (Teo Arden's, and she brings it in and says it is not hers); a review that may find against and may find for and shall enter both, **because a review that can only find against somebody is not a review, it is a trial** (hers); and a finding posted in full with **no person thanked in it** (Wenna Pruitt's, and she does not read it out). The review was held **Thursday the twenty-fifth, eleven to twelve ten**; the ground was the flood on Kade's own condition, named by a ratepayer of sixty-six in 4B who said she had lived at the bottom of a flood since nineteen hundred and one and had never once been asked what she thought about it. The finding is **four paragraphs in chalk in a copyist's hand outside a pump house**, with copies in the council's file and on the works' desk, and it also records that the works ended the diversion eleven weeks before the review on their own tables, that the process could not have stopped it and did not claim to have, and that **the certificate saying the main is not to be shut is dated the twenty-eighth of the second month** and cannot be reconciled with the works' tables of the nineteenth by anybody in this city.
5. **Rook files the toll-ledger demand herself, on arithmetic — Ch 49.** The clause she left out of her own demand of the thirteenth of the fourth month, of which she was the mover: that the Assembly receive as evidence and publish in full with the minutes the toll ledger of the city in the two sets of columns; that the second set be a **standing requirement of the Office of Public Reckoning's audit and not a matter of the Office's practice**; and that **where a duration was not recorded, that fact be printed in the ledger, in the column, and not elsewhere.** Her reasons: *an audit is a thing that is done, an office closes an audit and the file goes in a drawer and a drawer holds a card about nine hundred times;* and *a demand is a standing thing, read twice, entered on the minutes, discharged, and read again next year, and it is the only instrument in that building that survives a change of clerk.* And: *I am not asking this Assembly to feel anything about a man whose name is in a column with nothing beside it. I am asking it to build the column, because a column exists for about forty years and a feeling does not.* **Carried nine to four, the four not the clerk, read twice, entered on the minutes, and the chair did not thank the four and did not thank anybody.** **The demand was blocked in the first month of this year, not three years ago.**
6. **Orrin's licence is suspended, removing his right to certify a machine's local toll; the bench stays open on a lease and a reputation — Ch 48, the Wednesday the first of the sixth month, at ten in the morning, at the counter of the licensing floor.** A **determination of the licensing floor**, not notice nine's question: a question is about returns, is not read to the holder, is answered in writing in twenty-one days, **cannot suspend anything**, alters no term and requires no interview; a determination is about an act, is made on a day and takes effect on a day, and is the only act in this city that can be got at with one. **Line 4 is the operative one: *that this hall does not find that the holder acted in bad faith; and it is determined that the licence of the holder be suspended in accordance with the standing orders of this hall; and that the term of the licence is not altered by this determination; and that the licence expires on the nineteenth of the third month next year.*** Line 2 is the hinge: *a local toll is a district toll, and a district toll may be certified only by the holder of a licence of the second class or above, or by the district that owns the reserve.* Nobody applauded; the hall's oldest clerk put her hand flat on the counter. The bench stays because a lease runs to Michaelmas, and a man in a good coat can still bring a carriage clock to the counter and be told *you can have it back on Saturday, and I will tell you what is wrong with it, and I will write you what I did, and you can take it to a man with a licence if you want the paper.*
7. **The ninth tick remains unexplained — Ch 50 alone.** The eighth of the sixth month, a quarter to twelve, the **second step** of the Cathedral. Nine strokes; **it arrives with eight of them and then one over**; the ninth is the last one and the one with the shove in it. **No night, no face, not one word, and it cost him nothing at all.** Not a warning, not an answer, **and not Aftershock.** The third and last entry there will ever be: *Three times in five months, and I have a book with two thousand pages in it and a tape and a level and a licence that does not let me certify anything at all, and I am going to go on not finding it, and the next one will be the fourth, and it is still, exactly, unexplained.*

### 2a. The two resolution-adjacent facts, and the posted document

**The suspension mechanism, Ch 48.** A determination of the licensing floor, made by the master at the counter in the guild's own type, read out twice, published, four numbered lines. The instrument that produces it is an ordinance clause: *where a master of this hall declines a certification which the ordinance permits him to make, the master's class is subject to review, and the master's class is determined by the licensing floor, and the determination is published.* **The same clause produces the review of Hask. Both instruments are the guild's; neither is the Assembly's, the works', the Office's or the Directorate's.**

**The two sets of columns, Ch 48, the third of the sixth month.** The Office's requirement is one line, the first line anybody in this city has ever written down about a person: *that every body toll recorded by any body in this city be recorded with the name of the person, and with the duration of the interval; and that where no instrument recorded the duration, the column shall be left empty and shall not be filled by inference.* The columns are **MINUTES, DISTRICT, NAME, DURATION**, and the headnote says, in the Office's type, that the first two have been kept in public for forty-three years and the stair carries them in chalk every morning of the world; that the second two carry, for every toll taken from a living person's attention, coordination or memory, a name and a duration; and then: **an empty column in this book is a statement that the interval was not recorded, it is not an omission of this office, it is not a mystery and is not to be filled in by inference, and any person who fills one in by inference commits an offence against this office and against the person whose name is in the column beside it.**

**The posted outage document, Ch 47, the Monday the twenty-ninth for the Tuesday the thirtieth.** Nine numbered paragraphs of foolscap, hand-written in daylight, **five hundred and fifty words**, on **four boards** — the ward board outside the Weir Two pump house, the works' yard board on the canal road, the ward board at Green Span hospital, and the licensing floor of the Guild of Horologists — and not to be taken down by anybody but the person named in the last paragraph, who has no authority to take it down. **1.** Conduit renewal, Green Span hospital line, canal crossing, east span, feed off the line, district held at one rate by hand. **2.** Date: Tuesday the thirtieth of the fifth month. **3.** Hours: **six**, ten past one to ten past seven in the morning, **six hours and not six hours approximately.** **4.** Twenty-seven street clocks and four standpipes on the canal front hand-wound throughout, on a schedule made **house by house by knocking, and not by a clerk.** **5.** The men whose hour begins at five begin at six, six named, and **that hour belongs to the six men on the street and not to any office in this city, and no document in this city has ever been able to give it back.** **6.** The ward's slack across eleven patients and three staff is **forty minutes, and six hours is nine times that**, spoken by L. Vale in the nurses' room in front of eleven people and entered on the ward card with the date, four names on the card, one of them **E. Cobb, night sister, nineteen, paid at the ward rate, who has asked twice to be named and has not been taken off.** **7. TOLL: three hundred and sixty minutes. Paying district: Lower Weirs** — the first time in the series that the paying district is the district being charged — with the book's own distinction under it: *a clock toll is a quantity of minutes and a paying district; line 6 is not a clock toll and is not paid in minutes by anybody, and it is on line 6 instead of on line 7 because line 7 has a paying district and line 6 does not.* **8.** Four places. **9.** Certified, *M. Vale, class three*, with: *I have no licence to certify a district toll. I have signed this because a posted document that cannot be certified is a request, and if the work is done on a request then the work is done at night and the posting is furniture. I expect to be suspended.* The district's own labour for the six hours is **twenty-seven shillings** at four shillings and sixpence the hour. There is no fund, no line, and nobody is going to be paid it.

## 3. What Volume 02 inherits — eight things that are load-bearing and temporary

1. **The standing protection is TEMPORARY, and this is the single most important fact in the inheritance.** **It expires at the first sitting of the committee after the report, and not before. The report is due in the ninth month and may be late, and the committee has not undertaken to renew the protection and has no power to promise that it will.** About three months, against an audit of about fourteen. A discretion with a shorter fuse than the thing it is waiting for, which is the cruelty Sel Kade named in the fourth month in another form. **Volume 02 must not open with the protection permanent, or safe.**
2. **The ledger's empty column, and the headnote's prohibition.** The column exists, the row is written, and **the blank is not a mystery to be solved.** No later volume may solve it, estimate it, derive it from a health record, or put a number in it; the Office has already refused to derive one in writing. Inherited with it: **about sixty-one calibration-strain entries in a year in one committee's files in this city**, with the same empty space opposite them, which will now be audited against a column that exists — a fight about the size of a population, not about a man, and the clause Rook's own April demand deliberately left out.
3. **The eighteen-year ledger, the annex, and the 212.** Series 1 to 9, about a thousand pages, no name on any of them, at the counter and for sale at a price that covers paper. **212 is the figure of the annex and is never an entry in a shift book and is never added to any other figure in this series, by anybody, including a reader.**
4. **The maintenance-deferred class, to be abolished within twelve months, any site in it entered in the ordinary class or not at all.** Two prices, neither a scandal. Entering forty-one heads in the ordinary class costs **two shillings the head for a year, and one shilling where a man is the occupier — £4 2s a year**, the figure Orrin paid out of his own pocket on the first of the fifth with a wet stamp and a woman who said *don't*, and it has to be found again out of a tin that was empty in the fourth month. And either way the ward is re-rated, because correcting the return takes the standing back out: **one shilling and tenpence in the pound against nine pence, on £48,000, £2,600 on the ward, thirteen shillings a year on a rated house of twelve pounds — nearly twice the price of the form that caused it.** **No figure of eleven shillings a head exists in this book; an earlier state file said so in error and has been corrected.**
5. **The twelve-month inspection interval, with no first inspection in it yet.** Every item inspected at the interval stated by the works' engineer and **not exceeding twelve months**, from the date of adoption, **the first inspection falling in programme year one**, and **the interval and the results of inspection to be published in the district's own sheet and not filed only.** **Eleven a year for three years, thirty-three of them, at £12 9s 4d: £411 8s**, which took the published total from **£4 202 10s 6d to £4 613 18s 6d.** Somebody else's obligation; it costs the city four hundred and eleven pounds and **gives nobody an hour.**
6. **The tell-tale, which nobody has agreed to make a line item of.** Eleven shillings and fourpence for a fortieth of an inch, visible from a towpath. The works' engineer has asked whether the district can have one on every bracket of that class and has said he does not think it will be agreed, **because there are six hundred standpipe brackets on the same sheet and nobody has ever asked to see whether a standpipe bracket has moved.** **The asking is the thing.** A six-hundred-bracket line item at about three thousand three hundred times the price of one pointer, **which is £1 890 against a tell-tale at 11s 4d** — 453 600 pence against 136 — and both figures are given so that the ratio can be checked instead of taken. Nobody has proposed it and it must not become cheap.
7. **The tank.** One tank, in the pumping ward, doing two jobs it was never sized for: the hospital's clock pressure and the canal front's four draws a night. **Thirty-four pounds at the hospital end against forty-one at the works' end, on a branch that should read forty to forty-two, short for eleven years, with no defect against it because a joint is a joint.** **The low-level alarm was inhibited from the second of the third month and no report was ever made, and that inhibition is the reason there was no number for a reserve that spent eleven weeks under two days.** Four thousand gallons on the sixteenth, one day; about seven thousand on the eighteenth, one and three-quarter days; the works' tables say two. For thirty-six hours it filled instead of emptying because the make-up stopped, **and that is the only reason it has not been reported empty.** The diversion ended on the nineteenth on the works' own tables, in a form, with a reference number, and **nobody in this city has told the district works anything about the seven pounds.**
8. **The two clocks on the Quarter hill.** **Two readings are on the page:** six minutes and forty seconds apart on the twenty-ninth of the fourth, and eight minutes and twenty seconds on the first of the fifth (`chapter-0041.md`). **A third figure — sixteen minutes and forty seconds on the eleventh — is a projection and not a delivered reading.** It is what the first two give if the step holds: ten more nights at fifty seconds, added to eight minutes and twenty. It is recorded here so that Volume 02 does not mistake it for something a chapter already said, and **a Volume 02 writer who wants it to be true must put it on the page before it is used, and a writer who wants it not to be true must break the fifty-seconds-a-night step first and say so.** The line steps **fifty seconds a night in one step at the fourth draw** and every window on that hill can see it. No form, no column, no office that has to answer for it.

**Also inherited, and not temporary:** Orrin's private reinstatement, **£4 2s, form GH-9, forty-one heads, a year, lodged alone and never disclosed** — and the fact that for that year the hall's own four-line answer is **not true** of those forty-one heads, and that at the end of the year the crew falls out of the standing again on the day the form runs out, with no hearing and no vote. The galvanising and wire works at **fifty-eight of ninety-one**, with a pay book and thirty-three men gone to the yards, the docks and two districts. **A book of initials in a kitchen on Weir Lane with no key written down, on purpose**, and no index of it anywhere in this series. The Advertiser page at **eleven shillings**, the council's tin empty, not run since the ninth of the fourth month. A classification, **CS/114, that lapsed on the eighth of the fifth month**, and an empty nail on a board on the ground floor of the Rise that a licensed man went up and looked at four times in a fortnight. Two working women in the Weirs who disagree about something that matters, are both right, and **refuse to choose.**

## 4. The open questions that are deliberately open

**None is to be answered in Volume 02 unless `outline/volume-02.md` says so.**

1. **How many other people are missing from the civic model, and why does the Directorate keep a sealed room under the Cathedral full of the names of the people it stopped counting.** Asked out loud on a stair in Chapter 50 as *How many more?* and **not answered, on purpose**, with no column anywhere in the city for the size of a thing nobody has counted. `outline/series.md` gives the scale to Volume 02's midpoint, the Archive index to Volume 02, the Gate hub to Volume 04 and what the records were used for to Volume 09. **Volume 01 has committed to none of those figures and neither may a Volume 02 writer until the outline says so.** What it has committed to is the room: *a box under the Cathedral with a keeper, that is not open to petition, that is not empty, and that has a name on it and no date on it and is the only room in this city that anybody has ever been refused entry to twice.*
2. **The lived intervals taken by severe body tolls.** Some families know; some never will. **The blank in the ledger is not a mystery to be solved**, and `outline/ending.md` §7 lists this as permanently unresolved. A character may want it answered; the book may not answer it, and the narration may not turn it into a clue.
3. **The ninth tick.** Three hearings in five months — Chapter 1 under the crown case, Chapter 3 through the pump house floor, Chapter 50 on the Cathedral steps — **and no more.** No mechanism, no cost, no night, no face, no word, not a prophecy, not a warning, and **explicitly not Aftershock.** Chapter 29 also records the silence, and the sentence that *one day one of those two facts will turn out to be the other* is a line about a man with no paying volume in this series. **Volume 04 owns the mechanism, with the device kept live.**
4. **The uncharged minutes.** **Three charged, five found, two nobody's.** The Halloway Street pillar clock is within tolerance on four readings and **the district file is two minutes short of the truth and always has been.** Nobody has asked the Quarter what it did with the difference, and it is the only quantity in the book that is growing.
5. **The Teo–Wenna disclosure argument, open since the ninth of the fourth month.** *Should we print it* has become *a printed page has no address and a list of addresses has twenty-two doors in it.* They agreed in Chapter 22 not to settle it and said they would not be the same arithmetic in a year. **It may be resolved in Volume 02 only by a deliberate decision, and both sides are right.** And the **book of initials with no key written down** is still in a drawer in a kitchen on Weir Lane, and no volume has ever had an index of it.

## 5. Canon a Volume 02 writer must not contradict

**The three tolls, in the order taken.** Ch 5, the first — the minute, out of an already-empty reserve, unaccountable afterwards. Ch 9, the second — **two days of fine motor control gone from his right hand and a permanent ring above a given pitch.** Ch 19, the third and largest — **thirty-eight minutes**, after which what is left is the loss of the **judgement and not the hearing.** A reading cost is not a toll and is entered with the reason written under it: the ninety-second Northglass reading and the hundred-and-forty-second Chapter 25 use are both **reading costs**, and **the count of tolls is three.**

**The two hand corrections, and no more.** Eleven minutes on a lane in the Weirs and eight on a bracket at the Ferrywright corner, done with a bar, with no receipt and no form. **The count is two and it is asserted on the page in Chapter 41.** Both are now inside an institutional form — the W-4's eighth line and the paragraph under it in Chapter 27. A later volume may not add a third by implication.

**The three rules, and what each cost him.** **One** — a blind margin and a toll named in public, in a room, to somebody who can object — obeyed in Chapter 19 and **produced nothing**, and Leda said so in a corridor. **Two** — a toll is what happens when you ask something you cannot pay for; ask one thing, about a named load, and know before you ask. **Three** — *no result of mine goes out of this room without a person named who can refuse it* — written on a Sunday in the second month, tested for the first time in Chapter 41 by **a piece of paper with a price on it that a woman could send back**, and satisfied on the thirtieth of the fifth by a document on four boards: *nothing of mine went out of that room without a person named who could refuse it. That is the only line I have got out of the whole of the year and I have got it the hard way and the price of it is on a sheet in four places, and I would do the hour again, at six in the morning, in front of the people it is done to.*

**Chapter 42's four places, four numbers and one blank.** The works' permit book on the canal road, page 61; the grey book, in longhand; chalk on the coping stone, legible from a boat; and a card in a nurses' room at Green Span. The numbers: **fifteen thirty-seconds of an inch now, twelve thirty-seconds two years and three months ago, three thirty-seconds of difference, a twenty-fourth of an inch a year — with the division written on the stone.** The blank: the district's own column, because **a district is not a ward, a pumping district is not a ward, and there is no form by which one could be put anywhere.** Two correct documents in two buildings a mile apart, neither mentioning the other — the diversion note and the permit-to-inspect page — and **neither of the two people who signed them is anybody's villain.**

**The two kinds of toll, and they are never merged.** A **clock toll** is minutes and a paying district. A **body toll** is an interval of a living person's attention, coordination or memory, and it had **no column in this city until the third of the sixth month.** The posted document keeps them apart on a public sheet — **paragraph 6 is the ward, paragraph 7 is the toll** — and **the blank in the duration column is a stated absence and not an omission.**

**The 212** is the Long Nine, eighteen years earlier, the figure the annex is about, **never an entry in a shift book and never added to another number.** **The absence of twenty-three:** not in any form anywhere in this volume; it is Volume 14 and it does not exist yet.
