# Chapter 59 — An Hour, on a Card, With a Date On It

On the Sunday morning of the third of the seventh month a sister stood in a nurses' room on the third floor of the Green Span hospital at about half past eight and said out loud, in front of eleven people, that the branch her hospital's clocks ran on was one hour and twelve minutes and forty seconds out of the district reference, and then she said the number that the room actually needed, which was forty minutes, and the two numbers are not the same kind of number, and she said that too, before anybody could divide one by the other.

---

She had it off a wall. That is the whole of where it came from and there is no other source of it in this city.

The two public clocks on the Quarter hill are four hundred yards apart, and the one on the south side is one hour five minutes out. The hospital's clock branch is further out than that and nearer than Weir Lane, and it came off the same six readings a man took on the ninth of the sixth and chalked on a board in a pump house, and on the Sunday morning of the third of the seventh Leda Vale went and measured it herself, with a watch, against a watch, in a corridor, and she wrote the figure on the back of the ward card before she went up to the nurses' room with it.

**One hour twelve minutes and forty seconds.**

It is more than the Quarter hill's south clock and it is less than Weir Lane's three tenement clocks, and the difference from the hill is **seven minutes and forty seconds** and the difference to Weir Lane is **fourteen minutes and fifty seconds**, and the line's proportions are not equal, and the man who wrote the six figures down has said in a notebook that he does not have the division and is not going to guess at it. She read that sentence off the board on the Saturday, because she goes and looks at things, and she copied it into the back of her own book.

---

The ward's total slack between its free clocks and its line clocks, across eleven patients and three staff, is **forty minutes**, and it is on the best night of the year, and Leda Vale has been asked for it four times over two years and there is no column for it and there is not going to be one tomorrow.

That is the whole of the cost. It has been the whole of the cost since the twentieth of the fourth month and she said it on the Saturday evening of the twenty-seventh of the fifth to eleven people in this room in front of a card, and the card is still on the board with the six hours and the forty minutes and four names on it, and none of the four names has been taken off.

"Here is what I am going to say and I want it written down and I want it read back, and then I am going to go and do the medicine round, because I have a ward at half past eight on a Sunday like any other Sunday.

"Forty minutes is how far this ward can drift before somebody has to hurry. It is not a complaint. It is the number. I have told you the number four times over two years and I have been told by three different offices that there is no column for it, and this morning I am telling you a bigger number, and the bigger number is one hour twelve minutes and forty seconds, and that is not a slack.

"You are going to try to divide one by the other, and you are going to get one point eight, and one point eight is not a thing you can hand a medicine on. That is not what this is. That number is how far behind the district reference the line this ward runs on is, and my slack is how much room I have before it matters to a person. They are not the same kind of number and I am not going to be the one who divides them in a room.

"What it actually means, and I have thought about this for two days and this is the sentence: **it means the drift is already larger than the room I had to put it in, and it has been since before the twenty-fourth of the fourth month, which is ten weeks and not a day less, and nobody in this building has known, because the clocks in here are compared with each other and not with anything.**"

She put the card down on the table.

"There are three clocks in this ward and they do not agree with each other and now I know they do not agree with anything either. Elsie's is going to be about the worst, because the night clocks are on the far side of the branch and the far side is the worst side.

"**E. Cobb, night sister, nineteen, paid at the ward rate, doing the ward clock by hand.** Her name is on this card and it has been on it since the twenty-fourth of the fourth month and anybody who wants it off can come and take it off and it will be written down who and when, and nobody has, and I am not going to stop saying it in this room, and she is nineteen, and I want that written down as well and not only her name."

Nobody took it off. There is a line in the card that says so and it is dated the third of the seventh month and it is signed by the senior sister and read back, and the four names are the sister, the two staff nurses on the long day, and Elsie Cobb.

---

What Elsie Cobb said about it, and she is nineteen and she said it flatly and without any performance, and it is the sentence the whole batch is built under:

"I have been doing that ward clock by hand since the twenty-fourth of the fourth month and I get the same money either way, and I would like somebody to write down that I would do it if the money stopped, and I would like it written down that I have asked twice for the card to have a column on it for what the free clocks cost the ward, because I am the one who is standing there at four in the morning doing the arithmetic with a watch and a piece of card, and I have been keeping the piece of card since the twenty-fourth of the fourth month, and it is in my apron, and I have never shown it to anybody because I did not think anybody wanted it."

Senior sister: "Show her."

Elsie Cobb put a folded card on the table. It was a medicine docket, and on the back of it, in pencil, in a small hand, there were about seventy columns of figures running down the sheet, one for nearly every night since the twenty-fourth of the fourth month, and two of them are marked *days* and left blank, and the top line said: *Free v line. Worst gap. Who was waiting.*

"Nobody has asked her for that since April," said Leda Vale.

"I know," said Elsie Cobb. "That is why I have said that I would do it if the money stopped."

---

**At about eleven o'clock on the Wednesday the sixth of the seventh month he went up Cathedral Rise to the Office of Public Reckoning and sat in the one window with a number, and the number was on two pieces of paper in a coat and neither of the pieces of paper was a document.**

The Office has four staff and a written right to be ignored. It audits the Toll Stair, which is a decision about a stair and therefore a decision about nothing, and it audits tolls, and since the third of the sixth month it audits a second set of columns in a glass case in a pumping ward on the canal road.

Mara Venn took the number on a form. The form is four lines long and has been printed since the second of the third month and it carries no fee.

> *That a document exists, being a record of clock error in the Lower Weirs pumping district, taken on the ninth and the eleventh of the sixth month, being six readings against the district reference, in the hand of the person who took them, without a licence, and not certified by anybody.*
> *That the Office has no function in the matter and does not certify it and does not adopt it and does not propose it, and has no power to obtain a return of a district's own error, and asks only that it be recorded as existing, with a date, and a reference.*
> *Received and noted is a complete answer.*

And then, before she stamped it, Mara Venn said, "There is no form."

"There is always a form," said Orrin Vale.

"There is always a form, and this one is mine, and it is a note asking an office to write down that a document exists, and it goes to a lot of offices and about a third of them answer." She wrote a second thing on the same sheet and it is not a note. "This is a line on the office's own form R-9, which is a form for *an observed condition of a civic machine not otherwise provided for*, and it has been in use since the office was founded, and it is the only form in this building that can take a clock's error rather than a toll, and it is not a form anybody has ever used, because for eleven years the only thing anybody has ever brought this office about a clock is a toll."

She stamped it and wrote a reference and read it back.

> **R-9, received, the sixth day of the seventh month. Reference 419. Condition observed: accumulation of error, distributed, on a civic hourline, Lower Weirs pumping district. Not certified. Not adopted. Not proposed. Existence of the observation recorded.*

"That is everything my office can do with it," she said. "It is one reference on a piece of paper in a drawer in a room with four people in it, and it gives your district no hours, and it will not appear in any return, and somebody in that building will open the drawer in about four years and will not know what to do with it, and will file it, because that is what this building does and does extremely well.

"And I am not going to pretend that is a victory. You have got a form number and I have got a form number, and neither of us has got a column."

"Does the drawer have anything in it this month," said Orrin Vale.

"Thirty-four cards," said Mara Venn. "It had thirty-one on the sixth of the sixth. It had eleven on the seventeenth of the third and twenty-two on the eighteenth of the fourth, and it grows about four a month and it has never once been emptied, and one day somebody is going to ask what the point of a drawer is, and the answer is that a drawer is how an institution remembers that it was ever asked, and there is no shelf in this city that does the same thing better."

---

**And the thing that ends the chapter is that he was asked, at about four in the afternoon on the Wednesday the sixth of the seventh month, in a corridor on the third floor of a hospital, to look at a clock.**

The request came from Ellen Reeve, who has been the Green Span hospital's engineer for twenty-two years, and who packed a gland wrong in August of last year and said so before letting anybody near her pump, and who is here because she is good at her job.

It is a new timer, on the new circuit, on the wall by the lift shaft, and it is a Green Span hospital timer and it is eleven seconds slow, and it is the hospital's own and not a district's, and it is inside the ward's own reach.

"I am not asking you to certify it," she said. "I know you cannot and it is on a board in a hall. I am asking because you are the man who put a name in a column on a stair in the sixth month, and because I have a wall of six timers and a rule that they are all within a second of each other, and they are not, and I have had two of them out to the works and the works have had them out to a man, and he charged us eleven pounds and told us they were within tolerance."

"And within tolerance of what," said Orrin Vale, "is the question I actually want answered."

Orrin Vale put his palm on the case and then took it off, and then he did something he has not done in six years, which is to ask for the sound rather than the strain.

He put his ear to the case. He listened for about eleven seconds.

"I cannot tell you," he said.

"No," said Ellen Reeve.

"I can hear it running. I can tell you it is running and I can tell you about the room. I cannot tell you by the sound whether that movement is going true, and I have not been able to for about a year, and I have a permanent ring above a certain pitch in my right ear and I lost the judgement and not the hearing, and the judgement is the whole of a clockmaker's trade, and it went in the second month of this year and it is not coming back because nothing was strained.

"There is a bench in Verge Lane with two wheels on it and a lease to Michaelmas and I can clean a barrel and bush a wheel and beat a clock to a rate, and I beat that timer to its rate against the Quarter's public clock with a watch and a second watch and a sounding board in about nine minutes, on the last Friday in June, and the invoice for that one is eleven pounds and eleven minutes, and it is not on this invoice, and it is the best work I have done this year. And I could not tell you from the end of a corridor whether it was right. I had to measure it."

"Then how do you do your job," said Ellen Reeve.

"I measure it," said Orrin Vale. "Every time. With a watch. Which takes eleven minutes instead of about a second, and costs eleven pounds instead of nothing, and there is not one customer in Morrowfen who has ever preferred the second way, and about a hundred and forty of them who cannot afford the eleven minutes, and that is the whole of what happened to me in the second month of this year and it took nine words to say and about a year to be able to say them out loud."

He wrote it that night, and it is the last paragraph of the entry and it is the reason he is going to be spending the winter in the Weirs.

> *Sixth of the seventh, Wednesday, about four in the afternoon. Green Span, a corridor, a new timer, eleven seconds slow, a woman of forty-four and twenty-two years in the building who asked me a question she had a right to ask.*
> *I said the sentence out loud for the first time since the second month of this year and it did not take any courage, which is a thing I expected it to take, and the reason it did not is that nobody in that corridor had any idea there was anything to be brave about, and the fact that a thing is true and is expensive to do is not the same as a thing that is hard to say, and I have spent a year finding that out.*
> *R-9, 419, the sixth. Six readings in a pump house and a form number in a drawer and a night book in a room and a card on a board in a nurses' room with a date on it and four names on it, one of which belongs to a girl of nineteen who has kept a piece of a medicine docket since the twenty-fourth of the fourth month and has shown it to nobody because she did not think anybody wanted it, and I have put that on the card, and somebody read it back.*
