## Day map — the governing map wins, and the prose has been corrected to it

`outline/volume-03.md` §The batch 0001 calendar, fixed and absolute is authority: **first month thirty days, 1 Sun to 30 Mon; second month twenty-eight days, 1 Tue to 28 Mon; third month thirty-one days, 1 Wed to 31 Fri.** Its own fixed day map is also authority, and it named the Sundays: the 15th, 22nd and 29th of the first month and the **6th and 13th** of the second, and it named which chapters contain one.

**The prose was a day out from the seventh of the second month onward and has been corrected.** It had been written as though the first month ran thirty-one days, which puts the 1st of the second month on a Wednesday; the map puts it on a Tuesday. Six weekday labels and four date labels changed. Nothing else in the chapter moved.

- 101: 13th–14th first month (Fri–Sat, no Sunday)
- 102: 15th–18th first (Sun–Wed, Sunday 15th, market shut, reason on page)
- 103: 19th–21st first (Thu–Sat, no Sunday)
- 104: 22nd–25th first (Sun–Wed, Sunday 22nd, no counter on Sunday)

- 108: 7th–9th second (Mon–Wed, no Sunday, recovery chapter)
- 109: 10th–13th second (Thu–Sun, **Sunday 13th**, arcade and assay shut)
- 110: 15th–18th second (Tue–Fri, no Sunday)

Sundays fall in 102, 104, 105, 107 and 109 and not in 101, 103, 106, 108 or 110, **which is exactly what the outline's fixed statement of this batch requires.** No Assembly, committee, guild counter, market or mutual sits on a Sunday. Pindar Row trades Tuesday and Friday, is shuttered on the Saturday of Chapter 107 and busy on the Friday of Chapter 109, and shut Sunday with the reason on the page in 102, 107 and 109.

**The refusal-to-brass interval.** Hale writes on Thursday the 3rd, the council refuses in its own words on Thursday the 10th (seven days), the minute is published, and **on Tuesday the 15th, five days after the refusal and twelve after the asking, a householder on Sluice Row spends a Q100 into a mantel clock.** The 15th is a Tuesday and Tuesdays are trading days; the four trading days in between are the 4th, 8th, 11th and 15th, which is the four-trading-day span in Hale's book. An earlier draft had the token in the clock on the day of the refusal and called it eleven days after, and that error was not in the first review.

## Corrections made to the prose in review, for the next writer

- **Meta language removed from eight chapters**, including five instances the first review did not catch: 101 "he had spent two volumes" and "the whole volume would be measured against"; 102 "which was the second chapter in a row" and "the volume would turn on it"; 103 "no chapter of his life" and "the offence of the first volume"; 106 "the whole of the last two volumes" and "which was canon"; 109 "the four economics of the volume"; 110 "what the batch had been walking toward" and "the volume would keep it so". The words *chapter*, *volume*, *batch* and *canon* now appear in the ten files only in the `# Chapter` heads, and a case-insensitive sweep for them in the prose returns nothing.
- **Coss is two people and the page says so.** Mr Coss is the printer of the Bellweather Road office; Miriam Coss is 34, of 9 Bellweather Road 4th floor, no relation. 105 puts the collision in front of the reader and has Orrin write her name on the back of his hand. 109 opens by saying why he writes *printer* and puts the surname nowhere. **A bare "Coss" in a later chapter means the printer.**
- **The sentence itself is said once by Teo (102) and once by Hale (110); the four-door sheet under the assay counter in 109 is the keeper carrying the same idea in a hand nobody can read, and the narrating sentence about streets and doors in 110 is the narrator, not a character.** The 107 repetition was cut so that Teo's saying it a second time in Chapter 120 is the second time, as the batch 0002 card requires.
- **The Sunday rule is stated four different ways in four chapters** — a market with no day to close on (102), a counter that cannot shut cannot be found (104), a public counter a person can walk into for nothing (105), nothing in this city keeps a counter on a Sunday (107) — and the arcade's own board carries Hale's reason in 109.
- **Length.** The ten chapters run 2,328 to 2,907 words, total 24,687 by `wc -w` including heads. Paragraph shape now matches the house: average 48 words a paragraph, 20% over eighty words, longest 168, against volume-01 at 46 and 19% and volume-02 at 49 and 22%. Nothing was padded and no scene was cut; the added material is dialogue, physical action, rooms, and consequence inside scenes that already existed.
