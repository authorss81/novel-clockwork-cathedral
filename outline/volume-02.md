## Volume number and span

Volume 02, Chapters 51 through 100. Five batches of ten. The volume's name is a piece of administration: **an unpriced ward is a ward whose work appears in no schedule, so nothing in the city can be charged to it, and the first consequence of that is that nothing in the city has to answer for it.** The name is fixed by `outline/series.md` and may not be changed.

## Central pressure

A wave of clock slips and water failures spreads through the Lower Weirs, and it spreads along a line the city already built and already owns. The error is not a fault in anything. It is a **ramp**: every machine on the Weir Two branch is a further step from the root, and the branch takes its beat off the odd teeth, so the far end of the line is a very long way out, and the line has been given four pumping cycles a day since the fifteenth of the third month, lawfully, on a schedule the Water Users' Committee has twice re-voted.

The Directorate's answer is the volume's central antagonist pressure and it is **not villainy and cannot be defeated by being wrong**:

> A district whose own instruments are not reporting cannot be a district the model is wrong about.

That sentence is true. It is also the reason a district with a nil return is invisible: there is nothing in the return to fail, and a model cannot be shown to have mispredicted a thing that is not in it. The volume's antagonist is a **nil return**, and the fight is over the arithmetic that would fill it in — which is the same arithmetic that raises the ward's rate, which is the arithmetic Orrin already paid eighty-two shillings of his own money for in the sixth month of Volume 01, alone, without telling anybody.

The pressure that drives all fifty chapters is therefore not *will Orrin find the machine that is doing it.* It is: **a district that cannot prove its own labour is a district that cannot be shown to be failing, and a district that cannot be shown to be failing is a district that gets the bill.**

## Starting state

- **Orrin Vale, 32,** holds a **class-three maintainer's licence that was suspended on the first of the sixth month** by a determination of the guild's licensing floor, lawfully, in four true lines, for certifying a district toll he had no standing to certify. **The term is unaltered: the licence expires on the nineteenth of the third month next year**, and nobody shortened his clock. He can clean a barrel, bush a wheel and beat a clock to a rate. He cannot certify a civic machine and he cannot certify a local toll.
- **Vale's Bench is open on a lease to Michaelmas** and a sign that has said **HOROLOGER. REPAIRS TO ANY DESCRIPTION.** in the same type for eleven years. He is not ruined, not redeemed, and not asked to leave.
- **He has three keeper's tolls on the page** (Chapters 5, 9, 19) and no more may be taken in this volume without a volume outline that says so. **He has lost the judgement and not the hearing**: he can hear a watch at thirty feet in a still room and can no longer tell by the sound alone whether a movement is running true.
- **He has put £4 2s of his own money into form GH-9** for forty-one heads, a year, the same series that took the Weir Two night crew out, and **has told nobody**, and the four-line card that came back says the crew is part of a certified shift for one year, and the crew falls out of the standing again on the day the form runs out with no hearing and no vote. **That date is the nineteenth of the third month next year and it is the hinge this volume turns on.**
- **He has a small reputation and a private agreement with Mara Venn** — a page, dated, in two hands, witnessed by nobody: neither of them uses a result without naming its blind margin. It is not a romance and it is not a licence on either of them.
- **He is the only person in Morrowfen who knows both facts about those forty-one heads**, and Volume 02 must not let him tell anybody.

## Starting power level

**Stage 2, Aftershock, practised with a price he can quote before he pays.** He can feel the next short-term failure of a named load on one connected machine or one immediate assembly. He cannot see intent, cannot see a district, and cannot see anything the network has routed away.

**Ending power level:** **Stage 4, Civic rehearsal, first attempt, badly and successfully.** Under Ivo Pell's supervision he reaches **Stage 3, Choice trace** — connected to the Loom, he senses the three strongest pressure paths of a concrete proposal, and everything he learns that way is inadmissible until three witnesses have signed and a toll has been filed. He then makes a **civic rehearsal** across three independent hourlines with two operators he does not trust, and it works, and the only safe route costs that same neighbourhood a long visible outage, and he has to go back and tell them so in person.

**The growth is not a power.** It is that he stops treating a blind margin as an inconvenience in a footer and starts treating it as **a place** — a place in this city, with a floor, a keeper, and a name on it and no date on it, which is how he gets from a line of small print to a room in Chapter 75.

## The rules of the machine that matter in this volume

Carried from `bible/power-system.md` unchanged. The ones this volume leans on:

1. **The question spindle accepts a concrete named action only.** Volume 02's central question cannot be *should the Weirs be inside the model*, because that is not a thing anybody could physically do. It has to be a valve, a pump, a gate, a draw, a rate, a form, an index.
2. **A Reading has four parts** — trace, pressure, blind margin, toll — and the blind margin is a required line printed smaller than the operator's signature.
3. **A Reading is valid only for the conditions and records present when it was made.**
4. **Where a toll lands is a function of reserve, not of consequence.** Not used for a toll in this volume; used for its corollary, which is that **a district with a nil reserve is not asked, because asking costs money and there is nothing to draw on.**
5. **The affected-district resident station on the witness rail has been replaceable for about twenty years by a written district standing, and the Lower Weirs has no standing under the water act.** **This is the volume's central machine.** The form that would change it is a standing withdrawal and reinstatement, series GH-9, at **two shillings the head for a year and one shilling where a man is the occupier**, and **Orrin has already paid £4 2s for forty-one heads for one year and the year does not run out until the nineteenth of the third month next year.**
6. **Body tolls are recorded as *calibration strain***, with no name and no duration, on a phrase roughly forty years old — except in the pumping ward's public ledger, which since the third of the sixth month carries a name and a duration, and **the first duration is empty on purpose and the empty column is not a mystery to be solved.**
7. **A private sense is not a Reading.** No blind margin, no record, an unfiled cost. **A stage gained in this volume changes his options and his responsibilities and does not change this.**
8. **No certainty permit exists yet.** It is created in Volume 03, out of a Reading strike. No character in this volume may act as though permits are normal, and the volume's next-volume question is *who can issue one, and why did the original safeguards make one impossible.*

## Escalation sequence

**Step one — the ramp and the nil return (Chapters 51–55).** Orrin measures the Weir Two branch end to end in daylight with two watches and a notebook and finds that the error is a ramp and not an offset, that it has been growing for eighty-five nights, and that the far end of the line is out by more than an hour and forty-five minutes. He can do nothing with it. A Directorate auditor arrives with a form and a table and a sentence that is true, and the true sentence is the volume's problem. Teo tells him who got Vance killed and is right. He practises on a dead pump and Ivo Pell finds him and says the sentence that turns the volume: **you cannot feel a thing that is not connected, and everything that has been done to this district for eighteen years has been done by disconnection.** The Archive of Unlisted's **index** is requested and is neither granted nor refused, and a form number Della Marsh gave away in the second month for four shillings is the first item on it.

**Step two — the training and the bill (Chapters 56–70).** The tenant review process is used again, by somebody other than the woman who wrote it. The maintenance-deferred class is abolished and the class's last Weirs site has to be entered in the ordinary class or not at all, and it costs Bessie Crane sixty-two shillings a year. A hospital clock skips. Under Ivo Pell's supervision and with a price quoted in advance, Orrin reaches **choice trace**, and learns that the Loom will show him three pressure paths about a district he is not connected to and about a man he cannot see, and that what he is shown is inadmissible, and that Pell is right that it should be.

**Step three — the midpoint: the index, and forty thousand people (Chapters 71–80).** The index is published in a form that cannot be refused. **Between forty and sixty thousand residents have been removed from the civic model, more than half of them in the Weirs, and they were removed in dated batches rather than by accident.** Ada Vance, thirteen, reads a line in a batch index and finds her father's name in the batch that did it. The Directorate's position is that a batch is a batch and the index is a public courtesy. A Northglass factory finds out what a nil return is worth and stops paying a safety toll, which is the same omission being used on purpose for the first time in the series, by a person who is not a villain and is not a plant. Teo organises a strike that risks the hospital's supply.

**Step four — the strike, the lapsing protection, and the set-up (Chapters 81–90).** The strike holds for nine days and the hospital's supply is at risk on the eleventh. The guild's report is late into the ninth month and arrives into the tenth, and **the standing protection lapses on a day and not before**, and nobody extends it. The neighbourhood that would be saved by the rehearsal is the neighbourhood that pays for it, and Orrin cannot find a route that does not cost that street, and he is asked by two operators he does not trust to try the only instrument he has left.

**Step five — the rehearsal, the outage, and the settlement (Chapters 91–100).** The three-clock rehearsal works. The only safe route costs the same neighbourhood a long visible outage. He goes back and tells them so in person, in a room, and the room decides, and the room's decision is not his. Sel Kade leaves the appointed warden post. The Directorate publishes the Archive index. The neighbourhood receives control over its own local readings. **Anja Voss's offer of access is the volume's last line and it is not a bargain and it is not an answer.**

## Midpoint reversal — Chapter 75

**The Archive index is published and it is a scale, not a story.** Forty to sixty thousand residents removed from the civic model, more than half in the Weirs, removed **in dated batches**, eighteen years of them, each batch a form with a series, a head count, a price and a countersignature.

**The reversal is of quantity.** Volume 01's argument was about one man and one column and one empty cell, and the reader was right to be satisfied with that. Chapter 75 says the empty cell is one of about fifty thousand, and **the total is not a rounding error and it is not a scandal, because the removal was priced, batched, countersigned and lawful at every step.** Ada Vance, who is thirteen, finds her father's name in the batch that did it, by reading, and nobody in the room can comfort her because the room is not wrong.

**After Chapter 75 the shape of the volume changes.** Before it, a man is trying to find out whether a district is inside a machine. After it, a district has learned that it is a **place where something is done to it in batches**, and the argument is no longer *is it true* but *who may stop the next one, and where would a person go to refuse.*

## Volume climax (Chapters 95–99)

A pressure surge on the Weir Two branch that no single Reading could settle, because there is no body with standing to hold one and the one form that would create a standing is a form Orrin filled with his own money and the year on it does not run out until the nineteenth of the third month next year.

He borrows **two operators he does not trust** and runs a **three-clock rehearsal** — a shared method, not a private sense, with three independent hourlines and a common question and two other people's hands on machines. **It works.** It diverts the surge out of a Weirs neighbourhood.

**And the only safe route costs that same neighbourhood a long, visible outage** — hand-wound clocks, a posted timetable, hours belonging to named people, a stated number of minutes, and a paying district. He has to go back and tell them so in person, in a room, before anything is done, and **the room decides, and the room's decision is the volume's climax, and his is only to put the arithmetic where they can see it.**

This is the mirror of Volume 01's climax, and the difference is everything the series is about: in Volume 01 he put his name under a number he had no standing to sign because there was no room. **In Volume 02 there is a room, and the number is in it, and the room refuses it, and he is the one who has to go back with the refusal in his hand.**

## Concrete resolution

All five of these must be on the page in Chapters 96–100. A volume does not end on a mood.

1. **The neighbourhood receives control over its own local readings** — not a licence, not a permit, not a certainty instrument, and not the Council of Hours, which does not exist. A right, held by a body the district already has, over what may be asked of its clocks, on terms the district can be held to, with the minutes published and no person thanked in them.
2. **The Directorate must publish the Archive index** in full, on a stated date, in a place that can be petitioned, and it does so because a number in a set of minutes whose source is a box can be called a fabrication, and the Directorate wants the number to be citable.
3. **Sel Kade leaves the appointed warden post and becomes a tenant-council representative**, carrying the political consequences of the diversion rather than disappearing from the story. **She is not redeemed and she is not softened and the chapter must say what she loses**: the water, the hours, and eighteen years of being the person who had to be asked.
4. **The three operators are named and paid or unpaid as the case is**, and the rehearsal has a printed method with a maintenance schedule and six names on it, which is Volume 09's counterweight ward arriving early as a **list** and not as a device.
5. **Anja Voss offers access** — to the sealed rooms, or to a sealed unit, or to the machine — **and the offer is not taken and not refused, and it is the last line of the volume.** The Continuity Minister is not named in this volume and her door is not opened.

## Final chapter image

The pumping ward's outer room, the glass case, the key on a string, and the ledger with two sets of columns — and beside the case, on the same ledge, a **second book**: the neighbourhood's own reading minutes, in a hand nobody at the Office can read, with the neighbourhood's figures in it and the Office's figure nowhere, and a chair, and a pencil, and ink, and **a line at the head of the book in the same five-line shape as the Office's headnote, which says that where no instrument recorded the duration the column is empty, and that the empty column is a statement and not an omission, and that any person who fills one in by inference commits an offence against the people in the column beside it.**

Two books. Two headnotes. The same rule, written twice, in two buildings, by people who have never met.
