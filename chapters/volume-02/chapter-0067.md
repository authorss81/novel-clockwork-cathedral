# Chapter 67 — Three Paths, and Forty-One Seconds

A question spindle takes a named act. Not a decision, not a judgement, not a wish: a thing somebody could physically do to a particular piece of pipe on a particular day, with a quantity and a date on it. Ivo Pell said that in four sentences on the Friday morning and then made him write one out and read it back, and when he had read it back correctly he said, "Now you may come in."

He came in on the Friday the fifth of the eighth month at about eleven in the morning, with a piece of paper in his hand and no licence, and Ivo Pell in an operator's coat he is not borrowing this time.

---

**The room is down a corridor and it is a working room and not a temple.** A long bench, three stations on it, a frame of about nine feet with a bar across it that moves when the room is working, a rail down one side with three chairs at it, and a man who keeps it who is sixty-one and does not look up and is not introduced, because nobody in that building introduces anybody.

At the bench there is a spindle: a brass cone about the length of a pencil in a block, and you put your question into it in the form of a paper, and the spindle takes it or it does not.

"Two things before we start," said Pell. "The first is that you do not get to ask whether a thing is right. The spindle will not take it, and it does not take it by being silent, it takes it and gives you back something with a shape in it that you will want to describe, and if you describe it I shall stop the session."

"Then what does it take."

"**A named act. A thing somebody could physically do, to a named thing, with a number and a day on it.** You have one. Read it out."

> "That the daytime draw upon the canal front, from Bellweather Road to the Weir One gate, be worked in two pumping cycles in place of four, from the first day of the eighth month to the thirty-first day of that month."

"That is a question. Nine words would do. Twenty-one is not wrong, twenty-one is careful." Pell looked at him. "The second thing is that everything you are about to feel is inadmissible, and I am going to say that now and not at the end, because at the end you will not hear it, and I have watched four men not hear it and I am not doing it again with a fifth.

"**Nothing that happens in this room goes out of it in a form anybody can use, until three people have signed and a toll has been filed, and the three have to include a person of the affected district, and there is no standing in the affected district, and there is nobody to put in that chair.** So you are going to pay, and there is going to be no return on it, and the reason there is going to be no return on it is a form in a guild hall that you filled in yourself in May with your own money, and you did that on purpose, and you told nobody, and you are now going to find out what that was worth."

---

**He tried the other one first, because he had it in him, and it was a mistake, and Pell let it happen, and afterwards neither of them pretended it had not.**

"Before that," said Orrin Vale, "there is one I have been carrying since the fourth of August and it is the one I actually want to know."

"Go on."

"**Whether the decision of the Water Users' Committee of the first instant was right.**"

He put it into the spindle, and the spindle took it, and the room made a noise.

It is not a hum and it is not a bell. It is a sound like a chair being dragged across boards and then the chair being dragged back, about four times, and it goes on for longer than a question should, and there is no shape in it he can hold, and at about the forty-second second he understood that it was not going to give him anything and that he was going to keep standing in a warm room listening to it because that is what a man does.

Pell had a hand on his shoulder. He had put it there without saying so and he took it off when the noise stopped.

"One hundred and eighty seconds," said Ivo Pell, looking at a watch. "Write it down as a reading cost with the reason written under it, which is the practice from the seventeenth of the second month, and the reason is that you asked a question about whether something was right, on a Friday morning, in a warm room, and there is no named act in it anywhere and there never was."

"It is the question I wanted to ask."

"It is the question you wanted to *have had asked*. That is a different thing and it has a cost and the cost is a hundred and eighty seconds of a man standing in a warm room being told nothing, and I want you to notice something about the two numbers in this room before we go on, because you will not notice it now and you will notice it in about a fortnight and you will write it down then.

"**The expensive one just cost a hundred and eighty seconds and gave you a noise. The cheap one is going to cost forty-one and take a piece of you that you do not get back. Write those two numbers side by side and look at them for as long as you like, and then never look at them again.**"

---

**The second one went in on the Saturday the sixth at about ten, and it took four days and a witness rail to say, and he spent two of them on his back.**

He was connected at the bench, at the third station, which is the one a reader uses, and the frame moved once and then held, and the first thing he had was heat.

Not a picture of a joint. **Heat, at the far end of the hospital branch, about a third of the way out from Green Span, in a place he cannot see and does not need to see, in a band about two feet long.** He had it for about nine seconds and then it went, and the band was not a leak and was not a gland and it was in a main that is 1911 and is on the works' schedule as *a joint is a joint* with a line item of two pounds fourteen shillings and sixpence in programme year three.

The second thing was vibration, at the Weir One gate, small, high, in the rack — and a gate rack that has been driven four times a night for nine years and is about to be driven twice, and the note was not the note of a machine that is going to fail and was the note of a machine that is about to be asked for something it has not done since about 1946, **and the difference between those two things is a thing he can feel and a thing he cannot, and it took him about a minute to work out which one he had.**

The third was resistance, in the pumping ward's tank, and this one was the largest and it was the one at the edge of which there was something else, and he could not get at it.

"There is a fourth," he said.

"There are three," said Ivo Pell. "It has three and it will not give you a fourth, and it can leave one out as easily as it can give a wrong one, and I am not going to let you spend the next hour trying to get at the fourth, because **the fourth is the one that is not in it, and you will not get it out of the machine, and if you got it out of the machine you would be a man who gets things out of machines, and there is no form in this country for that.**"

"Then tell me what the third one is at the edge of, because it is the largest one and there is something in the corner of it."

"The largest one is your tank," said Pell. "The tank at the pumping ward, one tank, doing the hospital's clock pressure and the front's draws, thirty-four pounds at the far end against forty-one at the works' end, short for eleven years with no defect against it because a joint is a joint, and the make-up comes out of the ward's reserve, and the reserve has been under two days since the second of the third month, and the low-level alarm has been inhibited since the same date, and no report has been made, and making one is a duty."

"I know all of that. I found the inhibition."

"Then you know why the machine cannot tell you what happens at the edge of it, and it is not because the machine is weak. **It is because the one thing that would warn anybody that the tank has gone under two days is switched off, and has been for five months, and the switch is in a building and the reason for the switch is in somebody's head.** And that is not an omission. A machine does not leave out the switched-off alarm. It has never been in there. The alarm is not part of the thing the model represents, because the model represents the tank, and a tank has a level, and **an alarm is a person having written something down**, and this city has established, over eighteen years and at two shillings the head, that a person having written something down is not an instrument."

They sat there for a while.

"Whose alarm is it," said Orrin Vale.

"It is a duty on the office. The office is a warden's office. The warden has announced that she stands down on the first of the ninth, in advance, in a room, in a book, and the duty goes with the office or it goes nowhere, and **that is the fourth thing and there is no chair in this room for it.**"

---

**And then it was the forty-one seconds, and neither of them had decided when it started, and only one of them knew it had.**

What it was, afterwards, as far as either of them could reconstruct it: the bench had his left hand on it. The frame had been moving. Pell had said something about the third station and about a chair. And then there was a gap, and the gap was not a blank.

**It was a room he had walked through.** He could give the two ends of it exactly. He could give the second before the gap: Pell's hand arriving on his shoulder from behind and to the left, and the words *the third station*. He could give the first after it: Pell's hand leaving his shoulder, and the stopwatch in Pell's hand turned face up, and the room quiet, and a smell of hot brass.

Everything in between was **full.** It was not missing. There was a corridor in it, and a door, and a smell of paper, and a thought about a rate notice, and they were all there and they were in the wrong order and they were not his in the way that a memory of the morning is his, and the whole of the forty-one seconds was **accounted for and in the wrong sequence, and he had not known for one second of it that anything had happened.**

"That is forty-one seconds," said Ivo Pell.

"How long."

"Forty-one seconds, from my hand going on to my hand coming off, and I counted, and I counted because I have had my hand on the shoulders of four men in that chair and I have never had to count before and I counted because I could feel that it was not ordinary." He put the watch away. "And you did not lose them. That is the thing to write down, because it is the thing that will frighten you later and it is the opposite of the thing I would have expected. **You did not lose forty-one seconds. You lost the order of forty-one seconds. Every one of them is still there and they are all in the wrong place and you have never in your life noticed an order being wrong.**"

"That is a very small thing to say to a man in a warm room."

"It is the whole of what this profession does to a person and you have three of them on a page already and they were all different, and this one is a different one again, and I am not going to soften it, because I told you in a pump room in June that I do not teach men who take a toll, and you have not taken a toll, and you are about to, and the only service I am going to render you is to tell you the price out loud while it is happening so that nothing that happens afterwards can be called a surprise."

He wrote it in the shop book that night, in the room over the chandler's shop, with the reason under it, and the reason is the practice and the count is the thing.

> *Sixth of the eighth. The Question Hall, a room down a corridor, the third station.*
> *A keeper's toll, and the fourth. The count of keeper's tolls in my life is now **four**: the minute in the second month of last year, out of an empty reserve, which I cannot account for; two days of the right hand in the fourth month; thirty-eight minutes in the tenth, which took the judgement and not the hearing; and this.*
> *This one: forty-one seconds, in which I lost the order of them and did not know that I had. I can give the two ends exactly. I can give everything in the middle. The middle is in the wrong sequence and it is full, and that is worse and not better, and a man who could tell he had lost something could put it in a book.*
> *And it has taken, and it does not give back: **the ability to hold the order of assembly of a movement in my head without writing it down.***
> *A hundred and eighty seconds of noise for a moral question, and forty-one seconds for a named act, and the cheap one is the one that has cost me a thing I use every working day of my life. I have written both numbers and I am not going to look at them again, as instructed.*

---

**And the toll was filed, and there was a body toll due, and the body toll was not paid by anybody, and that is how it is inadmissible and that is also true and correct.**

The toll is filed at the counter, on a slip, in a clerk's hand, and the clerk is the one of twenty-six who gave him the three requirements in eleven minutes on the eleventh of the seventh and who did not look up. The toll is **one district minute and one witness interval**, and the district minute went to the Lower Weirs, because the Lower Weirs is the affected district and the toll is always paid by the affected district, and nobody in that building had to be asked and nobody in that building could be.

**And the body toll is not paid.** If a Reading is properly run, the same question costs a body an interval as well, and this one was not properly run, because a Reading needs three witnesses and the third has to be a person of the affected district, and there is no standing and nobody to put in the chair. So the two hundred and forty minutes of slack across eleven patients and three staff that a sister on the third floor of Green Span has on a card with a date on it and four names on it **was not charged to the district and was not charged to anybody, and the hour that was actually spent will be spent again in November when a man has to decide a thing on a gate at Weir One and there is no form on which the ward's time is in the argument.**

And that is the sentence Ivo Pell said with his hand on the frame on the Saturday, in that room, and Orrin Vale wrote it down and did not hear it at the time and heard it in about three weeks.

"You have paid a minute of the Lower Weirs' clocks and you have paid forty-one seconds of your own, and there is a body in a hospital on the third floor of that district who would have paid a second interval and has not, and **she is not going to be told, and she is not going to be asked, and that is correct and it is also the reason your four lines are in a book in this building and not on a board in a ward.** Everything about that is lawful. Write it down."

---

**On Monday the eighth of the eighth month at about eleven in the morning he found out what had been taken, and he found it out on a bench, and it took him about four minutes, and it was not dramatic, and he told nobody for a day.**

He was stripping a movement. It is a clock of about 1880 that came in on the Friday and it is a good movement and it wants doing and the customer wants it by the Wednesday.

He took the plates off, and the train wheels out, and the barrel, and he got to the place where the third wheel and the second pinion and the great wheel go in, and he stopped.

**He could not remember whether the third wheel goes in before the second pinion is fitted, or after, or whether it makes any difference at all and he is only thinking it does.**

He has assembled that movement, and four hundred like it, and in this order, in his head, on a bench, standing up, since he was nineteen years old. He did not have a book with him. He did not have a book with him because he has never needed a book with him, and there is no book, and there is nothing on any shelf in that shop with the order of assembly of a movement in it, because **it has never once been written down, and a thing that has never been written down is a thing that is in one man's head, and that man is a man in a shop with a lease to Michaelmas and no licence.**

He went and got a pencil and a new notebook — a cheap one, about eleven inches by four, the kind a man buys for a shilling at the end of a counter because he has to write something down on somebody's counter while the somebody is looking at him — and he wrote the order of assembly of that movement out in pencil from the top of a clean page, forty-one lines of it, in about seven minutes, and then he did the work.

**It took four minutes longer than it would have done, and the customer got the clock on the Wednesday and was charged twenty-two shillings, and nobody in this city will ever know that anything happened on that bench.**

And the second narrow book is on the shelf behind the bench with a pencil bound into it with a piece of tape, and it is the second book in his life that is not a form and not a return and not in anybody's office, and the first is the grey ledger with two thousand pages in it.

> *Eighth of the eighth. A movement of 1880, four minutes lost, twenty-one lines of pencil, a shilling for the notebook.*
> *From this week I write the order of operations before I begin anything. Not because I have to. Because the man who is fifty-eight in this trade in about twenty-five years will not remember that he used to know it, and **the whole of what this profession does to a person is that it makes him unable to imagine a man who did not do it that way, and it is not dramatic and nobody writes about it.**
> *Nineteen orders in four days. I have not needed one of them. I have reached for the book nine times and had the page in front of me and written out an order I already knew by heart, and there is not one shilling of value in nineteen pages of pencil at four pence the sheet, and I am going to keep doing it, and the knowing that I am going to keep doing it is the part that is new and the part that is not a gift.*

And on the Tuesday the ninth, at the top of the stairs, going down to the ward's, a woman of nineteen came past him with a folded piece of card in her apron and a pencil behind her ear and did not stop, and he looked at her for about two seconds longer than a man looks at a person on a stair, and she did not look back, and **that is the whole of what Chapter 67 is for, and neither of them ever mentioned it.**
