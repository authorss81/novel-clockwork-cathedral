# Chapter 53 — Who Got Vance Killed

The room over the chandler's shop on Weir Lane has a table that is too long for the room and eleven chairs, and on the Wednesday morning of the fifteenth of the sixth month there were eleven people at the table and two more in the room, and one of the two was twenty and had not been invited and had come, and nobody asked him to leave, and nobody thanked him for coming either.

---

The note had been at the shop on Verge Lane on the Tuesday, folded in four, in a young man's upright hand, and it had eleven lines on it and no signature, and at the bottom it said *Thursday*, and the Thursday was two days after it arrived, and it was a Wednesday, and he did not go on the Thursday, and that was the first thing Teo Arden ever did to him that he did not see coming and he thought about it for a week.

Wenna Pruitt wrote the other note, on the back of a rate notice, and hers was signed.

> *Wednesday, the fifteenth, half past nine in the morning, the room over the shop.*
> *I have not called this meeting. I have put a room in it because a room is what I have and a room is not the same thing as a meeting.*
> *Mr Arden has told me he intends to be present. I have not asked him to be and I have not asked him not to be and I am not going to pretend to you afterwards that I did one of those two things.*
> *It is a chandler's shop and he does not know we are up here and he has not asked what it is about.*

---

The eleven were: Bevin Sarr, who had the front and the board and the night book. Wenna Pruitt, who had the council's books and no money. Ivy Pike, who signed for the Weir One gate. Bessie Crane, of the Bellweather Road laundry, thirty-one women on a Friday. Hanna Stroud, landlord of nine blocks, who pays her own renters on the first and has never been two months late. A night porter of the Green Row kind whose name is on the works' own maintenance line in four-inch capitals. A man of sixty-eight called Alder who cannot read at the height a thing is chalked and has been on the night side of this ward since before the war. Dilly Amnersley, of 4B at 12 to 20 Ferrywright Row, who is sixty-six. The chandler, who does not know. A woman of about sixty with a shorthand book who is not in the council. And Orrin Vale, who has no licence and two benches and a lease and was asked to come by a note with no signature on it.

Ada Vance was there because Ada Vance is thirteen and lives over the second court and had been left in the room by an aunt who had to go to work at six.

---

He said it after about forty minutes, and he said it standing up, which nobody else had done.

"Right," said Teo Arden. "You have got a number on a wall and you have got an office's four pages, and everybody in this room has read them or heard them read, and I am going to say the one thing that none of you are going to say today, and then I am going to sit down, and I would like it minuted if anybody in this room is minuting, and I know nobody is, and I would like it minuted."

Nobody was minuting. The woman with the shorthand book had a book shut on the table in front of her and both hands flat on it, and she did not open it, and that is the whole of what the room is for.

"You filed a question," said Teo Arden.

"I did."

"Out of the Quarter, because the Quarter holds the most and has the least to lose, and you said so yourself at the time and you wrote it in the same hand you signed the question in."

"Yes."

"And it charged three minutes of the Quarter's clocks, which is nothing, which is a rounding error, which is a district with a full reserve paying a rounding error for an answer about somebody else's bridge." He was not shouting and he had not come close to shouting, and the room was extremely quiet. "And the answer was right. The bridge stood. That is the whole of what your question did, and it worked, and everybody in this city knows it worked, and there is a notice on a door in a pump house four miles from here that says it worked and every word of it is true."

"The valve was shut," said Orrin Vale. "The Reading said the valve was shut."

"It said the valve was shut and the valve was shut," said Teo Arden, "and a man went down a ladder to shut it again, and the pump that should have stopped did not stop. And you know all of that, and I know all of that, and I have known all of that since I was seventeen and he was teaching me."

"Then say the rest of it."

"I am saying the rest of it. It is this." He put his hand flat on the table, and his hand was twenty years old and there is a week's filing in it and no calluses. "The model was right. Perry was wrong. That is not a thing anybody has to be told, it is a thing everybody in this room has understood since the eighteenth of the first month and nobody has said out loud because it is unbearable to say. The model was right and a man went down a ladder and died.

"And you want to know how that is possible, and the answer is that it is possible because the model did not have him in it."

Nobody in the room said anything.

"Not a margin. Not a line in a footer. Him. There is no version of the seventeenth of the first month in which there is a Perry Vance in the model, and there is no version of that in which the answer came out with a different number, and there is no version of any of it in which your question was the wrong question. I want that said in this room by somebody other than me, because I have been saying it to myself since the eighteenth of the first month and I have never once been able to make it sound like a discovery."

He turned round then, and looked at Wenna Pruitt, who had not spoken.

"I am not saying this at her and I am not saying it at him. I am saying it at the table, because the only way anybody finds out that a model is wrong is by being in it, and the only people who were ever in it were the ones a form paid two shillings a year to take out, and he is the man who put his own money into the same form on the first of May and did not tell one of you, and I know he did it because I read the counter's day book over a woman's shoulder in a queue at a tram stop in May, and I did not say anything then either, and it is six weeks now, and I would like somebody in this room to have that number, which is six weeks."

"Wenna," said Orrin Vale.

"No," said Wenna Pruitt. "I have not got anything ready and I am not going to pretend I have. Mr Arden is right about the two shillings and he is wrong that the form paid them, and both of those can be said in one sentence and I would like somebody to say both of them in one sentence, and nobody is going to, so I will."

She had her hands on the table too.

"The form did not pay anybody. The form took them out. A district that has proved what it did is a district with a return, and a district with a return is a district whose rates are made, and the two shillings is what it costs to be in the book and the rest of the bill lands on the same thirty-one women in my laundry and on the same people in this room, and Mr Arden knows that, and he has known it since February, and he is not wrong that they were taken out and he is wrong about the word *paid*, and if we spend the next hour on that we will have spent it well and we will not have got anywhere."

"Then what has he got to do with it," said Teo Arden, not angrily, genuinely asking.

"He has got the number and he has got the office's four pages, and neither of them can be acted on, and he cannot be, and that is the whole of my answer and it took me a long time to arrive at it and I would have arrived at it sooner if anybody had ever put a form in front of me and said *this is the same form*."

---

It went on for another two hours and it was not a row, and nothing was decided, and four people said things that were the best things said in the room and three of those were people who were not going to get their way.

Hanna Stroud, who owns nine blocks, put it as an insurance question and got nowhere, because the four pages on the board are not a document and the returns a district can produce are *unavailable* in the eyes of an insurer who has asked for them twice, and the officer she has paid four pounds ten to write to her has written back that the district's returns are not held by the district.

Bessie Crane put the money on the table before anybody else would, because she had it in her head and it had been in her head since the Monday: **thirty-one women, and if the hours go in the return then the rate goes up on a laundry that is already a penny a sheet under the trade price, and the rate does not care whose hours they are.** She said it in about fifteen seconds and then said, "I am not asking anybody to do anything about it. I am telling you the number so that nobody in this room can later say nobody told them the number."

The man of sixty-eight, Alder, said that he has wound the night side of Weir Two by hand since he was thirty-one, that nobody has ever paid him for it, that there is no form for it, and that when the front went dark for six days in May he was the one who went round with a bucket and a lamp, and that he would do it again, and that he was not saying it for a reason. And then, after a pause: "But I would like it written down that it happened. That is all. I would like it written down that I did it. There is a board and a woman keeps it and she is the only person in this city who has ever let me write on it."

And Dilly Amnersley, who is sixty-six and who came, said almost nothing, and the one thing she said was to ask whether anybody had told the woman at the second court, whose standpipe had been running since the Tuesday into a bucket.

Nobody had. Orrin Vale had been in the district on the Thursday and had told nobody.

"Right," said Dilly Amnersley, and that is all she said, and six weeks earlier she had been asked what she wanted for four clocks, and had been listened to at greater length than she is being listened to now.

---

Ada Vance was asked, near the end, by Wenna Pruitt, and it was a good question and it was asked properly, which is not a thing that happens to her very often.

"Ada, we are not going to ask you about the model. We are going to ask about your dad at work, and you can say as much or as little as you want, and nobody is going to write it down."

"He was on the nights," said Ada Vance.

"I know he was on the nights."

"He got up at ten past three because the men got up at ten past three, and he said you could get up at ten past two and have your tea by half past three but he didn't because he said if the whole crew's up at ten past three then the whole crew's up at ten past three and if you're the one that isn't then you're the one that has to explain it." She was looking at the tablecloth. "And he used to bring the tally back and put it on the dresser and my mam said put it away and he said it's the only thing I've got, and then he'd put it away."

"Who put the other one away?"

"The other one?"

Ada Vance looked up, and did not understand the question, and said so, and Wenna Pruitt said, "Never mind. Thank you, Ada," and Ada Vance said, "I'm not in trouble am I," and Bevin Sarr said, "You are not in trouble, love, and you can go down and play if you want," and Ada Vance said she would rather stay.

She stayed for another fifty minutes. She did not understand about half of what was said and she understood the other half better than the people who had said it.

---

The disclosure argument had moved and it moved in a way nobody had planned, and it is the third thing and not the first, and it is the part of this that is not settled.

"You have put a number on a wall in a pump house," said Teo Arden, to Orrin Vale, not to Wenna, and she let him. "That is the right thing and I am not going to argue about it. Here is what happens next. Somebody comes. Not the works, not the hall, somebody with a badge and a form — and the badge does not matter, the form does. They ask the woman who keeps the board what the board is for, and she says it is a night book, and it is, and there is no line on any form in this city for *a night book that a woman keeps on a wall*. And they take a copy. And then there is a copy in a building, and the copy is a document, and the document is a list of numbers, and the numbers have no names on them because she will not put names on them, and now a building has a document that says the Lower Weirs is out of time and no person in it at all."

"That is right," said Orrin Vale.

"Then here is mine," said Wenna Pruitt.

She had her book open. She had been not saying anything for two hours and she had the book open.

"He is right that it is a list of routes, and I have said that about a printed page since February and I am not going to pretend I have not said it. A chalk number in a pump house is a number nobody has to answer for, and he is right, and that is the only door in this city that is not shut.

"And here is mine, and it is the same sentence with the other end of it. A list of addresses is a list of routes. And a list of routes is a list of visits. And a person decides which door gets a knock. Not the person who is on the list, because the person on the list is the person being knocked on, and the person being knocked on does not get to say no, that is the definition of it.

"And when the list is a list of nineteen houses in a street, then the twenty houses in that street who are not on the list are the ones who have to find out what happened, from somebody else, afterwards. And the twenty are not on the list because the person who made the list did not know they were there."

"Which is a bad thing," said Teo Arden.

"It is a bad thing," said Wenna Pruitt, "and so is the other one, and neither of us has a way of holding both, and that is not a thing that is going to be settled on the fifteenth of the sixth month in a room over a shop that is not ours."

They had said in the second month of the year that they would not be the same arithmetic in a year. It is eight months away and it has not come yet and it is going to come, in a different room, in worse weather, and one of them is going to be wrong and both of them are going to be right.

---

At about twenty past twelve the chandler came up with a message that he was sorry, that he was sorry, that he would not be two minutes, and went down again.

Orrin Vale said the eleven words.

"I am not going to answer him," he said. "He is right about every part of it and there is one thing I can put on the table and it is not an answer to what he said, and it is the answer I gave to a hall in April and it is in a file in that hall and nobody has read it.

"When the hall asked me for a return for Weir Two and I could not give them one, I wrote four lines back and eleven of those words are: *that no person in the district was asked.* That is the answer I sent. That is the whole of what I have got, and it is a sentence about a form and it is the only sentence I have ever written that I expect to outlive me, and I gave it to a building because the building was the only thing that would hold it.

"And I have been sitting at this table for three hours working out how to tell eleven people that I know the answer to his question and that knowing it is worth exactly nothing, because I said it in April, in writing, to a counter, and forty-one men went back on a form in May for a year out of my own pocket and it did not cost one of them a minute and it did not bring one of them back."

Hanna Stroud said, "It cost the ward a rate," not unkindly, as a person stating a fact about property.

"Yes," he said. "One shilling and tenpence in the pound against nine pence, on forty-eight thousand pounds, two thousand six hundred on the ward, thirteen shillings a year on a house of twelve pounds, and nobody in this ward knows I paid for the form that caused it, and they are not going to find out from me, and I have made my peace with that in a way that I am not going to describe in this room."

Nobody described anything. The chandler's floorboards did what they do.

---

Then Teo Arden put a folded paper on the table, and it was the note from the shop, and he did not give it to him, he put it down in the middle of the table and stepped back, and he said:

"That is eleven things. That is the list. I wrote it in about a quarter of an hour on the Tuesday and it is not clever and it is not a programme and there is no meeting behind it and there is no minute book and there is going to be no president, and I am not going to be anybody's anything.

"You are not to come down here and find things out. You are not to walk the branch with a notebook. You are not to ask anybody a single question about this district, because the moment you ask one question, one of the people in this room has to answer it, and from then on this is a room with a man in it who asked.

"So: you turn up. At the hour. On the day. You do what the person on the list tells you to do, and if the person on the list tells you to carry something, you carry it, and if the person on the list tells you to stand somewhere, you stand there, and if the person on the list tells you to go home, you go home, and you do not write anything down and you do not say anything about having been here afterwards."

He turned round and looked at the table.

"And I am not going to be anybody's anything, and that is not a modesty, it is an arrangement. **I am choosing the men and I am not giving the orders.** You will find out on the day who gives them, and it will not be me, and if anybody tells you I gave them one, they are lying and you can say so."

He sat down.

"The first thing is a basement," he said. "Number fourteen, Ferrywright Row. There is a cellar under it that has been under about four feet of standing water since the flood in the first month and nobody has looked at it since the flood, and the water is coming out of a wall, and I know it is coming out of a wall because I have been in there twice and I did not go in the second time."

"That is a fire exit," said Bevin Sarr.

"Then it is a fire exit that has been under four feet of water since the twelfth of the first month," said Teo Arden, "and this district has been out of time by more than an hour at one end of a branch since the seventeenth of the fourth, and I am not a pump warden and I do not know what a fire exit has to do with a clock. That is the first thing on the list and you are to go down there on the Saturday with a lamp and a bucket and you are not to tell anybody what you find until we have all been down there."

It is on the list. It is item one of eleven, and it is not what any of the eleven people in that room expected, and the reason Teo Arden knew to put a cellar at the top of a list of eleven things is that he has been in it twice, and the second time he did not go in, and there is a difference between those two facts that he has not explained to anybody and has not been asked about.
