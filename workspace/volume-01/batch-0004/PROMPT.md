## The batch in one paragraph

Chapter 25 made a water schedule binding and unappealable, and Chapters 26 to 30 itemised what it cost and then watched a district decide that it would not speak about it. This batch is the consequence of a decision not to speak: **clocks begin to slip, and the slippage is not a mystery, it is a network carrying a schedule that was designed without the people on it.** The repair work acquires a deadline and a constituency, which is the phrase the whole book is about, because a constituency is a body of people a decision can be *held to*, and the Weirs have just spent four weeks learning that they cannot be. A woman who diverted a reserve in **March**, before the story's first month, to put water into seventy-one houses has that diversion documented, with a schedule and a named beneficiary, by an office that has the best possible reason to do it and no interest in her at all, and she defends it as a defence of jobs and she is right about the jobs. The people who stole the labour book steal hardware instead, and it costs a tenant block something real. And the Cathedral does the thing institutions actually do to scandals they cannot answer: **it absorbs it, into a category, with a schedule and a budget line.**

## Batch goal, shape, and turns

- **The opening:** the slippage is found by a clockmaker who cannot hear a movement any more and has to guess, and the guessing has a shape. Clocks in the Quarter and the Weirs disagree with each other, and the disagreement moves, which is the word nobody in the book has yet been given, because nobody has a form for it. **It is the batch's job to put the shape of slippage on the page as an ordinary, mechanical, man-made thing, and to make Orrin the only person in the city who can say so and the only person nobody will believe.**
- **The middle:** Sel Kade's diversion, documented. **The document is correct.** The defence is a defence of jobs. Both of those are the point.
- **The cost:** the Loose Minutes take back hardware, and a tenant block pays, and Teo is right about the hardware and wrong about the block and neither of them can be said to be the villain.
- **The end:** the Directorate's counter-move, which is institutional and impersonal, and Orrin's own visit to a door on the Rise, on his own, where he is not admitted, and which he tells nobody about.

## Style

Rain-lit, tactile, grounded in repair work and in rooms where paper is read out loud. **This batch's measure is a tape measure and a stopwatch, not a metaphor.** Orrin's competence must be visible in the doing: he finds the shape of a moving error by timing four clocks against one watch, in a street, in the rain, because that is what a clockmaker does and because nothing else in the city can do it. He is not a scholar of politics and can misread a room; in this batch he is in rooms he is not a member of more often than he is in his own, and the reader should feel the difference every time. His interiority is procedural — he counts, he measures, he works out what a thing will do — and it should sound like a craftsman thinking. Show information through work, conversation and consequence; do not deliver lore. Let the important moments breathe. Keep paragraphs to two to six sentences, with a one-line paragraph only as a deliberate beat. Vary rhythm; do not lean on *held, steady, plain, counted, breathed, wrote, nodded*. The System is the Consequence Engine: one sparse physical notice in plain declarative language, at most one per chapter, and this batch should not need one at all.

**Chapter 39 is the one to get right.** The reader has watched a correct answer save a bridge and leave out a crew, and then watched the same thing happen again, lawfully, in the correct form, to a category. **If the reclassification is dramatised as a conspiracy the volume has no argument; if it is dramatised as a joke the book has no fear.** It is a competent institution doing the best available thing with an inconvenient fact, in type that is well set, on a schedule that is published, against a budget line that is real.
