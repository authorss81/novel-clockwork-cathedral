## Volume number and span

Volume 03, Chapters 101 through 150. Five batches of ten. The name is fixed by `outline/series.md` and may not be changed. **The name is the market's unit and not its slogan: a token is a hundred minutes, the smallest lot there is, and a hundred minutes is four months of a district that is losing fifty seconds a night.**

## Central pressure

**A market has opened in the buying and selling of minutes that official tolls took out of people, and the instrument it uses is a piece of brass the size of a half-crown.**

An **hour token** is a small brass marker, about twenty-two millimetres across and three thick, with a raised rim and a hole, stamped on the head with a district letter and the figure 100 and on the rim with a year and a die number, cut from the surplus plate of a public clock that held more surplus than it needed at a return. It is an honest object with an honest history and a hundred and forty years of law behind it, and **the whole of what the law has ever said about a token is what it is made of.** It may be held, cut, sold and bought. **What a token is for is not in any form in this city.**

A token is a credit on a clock's own surplus. It can be spent in the clock it was cut from, to hold a wheel off a dead escapement for a stated time, and that is what the hall's rule was written for. But a civic clock on a line shares its rotation with the rest of the line, and a hundred minutes of surplus put into a clock in the Clockwork Quarter comes out of the Quarter's margin and goes into the network, and the network gives it to the next clock that is behind. **The market works in one direction, and the direction is out of the most synchronized district in the city into the districts that have none of its margin, and the Quarter is not being robbed and the Weirs is not stealing, and nobody breaks a rule, and the loss lands where it has landed for nineteen years, which is nowhere with a name on it.**

**A token costs four shillings and sixpence and buys back a hundred and twenty nights at fifty seconds a night, which is four months. A night of drift costs about half a penny. A year of drift on one clock is five hours and four minutes and ten seconds, and a district's clocks in this city are between two and four hours out, and the price of the whole of it is about thirteen shillings, and the two figures have never been in one sentence in this city.**

The volume is about what happens to a city of people who are buying back the time official tolls took from them, and **the answer is that the buying is the injury being dressed, and the dressing works, and it is unlawful in its purpose and lawful in its premises, and the line between the two is a hole in a form about a hundred and forty years old, and there is no instrument anywhere in Morrowfen that can say which side of the line a given minute is on.**

## Starting power level

**Stage 3, Choice trace, reached in Volume 02 under Ivo Pell's supervision, and Stage 4, Civic rehearsal, first made in Volume 02 and used four times in a month.** Orrin can sit in a room with two operators and take a team verdict. Everything he learns that way is inadmissible until three witnesses have signed and a toll has been filed, and **in this volume nothing is ever filed.**

**Ending power level:** **Stage 4, usable and not stable.** Civic Rehearsal has stopped being a private skill and become a shared method he does not control. He can sit in a room with operators he cannot overrule and take their verdict over his own. **The rooms are improvised, there is no refusal procedure to appeal to, and he spends district-minutes he has no authority to spend, and the volume says on the page that this is what a method becomes when it is handed to people and that it is not an improvement.**

**The growth is not a power.** It is that he stops being the instrument. In Volume 01 he put his name under a number he had no standing to sign because there was nowhere else to put it. In Volume 02 there was a room and he was not on it. **In Volume 03 there are forty rooms and he is in none of them, and the volume's last scene is a man in a kitchen who is not a clockmaker telling him that the clockmaker is not the person to ask.**

## The rules of the machine that matter in this volume

Carried from `bible/power-system.md` unchanged. The ones this volume leans on:

1. **The question spindle accepts a concrete named action only.** The volume's central question cannot be *should people be allowed to buy their own time back*, because that is not a thing anybody could physically do. It has to be a token, a die, a punch, a pitch, a clock, a reserve meter, a fare table, a bus, a dose.
2. **A Reading has four parts** — trace, pressure, blind margin, toll — and the blind margin is printed smaller than the operator's signature. **There is at most one Reading notice in the whole of this volume and it is in batch 0004, and it is a real notice with a real blind margin on it, and the whole volume's plot turns on a document nobody forged.**
3. **A Reading is valid only for the conditions and records present when it was made.** The volume's antagonist pressure is an assurance copy: a quick unofficial re-run made to calm a frightened floor. **It is not a Reading, it carries no filed toll, and the word is the Directorate's and is said with a straight face.**
4. **Where a toll lands is a function of reserve, not of consequence.** Not used for a toll in this volume; used for its whole corollary, which is **that a district with a full reserve is the only district in the city from which minutes can be taken, and that is not a punishment, it is arithmetic, and the Quarter is not guilty and the Weirs is not innocent.**
5. **Body tolls are recorded as *calibration strain*, with no name and no duration,** except in the pumping ward's public ledger, which carries a name and a duration, and **the first duration is empty on purpose and is not a mystery to be solved.** In this volume a woman of about thirty-four on Green Span has a chart with the phrase on it and no duration, and **no chapter may put one there.**
6. **A private sense is not a Reading.** No blind margin, no record, an unfiled cost. **The count of Orrin's keeper's tolls is four at the start of this volume and does not move in batches 0001 and 0002, and the two places in this volume where he is tempted to use one are the two places where somebody stops him.**
7. **No certainty permit exists at the start of this volume, and it is created in the volume's own aftermath, out of the Reading strike, by a frightened Assembly acting under the Director of the Cathedral's authority.** No character in batches 0001 to 0004 may act as though one is normal, refer to one as existing, or wish for one. **The reasoning that produces it must be on the page in Chapter 145 and the reader must be able to argue with it.**

## Escalation sequence

**Step one — the object and the price (Chapters 101–110).** A token is on a bench and a customer does not know what it is. An arcade in the Quarter. A hospital clock nurse buys a hundred minutes in a kitchen. A die drawer with nine district punches in it and blank space. A licensing officer who is right about the law and wrong about the harm, because the whole of what the law has ever said about a token is what it is made of. A broker's rate card, which is the City's own published daily numbers arranged to answer one question. A crowd on an ordinary Tuesday. A woman of thirty-four on the fourth floor of a boardinghouse who has paid four shillings and sixpence for a hundred minutes of brass that was not there. **And a refusal, in the council's own words, binding one man and nobody else.** Ends on a broker's book and a list of streets that is a list of doors.

**Step two — the rooms and the hospital (Chapters 111–120).** The answer to Anja Voss's letter, given in Volume 02 and unanswered. **The sealed rooms are opened and what is in them is not a mechanism, and the machine is seen and not named, and the unit on the floor below the north gallery is described by a woman who signed a paper about it and does not know what is in it.** A pop-up room in a back room over a shop with three clocks and a hand-crank and a hole in the window, and the first time the words *reading room* are used in this volume by a person who means it, and nobody corrects him, and the chapter says what has changed and what has not. A hospital ward's medicine times. Imani Rook in a room, counting her ward. **A business with a body: a limited company in a Weirs back room with an objects clause that would go into a public register for ever.** Ends with the market's numbers arriving somewhere they cannot be un-arrived.

**Step three — the midpoint, in a kitchen, and the refusal of the moral position (Chapters 121–130).** **Mara Venn's audit of the market, lodged and published with the working, proves that for a set of households in four streets the market is not criminal and is not even irregular: it is the only instrument in the city by which they can buy back time that official tolls took from them, and the arithmetic that proves it is the arithmetic of this volume, and it is the same arithmetic that proves a counterfeit works.** The book refuses to let the reader keep the position it started with. **Nobody is raided. The batch ends with the Loose Minutes refusing to distribute what the audit produced, for reasons of their own, and both sides being right.** And the certainty permit's ground is prepared by a frightened hospital asking for a number it can act on.

**Step four — the falsified notice and the street that bought counterfeit hope (Chapters 131–140).** **Corvin Hale falsifies a Reading to move a labour contract, using a real notice with a real blind margin attached, and he does not forge the document, he re-runs the question, and his method works because the law already permits it.** The correction is Orrin's and it is public and it costs him the only thing he has left, which is the ability to say a thing in a room and be believed: **it exposes which neighbourhoods have been buying counterfeit hope, including the street Ada Vance lives on, and Ada is thirteen and is in it and is asked nothing and nobody comforts her.** The one Reading notice of the volume is on the page here. **Orrin's licence expires on Sunday the nineteenth of the third month and the renewal is a determination and not a renewal, and the class-three term is unaltered and it is restored only as a provisional, and the day it is restored is the day the hall's answer to his answer is read out again.**

**Step five — the strike, the cost, and the permit (Chapters 141–150).** **A coordinated Reading strike leaves the city without forecasts for a day.** The network holds through ordinary repair work by ordinary people: a bus run on a conductor's own watch, a ward kept on two watches, a gate turned by hand, a market that shuts on the Tuesday because nobody could tell anybody what time it was. **Several workers pay permanent hour-sickness doing it and the volume's cost is human and is never resolved and is not paid off in the last chapter.** The Assembly establishes a temporary public toll fund and licenses community Reading rooms. Mara loses her post for the audit and does not appeal. **And a frightened Assembly, acting under the Director of the Cathedral's authority, issues the first certainty permit: an official may cite a Reading and act without public review. The reasoning is on the page in Chapter 145 over about nine pages and the reader should be able to argue with it, and the permit stays, and it is the last institutional fact of the volume.**

## Midpoint reversal — Chapter 126

**Mara Venn's audit of the market is published with the working, and the working shows that for about two hundred and forty households in four streets the market is the only way to buy back time that official tolls took from them, and that no instrument in this city can do it lawfully, and that the reason is a hole in a form about a hundred and forty years old.**

**The reversal is of the reader's position, not of the facts.** Volume 03 opens with a market, and the market is a small criminal trade in brass, and the reader is entitled to be suspicious of it, and is. **Chapter 126 says the suspicion is correct about the counterfeits, the warrants and the man in the arcade, and wrong about the four streets, and it does not say the reader was wrong, because the reader was not asked anything and was given a true subset.**

**After Chapter 126 the shape of the volume changes.** Before it, a man is trying to find out who is stealing time. After it, a district has learned that **the time was taken first and the buying came second and the two facts are in different buildings and neither is in a form**, and the argument is no longer *is it stolen* but *who is holding the ledger, and what happens to a person who is found to have bought a minute they were entitled to.*

## Volume climax (Chapters 145–148)

**A coordinated Reading strike, called by nobody who admits to calling it, and the city without a forecast for a day.** The strike is not a political act and the Loose Minutes are not an organisation and there is no committee; **it is two hundred and forty people independently deciding, on the same morning, that they will not be the person who holds a machine for a question they were not asked.** It is the first thing in this book that happens because of the right to refuse and it is the first thing in this book that goes wrong.

**It works.** Water, trams, gates and two hospitals keep running through the day on ordinary repair work by ordinary people, and the day's work is written down afterwards in about nine books in nine buildings, and **the nine books do not agree.** **And several workers pay permanent hour-sickness doing it** — a night sister of nineteen who is left with a gap she will not describe, a signal fitter who cannot hold a fine tool, a conductor who has lost an afternoon and does not know what she did with it — **and the volume never resolves this and the last chapter does not pay it off and the Council of Hours in Volume 14 inherits the claim.**

**And the aftermath is a permit.** Not because anybody is wicked. Because a hospital director, a district works engineer and a tramways clerk are three people who each need a number they can act on at seven in the morning, and there is no instrument that produces one in an hour, and the only instrument in the city that can is a Reading, and the Reading takes a day, and somebody writes a four-line instrument that says an official may cite a Reading and act without waiting for anybody. **The reasoning is sympathetic, specific, and wrong, and the volume does not tell the reader it is wrong; it tells the reader what it costs, in Chapter 149, in a ward.**

## Concrete resolution

All six of these must be on the page in Chapters 141–150. A volume does not end on a mood.

1. **A temporary public toll fund**, established by the Assembly, out of a levy on districts with a non-nil return, **for the maintenance of clocks in districts that cannot afford their own maintenance.** It is temporary, it has a date, it is not a fund anybody can draw a district's water out of, and **it is administered by a body that is not a district time council, which does not exist yet and is not created here.**
2. **Community Reading rooms are licensed** — the first licensed Reading rooms in Morrowfen — with a published rule that a room must state its question in writing before the hour, must name the people of the affected district who may refuse it, and **may not be asked a question about anything outside the district its hourlines reach.** The licensing is fought over and the rule that wins is the refusal rule, because it is the only clause anybody can be held to.
3. **Mara Venn loses her official post for the audit**, in a determination of four lines, on the ground that an office which audits cannot also be the office a person appeals to, and **she does not appeal it and does not go quietly and is not thanked and is not given another post inside the building.**
4. **Orrin's licence is restored only as a provisional**, a class-three licence held on conditions, with a date and a review, and **the conditions are that he may not certify a district toll, that he may not enter a sealed room without a person named who may refuse, and that he may not be alone with a machine he has not been asked about.**
5. **The permits stay.** The Assembly does not repeal them, because the argument for repeal loses by nine votes, and the volume says on the page what those nine votes were and who was in the room.
6. **The hour market is not closed and not legalised.** Two hundred and forty households in four streets are left holding a hundred minutes each on the strength of an audit, **and the audit says the instrument is a brass marker and the law says nothing about what it is for, and nobody in this volume invents an instrument to say, and the word for the thing nobody invents is not coined here.**

## New question for the next volume

**Who benefits from turning a public tool into a licensed one, and what did the original safeguards make impossible?** Volume 03 creates the permit out of its own catastrophe and licenses the rooms out of its own disaster, and **the volume's last institutional act is to make two unlicensed practices into licensed ones on the same week, for the same reason, by the same people, and neither of them is the villain and both of them are the reason Volume 04's flood plan can be approved with nobody from the Weirs among the protected.** Volume 04 must be able to open on the reader understanding that the last thing Morrowfen did was make a licence.
