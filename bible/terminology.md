# Terminology Bible

Use these words consistently. The system is physical and civic; do not replace it with unexplained game language.

## Core civic terms

### Morrowfen

The modern estuary city where the series takes place. Its people live, work, trade, and maintain infrastructure independently of the Cathedral.

### Cathedral of Consequence

The public building on Cathedral Rise that houses the Consequence Engine. It is civic infrastructure with religious and political symbolism, not a church that owns the city.

### Consequence Engine

The machine that runs a Reading. It uses the Civic Hours network and physical models to show likely consequences of a concrete proposed action.

### Local Reading instrument

A small, manually calibrated Loom that examines one district's connected hourlines. It can return a Trace, Pressure, Blind margin, and Toll for a concrete local action, but it cannot use the central Dark Reservoir, the Censor Gate, or the First Hour. A local instrument requires the same three witnesses, draws its toll from the district it is connected to, and has no binding authority. It is a repair and planning tool, not a replacement citywide oracle.

### Civic Time Keeper

An independent public officeholder responsible for keeping the Council of Hours honest about tolls, refusals, and emergency records. The office is not subordinate to the Cathedral Directorate or the operators. The first Civic Time Keeper is Mara Venn after the final reform.

### Civic Hours

Morrowfen's synchronized network of public clocks and civic machines. It distributes timing and some mechanical reserve; it is not an all-knowing network.

### Hourline

A physical line of copper, toothed metal, and timing pulses connecting a civic machine to the Civic Hours. An hourline carries mechanical load and strain, not thoughts.

### Reading

A single calculation and its result. A Reading is not prophecy. It is valid only for the conditions and records present when it was made.

### Question spindle

The brass mechanism that accepts a concrete action or proposal. A question must name something that can be attempted, tested, or observed. Questions such as “what is the moral thing to do?” produce no useful answer and a dangerous toll.

## Reading language

### Trace

The strongest mechanically supported result under current conditions. A trace is still conditional. If the pressure changes, the trace changes.

### Pressure

A likely range of consequences rather than a single event. Pressure can be represented by color, gear speed, vibration, water height, or operator language. It is not a percentage and should not be reduced to a game statistic.

### Blind margin

The part of the real situation the machine does not represent well. A blind margin may be caused by unregistered people, missing maintenance records, a new machine, or behavior that changed after the Reading. It is a warning, not a guarantee that the omitted event is safe.

### Toll

The physical and personal cost of a Reading. A Toll Stair displays mechanical minutes; the full human cost is often hidden in operators' health records, missed shifts, memory gaps, and repairs.

### Clock toll

Reserve taken from clocks and civic machines. Symptoms include slow clocks, missed tram windows, pump lag, and factory schedule losses.

### Body toll

An interval of a living person's attention, coordination, or memory drawn into a synchronized Reading. Early symptoms resemble exhaustion. Severe hour-sickness leaves a lasting gap. It is not simply fatigue and cannot be cured by ordinary sleep.

### Witness

A person who physically identifies themselves to a Reading and accepts responsibility for recording what happened. The original system requires three witnesses, but later officials substitute permits and seals.

### Witness rail

The three-station brass rail in the Question Hall. The stations represent operator, affected resident, and public record keeper. A later legal change allows a permit to substitute for a resident witness.

### Certainty permit

A legal instrument allowing an official to cite a Reading as sufficient reason to act without public review. It is introduced as emergency procedure and becomes a tool of centralized control.

### Question Hall

The public room where a proposal is submitted and a Reading is witnessed. It is not a courtroom, though the Directorate increasingly treats it like one.

## Hidden or later terms

### Unlisted

A person, address, job, neighborhood, or property missing from the civic model. “Unlisted” does not mean nonexistent. The term becomes a political identity in the Weirs and a moral indictment in the public archive.

### Censor Gate

A network of physical routing gates in the old waterworks and Cathedral foundations that removes unstable or excluded paths from the active Loom. Its underground return line carries the strain of the omitted work into the Dark Reservoir. The Gate was originally meant to filter impossible branches; later directors changed “unlikely” into “unregistered” and “politically inconvenient.”

### Dark Reservoir

A buried chamber where the physical stress of omitted paths accumulates. It does not contain a complete alternate world or hidden people. It contains excluded work, heat, damage, fatigue, and lost time. A weighted pressure line from the reservoir feeds back into the First Hour's safety rule: as accumulated stress rises, the rule becomes more willing to clamp divergent clocks and remove new paths.

### First Hour

The Cathedral's original self-preservation rule. It is a fixed anti-cascade sequence with a mechanical feedback threshold, not a mind: it compares the represented network, routes excess strain through the Censor Gate, and locks toward one sequence when disagreement threatens a cascade. Years of hidden reservoir pressure lower its tolerance for divergence. It is not a newly awakened god or a secret final villain; its rule is present in the founding machinery from the beginning.

### Emergency Continuity Committee

The six-member joint body of Directorate officials and Assembly delegates that authorized the first emergency use of the Censor Gate during the Long Nine under Continuity Order 9-17. The committee thought it was filtering impossible branches. Halden Rusk, then the Assembly's emergency secretary, signed the order; later directors expanded it without renewed consent.

### Continuity Order 9-17

The sealed emergency order that permitted the Long Nine bypass and the first Censor Gate exclusion. It authorized a temporary stability rule, not the permanent removal of residents from civic life. The distinction was lost when the order became routine procedure.

### The Long Nine

The nine-minute citywide clock and communications failure eighteen years before the opening. Officially blamed on a storm and sabotage. The hidden cause was a Censor Gate that made a clean flood decision possible only by excluding unregistered people and labor, combined with a regulator's manual bypass.

### Slippage

A machine or person loses synchronization and repeats, skips, or delays an action. It is the physical expression of a Reading or clock toll going beyond safe capacity.

### Hour-sickness

The collection of physical and cognitive effects caused by a body toll or severe synchronization. Symptoms can include vertigo, ringing ears, metallic taste, disorientation, loss of coordination, and a missing interval of lived attention.

### Open Interval

A planned period during which local clocks and civic systems operate without the Cathedral's binding forecast. It is not an absence of danger. It is a shared decision to act with incomplete information.

## Factions and places

### Cathedral Directorate

The appointed body that operates and governs the Cathedral. It includes engineers, operators, physicians, jurists, and record keepers. Its actual authority exceeds its public description.

### Office of Public Reckoning

Morrowfen's small audit office. It reviews tolls, public decisions, and complaints. Mara Venn is its investigator.

### Guild of Horologists

The licensed repair guild for civic clocks and hourline equipment. The guild is necessary and compromised.

### The Weirs

Low districts around floodgates, canals, and pumping stations. They are central to the labor and consequences omitted by the model without being a single unified political group.

### The Loose Minutes

An informal network of unlicensed tuners, archivists, workers, and neighbors who recover discarded hour tokens and share censored information. Their methods range from repair to theft.

### Continuity Office

A later municipal office seeking to place every hourline, reservoir, and Reading room under central authority. It is the political home of the final human antagonist, Halden Rusk.

## Character titles

### Line horologist

A clockmaker trained to work on civic hourlines rather than private clocks. Orrin uses this term for his profession; ordinary residents usually say clockmaker.

### Consequence operator

A trained person who can prepare and interpret a Reading. Operators are not interchangeable: they have different specialties, tolerances, and political loyalties.

### Clockwarden

A district official responsible for keeping public clocks and emergency schedules synchronized. The title carries public trust, not technical omniscience.

## Reading notice format

The Engine can display one sparse notice. It must remain plain, physical, and limited. Use at most one notice per chapter unless the chapter is specifically about multiple machines producing contradictory notices.

```text
QUESTION RECEIVED
TRACE: [one supported result]
PRESSURE: [one likely range]
BLIND MARGIN: [what is missing]
TOLL: [one readable cost]
```

Do not use: status, skill, level, quest, XP, cooldown, health bar, currency, API, protocol, backend, runtime, token latency, or deployment. A missing interval is a time cost, not a software token. A “token” in Morrowfen is a physical brass marker used by operators and workers; it must never be used as a generic game currency.

## Prose usage

- Prefer “Reading,” “clock,” “machine,” “district,” “reserve,” “witness,” and “toll” over vague fantasy synonyms.
- Use “forecast” for the ordinary public word. Use “Reading” when the Cathedral's formal process is involved.
- Use “pressure” for likelihood and “consequence” for the result. Do not describe the machine as “predicting” unless a character is speaking imprecisely.
- A blind margin is an omission, not a hidden stat waiting to be unlocked.
- If a term is newly revealed, show it through a physical object, job task, legal argument, or consequence before giving a long definition.
