# Chapter 95 — The Fifteenth Night, and the Sixteenth, and the Seventeenth

On Friday the fifteenth and Saturday the sixteenth of the twelfth month three people read three clocks in three buildings at half past seven at night, on Saturday the seventeenth they read the same three clocks at half past seven in the morning, and the three figures in three hands do not agree with each other and are not meant to; and what the rehearsal establishes is not a number and not a forecast, it is that three clocks on one line are the same length of iron seen three times, and that a proposal can be traced across a district by three people standing in three places, and that nobody can be shown where a person's hours are in it; and it cannot stop one single thing in this city, it has no seal and no form and it certifies nothing, and the count of keeper's tolls is four and did not move; and on the Wednesday of that week a man of thirty-two sat in a room over a chandler's shop at about half past four in the afternoon and had to open his mouth and say three hundred minutes out loud to a room, and the week before, twenty-two households had been told at their own doors and one of them had said no at a door in nine seconds.

---

**Friday the fifteenth, at about half past seven at night, in the dark, in three buildings four miles apart, and nothing whatever was done to any of them.**

Ivo Pell at the Weir Two pump house, at the station clock, with a lamp and a watch and the pump running. Sabine Kaye at the Quarter hill, at the south public clock, which is on a bracket over a door and is lit from a lamp two feet away and is the only one of the three that is in the open. And Orrin Vale in the outer room of the pumping ward at the clock above the glass case, with Bevin Sarr in the room because the case has to be unlocked to get at the key on a string and the key is on a string and the string is eleven inches.

Nobody came. That was arranged on the Thursday and it was the only thing that was arranged: **Ivo Pell said on the Friday the ninth, at a gate, in eleven words, that there would be no second person in any of the three rooms on Friday night or Saturday morning, and the reason he gave was that a rehearsal which requires anybody to get up early will not be run, and a rehearsal which requires anybody to stand about watching will not be run twice.**

They read the three clocks at half past seven and wrote three figures down and put their own names against them and handed nothing to anybody. Then they left, and the three of them walked to the bus stop separately and about nine minutes apart, and nobody telephoned anybody.

**And the whole of what the Friday night established is one sentence, and Orrin Vale wrote it down in the grey book at about ten o'clock and it is in his own hand and it took him four minutes to write.**

> *The three clocks say at half past seven at night on Friday the fifteenth exactly what they say at half past seven in the morning on Saturday the seventeenth, to within a second, all three, and I did not expect that and I have been wrong about something for six months because of it.*
> *A clock that is a displacement does not care what time you look at it. **A step has no clock in it.** For six months I have been treating the six figures on Bevin Sarr's board as a rate, because a rate can be watched, and a figure at a half past seven in the morning can be compared with a figure at a half past seven in the morning next month, and I have been doing that, and it works, and it is a lie of a particular kind that I have not caught for six months because I built it.*
> *The comparison that is worth anything is **one clock against another clock, at the same minute, on the same day.** A displacement is a length of iron. **A figure is a photograph of a thing that has already gone past.**

And then the three of the night's figures went into the top of the narrow book and not the grey one, because the grey book is his and the narrow one is the second book on the shelf behind the bench and it has been the second book since the sixth month, and both of them are in the same room and neither of them is in anybody else's.

---

**Saturday the sixteenth. The seventh day of a notice, and the last day before the thing, and the row in daylight, in public, with the sheet on a wall and nobody's name at the bottom of it.**

The sheet is a foolscap sheet and it is four feet by three and it is the second of the two sheets that have been on the board in the outer room of the pumping ward since the fourteenth of the November. It is not in a glass case. **A sheet in a glass case is a public document and a sheet on a board is a notice, and the difference between them is a key on a string, and the whole of what happened this year is that a thing nobody can take off a wall is the only kind of notice anybody in this district has ever been able to read.**

It carries the three clocks and the question and the minute and the four lines of the cost and the column, and the column now has twelve names in it and twelve clocks in thirteen houses, and the clock in the second court is the ninth line and there are two houses against it and one winder, and every line has a time against it, and the times came out of doors.

> *THE THREE CLOCKS. Pumping ward, canal road, outer room. Weir Two pump house, station clock. Quarter hill, south public clock.*
> *THE QUESTION, in the same words, written down before the day: **at the minute stated, read each clock named below and write down, in figures, what it says, against the district reference of the Cathedral public clock; and state the time of reading, and state who read it.***
> *THE MINUTE: half past seven in the morning. Chosen by I. Pell in the ninth month. Not chosen by either operator and not chosen by me and not chosen by anybody in this district.*
> *WHAT SATURDAY IS NOT. **It is not a reading. It is not a finding. It is not certified, there is no seal, there is no form, it is not on any rail, nothing is filed, and nothing about it can be made admissible to anybody, and there is no form in this city on which any part of it could be.** The station on the rail takes a person of the affected district; there is none, and there has not been one for nineteen years, and the form that would make one costs two shillings the head for a year, and **nothing about Saturday is going onto that rail by any other road and the three of us know exactly what the other two roads are and none of us is going to take them.**
> *WHAT SATURDAY COSTS. Three hundred minutes. Seven in the morning until noon. **Toll: 300 minutes. Paying district: LOWER WEIRS.** A toll on a clock is a quantity of minutes and a paying district, and the district it names is four streets from Sluice Row and has none of Sluice Row's water and is not able to be told anything about it, and Sluice Row is not in the Lower Weirs pumping district and cannot be consulted and cannot object and has been told at twenty-two doors in person on the tenth of the twelfth in daylight, and there is no form on which that could have been done and it was done anyway.*
> *THE COLUMN. **Twelve clocks, in thirteen houses, and twelve names. The notice of the fourteenth of the eleventh says eleven and cannot be corrected, and was taken down and put up again on the sixteenth with twelve on it. One man at number eight said no at his door in about nine seconds on the tenth and was asked again on Friday the sixteenth at about eleven in the morning and said yes.** Nine households on that row have no clock on the cistern and their water goes off for five hours and there is no column for them and no form on which to make one, and the column is a column of clocks and a column of clocks is a thing a machine wants and a column of households is a thing a person wants, and both of those are on this sheet and the second of them has twenty-two lines on it and the first has twelve.*
> *NOBODY SIGNS THIS. Three of us will each write a figure and our own name against it and hand it to nobody. **There is no column on this sheet for a signature and there is not going to be one.**

They put it up on the ward board at about ten in the morning and on the board at the Bellweather Road washing-yard and on the board at the galvanising and wire works' gate, which is not theirs and which a works' clerk has agreed to for the fourth time and has not been thanked for and does not want to be, and **they also carried it to the bottom of Sluice Row and put it up on the wall of the second court, at about shoulder height, beside the passage clock with the numerals gone, which is the first time a notice has been put up inside a court in this district and it took about nine seconds with a drawing-pin and a scrap of lino.**

Hanna Stroud did that one and would not let anybody else do it and said the reason, which is that **a notice on a wall in a court is a notice nobody will take down, because nobody in that court has ever had a key to anything and the first person who takes a notice down is the person who put it up.**

---

**Saturday the seventeenth of the twelfth month, at about half past seven in the morning.**

The three of them were in their three places at ten to seven and they were alone in all three, and the day was clear and cold with about an hour of good light left in it, and there was nothing to be done anywhere in this city about anything.

**Ivo Pell** at the Weir Two pump house, with the pump running and a lamp and his own watch set against the station clock twenty minutes before, and a sheet of paper folded in his coat and a pencil and about eleven minutes of work to do after.

**Sabine Kaye** at the Quarter hill, at the south public clock, which is a bracket clock over a door on a public way with a lamp on a bracket two feet away, and which she had to get a ladder to for the first time in eleven years and did not need the ladder for and would not say so.

**Orrin Vale** in the outer room of the pumping ward, at the clock above the glass case, with Bevin Sarr in the room and the case unlocked and the ledger in it with two sets of columns and one empty one, and the key on a string eleven inches long on a nail.

At half past seven all three of them read their clock against the district reference of the Cathedral public clock, and the Cathedral public clock is the city's reference and not the Quarter's, and Orrin read it off the ward clock with a watch in his hand and two hands and not with his ear, **because he can hear a watch at thirty feet in a still room and can no longer tell by the sound alone whether a movement is running true, and has not been able to since the sixth month, and reads everything off a dial.**

Then they wrote down what their clock said, and their own name, and the time of reading, and they handed the sheets to nobody.

> *Saturday the seventeenth of the twelfth, half past seven in the morning. Nights since the sixth of the tenth: 26 in the tenth, the sixth to the thirty-first inclusive; 30 in the eleventh; 17 in the twelfth, the first to the seventeenth inclusive. **Seventy-three nights at fifty seconds is 3,650 seconds, which is 1 h 00 min 50 s.***
> *Weir Two station clock, frozen figure of the first of the eighth 48 min 00 s, and the clock said: **2 h 29 min 00 s.** Read by I. Pell.*
> *Pumping ward, outer room, frozen figure 57 min 20 s, and the clock said: **2 h 38 min 20 s.** Read by O. Vale.*
> *Quarter hill, south public clock, frozen figure 1 h 49 min 10 s, and the clock said: **3 h 30 min 10 s.** Read by S. Kaye.*
> *The separation of the first from the second is 9 min 20 s and of the second from the third is 51 min 50 s, and those are the separations of the first of the eighth to the second, and they have not moved by a second in seventy-three nights, and **that is the whole of what Saturday is.***
> *Written at the foot of the same sheet on Monday the twentieth, in the same hand, and it is the second paragraph of the same three sentences: **The three figures do not sit on the line. Seventy-three nights at fifty seconds puts the first of the three at 1 h 48 min 50 s and it is 2 h 29 min 00 s, and the second at 1 h 58 min 10 s and it is 2 h 38 min 20 s, and the third at 2 h 50 min 00 s and it is 3 h 30 min 10 s. The difference is the same at all three and it is 2,410 seconds, which is 40 min 10 s, and it came on between the Friday and the Saturday and nobody in this district knew it was there until Monday morning.***

**And the three figures do not agree with each other, and they were not going to, and Pell said so at about ten past eight in a pump house with the pump running, in about nine seconds, and it is the whole of his contribution to the morning.**

"They do not agree. **They are not supposed to agree. They are the same eleven miles of iron read three times and if two of them agreed then one of the two people had made them agree.** You have got three numbers and no two of them is a district, and you have got two numbers that are lengths of pipe, and if you put the two lengths of pipe on the sheet you can find out where a thing can be taken out of that line, and that is the only use this has, and it is a use a plumber would understand and an operator would not."

He said that and then he said the rest of it, and it is the sentence a year of this has been walking towards and it took him about a minute to say and he said it in a pump house with a lamp.

"**A proposal can be traced across a district by three people standing in three places.** That is what Saturday is. It is not a forecast — there is nothing in it about what is going to happen; the question asks nothing at all and that is not an accident, it is the only reason it is safe. It is three people and a minute and a question in the same words and three hands, and what it buys is that **a thing that is going to be done to eleven miles of iron can be found by people who are standing in three rooms, and not by one man with a sense, and not by a machine in a building four miles away that has never heard of any of the three of us.**"

"And the third thing."

"**Nobody can be shown where a person's hours are in it.**" He put his hand flat on the pump house bench, which is a thing three people in this district do and it means nothing and it has never meant anything except that a hand is going on a surface. "Three clocks. No people. **Eleven miles of main, two stations, four stopcocks, six clocks, one hospital branch entered as a draw and not as a supply, and no man on it, and there is no form in this city on which a man can be entered on it, and the reason there is no man on it is not an oversight and it is not a rule, it is that a man is not an instrument and a man's shift is not an instrument, and if you put a man on that map then in about four years you will have put his hours on it, and his hours are the only thing anybody in this district has ever been able to prove.**"

**And that is the moment the pump house's gauge came up, and it was not a thing anybody had arranged.**

The boundary make-up at the Northglass gate comes on the works' own schedule and it has been coming on it since the Friday the seventh of the October, and it comes on four times in a day, and at seven in the morning it comes on the same as it has come on since the seventh of the October, and at about half past seven on the morning of the seventeenth of the twelfth **a man of forty-one did not put a bar in an override, and there was no order to put one, and the seat had not been shut by hand since the second of the third month, and the clock-drive lifted it in about nine minutes the way it has lifted it every morning since the seventh of October.**

**And the branch took it, and the front's clocks went a further step, and nobody in this city knew by how much until a man in a shop on Verge Lane read them against the Cathedral on Monday morning with a rule and a fold of paper. It is 40 min 10 s and it is the same 40 min 10 s at the pump house, at the ward and on the hill, and a step that is the same at three places is not three faults and is one.**

The figure chalked on the wall in the outer room of the pumping ward on Friday the sixteenth evening was the figure for half past seven on the seventeenth morning, and at half past seven on the seventeenth morning it was already out of date, and that is not a fault in the figure. **A step is a displacement and a displacement is permanent. A figure is a photograph of a thing that has already gone past.** He wrote the third line of it on the wall on the Monday morning with a date on it and no name on it, and Bevin Sarr copied it into the ward's night book in her own hand and read it back before she wrote it, which is what she does.

**And the surge is dated now, and it is not a special one, and that is the finding of the twentieth and it is two sentences long.**

The branch was surveyed for two cycles a day in 1911 and has been given four since the sixth of the October, lawfully, on a nine-line notice, and the make-up at seven in the morning is one of the four and it comes on whether anybody is reading or not. **And the branch cannot take the seven o'clock make-up and hold, and the reason it cannot is that the line divides by eight and takes its beat off the odd teeth and the further out a machine is the more of the step is left in it, and the far end of this branch is a gate with nineteen households drawing off the main under it, **and on the seventeenth of the twelfth the far end of this branch is three hours and thirty minutes and ten seconds out, which is the seventy-third night and a step of forty minutes and ten seconds, and in four months it will be an hour and a half further out still and nothing about that is anybody's fault.**

**And the only place in this district a pressure can be put is the brick cistern under a row of twenty-two houses, and it was on a wall in chalk on the fourteenth of the November, and the rehearsal found it on the seventeenth of December with three figures and a map that took four minutes, and a pump warden said the same thing in a room in November and nobody wrote it down because nobody writes the name of the meeting.**

The next make-up that cannot be held is on Saturday the twenty-fourth at seven in the morning, and the cistern takes five hours to fill from the canal, and five hours is from seven until about noon on a Saturday morning in daylight, and **there is nobody in this city who can vote on it and nobody who can be asked and nobody who can be told, and the toll on it is three hundred minutes and the paying district is the Lower Weirs, four streets away, with none of that street's water.**

---

**Sunday the eighteenth. Nothing happened at all, and it is the second time in a year and the reason is the same reason and it is not a coincidence and it will not be fixed.**

Nothing in this city is scheduled for a Sunday. No Assembly and no committee and no guild counter and no water committee and no mutual sits on a Sunday, and the reason is not constitutional, it is that the people who do the sitting do not turn up on a Sunday. **And there is no form in this city on which a Sunday may be used for anything, and a rehearsal is not a sitting and a cistern is not a sitting and twenty-two households are not a sitting, and the row of twenty-two houses got its fourth, fifth, sixth and seventh days on weekdays, on foot, in daylight, in December, and not on the Sunday, and nobody anywhere in this district was surprised by that and one woman was, and has said so in a room, and is right to.**

He went to the ward on the Sunday morning at about nine and read the three clocks himself, alone, off a bench light, and wrote nothing down.

**On the Monday the nineteenth, the three sheets went onto the ledge under the glass case, beside the toll ledger and not in it, in a folder, and there are three of them and they are in three hands and no person in this city holds all three and that is the design.**

Nobody in that room read them. That is the fourth time somebody in this district has read a thing written by three other people and has not checked it, and the reason is on a wall in his own hand from the ninth of the October, and the reason is that **if I check them then the only thing this district has ever produced by three ordinary people is a thing that had to be produced by me, and that was the whole of the argument in the sixth month and I was on the wrong side of it in the sixth month.**

The count of keeper's tolls is four. It was four on Friday the fifteenth, at half past seven at night, in three buildings, and it was four on Saturday the seventeenth at half past seven in the morning, and no private sense was used on either night or either morning, **and he put his hand on nothing at all: not on the ward clock and not on the pump and not on the main, and there is a line about that in the narrow book on the shelf behind the bench and it is the sixth line in that book and it is the only line in it that is not a list of steps.**

And on the Tuesday the twentieth, at about four in the afternoon, in the outer room of the pumping ward, with the sheet on the wall and the column on it and the times against the names, the arithmetic was done for the second time on the public board of the district works' yard, and it came out the same, and what came out of it is the whole of what is available and it is a road.

> *Tuesday the twentieth of the twelfth, from Ruth Ashby's list of the fourteenth of the sixth, which took four minutes and has no man on it.*
> *A pressure on the line cannot be taken out at the head, because the head is a works' asset and a works' man opens it and a works' man is given an engineer's written order, and there is no form in this city on which an engineer may be asked for one by a person of a district that has no standing under the water act.*
> *It cannot be taken out at the ward, because the ward's reserve is a tank and not a reservoir, and it has been exactly balanced at about fourteen hours since the second of the third month with the low-level alarm inhibited since the same date, and no report has been made and no form requires one.*
> ***It can be taken out at Sluice Row.*** *A brick cistern under the row, put in in 1889 off the canal and not off this main at all, out of use since 1911, with a handwheel at street level in a vault at the north end, and twelve clocks in thirteen houses and two standpipes on it, and it is the only water in this district that is not the branch's, which is exactly why it is the only place a pressure can be put, and that sentence has been on a wall in this district in chalk since the fourteenth of the November and it is the same sentence.*
> *Cost, at the seventh in the morning on a Saturday: **five hours, three hundred minutes, twelve clocks hand-wound, two standpipes dry, twenty-two households, nine of them with no clock and no column.***
> *Toll: **300 minutes. Paying district: LOWER WEIRS.** Four streets away. None of that street's water. Not able to be told, not able to be consulted, not able to object, and not being able to do any of those three things is not an injustice done to a street; it is the ordinary condition of a district with no standing under the water act, and it is the reason the street is the cheapest safe route in this city and it will stay the cheapest safe route for as long as there is no standing.*
> *And nobody can vote on it. A schedule is made or varied by the Water Users' Committee on seven days' notice, and that is the only instrument in this city for changing when water comes to a place. **A rehearsal is not a schedule. There is no form on which a rehearsal may be put to a vote and no committee's standing reaches one and the Water Users' Committee could not post a notice of it even if it wished to, because a thing it does not make is a thing it cannot give notice of.** Nobody can vote on it because it is not the kind of thing anybody votes on.*

And on the Wednesday the twenty-first, at about half past four in the afternoon, he sat down in a room over a chandler's shop in Bellweather with a stove in it and a bell that has not worked since 1949 and a shelf of minute books in which the most recent volume is 1948, and there were eleven people in it, and he read the four lines out loud.

**And the four lines are the notice, and the notice is the same four lines that went to twenty-two doors on the tenth of the twelfth, and that was the point of it and the point of it is that they were not changed in eleven days, and a thing that does not change while it is being passed around is the only kind of notice in this city that has ever been true.**

Then he said the number.

"**Three hundred minutes.** That is what comes off the clocks of the Lower Weirs, and the Lower Weirs is four streets from that row and has none of its water, and Sluice Row is not in the district and could not be asked, and the row has been told at twenty-two doors and did not have to be asked and the eleventh of the twenty-two said yes twice."

Nobody said anything for about half a minute.

"Then somebody go and tell them that the number has been said in a room," said Wenna Pruitt. "**Because it has not been said in a room before, and that is the only thing that has changed in this district since the fourteenth of November.**"
