## Fixed setting

- Morrowfen: estuary city of roughly 2.4 million on a reclaimed tidal basin between the Sable River and the Morrow Sound. Electric trams, antibiotics, pneumatic mail, printed records, mechanical calculators, reliable civic clocks. No ubiquitous digital network.
- Districts: Clockwork Quarter, Cathedral Rise, the Weirs, Green Span, Northglass, the Undercity, the Outer Sluice. Neighboring towns: Bellweather, Harrow, Saint Perrin.
- The Cathedral of Consequence is civic infrastructure with religious and political symbolism. It is not a church that owns the city and not a prophet.
- The Consequence Engine runs a **Reading** through the **Consequence Loom** using the **Civic Hours** network of **hourlines**. A Reading returns four parts: trace, pressure, blind margin, toll. Maximum one notice per chapter.
- The Hour Compact is forty-three years old. Its four safeguards: concrete action only, benefit and cost both shown, three independent witnesses, recorded physical toll.
- The Long Nine was eighteen years before the opening: nine minutes of citywide clock and communications failure, 212 dead, mostly in the Weirs. A storm met a failing tide-gate network while a Censor Gate was already in use.
- Continuity Order 9-17 was authorized by the six-member Emergency Continuity Committee. **Rusk was not one of the six.** As the Assembly's emergency secretary he drafted, numbered, circulated, and countersigned the order as the record, then spent eighteen years defending its expansion. His guilt is procedural and institutional, and that is enough.
- The order authorized the first emergency use of the Censor Gate. It was meant to filter impossible branches. The First Hour chamber was sealed by a separate Directorate order, 9-18, three days after the Long Nine. Edwin had no part in it. All six committee members are dead by the final volume; Rusk is the only one left who can be asked what it was for.
- Edwin Vale, Orrin's father and a senior regulator, opened a manual bypass during the Long Nine knowing the network would separate for nine minutes. He was listed missing, no body recovered. He left no prophecy, no guide voice, no secret master knowledge, and he did not solve the system. His notes are hidden in timing tolerances.
- The Censor Gate routes omitted paths through a buried return line to the Dark Reservoir, which stores excluded work, heat, fatigue, and lost time. It is not a collection of alternate futures or hidden people.
- The **routing plates** in the Gate hub decide what is routed away, engraved with categories rather than names: the earliest read *unstable path*, later sets read *unregistered* and then *not represented*. Removing them is the only irreversible act of the ending.
- The **return line** carries omitted strain to the Dark Reservoir. The **feedback float** rises on the reservoir, the **feedback line** carries its weight, and the float presses on the First Hour's **threshold spring**. The First Hour is the founding anti-cascade rule. As pressure rises it clamps divergent clocks toward one sequence. It is not a mind and not a late awakening; it holds a calibration history, not a memory.
- A **consensus lock** is the First Hour's clamp action. The **binding plates** are why an external order can be applied to the rule as a command rather than a recommendation. The Continuity Office's **command line** can carry such an order during a declared emergency. The reform's **kill-switch** is three release handles, any two of which sever **only the command line**; it removes the authority to order, not the rule, and it does not reach the Gate hub. The kill-switch and the routing plates are different instruments and neither substitutes for the other: the kill-switch is reversible and takes nine days, the plates come out once and cannot be put back. The climax order is fixed — hold the clocks apart, then take out the plates, then cut the command line.
- The emergency clause of the regional water and power scheduling act lets a declared regional emergency be answered "by any measure the responsible office certifies is necessary." It predates every reform, cannot be repealed by the Assembly alone, and is Rusk's instrument in the final volume.
- Toll types: clock toll (reserve out of public clocks), body toll (an interval of a living person’s attention, coordination, or memory — hour-sickness), network toll (slippage across districts), and the unrecorded **keeper’s toll** that a private reading takes from the machine’s reserve and then from the reader’s body. Officials call body tolls “calibration strain.”
- **Who pays a toll:** a question is filed naming a *paying district*, and the engine draws that district’s stored clock rotation. Nothing in the Compact requires the paying district to be affected, asked, or consenting, and the draw is proportional to what a district holds — so the cost falls hardest on districts with the least reserve and is a rounding error for districts with the most. An unaffiliated submitter may name any district in the city. Objecting after the fact is a matter for the Office of Public Reckoning and is not a defence against the charge. **Orrin files his Weir Two question charging the Clockwork Quarter in Chapter 4**, three minutes, because that district holds the most reserve and is the least affected. This is the moral engine of the series and it must not be softened or re-derived in later volumes.
- **A private sense is not a Reading.** Orrin’s stages 2 through 7 are listening, not calculation: no blind margin, no filed record, no admissibility, and in the private stages an unfiled keeper’s toll. Stage 7 suspends the central forecast; it does not suspend the requirement to record a toll. His sense is blind to whatever the network has routed away, so a group of people holding records is always more useful than he is. Using it alone is his flaw expressed as a technique.
- **Status of the four safeguards at Chapter 1:** (1) concrete action required — intact and enforced; (2) show the cost and state the omission — on paper, gutted by a footer nobody reads; (3) three witnesses — the affected-district resident station is replaceable by a written *district standing*, which is how Weirs Readings have been witnessed by Green Span residents for twenty years; (4) record the toll — clock tolls filed, body tolls recorded as calibration strain with no name and no duration. The certainty permit does not exist yet; it is created in Volume 03.
- Reform-era institutions, not to be named before they exist: district time councils, Reading rooms, the Council of Hours, the independent Civic Time Keeper, local instruments.

## Fixed people

- Teo Arden, 20. Unlicensed tuner and organizer in the Weirs. Apprentice, friend, ideological foil. Arc from covert theft to an accountable repair cooperative.
- Edwin Vale. Missing since the Long Nine. Limited human decision, not a myth.
- Anja Voss, 49, Cathedral Director. Sincere, not secretly evil, and not absolved.
- Halden Rusk, 58, Continuity Minister for the two years before the opening and still the holder of the post in the final volume. Final human antagonist. Defeated by records, refusal, and removal of the machine's monopoly. Not killed.
- Sel Kade, 44, Weirs pumping warden then tenant-council representative. Understandable, locally beneficial, structurally unjust.
- **Perry Vance, 41,** unlicensed tuner, twenty years on the Weir Two night pump crew. He is the man the opening Reading left out, and he is **not** Teo. He is the inciting death of the series and an accident, not a martyr.
- **Ada Vance, 13,** his daughter, a tram-hopper to school in Northglass. Volume is roughly a year, so her ages are fixed: 13 in Volumes 01–02, 20 in Volumes 08–09, 25 in Volume 13, 26 in Volume 14. She appears in Volume 01 as the child on the six o'clock tram, in Volume 02 as a thirteen-year-old witness in the archive fight who finds her father's name in a dated batch, in Volumes 08 and 09 as the adult running her own family's unlisted-records claim, at the Weirs council table in Volume 13, and in Volume 14 filing the batch into the public archive. She is an adult every time she speaks from Volume 08 on.
- **Corvin Hale,** the market broker who falsifies a Reading in Volume 03 by attaching a real blind margin to a real notice. A small criminal, not a plant.

Its **renewal expires on Sunday the 19th of the third month**, the **W-4 work book** is due with the fee, and the **optional interview is Tuesday the 28th of the second month**. It is suspended in Volume 01's resolution, costing him the right to certify a machine's local toll, and restored only provisionally in Volume 03. He certifies **nothing** in batch 0002 that he has no standing to certify, and the offence is reserved for Chapters 44–49.
- Gerren Hask, 55, guild master. The first antagonist is paperwork, not a monster.
- Ivo Pell, 36, veteran operator. Teaches, then joins the Continuity Office, then testifies.

Each has one clean function and none is a disguised existing character. Their guards: **Alma Festinger** is not a dupe, the law never required her to know, and she is **not asked again anywhere in Volume 01** — the second asking is Volume 06, in a gallery, and the reader should recognise her face; **Della Marsh is never thanked on the page**; **Hester Lomas is the face of the cost of paperwork, not an organiser, and does not recur after this volume**; **Ellen Reeve is a working person, not a device**; **Hanna Stroud must stay reasonable for the rest of the book.**
- **Anja Voss is named once in Volume 01, in Chapter 4, on a signature, and takes no scene, no offer, and no bargain.** Her offer of access is Volume 02’s **closing** turn. **Halden Rusk is named once in Volume 01, in Chapter 1, on a Continuity Office inspection authority, and takes no scene.** He is in the chair from Chapter 1 onward and is never a character in this volume. **Nella Rook is not named in Volume 01.**
- **Vance's death sequence is fixed and is the inciting event of the series:** the head passes after the gate opens, the opened gate and the running second stage drive the hand valve open again, the crew is not on the board, and **Vance goes down on his own initiative to close the valve he had shut himself in daylight that same afternoon.** Chapters 3, 4 and 5 are all the 17th, so the valve was shut that afternoon and he died that night; no later file may say "the day before" or "yesterday". Orrin is at the pump house beforehand, feels the failure correctly, and is refused at the door by a watchman who is not corrupt. Orrin is not the cause and is not blameless, and Chapter 5 says both. He certifies nothing in Chapter 9; the licence offence that costs him his trade is reserved for Chapters 44–49.
- **The reform provision is “a name and a duration” for a BODY toll, not for a clock toll.** A clock toll’s district and minutes have been on the Toll Stair for forty-three years. Body tolls have only ever been recorded as *calibration strain*. The Volume 01 resolution creates those two columns, and the first entry under them is Vance’s name with **the duration left blank**, because no instrument recorded the interval his shift lost. The blank is the point and the volume says so out loud.

## Fixed ending constraints

- The Returning Tide is a physically plausible cascade, not a magical apocalypse. The Sluice fails because of Volume 06's deferred inspection; the rail bridge is torn out so the river can pass; the tide still has to go somewhere and the Weirs are still the lowest ground. No new cosmic enemy, god, or invasion.
- The final plan is distinguished from the First Hour's by consent, not by arithmetic, and the volume must show the four variables the machine cannot carry: a counterweight somebody else agreed to, a district deciding for itself, a named end to the loss, and a choice made before the water arrived. The public plan is still worse for some people than the machine's, and the book says so out loud.
- Orrin's final act is to remove the Censor Gate control and routing plates, publish the full cost map, and open an interval in which districts act without one binding forecast.
- The plan costs twenty-three named deaths, eleven days without power in the Weirs, a season of Northglass rail, and the permanent fusion of the central engine. The losses are recorded, not softened. **Twenty-three is the tide only.** The Long Nine dead are 212 and are a different number about a different event; never add them, never let one stand in for the other.
- The ending state: a Council of Hours with rotating district delegates and no command authority, a public archive of tolls and refusals, a maintenance budget protected from emergency diversion, and a kill-switch split among three independent stations. The Cathedral becomes a public workshop, archive, and equipment hall with limited local instruments.
- Fully answered: the ninth tick, the missing tolls, the Unlisted, the Long Nine, Continuity Order 9-17, the Dark Reservoir, the First Hour, Rusk's role.
- Intentionally unresolved: the original engineers' private thoughts, the lived intervals that cannot be returned, and whether the First Hour had any rudimentary awareness.
- Final image: bells out of order across the city, a public clock with its prediction plate removed, and a city that keeps acting.
