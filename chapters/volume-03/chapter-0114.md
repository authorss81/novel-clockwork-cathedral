# Chapter 114 — Two Watches

On Thursday the second and Friday the third of the third month Leda Vale sat down with a night sister she did not choose and had been given, and taught her a method, and the method took ninety minutes and consisted of one sentence.

"You do not hold the clock. You hold a watch. Two of us, two watches, and we set them against each other in front of everybody at every handover and we write down the difference, and the difference is the number that matters and the clock is not the number."

The night sister's name was not written on the card and Orrin did not ask it, because the sentence in the ward book that had begun and ended in a week was written by the sister who wanted her own name on a piece of paper, and there is a difference between a woman of nineteen insisting that she be visible and a woman of thirty-four being made visible by somebody who finds her convenient.

The night of Saturday the fourth into Sunday the fifth of the third month was chosen because it was a Saturday, and because on a Saturday the ward's staffing is at its worst and the free master had been left with a dirty pallet again since the Tuesday, and because Leda had made a rule in October and had said it out loud in a room and had not acted on it.

The free master is an honest clock. It is the only clock in the building that is honest, and it is on the landing in a case at the top of a flight of stairs where four people can hear it and nobody can see it, and it keeps time for eleven patients and three staff. Against it they write the line clock, which is three hours and about forty minutes out and stepping fifty seconds a night, and which tells nobody anything except that the city's clock is behind, and the writing of the two is the whole of the ward's trade in clocks.

On the fourth of the third month the line clock stood at three hours, forty minutes and ten seconds out of the district reference. That is arithmetic and the working is written out because a number a person cannot check is a number nobody can argue with. It was two hours and one minute out on the fourth of the eleventh, and it gains fifty seconds a night, and to the fourth of the second is ninety-one nights, and ninety-one nights at fifty seconds is four thousand five hundred and fifty seconds, which is one hour, fifteen minutes and fifty seconds, and two hours and one minute plus one hour and fifteen minutes and fifty seconds is three hours, sixteen minutes and fifty seconds. The ward has kept a line against the free master's case since September, in a hand at the top of it, and that line's entry for the fourth of the third is three hours, forty minutes and ten seconds, and the count of nights between the two of those entries is in the table and not on this page, because the second of the two months in the middle of it does not agree with itself and only a table settles it and the table has never once been checked by anybody but a sister on her round.

And a hundred minutes of that is a hundred and twenty nights, and the whole of the ward's slack on the best night of the year is forty minutes, and two tokens last two hundred and forty nights, and the branch has been stepping for about three hundred and sixteen nights, and two tokens cost nine shillings, and the ward has no fund.

They wrote that out in the nurses' kitchen and none of it was thanked, because it is arithmetic and arithmetic is not thanked, and Leda said the sentence she had been saying in rooms since December, which is that the number is said out loud before the brass is spent and not after.

The night itself went like this.

The free master's pallet fouled again some time after eleven, and after half past eleven the free master stopped keeping time altogether and lost about four minutes an hour, which on a fouled pallet is not a fault of rate but a fault of letting go, and nobody noticed for two hours, and when the second dose went out on the dead master the interval on the card for the ninth patient read what it read.

At about ten to three Cobb came round the end of the corridor with the second tray and stopped, and put it down on the windowsill, and did not go on.

"Nine minutes forty," she said. "That is the fourth one for her and it is the same as the one before and the one before that, and I have been writing the same figure down four nights in a row and I want somebody to look at it."

Leda came and looked at it. There was nothing to look at that a chart could not show, and the ward sister of about fifty came too, because when Elsie Cobb stopped in a corridor at ten to three with a tray on a sill then a ward sister comes, and that is the arrangement and it works.

"Nine forty is a long time," Cobb said. "The card says the free master says eight minutes. The card has been saying eight for two hours and the master has been wrong since half past eleven, and if I use the master then I write eight and it is a lie with a pendulum on it, and if I use the watch then I write nine forty and it is true, and the drug is going out either way in about four minutes because I am standing here. That is the fourth night. It is the same figure four nights running and it is not the same woman, it is a different one each night at about this hour, and I would like somebody to look at the second one on the card and tell me what a dose of that is supposed to be."

"It is going out now," said the ward sister.

"Then it is going out," said Cobb, and picked the tray up, and went down the ward, and at the bed wrote on the card in pencil and then in ink, and the figure she wrote was nine minutes and forty seconds, and under it she wrote *by watch; free master fouled*, which is five words more than the card has ever had on it, and there is no column for those five words either, and she put them in the margin.

Leda and Cobb did not use the free master for the intervals. They set their two watches against each other in the corridor at seven, at eleven and at three, in front of whoever was standing there, and wrote the difference each time: four seconds, six seconds, and at three in the morning eleven seconds, when one of them had been on her feet for a long time and the other had not.

Then the intervals went into the two watches, and the two watches went into the card, and the card went on the board across the corridor where the whole of the ward could read it, and it is a card and not a column in a case and it has four names on the top of it and a date, and the fourth line is Cobb's, and she has never once asked for the fourth line to be taken off.

The total for the night across the eleven patients was six hours and forty-one minutes.

The old card had said six hours and twenty-four minutes, and the difference is seventeen minutes, and the seventeen minutes is four and a quarter hours of a free master standing at half past eleven, and the free master was the instrument the ward had trusted for eleven years, and it was an honest instrument that had been fouled by a pallet, and there is no column in any building in this city for the difference between what a machine said and what a pair of watches said.

The worst single interval of the night was eleven minutes and forty seconds, and it belonged to the ninth patient at about half past nine, an hour before the master fouled, which is the point of it, and Cobb wrote it without softening it, and nobody in the corridor asked who the ninth patient was, because the ninth patient is a number the ward uses and not a person, and Leda had put the two columns in that card in December, *pain* and *waiting*, precisely so that a number could be on a board without a face being beside it.

Beth Ollerton was thirty-four and had come in in October and was the eighth of the eleven.

Beth Ollerton's chart was on the board by the bed with the other charts and had a word on it in a column headed with a date in October, and the word was *calibration strain*, and there was nothing after it.

On the Sunday morning the student came in at eight, which was the end of her shift and not the beginning of it, and asked Leda whether she was allowed to put a duration against it, because there was one in the book at the end of the bed, at the end of the last month, in somebody's hand, and if the word was calibration strain then surely the time was somewhere and if the time was somewhere then it could be found.

Leda said no.

She said it the way she said drug charts, flatly, and then she said why, and she said why to eleven people in a corridor over about ninety seconds and she did not look at Beth Ollerton while she said it and she did not look at Orrin either.

"The word means the office has not got the interval," she said. "It does not mean the interval did not happen. If a person was in a room and something was done to her body and nobody timed it, then nobody timed it, and the honest entry is the one with nothing after it, and if you put a number there you have invented it, and you will have invented it in a ward book, in a hand, with a date, and in about nine years somebody will be asked to check whether the body could stand it and the answer will be a pencil mark a student made at eight o'clock on a Sunday because there was a number in another book."

"Somebody might have timed her," the student said.

"Somebody might have," said Leda. "Somebody might have timed the other four on the same night and written them somewhere else and be dead. If you fill that column you destroy the only thing anybody in this city can use, which is the shape of the hole, and the shape of the hole is the only part of a rule a person in a bad situation can copy."

And then she said the other thing, because there was a person standing in the corridor who needed to hear it, and she said it to him.

"There is another book in this city with a column with nothing in it," Leda said, "in a pumping ward a mile from here, on a ledge where anybody may sit down and read it, with a name in it and nothing after the name. Two offices. They have never heard of each other and neither book cites the other. I am not setting them beside each other and neither are you, because they are not the same hole, one of them is a hole somebody could have written in and had a bad day, and one of them is a hole nobody has ever built, and a person who puts a number in either of them commits an offence against the woman whose name is beside it, and there is nothing in that sentence that is mine. It came off the inside cover of a book in a glass case with a key on a string."

Nobody thanked her and she did not look for it. The student rubbed out nothing, because she had not written anything, which was the best outcome and not the one anybody usually gets.

The Sunday itself is a Sunday and a ward works on a Sunday, and the room on the Sunday had no meeting in it and no counter in it and nothing on any door in it that wanted anything from anybody, and the whole of what the third month had to say about Sundays was said on the card and the card said a date and two watches and a difference of eleven seconds at three in the morning.

Later on the Sunday afternoon Orrin was on the landing between the two wards with the narrow book and the lamp, and the second ward came into the method on the Monday evening, and Cobb's column on the board was headed with the fourth of the third and the eleventh of the third, because a line that begins and ends in a week is worth more to her than a line that goes on for ever, and she had told him so in January in a bed and had not been thanked then either.

He wrote a next step in the ward book, in the three columns, fault observed, work performed, recommendation, and the recommendation column was a third of an inch wider than the other two because the guild found out in about 1908 that men will say anything at all if the box is big enough, and there has never been a column for *I am not sure* and there never will be, and he has now written in that column four times in two months and it is the only column in the city that has anything in it.

*Free master pallet fouling a second time in five weeks. Third pallet since the twenty-first of the ninth. Recommendation: the master is not to be used for intervals while it is on ward time, and the interval is taken by two watches, and the two watches are compared in front of the ward at every handover and the difference is written on the card. Two watches, two people, no third instrument in the room. That is the whole of the recommendation and it does not require a form, a licence, a signature, or anybody's money, and it begins tonight.*

He did not certify it and could not, and the entry said so, and the count of his own hand corrections was two and had not been offered a third.

Leda read it standing up and said one thing, which was that it was the first line in that book in the whole of the time she had been on the ward that told somebody else what to do on the next shift.

"That is not a recommendation," she said. "That is an order with a date on it."

"It is a recommendation," Orrin said. "There is no column that makes it an order and that is not a thing anybody misses until about four years later, when there is."

He went down the stairs and out into the first of the evening of the fifth of the third month, and the branch was three hours and forty-one minutes out and stepping, and the tank was about fourteen hours and exactly balanced with its alarm inhibited and no report made and no form requiring one, and he had told two people on the Wednesday where he was going and had been believed, and the two watches in a ward a mile away were two minutes apart and both of them right, and none of it was any use to anybody in particular, and all of it was written down in three places that did not agree.
