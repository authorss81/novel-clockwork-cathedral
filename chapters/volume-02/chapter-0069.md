# Chapter 69 — Six Hours Off the Timetable

The tram signal at the Green Span end of the Northglass line is a **four-inch box on a post with a white disc in it and a clock inside the box**, and the clock inside it takes its beat from the hospital branch of the Lower Weirs pumping district, and on Sunday the fourteenth of the eighth month a signal fitter with a watch and a second watch stood at the post for about eleven minutes and came away with a figure.

**One hour thirty-six minutes and fifty seconds out of the district reference.** And the hospital branch is the same figure, and the six clocks on the Weir Two branch carry the same step, and the number on the certificate of condition on that signal, in the district works' inspector's column, is **one hour twelve minutes and forty seconds**, which is what the same figure was on the third of the seventh, and the certificate is dated the third of the seventh, and the certificate is true.

That is the whole of the case and nothing in it is anybody's fault.

---

**The certificate is on the file and it is the correct document.**

> **CERTIFICATE OF CONDITION. Signal, Green Span terminus, Northglass line, clock-driven.**
> *Timing checked against the signal's own local beat. **Within tolerance.** Tolerance, five seconds.*
> *Dated the third day of the seventh month. Signed: a holder of the second class, licensing floor of the Clockwork Quarter hall. Countersigned, in accordance with the standing orders of the first of the sixth month, by a holder of the first class.*
> *District works' inspectorate. Observed offset from the district reference at time of examination: 1 h 12 min 40 s.*

Four things about that form and all four of them are correct.

**One.** A condition of a civic machine is certified against its own local beat and not against the district reference. That is the rule and it is a good rule and it is about a hundred and forty years old, and it exists so that a town can be made of machines that are all right in themselves and not all right with each other, which is a different complaint and is the one everybody raises.

**Two.** The offset in the inspector's column is not part of the certification. It is an observation, and observations go in a column that is not the certification column, and there is a reason for that, and the reason is that a certificate is a thing a person relies on and an observation is a thing a person is doing his own work with.

**Three.** The hall's standing orders, from the first of the sixth, require every certificate issued by a master to be countersigned by a holder of the first class and published monthly at the counter, and the signal's certificate is on the list, in the sixth line of the month, with the name of the countersigning holder at the foot of the list.

**Four.** Nobody has read the sixth line. The holder who wrote it is somewhere in the Sloughs, has never been to Northglass, and does not know what the Northglass line is; the first-class holder who countersigned it came in on a bus and signed nine certificates that month and does not know one of them.

The clerk of the licensing floor gave all of that to a man in about four minutes on the Monday morning, in the level voice of a person reading a form.

"He is right," she said. "All four of those are right and I will not have any of them disputed in this hall. That form is a good form. It is about a hundred and forty years old and it is a hundred and forty years old for a reason.

"And I will tell you the other half, because you have come to this counter eight times since January and I owe you the half that is on my side of it. **That form is right about the machine and it is wrong about the city, and it has been wrong about the city for a hundred and forty years, and it is wrong about the city in about four other forms, and this hall does not make the forms and this hall has never asked anybody to.**"

"What would ask anybody," said Orrin Vale.

"Nothing in this hall, because this hall is three hundred and forty people and a counter," said the clerk, "and a form is made by a printer and argued about by the Assembly, and the Assembly has a standing order about it, and the standing order says that a civic machine shall be certified against its own local beat. **And it was not a mistake. It was correct, and somebody had a reason, and the reason was that this city was built out of separate places that did not agree, and the rule made them agree with themselves.**" She put the file down. "And then a hospital branch and a pumping station and a tram signal all ended up on the same line about four miles apart, and the rule has been doing its job perfectly ever since, and there is one clock in this city that is right and it is the one on the Cathedral steps, and it has a name and a keeper and a form, and the tram's man times his junction off the box on the post."

---

**The ordinance is one line and it is a line about a fare table, and that is the whole of why a school had to walk.**

> *No service carrying the public shall be entered on a public timetable unless four timings in the running of the service, taken against the district reference, agree to within five seconds.*

Four timings. Five seconds. **The same five seconds.** The same classification of a signal's tolerance, the same number, the same city, two pieces of paper about forty years apart with a different subject on each.

The conductor times the run off the signal. That is not a practice; that is the method, and it is a good method, and it is what makes a timetable in this city worth printing. At the Green Span terminus the service is on the road when the disc in the box says it is, and the disc in the box says what the hospital branch says, and the hospital branch is **one hour thirty-six minutes and fifty seconds** out of the district reference.

So the service cannot show one timing. Not one of the four. It shows four correct timings against a signal, and the signal is wrong, and a conductor who corrects for the signal by hand is a man who has decided the box on the post is not the schedule, and there is no form on which that is permitted and there is a bus behind him.

**The service came off the public timetable on Wednesday the seventeenth of the eighth month, from eight in the morning until two in the afternoon, and it was not a strike and it was not a fault and nobody chose the day.**

---

**What Wednesday the seventeenth looked like, and it took about a week to find out properly because every one of the four institutions involved kept its own record and none of them kept anybody else's.**

The tram company put on a private hire: a hired motor bus and a hired tramcar, at **four pounds eight shillings against one pound three on the public table** for the same six hours and the same four and a half miles. The difference is **three pounds five shillings**, and it is not a large number, and it is a number nobody in this district can carry, because a tram that carries nobody has a fare table, and a bus hired for six hours does not.

The **galvanising and wire works** hired it. Ninety-one persons, of whom sixty-one are resident in this district, and the early shift begins at half past seven, and about twenty of the men come in on that tram, and the works paid the three pounds five shillings out of a gate fund that is in no rate and in no return and is a tin at the front office with a wages bill in it.

The **market** on Bellweather Road is on Wednesdays, and it is on that route, and the traders from the far side come in on the first tram, and about forty stalls opened between a quarter past eight and a quarter to nine, and nine of them did not, and the nine who did not lost a morning's trade and went home and came back on the Thursday.

The **school** — the one on the Weir Lane side, about two hundred and ten children in two sessions — has a bus of its own for the second session, and the bus route runs through the Green Span junction because the junction is the only way through, and **the hired bus is not on a public timetable and is therefore not in the box on the post.** The signal at that junction governs the road. It is the same signal. It is one hour thirty-six minutes and fifty seconds out, and it is clearing a road for a tram that is not coming, and the box does not know the difference between a service on a timetable and a bus full of children that is late.

---

**And the eleven minutes, which is the part that will be remembered by about four people and is in one column nowhere.**

At about twenty past eleven a hired bus with the second session of that school on it came up to the Green Span junction and the disc in the box was white, and a woman of fifty-one called **Perpetua Nye**, who has driven that line for twenty-six years and who times her run off that box and has never once in twenty-six years corrected for it by hand, put her hand on the stop.

She held it for **eleven minutes.** She held it with the tram behind her, which was not running, because the tram was on the private hire and the private hire was on the other side of the city collecting a works' shift, and the road behind her was empty except for a delivery van and a man on a bicycle.

There was no accident. There was no near one that anybody could prove. There was a bus of about a hundred and forty children standing at a junction in a district of a hundred and forty thousand people, on a hot afternoon, on a day nobody chose, because a woman did the only thing she had ever been trained to do and the thing she was trained to do is **refuse to go**, and refusing to go is the whole of a conductor's authority and it is not in a form.

**Eleven minutes at a Green Span junction is eleven minutes for two hundred and ten children, a market's ninth trader, and about twenty men of an early shift at a works.**

The school lost the tail of its morning. The market's nine went home. The works took the late hour out of the shift the way the works has taken it out of thirty-one other shifts in the district's history, which is to say it came out of the men's pay, and it is in a pay book in a ledger at the front office of a works in Bellweather and there is no column in it for the reason.

**And the eleven minutes are not anybody's fault, in the way that is the whole of this volume.** The signal was certified by a licensed man against its own local beat and is within tolerance. The hospital branch's error is frozen at one hour thirty-six minutes and fifty seconds because nine men with a property qualification voted on a Monday that the front should have two cycles. The clause about the fourth draw was typed onto a printed sheet in 1951 and there is no minute of it. The far end of the Weir Two branch is a gate and the gate is two hours and twenty-nine minutes and twenty seconds out. The certificate is true. The ordinance is correct. The conductor is a competent woman who has kept a timetable for twenty-six years. **Nobody in this city can put any two of those six facts in the same sentence and the sentence they would make up is the reason a hundred and forty children stood at a junction for eleven minutes on a Wednesday afternoon.**

---

**And the guild is in the room in an official capacity, and the guild is not the villain, and Gerren Hask is not in it, and the reason he is not in it is the reason he is a repairer under public oversight.**

The standing orders from the first of the sixth require a master's certificate to be countersigned and the list published monthly. There is a board on the licensing floor, four feet by three, and it has been there since the first of the sixth, and it is a list of nineteen lines a month, and it is there for the rest of Hask's life and he put it up himself in about four minutes on the morning of the first of the sixth, half an hour after he suspended a man at a counter.

**He may certify no shift alone.** Every certificate he issues is countersigned by a holder of the first class, and the countersigners are two men in the Sloughs, and the list is published, and the list is a hundred and forty lines a year of a hall doing its job in public and being entirely correct.

The signal's certificate is a class-two certificate and it does not involve him and could not. It is on the list because the list is a list of every certificate issued by a master and it is in a hall where four hundred people a week walk past the counter, and **the only thing anybody has ever said about that board in this city is that it is a punishment, and it is a punishment, and the correct use of a well-run system, and the man who put it up there did not do it as a punishment and would not be thanked for it and has not been.**

The only thing he said about it, and he said it to a man on a platform in July, was: **"It is a list. That is all it has ever been. A list is not a shield and it is not a sword and it does not put anybody's hand up, and there are a hundred and forty lines on it a year and about nine people read the whole of it."**

---

**And the last thing, and it is the thing Orrin Vale could not do anything about, and he did everything he could do about, and it is in a book in a pump house and in a card in a nurses' room and in a published minute and nowhere else.**

He could not move the hospital branch. Nobody can. The branch's error is not a fault and there is no adjustment for it; the fix is a decision about the number of draws on the canal front, and the Water Users' Committee has that, and it has used it twice, and it will use it again in October, and its reasons in October are as good as its reasons in August.

He could not re-certify the signal. He has no licence and may not sign anything his class does not permit, and the offence is spent, and there is no route by which a man with a suspended licence gets a signal re-certified except by asking somebody else to do it, and the somebody else did it correctly and is in the Sloughs.

He could not get the branch's figure into the certificate, because a certificate of condition does not take the district reference, and he asked at a counter on the Monday and the answer was that it never has.

What he did, on the Sunday, the Monday, the Tuesday and the Wednesday, was write the figure in four places with the date on it, and put one of them where a bus conductor would have to read it to do her job, which is not a wall and not a pump house.

**He gave it to Perpetua Nye.**

He did it on the Tuesday afternoon at the Green Span terminus, and it took about four minutes, and he did not tell her what to do with it, and he did not ask her to change anything, and he did not tell her that anybody in the city had to be told, because there is no such person.

"You time your run off that box," he said. "You have told me you have never corrected for it and I have believed you, and I am not asking you to correct for it. I have got a figure and it is the same figure as that box and I would like you to have it. There is a date on it. If it is ever different, it is because nine men met somewhere, and I would rather you heard that from me than from a box."

She looked at the paper for about a minute.

"I have known since about June," said Perpetua Nye. "I have a house at the far end of the Northglass side and my own clock at home is on the same line as that box. I have watched the pair of them for about eleven weeks and they do not agree, and they are both wrong, and I have never told anybody because I could not have said what I was complaining about." She folded it once and put it in her coat. "You are the first man anybody has ever handed me a number about my own work and not asked me to do something with it. I am not going to thank you and I will tell you why, and then I will hold that bus at that junction for as long as it takes, because that is not your number and that is my hands."

> *Fourteenth to the eighteenth of the eighth.*
> *A signal out by 1 h 36 min 50 s, a certificate that is true, an ordinance that is correct, a fare table, a school, a market, a works' early shift, and eleven minutes at a junction on a Wednesday that nobody chose.*
> *And the one figure is doing four jobs: it is on the ward card in the nurses' room at Green Span, and it is in the inspector's column of a certificate in the works' file, and it is in the district works' return, and it is the condition described in R-9 reference 419, which is a note in a drawer in an office with four people in it. Four buildings, one number, and there is nothing anywhere in this city that joins any two of them, and I have been trying for eight months to build the join and the join is not a document, it is a person, and a person is at the end of a bus.*
> *And a woman of fifty-one held a bus for eleven minutes with her hand on a stop and there is no form in this country that says she was entitled to, and no column in this city for the eleven minutes, and she was not thanked, and she said she was not going to be, and she gave the reason, and the reason was that stopping is her hands and not my number.*
> *And I have no licence and I have fixed four hundred and thirty-one clocks this year and got paid for about ninety of them, and on Wednesday I stood on a pavement outside a signal box for an hour and a half and the only thing I had to give anybody was a piece of paper with a number on it, and I gave it away, and I have been paid for the feeling of having given it away exactly zero times and the sum is a figure I did not put in a book and am not going to.*
