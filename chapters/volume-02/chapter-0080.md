# Chapter 80 — A Door in Northglass, and a Woman of Twenty-Four

The argument between two people in a room over a shop in Bellweather was opened on the ninth of the fourth month and it is closed on the fourteenth of the tenth, in a room with a stove in it, in a form neither of them wanted, by a decision both of them made deliberately, and both of them were right, and neither of them has withdrawn one word of the argument, and the thing that came out of it is a list of one line in chalk on a wall in a pumping ward four miles away, and nobody was thanked, and a woman of twenty-four walked in off the street in the middle of it and the reason she came is that nobody knocked.

---

**Monday the tenth of the tenth, at about seven in the evening, in the two rooms over a chandler's shop, and there were six people in them and one of the two rooms was not used.**

The argument is old and both halves of it are on the page and neither half has been softened by being held for six months.

**Wenna Pruitt's half:** *A printed page has no address. A list of addresses is a list of routes, and a list of routes is a list of visits, and a person decides which door gets a knock, and the person on the list does not get to say no. And the twenty houses on those two streets that are not in the column are the ones who find out afterwards, from a neighbour, and they are not in the column because the person who made the column did not know they were there.*

**Teo Arden's half:** *A list that sits in a kitchen and is not used is a list that somebody else will use in about four years without asking you, and the only reason it has not been used in six months is that four people in this district knew it existed. That is not a safeguard. That is four people.*

They said both halves on the Monday evening, in full, in about twenty minutes, having both of them ready, and then Wenna Pruitt put her hand flat on the table, which is a thing she does, and said:

"**We said in June that we would not be the same arithmetic in a year. It is a year. We are going to be a different arithmetic and it is going to be ugly and neither of us is going to like it, and I am going to say mine and then I am going to read yours out of your own book because you have not brought it and you have never once brought it.**

"**You will not knock on them. You have written that down in your own book on the day you were given them and not on the day you decided, and you gave the reason, and the reason was that a man decides on the day he decided and would put that date instead and then in four years it would read as a decision and not as a promise. That page exists. Everybody in this room has read it or will read it. It is the only promise in this district that is in writing and was made to a person who never asked for it, and it is yours, and it is load-bearing, and I am not going to let this room decide anything on the ground that it has been forgotten.**"

"It has not been forgotten," said Orrin Vale.

"**I know it has not. That is why I said it first, and it is not a compliment to you, it is a thing I do before a room starts.**"

---

**And then the compromise, and it is four lines long and it is on one sheet, and it is the ugliest document anybody in this district has produced this year.**

> *The list of twenty-six service pipes held in the book of the Bellweather and Weir Lane sick and burial mutual, with a date against each, is not to be printed, is not to be copied out, and is not to be used to plan any journey, any route, any list of calls, or any order of visits, by any person, on any ground, for ever.*
> *It is not to be added to any other list.*
> *No person is to go to any of the twenty-six addresses for the purpose of this matter or of any question arising out of it.*
> *The twenty-six households are not to be told.*

Four signatures, and the last two lines are the price, and the price is on the sheet in the room where it can be read, and nobody is going to be able to pretend later that it was not on the table.

"**That last line is the price,**" said Wenna Pruitt, "and I am not going to explain it, because I explained it in June and I have explained it in three rooms since and I am tired of it and it is true. The twenty-six are not told. They have a service pipe with a date against it going back to 1931 and they are not going to be told and they are not going to be asked, and the twenty houses on those two streets that are not in the column are going to find out that anybody at all has been interested in their street, from a neighbour, in about four years, and there is nothing in those four lines that stops it, and there is nothing anybody in this room can do about it, and I have looked."

"And what do we get," said Teo Arden, "for the twenty-six not being told?"

"**We get a promise that is in writing, that is signed by four people, and that names a thing that is not to happen instead of a thing that is not to have happened. That is the whole of the difference between a safeguard and a hope and it is worth what it is worth and it is not nothing.**"

---

**And on Tuesday the eleventh, at about half past seven in the evening, a woman of twenty-four came up the outside stair of a building in Bellweather and knocked on the frame of a door that has not worked since about 1949, and nobody sent for her.**

**Nell Sayer is twenty-four. She is a wire-drawer at the galvanising and wire works and she has lived at number nine, Weir Lane, for six years, and number nine is the ninth of the twenty-six addresses, and she is on the list with a service pipe last looked at on the fourteenth of March 1948, in a hand that is not Adela Marrow's.**

There were seven people in the room. Orrin Vale was one of them.

"I know what that is," she said, before she had sat down. "**I have read it. It is on the board at the pumping ward and it is on the paper you have got in your coat and I know that there are twenty-six of us and I know that I am one of them, and I have known since about the fourth of the seventh and I have not come before because nobody came.**"

Nobody said anything.

"So I have come, and I am not here about the list. **I am here because there is a thing on a piece of paper on that table with my street on it and a clock on it, and you are asking three people to stand under three clocks on the third of a month, and two of the three of you are not from here.**" She put her hand on the table. "I have worked at Bellweather since I was eighteen. I know what it is to be the person somebody's daughter is."

"Will you read the minute," said Teo Arden, and he read it to her, and it is four lines and a date and it took him about a minute and a half.

When she had finished she sat down.

"Right," she said. "**Then here is the whole of what I have come to say and it is one thing and I have been saying it to myself since about the seventh of the seventh.**

"**I am not going to put my name to it.**

"Mr Vale, you have not knocked on number nine and I am not going to say thank you for it, because you would have had to break a promise you made in your own book to a woman you have never met, and that is not a favour, it is arithmetic. And I want it on the record that nobody in this district has to walk up my stairs about anything.

"**And that is exactly why I am not going to sign.** The first of the fourth month, a woman of twenty-six at a counter in this city told you the form you filled in is a return and a return is not a party to a question. I have never met her and I know she is right, because I live on the same main. **The only thing that has ever made a person of this district count for anything in a room is a form, and the form is two shillings the head for a year, and two shillings buys a line in a list and a line in a list is not a person.**"

"Go on," said Orrin Vale, and his voice was not steady and he did not try to make it so.

"**So the day somebody knocks on number nine and asks me to be the person of this district, there will be one name on the paper and it will be mine. And I will be the woman who agreed to it.** And on the day the branch goes off — and it has gone off twice since the first of September and every person on that street knows it — the one who comes to number nine will be whoever the name on the paper is, and it will be me, and I will have four minutes' notice and a bucket and no form."

She looked round the room.

"**And nobody in this room is going to come to number nine. That is the entire point of the four lines on that sheet and I want it understood that I know it and I am not withdrawing anything, and I am not signing, and the two facts have nothing to do with each other.** There is no form that says I have agreed to be the one they come to. There is a form that says I am a head on a return and I have paid for it and it cost me two shillings and it got me nothing anybody could refuse me for, **and I would rather have nothing that is honest than a name that somebody will come and find me about.**"

Nobody in the room said a single word for about twenty seconds.

"That," said Wenna Pruitt at last, "is the best thing anybody has said in this building since the fourth of the fourth month, and I am not going to thank you for it and neither is he, and **you have just made his side of the argument true and yours true at the same time and I do not know anybody else in this city who could have done it in one evening.**"

---

**And on Wednesday the twelfth the door in Northglass was on the table again, and it was a door, and the price was forty pounds, and nobody closed it.**

It is in an envelope in the works' file with a minute on top of it that binds nobody, and the minute says that it was proposed, and the price, and that a member of the district refused it and gave a reason, and that no other person has been asked, and that the file is open to anybody who comes to that counter with a reason.

"You have not been asked," said Ambrose, to Orrin Vale, in front of eleven people, because he is about thirty and he has been at a counter eleven years and he does this on purpose.

"No."

"Then here it is again, and the price has not moved, and I am not going to argue with you about it, because I put it on a table in this office in the eighth month and a man of twenty said no to me in front of nine people, and he was right, and I have not said a word about it since. **Forty pounds. Eight hundred shillings. Four hundred men at two shillings the head for a year. A tin at the front office with a wages bill in it and no minute taken about it anywhere in this building, and the whole thing could be done in a morning.**"

"Teo," said Wenna Pruitt.

"No." He did not raise his voice. "**I said no in August for one reason and no in September for a different one and I have said both of them out loud in rooms and neither of them is this one.**

"This is the third. **I have been asked whether I would put my name to it, and I would, and that is the reason. If I am one of two or three men in this district who will put their name to a document, then a document can be made here, and a district that can make a document is a district that can be asked a question, and the whole of the reason there is not one is that there has never been a room, and a man in a room is a different animal from a man on a road and I have found that out in five weeks.** So: no, and the price has not moved, and I would like it minuted that the price has not moved and that it would not have moved at a hundred times that, which is what Mr Vale wrote down at a counter in the seventh month and which is the only sentence of his that I have ever agreed with."

"The minute is in the works' file and it binds nobody and it can be lost."

"**Then lose it,**" said Teo Arden. "That is what I am asking for, in the end. I am asking for a thing that can be lost, in a city where everything anybody important has ever written down has been kept for nineteen years in a room with a keeper and a lock, and I have been in eleven rooms this year and the only one of them that ever hurt anybody was the room with the four hundred and eleven forms in it, and the keeper of that room has never been thanked for it and is sixty-one."

---

**And on Thursday the thirteenth at about eight in the evening the rehearsal was proposed, in full, with its price, and it was not run, and it is set for the eleventh month, and nobody put their name to anything.**

The three clocks are the three clocks. The pumping ward, canal road, outer room, **57 min 20 s**. The Weir Two pump house, station clock, **48 min 00 s**. The Quarter hill, south public clock, **1 h 49 min 10 s** and going to be more, because the rate is back.

The common question is on one sheet and it is the one from the twenty-eighth of the ninth and it is in the same words and it asks nothing at all, and it is signed by nobody, and it is the safest paper anybody in this district has produced this year and it is safe because there is no question in it.

**The two operators are named, and neither of them will sign anything, and both of them said so before anybody asked them to.**

**Ivo Pell**, thirty-six, veteran operator, twelve years in the Question Hall, has four certificates to countersign this month, two in the Sloughs and one of them a bus, and who came and looked at what had been written about on the twenty-seventh of September and went away and said *a rehearsal* and would not countersign a piece of paper with nobody's name at the bottom of it and said he would not do it now and would not do it then.

**Sabine Kaye**, forty-four, a holder of the first class, over a shop, about four certificates a month, twelve years, a watch and a second watch and a bench, who has never been to this district and has said so and was asked whether she would come and said yes and then said what it would cost.

**And this is the table, and it was on the table on Thursday evening and it is on the wall in a pumping ward in a pumping ward's outer room in chalk, and it can be read by anybody who goes in there:**

> *What a rehearsal costs, the first time it is run properly, and nobody has run one.*
>
> *Three clocks, one common question, one minute written down in advance, and three hands.*
> *Two operators. **I. Pell** — will not put his name to anything; has four certificates a month and a fortnightly arrangement with a man in a shop which he does not describe as instruction and which he will not be thanked for. **S. Kaye** — will come; will not be paid; will not sign; has said that in her own words she will do it for nothing and write nothing down and that a thing written down by nobody is worth what it is worth and she is the nobody.*
> *One person of the affected district. **There is none. There has not been one for nineteen years. There is no form by which one could be had for less than two shillings the head for a year, and two shillings the head buys a return, and a return is not a party to anything, and a woman of twenty-four who lives on that main said so in this district on Tuesday evening and refused to be the one, and gave a reason that nobody in this room was able to improve on.***
> *A person to hold the sheet. **B. Sarr**, who has said she will hold it and has said she will not be thanked, and who has a board in a ward with six figures in chalk on it and a line above them about a rate and a figure.*
>
> ***The total cost of the first run: about four hours a week for five people for a month, of which nobody can be paid for any part of, because there is no ratepayer, and the four lines on the wall in the pumping ward are four lines on a wall.***

**And the thing that was said about the eleventh month, and it is the last decision anybody in this district took this month, and it was taken on a Thursday evening by a show of hands with no minute book newer than 1948:**

That it be attempted in the eleventh month, on a date to be fixed, with those two operators and those three clocks and that question, and that **nobody be asked to put their name to it**, and that the whole of it be done in the open, in daylight, with the sheet on the ledge under the glass case beside the ledger and not in it.

"That is not a plan," said Wenna Pruitt. "**That is a thing five people have agreed to attempt in a month, with the cost on the wall, and it cannot stop one single thing in this city, and if the rate is a rate in the eleventh month as well, which it will be, then the fifth of the month the three clocks will be further apart than they are tonight and the four lines on the wall will still be four lines on a wall.**"

"I know."

"**And you have not asked one person in this district whether they want it, and there is no form on which you could have, and the reason you have not asked is that you have spent six months establishing that asking is the only instrument and there is none. That is not a contradiction, Mr Vale. That is the whole of your position and I am not going to be the one to soften it for you.**"

---

**And on Friday the fourteenth of the tenth, at about half past seven in the evening, six people were in that room and nobody thanked anybody and the cost was on the table in chalk and the question was asked out loud, and it is the question.**

Somebody put the scale of what they had.

"There is a range on two boards in this city and it says forty to sixty thousand people came out of the model in eighteen years in four hundred and eleven batches at two shillings the head." Teo Arden had it written out on the back of an envelope. "There is a provisional sum of one thousand eight hundred and ninety pounds on a works' sheet for six hundred brackets nobody has ever looked at. There is a company in an envelope that is not formed. There are three clocks and a sheet and a person of the district who says no. There is a rail of a question room that has been filled from Green Span and Northglass for about twenty years by a rota and every one of those Readings was lawful. **And there is a box upstairs with a fee of three shillings on it and an application received and not produced and not refused and set for the twenty-first of the first month of the year after next, and a person may be present and may speak and may not be represented.**"

"**So where does a person go,**" said Wenna Pruitt, "**to refuse?**"

And nobody answered it, and she did not ask it twice, and it sat in a room in Bellweather for about a minute and a half with six people in it and a stove going and a cost on the wall four miles away in chalk.

Then Orrin Vale stood up and put his hand flat on the table, which is a thing two women in this book do and which he does about once a year and which he has never once done in a room with a woman in it.

"**I have got one thing to say and it is not an argument and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am going to say it and then this meeting is over.**

"**In the fifth month of last year, at about four minutes to ten past one in the morning, in a pump house on this canal road, in the dark, in front of about twenty people, I put my name under three hundred and sixty minutes, and I had no standing to sign it, and I knew I had no standing to sign it, and there was nowhere else in this city to put my name. That is what I did, and it cost me a licence, and the licence was suspended on the first of the sixth in four true lines by a man who had told me exactly what would happen about a quarter of an hour beforehand, and he was right, and I would do the hour again at six in the morning in front of the people it is done to.**

"**There is a room.**" He did not look at anybody. "There is a room in Bellweather with a stove in it and there are people in it and there is a number on the table and there is a cost on the wall in chalk in a pumping ward, and **the number is in the room, and I do not get to decide.**

"So I am going to stop talking, and I am going to go and do four hours of somebody else's clock tomorrow, and the eight guineas is already written out and the price is on it, and I am not going to come back here in a fortnight with a different version of it, and if I do, you will all know that somebody taught me to, and I will not have it."

He put his hand off the table and picked up his coat.

"Nobody is to be thanked," he said, at the door. "**Especially me, and especially not on the twenty-first of the first month of the year after next.**"

> *Tenth to the fourteenth of the tenth. Six months and four days since the ninth of the fourth, which is the day a man of twenty stood up in a room over a shop in Bellweather and said a printed page has no address, and neither of the two people in that argument has withdrawn a word of it, and both of them are right, and it is closed.*
> *The compromise is four lines on a sheet and it is a list, and the list is twenty-six addresses and I have not knocked on any of them and I am not going to, and it is in my own book dated the day I was given them, and it has been said out loud in a room twice now and it is going to be said out loud again.*
> *A woman of twenty-four came up a stair nobody sent her up, she lives at the ninth of the twenty-six, and she has refused to put her name to a piece of paper about her own street and has given a reason that four people in a room could not improve on, and she is the only person of this district anybody in this district has met this year, and nobody knocked.*
> *Forty pounds is still in an envelope in a works' file. The door is still open. A rehearsal is set for the eleventh month with two operators who will not sign, three clocks, a question with nothing in it, and no person of the district, and it is on the wall in chalk with the cost on it, and it cannot stop one single thing in this city.*
> *A scale is on two boards and the four hundred and eleven head counts want an audit, and an office of four people is going to ask for the box and has not been refused in writing and has not yet applied, and I have not been thanked and I am not going to be.*
> *And a person asked a question out loud in a room on the fourteenth of the tenth and nobody answered it, and the question is where does a person go to refuse, and I did not answer it, and I am not going to answer it, and I have got a bench and a lease to Michaelmas and a form that runs out on the first of the fifth of next year and a line of chalk on a wall with a date on it and no name on it, and I am going to be the man who was in the room when the question was asked and I am not going to be the man who decides.*
