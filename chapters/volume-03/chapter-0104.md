# Chapter 104 — The Beam on Weigh Lane

Sunday the twenty-second of the first month Orrin stayed away from the assay office, because no counter sits on a Sunday in Morrowfen, and a chapter that runs through one may not put a counter, a board, a rail, a review or a pitch on it.

He spent the Sunday mending a school clock for Monday and reading the row book's fifth page that Teo had left, which was about the maintenance schedule with six names and an empty seventh line and eighteen shillings and sixpence a week of which nobody could pay the eighteen shillings. The seventh line was empty because a master had pinned a strip of form inside a labour book saying he had read all of it and would not say, and Orrin understood the empty line better now than he had in December: some instruments cost more than the district that needs them, and the cost is not an argument against them.

On Monday the twenty-third Mara Venn came to the bench at nine with a folder under her arm and a look that said she had been up since six with columns.

"I have had a letter," she said. "From Hale. Broker. He writes to the Office asking whether he may be told the drift of the Weirs' clocks, with a date on it. He wants a figure he can put on his rate card without copying the City's numbers wrong."

"What did you tell him?"

"Nothing. The Office has no standing to give him anything. The drift is on the ward board outside the Weir Two pump house and any man may read it. About eleven people a day do. He is one of them if he walks."

She put the folder down. "I am auditing the market. Not the men. The arithmetic. For a set of households the market may be the only instrument by which they can buy back time official tolls took. I am going to lodge it with the working whether or not anybody wants it. It will cost me the post. An office which audits cannot also be the office a person appeals to. I have known that for nine years."

Orrin said nothing, because there was nothing useful to say to a woman who had just told him the price and the date in one sentence.

On Tuesday the twenty-fourth the arcade was full. Orrin went at ten and stayed till twelve, not buying, watching. The crowd on an ordinary Tuesday was the market's own argument: porters, conductors, two printers, a laundress with a basket, nurses, a chandler, nine or ten small employers who had discovered that a hundred minutes was a wage. Money changed hands openly. Nobody hid. Hale sold eleven tokens before noon at four shillings and sixpence, each with its district letter showing, each wrapped in paper with the price written on it.

A woman argued that the price had been four shillings last Friday. Hale showed her the rate card and the City's daily sheet underneath it and said the price was the price because the drift was the drift, and she bought one anyway and put it in her glove.

Orrin left at noon and walked to the Public Assay Office on Weigh Lane, which was a beam, a counter, and standard weights to half a grain, a public counter, free, about four minutes a weighing.

The keeper was a man of about sixty who had weighed a token nine times in nineteen years and every one of the nine was somebody bringing one back. He told Orrin so while he calibrated the beam, because keepers tell the history of their instrument to anybody who stands still long enough.

"Nobody brings them to be told what they weigh," the keeper said. "They bring them to be told they are real. Six that weigh one thousand eight hundred and sixty grains are real. Three that weigh one thousand seven hundred are not, but I have not seen those three yet and I am telling you the figures from the book."

"Will you weigh one of mine?" Orrin said. He had no token of his own, so he borrowed the printer's description instead: Q, a hundred, year and die on the rim. "In theory."

"In theory, four minutes, free, anybody may bring one." The keeper set the weights out. "The instrument that can tell a real token from a false one exists, costs nothing, and is not used. That is the whole of this office and it has been so for nineteen years."

Orrin watched the beam come to rest on nothing, which was the point: the beam was true whether or not anybody used it.

On Wednesday the twenty-fifth he wrote the assay in the grey ledger, and the crowd, and Mara's letter, and under them a line about Ivo Pell, whom he had not seen since September and would not see in this city, because Pell had left a method and a price and a sentence about disconnection and the city had kept all three.

*Pell, in a document: three clocks, one question, four minutes, and what it is not. Unpaid. The method is on two sheets printed Sunday twenty-sixth December at eleven. That is the only place he is in this week, and he is not in the room.*

Nobody thanked anybody in the assay office, and nobody thanked anybody in the arcade, and the keeper did not look for it. He had weighed nine tokens in nineteen years and was about to weigh the tenth, and the cost was nothing, and the four hours it would take a district to learn the habit was four hours a person had to give it.
