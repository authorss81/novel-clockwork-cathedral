## Batch goal

Take the consequences of the last batch. A counter-record was proved useful and proved dangerous in the same afternoon; a man was right about a pump and could not act; a licence is due and a form is waiting. This batch turns a district's own arithmetic into something that can be produced in a room, and then puts that room underneath a machine that omits the same people for the second time, lawfully, at ten times the scale, in front of all of them.

The batch's genre promise: **an organisation is a thing that can be wrong in a new way.** The Loose Minutes can prove the district was wronged. Once they can also decide what to do about it, they can be outmanoeuvred by a form, which is the thing the book is actually about.

## Starting condition

A fortnight after Chapter 20, which finished on Monday the 20th. Perry Vance has been dead a month. Three minutes is still the only number the city has published. Orrin has a copper wedge's worth of knowledge and a rule three days old that can stop him, a class-three licence expiring on the 19th of the month after next, an optional interview with Hask on Tuesday the 28th, and a W-4 form in his case. The Weirs have eleven copies of the guild's labour book in nine buildings, a woman who has lost nineteen years for making them, a landlord who has got her rent rise back, and no organisation.

The ninth tick is still unexplained. The Archive of Unlisted is still shut. Anja Voss and Halden Rusk have each appeared once, on paper, and will not appear again.

## Batch shape

- **Beginning (Chapters 21–22):** a method, and a domestic argument about what it is for. The counter-record acquires a procedure and the procedure acquires a bill.
- **Escalation (Chapters 23–24):** the guild's four true lines, and the interview that binds nobody and costs a fortnight of sleep.
- **Midpoint (Chapter 25):** the second and larger public Reading. The volume's failure becomes repetition.
- **After the reversal (Chapters 26–28):** the bill, the renewal, and the W-4. The volume changes shape and the batch does too.
- **Aftermath (Chapters 29–30):** work, figures somebody else counted, and a district deciding what it is.

## Batch midpoint (Chapter 25)

A second and larger public Reading on the Weirs' own water schedule. Properly filed, properly witnessed, technically correct, approved by people who have already read the blind margin once and voted to proceed. The water it saves is worth more than the bridge was. The people it omits are the same people, and they are in the room, and they are not on the rail.

**The reversal is of scale, not of plot.** Nothing in Chapter 25 is new. Everything in it is a repetition. The Cathedral's answer, when the omission is pointed at in public, is that an acceptable blind margin is a category the Compact recognises, that the alternative is a city that cannot make a decision at all, and that the committee which approved it had the figures and did the arithmetic. Every one of those statements is true. The reader's anger should be entirely reasonable and entirely useless.

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 21 — "Nine Copies and a Method"

**Type:** political or social pressure, with repair work in it.
**Immediate goal:** Get the Weirs' own hours into a form that a room can be made to read, without losing the only property that makes it theirs, which is that nobody signed it.
**Resistance:** a counter-record becomes a method or it stays a complaint, and becoming a method means somebody has to decide who holds it, who copies it, who may be asked for it, and who may not. The candidates disagree about all four. Bevin Sarr will not have her board leave the pump house and has a reason that is not sentimental. Wenna Pruitt has kept eleven years in a kitchen and will not have them in a drawer in a committee room, and she is right about both refusals. Teo has eleven copies in nine buildings and no index, and an index is a thing with an owner.
**Information revealed:** what an unlisted district's evidence actually consists of, and the fact that a method has a cost paid in labour by people who are not in it. Also the first time a group of Weirs residents goes somewhere on purpose, which is the batch's smallest and most important event.
**Action or decision:** they settle on a rotating custody arrangement with no owner, which is a compromise and is going to fail, and the chapter says so while it is happening.
**Emotional change:** the district discovers that being able to produce its own hours is the beginning of a set of obligations and not the end of a grievance.
**Power or resource change:** none. Institutional, not personal.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Bevin Sarr's board does not leave the pump house. The eleven copies remain in nine buildings. Nobody is given a title.
**Ending type:** cost, and a first small piece of organisation.
**Next-chapter pull:** three people want three different things out of the same eleven-year-old book and will not hold a meeting.

### Chapter 22 — "A District Is Not a Bloc"

**Type:** political or social pressure.
**Immediate goal:** Find out whether the Weirs can be asked to want one thing.
**Resistance:** a landlord, an employer and a tenant council want three incompatible things from one record and will not sit in a room together. Hanna Stroud is one of the landlord class and is not a villain and wants the rise back. The employers want the district to look steady. The tenant council wants the hours published. Each of them is right and the record cannot serve all three. **Sel Kade is in the room and is not on anybody's side**, because a pump warden's interest is the water and everybody else is talking about something else, and she says so and is resented for it.
**Information revealed:** the structural problem stated as a domestic argument. *The Weirs* is a place, not a people, and characters who treat it as a bloc are usually about to be wrong — the bible's rule, delivered as a row in a kitchen rather than a warning from a narrator.
**Action or decision:** the meeting breaks up without a resolution and without a quarrel, and the three of them walk out within an hour of each other, and Bevin Sarr tells Orrin that this is the eleventh time.
**Emotional change:** Orrin understands that a district's best evidence is a thing that gets used against the district, and that this does not make the evidence wrong.
**Power or resource change:** none.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the Loose Minutes are not a syndicate. Sel Kade is an obstacle to this batch's organisation, not its leader.
**Ending type:** discovery, in the shape of a limit.
**Next-chapter pull:** the guild is asked, formally, for its labour ledger, and answers in four lines.

### Chapter 23 — "The Guild's Four-Line Reply"

**Type:** investigation or discovery.
**Immediate goal:** Put the hall's labour ledger in a request that has to be answered in public.
**Resistance:** the hall replies with the four lines from Chapter 10, in a form fit to be printed. *The hall keeps a certification ledger, which is a record of machines. The hall does not keep a record of hours for work that is not part of a certified shift, there being no such hours, there being no such shifts. Form GH-9 is a request form and not a record. The hall's price schedules are the hall's own property.* Every word is true. The hall has also, quietly, started the renewal clock and has moved one date that nobody asked it to move.
**Information revealed:** that the hall's defence is not a cover story but a definition, and that a definition which is true can be unbeaten by anything short of changing the definition. Mara's proposed change, a *name* on a withdrawal form, is the thing nobody has yet said out loud in a room, and somebody should say it and be told what it costs.
**Action or decision:** the request goes in and comes back. Nothing is gained and one thing is now certain: the guild ledger will not arrive by asking, and every attempt to take it further is a decision somebody with less to lose will have to make.
**Emotional change:** the exhaustion of doing everything correctly and being answered correctly.
**Power or resource change:** none.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Hask commits no impropriety. Della Marsh's guards hold: she is never thanked on the page, and the four shillings bought a form number and a shut door, not the ledger.
**Ending type:** cost, incurred in daylight.
**Next-chapter pull:** Tuesday the seventh, ten o'clock, a warm room, and a conversation that binds nobody.

### Chapter 24 — "The Twenty-Eighth"

**Type:** political or social pressure, played as a domestic scene.
**Immediate goal:** Survive an interview that cannot be refused without giving a reason, and give no reason. It is Tuesday the 28th, four weeks after the notice was pinned under a brass weight and a fortnight before the renewal.
**Resistance:** Hask does not threaten him once. He asks how the year went. He has the answer ready for both possible answers and he delivers whichever one is offered without any sign of having prepared it. He mentions the W-4 as housekeeping. He asks, once, and pleasantly, about the thimble in the regulator's flank, and does not ask again, and Orrin understands inside ten seconds that he has been told the price of a fact.
**Information revealed:** what a leash is made of when the leash is made of nothing. Nobody pulls it. It is simply there, on a Tuesday, at ten, in a room with tea in it, and there is no way to be absent from it that is not itself a line in a file.
**Action or decision:** Orrin does not hand over the grey ledger, does not lie, and asks one question of his own, which Hask answers courteously and which does not help. He comes away with a W-4 form, a date, and no information at all.
**Emotional change:** the specific exhaustion of a man who has been told the truth kindly and cannot use it.
**Power or resource change:** four weeks of margin, spent in an hour. His ear is worse because he slept badly the night before, and this is a condition and not a cost.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the interview is optional and binds nobody, exactly as promised. Hask is never asked about the Long Nine and never mentions the governor. The renewal is the 19th of the month after next and is not a threat.
**Ending type:** cost, quietly incurred.
**Next-chapter pull:** the water schedule, which nobody in this batch has mentioned.

### Chapter 25 — "The Water Schedule"

**Type:** political or social pressure. **The volume's midpoint reversal and the batch's centre.**
**Immediate goal:** Stop a Reading on the Weirs' water schedule, or at least be one of the named parties to it, before it is run.
**Resistance:** it cannot be stopped, because it is lawful, because it is correct, because the water it saves is worth more than the bridge was, and because the committee that approved it did the arithmetic and had the returns and voted. The question is a named act. The witnesses are three and lawful and the affected-district station is filled by a Green Span resident on a rota. **The Room is a thing that can be wrong in a new way:** the Weirs' delegates are in the gallery, not on the rail, because a standing is a written claim and the Weirs have none, and nobody in the building thinks this is a trick.
**Information revealed:** the volume's whole argument, at ten times the scale, in the correct form, read aloud. The blind margin is a required line in a small footer and it is read by nobody. When somebody reads it aloud, the room finds that the line has changed from *unregistered night work is not represented* to a category rather than a crew, and that a category is harder to argue with than a man's name, because a man's name can be put in a form and a category cannot.
**Action or decision:** the room objects. The objection is well made, made in the right terms, made by people with standing, and it does not stop the Reading, because the Compact does not give an objection the power to stop one. The result is better than the Chapter 4 result. The toll is larger and lands where the reserve is, not where the consequence is.
**Emotional change:** the discovery that being right, repeatedly, in the correct form, is not a position. This is the volume's reversal and it must be felt as a floor coming away rather than as a revelation.
**Power or resource change:** none for Orrin. His sense is not used, is not asked for, and is not relevant, and the chapter should make that visible in a single line without comment.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the blind margin at eleven to the point against twenty-two for the trace line and fourteen for the signature, unchanged. No paying district on the notice. **The same people are omitted. They are in the room. They are not on the rail.**
**Ending type:** reversal, complete, in a room, with everybody behaving reasonably.
**Next-chapter pull:** the bill, itemised, in the right currencies.

### Chapter 26 — "What the Second One Cost"

**Type:** investigation or discovery, with consequence.
**Immediate goal:** Find out what the Chapter 25 Reading actually took and who actually paid it, as opposed to what was chalked.
**Resistance:** the Toll Stair has the minutes and the district. The minutes are minutes and the district is a district. Everything else — the interval out of a witness, the shift somebody worked late, the ward that was on the wrong side of a line for four hours, the school bell, the ferry timetable, the bridge joint — is nowhere, and has to be found by people going and asking, which takes weeks and produces a list with no column. **Mara can produce the list and cannot make it a record.** The Office is ignored once here and the note is worse than a refusal.
**Information revealed:** that a larger Reading costs more in more currencies, that the currencies land in different places, and that the only one anybody can point at is the smallest.
**Action or decision:** the blind margin is read aloud in a room by somebody who knows exactly what it says, and the room does the thing the Chapter 4 room did not have the standing to do, which is to write down that it was read, and that changes nothing.
**Emotional change:** Leda's ward is on the bill and she is the one who has to say so, in a corridor, about a decision taken in a room she was not in, and this time she is not wrong and there is no answer.
**Power or resource change:** none.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** keep the two kinds of toll apart in every line. No body toll is ever given a number it can be charged against.
**Ending type:** cost, itemised, and nobody's fault.
**Next-chapter pull:** a form with four columns and one of them blank.

### Chapter 27 — "The Licence, Renewed"

**Type:** political or social pressure.
**Immediate goal:** Lodge the W-4, the fee and the endorsement, and keep a trade.
**Resistance:** nothing dramatic and everything structural. The form is a good form. The fee is 4s 6d. The endorsement is a signature and Hask gives it and is warm about it. What Orrin discovers by filling in the W-4 is that a hall form has three columns and one of them is a set of entries in a form the hall chooses, and that a man cannot write *I am not sure* in it, and that the honest entry is the one that costs the most and helps the least. The renewal is a renewal. He is renewed.
**Information revealed:** that institutional adoption is not villainy and is not betrayal, and that the moment of transfer is a form-filling, and nobody has to decide anything for it to happen.
**Action or decision:** he lodges it. He does not put the grey ledger's contents in and he puts in what the form can hold, and the gap between those two things is the batch's quietest and largest fact.
**Emotional change:** not grief. The specific flatness of a man who has discovered that the correct thing and the useful thing are different documents and there is no form that carries both.
**Power or resource change:** none, formally. He is a licensed maintainer on the 15th and will be a licensed maintainer through the end of the volume.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** **he is not suspended and no chapter may describe the suspension as coming.** The suspension is Chapters 44–49, for certifying a local toll he had no standing to certify, and Hask issues it ahead of his own clock.
**Ending type:** decision, made correctly, and it costs something nobody can see yet.
**Next-chapter pull:** the hall now holds a fair copy, and fair copies are what halls are for.

### Chapter 28 — "The Ledger, Copied"

**Type:** investigation or discovery.
**Immediate goal:** Find out who has a copy of what the Weirs' night work now looks like, and in whose hand.
**Resistance:** the answer is the hall, and the answer is legitimate, and the copy is a *fair copy*, signed by a person, which means it is better than the original by every standard the building recognises. Della Marsh, or somebody in her room, is the one who makes it, and she is not asked to explain and does not offer to, and the arrangement is exactly as it should be and completely unusable by the people it is about, because a fair copy cannot show a hand and a hand was the only evidence there was.
**Information revealed:** the difference between a record and a copy of a record, and the reason a *true copy* signed by a copyist who never knew what a standby was can be worth less than a piece of painted board.
**Action or decision:** the Weirs keep the board. The hall keeps the copy. Both are correct and the district now has to decide which one it will use, which is a decision about being believed rather than about being right.
**Emotional change:** Bevin Sarr's refusal in Chapter 21 is vindicated in a way she predicted exactly and does not enjoy.
**Power or resource change:** none.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Hester Lomas may appear once, working, and the question of her job is real. She does not become a martyr and she does not recur after this volume.
**Ending type:** discovery, and a loss that is somebody else's fault and nobody's decision.
**Next-chapter pull:** figures somebody else counted.

### Chapter 29 — "Recovery, With a Number In It"

**Type:** recovery, aftermath, or emotional payoff.
**Immediate goal:** Work. Four days of it, ordinary, with a hand that will not close and an ear that will not stop.
**Resistance:** the ordinariness. There is nothing in a school bell for a man to hide behind, and the bodies he cares about are all in other districts on other people's money, and the one figure he can state — three minutes — is a fortnight old and is the smallest number in the book.
**Information revealed:** the first time Orrin can state somebody else's count as though it were his, because the district counted it and the Assembly printed it. He has become a person who cites.
**Action or decision:** he does four days of repair and goes and is told, in a kitchen, by three people who disagree, what the Chapter 25 Reading cost their street. He writes it down and it is in a book nobody can read and it is also, this time, in a printed return.
**Emotional change:** from shame to method, quietly, without a scene about it.
**Power or resource change:** none.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the renewal is over and nothing has happened. The Archive of Unlisted is exactly as shut. Ada Vance is on her tram and is not in this chapter.
**Ending type:** recovery, with a number in it, and a decision forming.
**Next-chapter pull:** the Weirs decide what they are, in their own rooms, without him.

### Chapter 30 — "What a District Decides"

**Type:** character and relationship work, with a political consequence.
**Immediate goal:** Be in a room he is not a member of, and not be the reason anything happens.
**Resistance:** the Weirs' tenant council, the mutuals, the pump ward and three blocks that do not agree with each other decide, in their own rooms, on their own reasons, that they will keep a rotating record and a rotating custody and will not take a position on the water schedule, because a position on the water schedule is a thing a district can be held to in a room. The decision is not a bloc, not a movement, and not what Orrin wanted. It costs them something real and visible and they do it anyway.
**Information revealed:** that consent is not agreement. A district can be organised, informed, furious, right, and still decline to be a single thing, and that this is a decision and not a failure of nerve, and that Orrin's rule — a person named who can refuse — works in the last direction he expected.
**Action or decision:** he is named in a footnote of somebody else's document for the second time and is glad of it. He goes back to work.
**Emotional change:** the first time being a footnote is what he wanted.
**Power or resource change:** none. He is the same man with more paper, worse hearing, and a district that will speak for itself and not on his behalf.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the Weirs are not a bloc and no character may treat them as one without the text showing that they are about to be wrong. Sel Kade is not recruited and is not on their side. Teo is in the room and is not grateful.
**Ending type:** decision and cost, with forward pull into batch 0004's slippage and Kade's documented diversion.
