# Chapter 55 — The Room With a Name and No Date

The fight, when it came, was never about the boxes. It was about whether an index is a public record, and it was lost on a Tuesday morning in a counter window on Cathedral Rise by two people who were both doing their jobs and neither of whom had ever lied to anybody.

---

**Sindri Lake keeps the public Archive room on Cathedral Rise.** It is a long room with a counter and a wire mesh and eleven feet of shelving behind the mesh and about four hundred people a week in it who have come about a shift record, a toll receipt, a permit, a rate, a death, a marriage, a will, or a mistake somebody's office made in good faith.

It is open to petition. It is the most useful room in Morrowfen and about four people in the city know that.

On the Tuesday morning of the twenty-first of the sixth month Orrin Vale came into it at about ten with a notebook and no licence and stood at the counter and asked, in the correct words, for the index of the Archive of Unlisted.

Lake said, "Certainly," and wrote it down, and then he said, "Which part of it."

---

That was the first thing and it took about four seconds and it is the reason the rest of the morning went the way it did.

"The index," said Orrin Vale.

"The Archive of Unlisted keeps, or is supposed to keep: a card index of names with the date each was taken out; a batch register, being the batches in which they were taken out, with a series, a head count, a price and a countersignature; an accession book, being what came in and when; and a shelf list. Of those four, the shelf list is a list of shelves and is in here, and it is eleven lines long and has been unchanged for nineteen years and is on the open shelf on the end for anybody to read."

"And the other three are not in here."

"None of the other three is in here," said Sindri Lake, "and I will tell you why, and I have told four people this in eleven years and I have never once been asked to stop, and I will tell you the reason first, because the reason is the whole of it and the answer is only the consequence.

"An index entry is a statement that something was taken out. It is not the thing that was taken out, and it never was, and it was never intended to be, and the day an index entry becomes a copy of what it indexes is the day somebody changes a head count in a room with no light in it and no window and no witness, and nobody outside knows for a year. The index is a *statement about* a set of records. The records are the records. The index is a public record of the fact of removal. It is not a public record of the removal, and I have got a rule about that which I made for myself in about 1919 and I have never written it down, and the reason I have never written it down is that a written rule is a thing somebody can repeal in a morning and my own practice cannot."

He looked up.

"And that is the honest reason. Here is the boring reason, and I am going to give you the boring reason as well, because you will not be able to do anything with the honest one. The index of the sealed archive is a record of the Cathedral Directorate. Records of the Directorate are produced by the Directorate, and this room has no function in the production of them, and the Office of Public Reckoning has no function either, and neither has the Assembly's committees, and neither has a ratepayer, and neither has a ward, and there is a form for requesting a public record out of this room and there is a different form for requesting a Directorate record and that one goes upstairs and the upstairs one has a fee, and the fee is three shillings, and the fee is not the obstacle. I have never been sure what the obstacle is and I have had eleven years to work it out and I have not."

---

Mara Venn came to the counter at about half past eleven with a folder and a form of her own, and the form of her own is four lines long and has been printed since the second of the third month and it is a note asking an office to write down that a document exists, and it carries no fee, and it has come back *received and noted* four times in nine years, once of which was after somebody rang the office up to thank her for it.

The four lines of her form, filled in, said this.

> *That a certain document exists, being the index of the Archive of Unlisted, maintained under the Cathedral Directorate, not open to petition.*
> *That a certain batch exists, being batch 21 of 4, transferred to the sealed archive on the date shown in the public index of transfers, comprising forty-one entries, series GH-9, standing withdrawal, maintenance-deferred class, at two shillings the head for a year, and countersigned on behalf of a district having no standing under the water act.*
> *That the Office has no function in the matter and does not ask for the records themselves, and does not ask to enter the rooms, and asks only for the index, and would be glad to be told that the index exists and is not produced and why, in a form, with a date on it.*
> *Received and noted is a complete answer. The Office would be obliged for it.*

Lake read it twice, which is the courtesy in that building, and then he said, "This is a good form," in a voice that was not at all warm, and then he said, "I have read this form before."

"You have read it before."

"I have read this form before," said Sindri Lake, "not this one, which is new, but its shape. Somebody in about 1921 wrote a form asking this room to write down that a document exists, and it was a better form than yours, and it came to the same place, and I remember it because it is the only time in thirty-one years a person in a counter window has been pleased to be beaten by a document."

---

**Della Marsh came up Cathedral Rise at about ten past one in the afternoon on her lunch**, which is a thing she has never done in thirty-one years and which she did that day because a woman telephoned the licensing floor at twenty to nine and asked her a question she could not answer out of her own head and would not answer out of the counter drawer without checking, and the thing she needed to check was in a copybook, and the copybook is in a room at the bottom of a hill.

She is sixty-one. She has kept the hall's paper for thirty-one years. She is frightened of an inquiry, loyal to nobody, and has never once been thanked for anything, and says so as a measurement and not as a complaint.

She came up on a bus, and she had her coat on, and she was given a chair in the counter window of a public archive room on Cathedral Rise and she sat in it, and she was not thanked for coming, and the person who telephoned her about it did not offer to pay her fare.

The number she had given a woman for four shillings in coin with no receipt in the second month of this year was **the transfer number of batch 21 of 4**, and it was written on the flyleaf of the hall's own copybook in a hand that gets smaller as it goes, and Della Marsh did not write it and has never seen it written except in that book.

> *M. Marsh brought the number out of the copybook and wrote it on the back of an envelope and did not give the envelope away and read the number off it twice and then folded it into her bag, and I did not offer to pay her and she did not ask and neither of us said one word about the four shillings in February, and that was correct of both of us.*

She was gone at about twenty past one and she said, on the way out, to nobody, "Thirty-one years, and it is the first time anybody has ever asked me for a number that I did not look up for them."

---

**And then Ada Vance was asked to be listened to, and was thirteen, and was not asked anything she could not answer.**

Lake sent for her. He sent for her himself, in a note, in his own hand, on the Tuesday morning, before Orrin Vale had thought of it, and the note said:

> *Miss Vance, twelve Fern Villas, is a person of interest in a public record and the room may ask a person of interest to attend, and if no person attends, the record is not made, and if the record is not made the room has to write that down as well. You are welcome to bring an adult. Nobody is going to ask you a question about a machine.*

She came at about two o'clock with an aunt who had to go at half past two, and she was told the room, and she was given a chair, and a woman of about sixty with a shorthand book was in the room and her book was shut and her hands were flat on it.

"Three questions," said Lake, "and no more, and I am going to ask them slowly."

The first was whether she was the daughter of Perry Vance, who was on the night crew at Weir Two on the seventeenth of the first month. She said yes.

The second was whether she knew where her father's hours went on the nights he worked.

Ada Vance thought about that for a while and then said, "He brought a tally back," and then, before anybody else could ask her to explain: "It was a card. It was a card with a number of hours on it and a bit at the top with a letter and a number, and he brought it home every night and he put it on the dresser, and my mam said put it away, and he said it's the only thing I've got, and then he put it away in the drawer under the plate cloth. And I have still got it, sort of. We moved in the March, so it is in a box in my aunt's now, and there is a number on the box, and I could not tell you what it is, and my aunt says it is a box of bills."

The third was whether she could remember any of the letter and the number on the card.

"It had a letter on it and then a slash," said Ada Vance, and then she said, "and I know because I used to draw it," and stopped.

"That is enough," said Lake, "and thank you, and I am not going to ask you one thing more." It was in a room with a counter and a wire mesh and about nine people in it, and none of the nine had said anything at all.

---

The request was received on the Thursday the twenty-third of the sixth month at about ten in the morning, and it was neither granted nor refused, and it is important that it was not refused, because a refusal is a thing a person can appeal and this was not that.

It is a form of four lines and it is in the Cathedral's own type and it says:

> **APPLICATION FOR PRODUCTION OF A DIRECTORATE RECORD.** Received. Not produced.
> *The record asked for is not a public record and is not produced to petitions. The record asked for is held. Application referred for consideration. Date set for consideration: the twenty-first day of the first month of the year after next.*
> *A person may be present at the consideration. A person may speak. A person may not be represented by another person, there being no party to a record, and the room has considered this and has not found an alternative and does not pretend one.*
> *Consideration is a date and not an answer, and no officer is named and no office is bound.*

Nineteen months. From the twenty-first of the sixth month to the twenty-first of the first month of the year after next, and he did that sum twice on the step of the counter, and both times it was nineteen, and both times it was a figure with no interest in it whatever.

"The date is the date," said Sindri Lake. "I have no power to move it and I have never been asked to move it and I would not if I were asked by anybody in this building. It is set by a schedule in an office upstairs that has a list of forty-one other dates on it, and every one of those forty-one is a record somebody has applied for, and the average of them is about fourteen months, and I have a card index of the dates in the drawer under the counter, and nobody has ever seen it, and nobody is going to."

"Why not," said Mara Venn.

"Because the card index is a list of names and dates and I am sixty-one years old and I am the only person in this building who knows which of the forty-one has gone quiet," said Sindri Lake, "and if I show you the list, then in about four years there will be a man in a room who can tell from a card index which applications to sit on. And you will not be that man, because you have not asked me for it and you have behaved properly all afternoon, and I am not going to be the reason a well-run office gets a new habit."

---

They were still on the step of the counter at about half past four in the afternoon and there was nothing else to do, which is when Mara Venn said the thing that she had not come up the hill to say and which she said in a very ordinary voice.

"There is a pencil mark in the public index of transfers," she said. "In the margin against batch twenty-one of four. In a hand I have seen on about nine other entries in about four years and I have never once asked him about and he has never once offered, and I know it is his because he writes his own entries in that hand and everything else in this building is a different man or a different century."

"I know about the pencil mark," said Orrin Vale.

"You know about the pencil mark."

"On the eighteenth of the fourth month. In a binder on the Rise, in a public index, in the margin against my own batch number. I have written it down and I have it in a grey book." He did not ask her how she knew. "What is written on it is *statement, not the thing*, which is Lake's hand, and it is the same phrase he has used this afternoon, and he has said it to me twice this afternoon without once saying he has written it down in a book somewhere, and he has not, because it is not a record, and a pencil mark in the margin of a public index by a man who is not obliged to make one is not a record and is not a document and is not in anybody's return."

"It is four marks in thirty-one years," said Mara Venn.

"Then it is four marks in thirty-one years," said Orrin Vale, "and it is the most valuable thing anybody in this city has done about that room in thirty-one years, and it is worth nothing, and there is nothing I can do to help whoever reads it and nothing anybody can."

Mara Venn said, "No."

And then she said the other thing, and she said it while they were walking, and she did not stop and she did not slow down, which is how she says things that she has decided to say and has decided to be unremarkable about.

"You are not going to be able to put a number in the minutes," she said. "I have been able to put exactly one number in a set of minutes in nine years and it was in your batch, and it was your own batch number, and a month later the only document on earth that said a district had been taken out of the arithmetic was an index entry in a public book with a pencil mark in the margin of it, and a man in an office with a letterhead wrote to me and called the number a fabrication, and he was not lying, and I could not prove one word of it, and I have never told you that and I am telling you now because in about six months somebody else is going to ask you the same question I was asked, and you are going to want to tell them you have a grey book, and a grey book is worth nothing at all in any room that is not the one it is in."

---

That night Orrin Vale wrote in the grey ledger, and the entry is the shortest he has written in six months, and the fourth line of it is the sentence he has been walking towards since the seventeenth of the second month.

> *Twenty-first to the twenty-third of the sixth.*
> *Applied at the public Archive counter for the index of the Archive of Unlisted: a card index of names with dates, a batch register with series, head count, price and countersignature, and an accession book. Three of the four. The shelf list is public and is eleven lines and has not changed in nineteen years.*
> *Received. Not produced. Not refused. Referred for consideration on the twenty-first of the first month next year, which is nineteen months, and a person may be present at the consideration and may speak and may not be represented, there being no party to a record, and the room has looked for an alternative and has not found one and does not pretend one.*
> *The first item on the application is a transfer number out of a copybook in a room at the bottom of a hill, brought up on a bus by a woman of sixty-one on her lunch, who has been thirty-one years in that building and has never once been thanked for anything, and who was not thanked today, and who told me on the way out that it was the first time in thirty-one years anybody had ever asked her for a number that she did not look up for them.*
> *And a girl of thirteen was asked three questions and answered them and one of them was a letter and a slash on a card her father brought home every night and put on a dresser and then put away, and the drawer is gone and the card is in a box of bills.*
> *And there is a place under the Cathedral with a keeper and a lock and a name on it and no date on it, and it has a floor, and it is not a mystery, and I have not been near it and I am not going to until I have standing, and the standing is a licence I do not hold, and a licence is a person, and the person I would have to ask is at the end of a bus and has told me he is not going to teach me anything.*
> *Nineteen months. I am going to be here on the twenty-first of the first month of the year after next with a piece of paper and a man who may speak and nobody to speak for, and I do not know what I am going to say, and I have written down today that I do not know, and I have written it down four times in a grey book in six months and each time I have been right not to guess.*
