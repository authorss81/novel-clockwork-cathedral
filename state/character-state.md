# Character State

State at the end of Volume 01, batch 0002 (Chapters 11-20). Read with `state/chapter-summaries.md` and `state/continuity.md`. A volume is roughly a year; ages are canon and must not be rounded.

## Orrin Vale, 32 - municipal clockmaker, line horologist

- **Wants, in the order he would say them if asked:** to get nine districts' worth of work out of a copybook and into a document somebody can be made to read; to stop the guild's version of the night being the only version; to find out what his father did in the storm eighteen years ago and whether the street photograph ever becomes a name; and, since Chapter 19, to work out what a rule is worth.
- **Has:** a class-three licence expiring on the 19th of the month after next, about eight weeks off; a W-4 work book due with a four-shilling-and-sixpence fee; an optional interview on Tuesday the 28th that he cannot decline without giving a reason; the copybook's contents in his head; a priced deferral in a public file with an inspector's name on it; a copper wedge hammered into a hospital pump by somebody else; eleven copies of the guild's labour book in nine buildings that he did not send; and a grey ledger that stopped being about machines about a month ago: a hundred and nine pages in the last four weeks, thirty-eight of them about other people's hours and not about machines at all.
- **His leash, now understood and not yet paid.** It is a form and a conversation and it does not need a threat: from the day the renewal card landed, the only record of what he has found in any office in the city is one the hall writes, on a form with three columns and none for *I am not sure*. **He has said *keep it* about the grey ledger, and that decision is the one the third rule exists to undo.**
- **Power:** Stage 2, Aftershock, now **practised rather than arrived at**, with a rule the reader can state: one question, about a named load, in plain words, with direct contact, and the price knowable in advance. He cannot see intent, cannot see a district, cannot see anything routed away, and cannot file, cite or defend a word of it. No Choice Trace, no Loom access, no filed Reading. **A private sense is still not a Reading, and Chapter 19 is the chapter that proves it.**
- **The ninth beat is not a stage and not part of this line.** Two hearings, in Chapters 1 and 3, and **in Chapter 15, on a tram at ten past five, he notices he was listening for it in the middle of the Quarter's ordinary perfect hour and it does not come.** It is a sound with a count in it that leans on a place he cannot name or locate: no picture, no word, no cost, no prophecy. He must never treat it as a free Aftershock. It is unexplained at the end of Chapter 20 and stays unexplained through Chapter 50.
- **Cost of the readings, in the order the page fixes them, and it escalates.** First, Chapter 5: the minute, with clean edges, and two incompatible accounts. Second, Chapter 9: two days of fine motor control in the right hand and the permanent ring. **Third, Chapter 19: thirty-eight minutes, and the permanent loss of the ability to judge a movement by ear** — he can still hear a watch at thirty feet in a still room and has tested it twice since the Tuesday; what he cannot any longer do is tell by the sound alone whether a movement is running true, which is the whole of a clockmaker's trade, and nothing had been strained, so it is not coming back — because he wanted to act and did not. **This is a precision loss and deliberately not a range loss, recorded so that Volume 08's long-range injury and Volume 14's clean single-machine hearing are both still available.**
- **His three rules, all on the page and all at the top of the grey ledger.** First, Chapter 10: *nothing of mine is used again without its blind margin and its toll named in public, in a room, to somebody who can object.* **Kept in Chapter 19. Did nothing.** Second, Chapter 17: *a toll is what happens when you ask something you cannot pay for. Ask one thing, about a named load, and know before you ask what it will cost.* Third, Chapter 20: *no result of mine goes out of this room without a person named who can refuse it* - worse than the first because it can stop him, because if there is nobody who can refuse he may not speak at all, and because he wrote it after reading back the page where he had **Sel Kade's** eighteen-year-old signature in his hands and did not take it to her.
- **Flaw, in action rather than in summary:** he said *keep it* about the one book he could not replace; he did not tell his sister about the lamp; he let Hask's question about work off the board pass with two words unsaid, which was the first lie of the batch and he did not notice giving it; and in Chapter 16 he decided not to put a document in front of the person it was about and gave himself a good reason on a stair.
- **What Chapter 19 costs him that is not a number:** he certified the pump and wrote the whole of his reading on the reverse of the certificate, which nobody reads, and knows it, and did it because the deal is the deal.

## Mara Venn, 33 - investigator, Office of Public Reckoning

- **Wants:** the same, and now with a method she has admitted out loud to a corridor and is ashamed of: three years of a committee being bored by a bridge, and then a clerk asking what the form number on it is. She is a patient woman doing an impatient trade, and in Chapter 20 Orrin understands that the book is faster than a person in a room.
- **Has:** a copybook's contents, a price schedule, a form number, a four-shilling debt to a woman who is never thanked, a petition lodged on the 20th, a day's hospital correspondence ending *no further correspondence on this matter*, and a demand filed in her own name by somebody else on arithmetic she asked for.
- **Does not have:** any authority over any other office, and any safe way to read a machine. Her mother's name is still not spoken and must not be until a volume the outline names. She said *Mara* to Rook once without thinking and did not take it back.
- **State with Orrin:** professionally allied and privately uninformative, and in Chapter 16 they agree about a bridge bracket and disagree about a whole case and do not resolve it in a corridor. She gave him, in Chapter 19, a page with his own decision on it, witnessed, because she knew he would need it in four months when he decided a rule was a thing that was true in a pump house.
- **Her stake in the argument, unchanged and now sharper:** a name-and-duration column cannot be drawn beside a figure nobody has counted, and her office is ignored three times in a fortnight by people who are not being cruel.

## Teo Arden, 20 - unlicensed tuner, organizer, the Weirs

- **Alive, and no longer only a suspicion.** He sent eleven copies of the hall's labour book into nine buildings on the Monday, and it cost a tenant block of thirty-one households a Michaelmas rent rise, and he has not apologised to Orrin because Orrin did not send it. That is the beginning of the actual working relationship: a disagreement about method with consequences on both sides.
- **Wants:** now stated in a document rather than a temper - a boardinghouse on the Kestrel side of Northglass with nine women on a stair that a hall's form says were never there, and a question about whether there is a way of doing that which is not his.
- **State at the end of the batch:** the Loose Minutes are on the page by name and are not a syndicate and disagree about disclosure. He is not recruited, not softened, and not stupid. He shimmed a hospital gland on a Sunday morning with a length of tram-line bond, painted it, and signed under the eighth course, and said in nine words that he would do it again on Thursday. He walks into Orrin's shop without knocking and does not say goodbye.
- **Not yet:** a member of any committee, a man with a plan he trusts, or a person Orrin can rely on. He is the first work they will do together and it is not an alliance.

## Leda Vale, 27 - emergency nurse, Green Span

- **From method to consequence, and she is the one who says it.** In Chapter 15 she prices his rule: the rule puts the people who can object in the room and the people who pay outside it, and outside it is everybody who cannot fight for themselves, every time. In Chapter 19 she tells him in a corridor that the rule did not fail because she could not object, it failed afterwards in a file, the next day, with *no further correspondence on this matter* in the corner in small type and nobody in the room to shout at - and that the worth of a rule is the argument, and the argument is all of it.
- **What she has put on the page:** the four districts on the back of a ward roster, unsigned and undated, on the duty board in the nurses' room where families read. She is the one who gets asked about it and she has a shift pattern that lets her say *I am not on until Friday*.
- **The lamp, named at last:** she was nine, in the chapel school on Weir Lane, about sixty people, the bells stopped, she counted, her aunt came at four with a basket, their mother three years dead. **She does not know what their father did and has never once pretended.** She has told Orrin that his not-telling is not a kindness, that she would have come, and that she cannot hold a lamp for something she does not know is happening. This is the reason his third rule exists.
- **Not a reward, and she leaves no hospital.** Her ward still has a morning in which two medicines arrive four minutes apart, and a girl of nineteen on nights is doing the ward clock's work by hand and being paid for it.

## Perry Vance, 41 - dead, the end of Chapter 5, the night of the 17th

- Unchanged. Still not Teo, still not a martyr, still not explained. In batch 0002 he is referred to nineteen times and appears nowhere, which is the point: the batch's whole argument is that a man who is not in a document does not exist, and he does not, and Orrin carries him in a grey book that is not in any office.
- **His record, as it now stands:** a line in a copybook, eleven times, with no name on it; a batch of forty-one entries; four names on a works' return and five on a board, the fifth being a boy who cannot be entered on a form; a hole in a ward's forty minutes with his name not in it; and a rule of Leda's about wards.

## Sel Kade, 44 - pump warden, Lower Weirs

- **Still not told.** Her eighteen-year-old countersignature on batch 21/4 is in the Continuity Office file and in Orrin's hands and he has not put it in front of her. Mara named the reason as a good reason with a door in it. The tenant review of the March diversion is owed by about Chapter 46 and is the reason she is still a character in Volume 02.
- **Still public and still unreviewed:** the reserve moved in March to Carrow Street, seventy-one people, said out loud on a public street. In Chapter 19 that same empty reserve is the reason the pump could not be fixed in time, and Orrin names it in a room as a fact and does not blame her for it, because she would have said yes.

## Gerren Hask, 55 - master of the Clockwork Quarter guild hall

- **His crime is legible to the reader and invisible to every character, and batch 0002 did not change that by a word.** He asked for the work book, explained that the hall cannot take it and would resign before signing that, explained the other half more plainly, offered an optional interview that cannot be refused without giving a reason, and said the words *it is not a threat* in effect without ever using them.
- **What is new is the mechanism, not the crime:** from the renewal card onward, the only record of what a maintainer has found in any office in the city is one the hall writes, on form W-4, with three columns and none for *I am not sure*. The licence expires on the 19th of the month after next, about eight weeks after the notice reached the bench. Hask is owed losing the right to certify silent shifts and publishing the labour ledger with a liability annex naming the night of the Long Nine as the night the hours stopped. **He is not destroyed; he becomes a repairer under public oversight.**
- **Never asked about the Long Nine on the page.** Della Marsh names the liability annex in Chapter 14 and refuses the figure, which is not Hask and does not touch him.

## Secondary people established on the page in batch 0002

- **Tobias Rill, 44,** Continuity Office inspector of hourlines. On the page, in person, and the most reasonable man in the book. He certifies that a line agrees with its own record and explains, without unkindness, why a district's own chalk board cannot be entered on a form, and why the fifth name cannot be entered at all. **He signed the trestle's £2 14s 6d deferral two years ago and is not a villain for it**; a careful man made a decision about a sum of money. He reads the Outer Sluice minutes in Volume 06 and his inspection history is public record in Volume 14.
- **Della Marsh, 61,** guild hall secretary, thirty-one years. The person a form number comes out of, for four shillings in coin and no receipt, on a rule of her own, a week after the asking. Frightened of an inquiry, not corrupt, loyal to nobody, and never once thanked for anything. She names the three doors to the labour book and the copybook's nine series, and she is right that a form with a price schedule and nine districts is not a crime but a practice.
- **Hester Lomas, 44,** hall copyist, nineteen years in the back room, who signed a true copy of the Weir Two line eleven times without knowing what a standby was, made the eleven copies over three nights, lost her place, and put her own name to the paper because one copy went to the man who sells the Advertiser's front page. She does not recur after this volume.
- **Ellen Reeve, 44,** hospital engineer at Green Span, twenty-two years. Packed the failing gland wrong in August, said so before letting Orrin touch the pump, packed it properly afterwards, and spent the following weeks in a room knowing exactly what a shimmed gland costs. Competent, and unable to certify a district toll either.
- **Hanna Stroud, 54,** landlord of nine Weirs blocks. Pay her own renters on the first, has never been two months late, took a rise off one block on the strength of the guild's own labour book, and got it back with her own twelve years of rent book and the district returns printed in the minutes. The insurer's letter says the returns are insufficient. Reasonable, and still reasonable.
- **Ivo Pell, 36,** veteran operator. On the page twice and contemptuous both times, and right both times: a bench man's ear is not an instrument, a man who practises on a dead pump is behind his trade, and a man who practises on a live one is a public hazard. He offers no lesson, takes no interest, and says *don't* on his way out. He is not a mentor here and Volume 02 must not find one retroactively.
- **Councilor Imani Rook, 40,** chair of the public-health committee. Filer of her own demand on arithmetic, in her own name, three chapters after blocking one, and she says in the chamber that she was out-argued and not persuaded. She pays for the record out of her own ward's clerical allocation at four pounds ten and she keeps the name-and-duration clause **out**, because she does not know the size of the population the column would describe. **Not softened, not converted, an obstacle and not a villain.** Appears in three more volumes after this one, not thirteen.
- **Alma Festinger, 56.** Not on the page in this batch and **not to be asked again anywhere in Volume 01.** The second asking is Volume 06, in a gallery, and the reader should recognise her face.
- **Ostrand and Ostrow** were not on the page in this batch. They remain two different men in two different districts, four districts apart, and the near-collision must not be merged, cross-referenced or renamed. **Ostrand is not Ostrow.**
