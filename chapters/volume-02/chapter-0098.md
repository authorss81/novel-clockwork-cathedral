# Chapter 98 — What a Body Is

On Saturday the thirty-first of the twelfth month the Registry of Transfers and Access published the index of batches in full — four hundred and eleven lines, in its own type, on foolscap, eighty-two pages, sold at a penny and not refused for a price — in a public room on Cathedral Rise that anybody may petition and that a person may be present at, and a person present may speak and may not be represented, and the first thing a stranger did with the four hundred and eleven lines on the Saturday was go and find nine of them that are the same line twice in about forty minutes, which is the fifth sentence of a blind margin that had been on a board since the twenty-ninth of the October and had not been provable from inside a room; and on the Monday the second of the first month, a hundred and sixty miles from that room or four miles, a second book had a line at the head of it in five lines, in a hand nobody at the Office of Public Reckoning can read, saying the same thing the Office's own headnote says about an empty column beside a name in a glass case, and neither office has ever heard of the other office, and nobody was thanked in either of them.

---

Saturday the thirty-first of the twelfth, at about ten in the morning, in the public Archive room on Cathedral Rise, and it is a Saturday and it is a room with tables in it and a counter and a public book on it, and **it is not a committee and nothing in this city sits in it and it is a room anybody may walk into and read a book in, and the words reading room are in this book about that room and about that room only.**

The four hundred and eleven lines went out in a run of five hundred at fourpence a page on the foolscap sheet, eighty-two pages, and the whole of what it cost the Registry to set its own book in its own type for the first time since 1920 is **one pound seven shillings and fourpence**, and the Commissioner entered it against the Registry's own stationery account in one line with the date on it, **and it is the first time in nineteen years that a word has gone out of that building that anybody could carry away in his hand.**

Perry Shaw is forty-seven and is the Commissioner of the Registry of Transfers and Access and he said the reason out loud before anybody asked him for it, in a room with about thirty people in it, and he refused to let it be called anything else, and then he said the reason of the reason, which he has said once before this year and once is enough.

"**A number in a set of minutes whose source is a box may be called a fabrication, and the Registry does not publish numbers that may be called fabrications, and it will not do so again.** That is the reason. It is not a dirty reason and I will not have it called one and I have said it before and I am not going to say it a second time this year.

"And it is not the reason anybody in this room came. **The reason anybody came is that the figure came back from a public room in October with a working on it, and a figure with a working on it can only be argued with by doing the working again, and there is one office in this city that audits anything and it has four staff and one window.** I did not publish a list because anybody asked me to publish a list. I published a list because the thing that is a citation is a document and not a number."

Then the reading, and it is a reading in the ordinary sense: a public book, read aloud, in a public room, by a Commissioner, on a stated date.

> *"The index of batches of the Registry of Transfers and Access, in full, being four hundred and eleven lines in accession order and not in date order, each carrying a series letter, a date, a head count, a price in shillings per head per year, and a countersignature, and against every executed batch a diagonal stroke through the number in the left-hand margin.*
> *Published in full on the thirty-first day of the twelfth month of this year, on foolscap, eighty-two pages, at a penny a copy, which is the paper and the stitching and not the printing.*
> *The register itself remains in the room in which it is kept, as it has since about 1920, and is not produced, and this office has no power to produce it and no form on which it could be required to. **What is published is the index and not the register, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what this afternoon is for.***
> *A person may be present at this reading and may speak. **A person may not be represented.** No person has been, and no person's name appears on any of the four hundred and eleven lines, and the diagonal stroke is an execution mark and it has been in that margin since 1920 and it is not a name and it is not a person and it is not a signature, and nobody has ever asked me what it is.*"

---

**And a man of thirty-eight in a good coat asked four questions and got four true answers, and the fourth one is the chapter.**

He was a schoolmaster and he was not from this district and he said so in the first thirty seconds, and he had a pencil.

"Does the printed line carry the stroke?"

"It does."

"Is the stroke a name."

"No."

"Is the stroke anything else."

"**It is an execution mark and it was in the margin of the book before I came into this building and it is the only thing on four hundred and eleven lines that is not a figure and a date and a price, and in thirty-one years nobody has asked me what it is and I have never told anybody, and I have just told nine people in a public room because I am about to print it four hundred and eleven times.**"

And then he went to the table and did it in about forty minutes, with a pencil, and it is the fifth sentence of the blind margin of a figure that has been on a board outside that room since the twenty-ninth of the October, and he did it in front of everybody.

"**Eleven and fourteen. Same series letter, same date, same head count, same price, same countersignature's office. And thirty-one and fifty-eight. And forty-one and sixty-four, and I have got nine of them and I have got them by price and date, because there is nothing else on the line to go on.**"

There was a silence of the sort that a public room does not usually hold.

"That is the fifth sentence," said **Mara Venn**, from the back, and she is thirty-three and she has four staff and one window and a written right to be ignored and she is the only person in this city whose office counts anything. "**I found the same nine in October, in three sittings, in this room, between ten and one, on three working days, with such working as the audit produced, and such working is made for the Office and does not leave the room.** And it has taken him forty minutes with a pencil at a table that anybody may read at, **and that is the whole of the difference between a figure and a list, and I have been the person holding the figure for two months and I would like it noticed that the sentence I put on a board under my own figure was that a person can be on two of these four hundred and eleven lines, and I could not prove it outside a locked room, and a schoolmaster has proved it in front of thirty people in three quarters of an hour, and the proving cost the Registry one pound seven shillings and fourpence and me a fortnight.**"

Nobody thanked the schoolmaster, because there is no form on which a person may be thanked for reading a public book, and he had said so himself before he started, and he gave the reason, which was that he was a schoolmaster and he did it for the same reason he would correct a child's sum, and that was not a thing anybody in a room needed to reward.

**And the fifth sentence is worth saying here, because it is the whole of what publishing a list bought and it cost one pound seven shillings and fourpence.**

> *The figure is a sum of four hundred and eleven head counts. **Head counts are not people. A person can be on two of those four hundred and eleven lines, because a standing withdrawal has no re-instatement column on it, and the number of people is lower than the number and this office cannot say by how much.** Nine pairs of lines are the same line twice, and a ninth of the nine were found on the thirty-first of December by a schoolmaster with a pencil in forty minutes from a printed page, and the Office of Public Reckoning found the same nine in October in three sittings in a locked room, and neither office could show the other its working, because there is a rule that such working does not leave the room, and the rule is right and the rule cost the sentence its proof for two months.*

---

**And Sindri Lake is sixty-one and is the keeper of the room and he stood at the end of his own table with his hands behind his back for the whole of the afternoon, and he is not thanked, and he gave the reason before he was asked for it, and the reason is the reason he has been saying since 1919 and he has never written it down because a written rule is a thing somebody can repeal in a morning.**

He said it at about half past two, to about nine people, at the end of the table, standing.

"**An index entry is a statement that something was taken out. It is not the thing that was taken out, and it never was, and the day an index entry becomes a copy of what it indexes is the day somebody changes a head count in a room with no light in it.** I have said that for about thirty-one years and I have never said it in print, and I am not going to start now, and I would like it noticed that I have said it this afternoon to nine people who are strangers and that I have never said it to a Commissioner.

"And I have let it be printed, and here is why, and I have had since the Friday. **The book is already public and the thing in the box is not.** A thing that cannot leave its room cannot be wrong in a room. A thing that is on a table in a printed line can be checked by four hundred and eleven people instead of by one, **and I have been the one for thirty-one years and I would like a witness, and a witness is a person who is not me and is not paid by anybody and can be rude to me.**"

"And you are not thanked for that."

"**I am never thanked for anything and the reason is that the day a keeper is thanked for doing his job is the day somebody asks him whether he could do a different one.**" He said it without any weight at all. "There have been two hundred and something people at that table since the fifteenth of the September and I have spoken to about nine of them and the queue fell from nine a day to two and I have never once minded, and I would like it noticed that the range on the two boards outside that door is still a range and it is still two boards, and the difference between those two things and a list of four hundred and eleven lines in a room anybody may petition **is the whole of the difference and it is not a matter of the range being right.**"

And nobody thanked him and he is sixty-one and he is a keeper.

---

**Saturday the thirty-first of the twelfth, half past seven in the morning, the third reading, and the method ran without anybody organising it.**

> *Nights since the sixth of the tenth: 26 in the tenth; 30 in the eleventh; 31 in the twelfth, the first to the thirty-first inclusive. **Eighty-seven nights at fifty seconds is 4,350 seconds, which is 1 h 12 min 30 s.***
> *Weir Two station: 48 min 00 s + 1 h 12 min 30 s = **2 h 00 min 30 s.** Read by I. Pell.*
> *Pumping ward, outer room: 57 min 20 s + the same = **2 h 09 min 50 s.** Read by O. Vale.*
> *Quarter hill, south: 1 h 49 min 10 s + the same = **3 h 01 min 40 s.** Read by S. Kaye.*
> *The two separations are 9 min 20 s and 51 min 50 s and they are the separations of the first of the eighth and they have not moved in eighty-seven nights, and a method that produces the same two numbers for eighty-seven nights is not a forecast and is not a rate and is a photograph of a length of iron taken three times, and **the only thing in it that anybody can act on is the difference between two of the photographs, and the difference between the seventeenth and the thirty-first is fourteen nights at fifty seconds, which is seven hundred seconds, which is eleven minutes and forty seconds, and a reader can be wrong by a second and cannot be wrong by much, and that is the whole of what a method is worth.**

**And on the Sunday the first of the first month nothing sat anywhere, and on the Monday the second, at about four in the afternoon, in the outer room of the pumping ward, a second book was written up.**

It is about a hundred and forty pages and it is bound in a piece of sacking and it lives on the ledge under the glass case, **beside** the toll ledger and not in it, and it is not in the case and it is not locked and there is a chair and a pencil and ink on that ledge and anybody may sit down and read it.

It is called, in the hand on the cover, **THE ROW: WHAT WE WERE ASKED AND WHAT WE SAID, AND WHAT IT COST, SLUICE ROW, OFF WEIR LANE, THE TWELFTH AND THIRTEENTH OF THE TWELFTH.** It has the timetable in it, and the twelve names and the times, and the nine households with no clock and no column, and the five hours and the three hundred minutes, and the paying district line with nothing in it, and a column for durations with nothing in it, and about sixty hours of winding, of which five are the hours of one household in a court whose clock is wound by a person who does not live in it.

**And the Office's figure is nowhere in it.** Not fifty-one thousand four hundred and eighteen, and not the four hundred and eleven, and not the series, and not the range, and not the wards, and not a person; and the two figures in the two books in that room are a woman's five sentences and a street's four lines, and neither of them is the other's, and **the row book does not cite the Office and the Office does not cite the row book, and no person at the Office of Public Reckoning has read either word of it, and the reason is not discretion, it is that the Office of Public Reckoning has no form on which a district's own book may be received.**

And at the head of it, in the same five lines and the same shape as the five lines on the inside cover of a ledger in a glass case a foot away on the same ledge, there is a line in a hand that is not the Office's type and is not the district works' hand:

> *The columns at the head of this book are the row's own. The first carries what was asked and the second carries what was said and the third carries the hours that belonged to named people.*
> *Where no instrument recorded the duration, the column is empty. An empty column here is a statement that the duration was not recorded. **It is not an omission. It is not a mystery, and it is not to be filled in by inference, and any person who fills one in by inference commits an offence against the people in the column beside it.***
> *Three hundred minutes came off the clocks of a district four streets from this row. The water came off this row. The paying district line above is empty because a toll of minutes has to name a district and the district it names is not the street it happens on, **and that is the whole of what has happened here in December and it is the whole of what has been wrong with this district for nineteen years, and there is no form in this city on which a person may say that a document is two places at once.***
> *Nobody is thanked in this book. No person in this book is thanked and no person in this book was thanked at the time and no person in this book is going to be thanked.*

Bevin Sarr read both headnotes out loud in that room, in full, the one from the inside cover of the ledger in the glass case and the one at the head of the book on the ledge, and then she put them down side by side on the ledge, and **they are the same rule in two buildings, written twice, about a column with nothing in it, by a woman of thirty-three in a city office and by about seven hands on a street off Weir Lane, and neither of them has ever heard of the other, and neither of them is a copy of the other, and the five lines are in the same shape because a shape is the only part of a rule anybody in a bad situation can copy.**

Nobody was thanked. A Commissioner paid one pound seven shillings and fourpence of his own office's money and said the reason out loud. A keeper of sixty-one said the reason he has never said in print and let it be printed anyway. A woman of thirty-nine with a pencil found nine lines in forty minutes and asked for nothing. A woman of thirty-three had found the same nine in a locked room in three sittings in October and was not thanked then either and is not now.
