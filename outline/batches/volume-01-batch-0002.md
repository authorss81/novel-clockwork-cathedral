## Batch goal

Turn a private discovery into a public fight, and let the fight be won on paper and lost in fact. Orrin has a form number, a price schedule, a countersignature and a private sense that has already cost him a man; what he lacks is the machinery that turns a document into a consequence. This batch gives him the machinery — an inspection file, a copybook, a form number looked up behind a closed door, a committee demand filed by an opponent, and a Weirs counter-record that costs somebody something — and then takes it away again in Chapter 19, where he reads a hospital pump correctly and cannot certify the repair, so the warning he was right about does nothing at all.

The batch's genre promise, in one sentence: **a copy of the ledger is not the same thing as a right to be believed, and the difference is a room, a witness, and somebody's signature.**

## Starting condition

One week after Perry Vance's death. The 15th was the certificate, the 17th the Reading and the death, the 24th the refused demand. **As delivered**, Chapters 11-20 run from the 25th to the 20th of the next month, with the class-three licence expiring on **Sunday the 19th of the third month** — about eight weeks after the notice, which is Chapter 10's own figure and governs over the six weeks this card originally assumed — and the ninth tick still unexplained. The full calendar is in `state/continuity.md`.

Orrin has: a clean certificate that makes him answerable, an objection on file that changes nothing, a public demand referred to the spring, a form number in his own handwriting in a book nobody can read, an eighteen-year-old governor on his shelf, and two days of lost fine motor control in his right hand, which are now six days old and nearly back.

## Batch shape

- **Beginning (Chapters 11–12):** an honest inspection that finds a discrepancy nobody can act on, and then the renewal's paperwork, which is not a threat and does not need to be.
- **Escalation (Chapters 13–14):** Imani Rook files the demand herself on arithmetic; Della Marsh is made to look up a number.
- **Midpoint (Chapters 15–16):** the human centre with Leda, then the discovery that the trestle's bracket has a paper trail of exactly the same kind as the crew.
- **Escalation to climax (Chapters 17–19):** practice on a dead pump, a hospital timer and a bridge joint; the copied ledger reaches the Weirs and costs a tenant block something; the hospital pump Orrin cannot certify.
- **Climax and aftermath (Chapters 19–20):** Chapter 19 is the volume's failure — right, unprovable, useless. Chapter 20 is the aftermath and the reading-back.

## Batch midpoint (Chapters 15–16)

The midpoint is not a reversal of plot but a reversal of scale. Up to Chapter 15 the reader is waiting for a document that names the night crew. From Chapter 15 the reader has documents, several, and the problem is no longer evidence. It is the difference between a record and a claim, between a copy and a hearing, and between a district that can produce its own hours and a city that is not obliged to read them. Chapter 16 supplies the evidence that the same practice deferred a bridge bracket two years ago, which means the omission is not only a moral instrument; it is also a maintenance policy, and the volume's climax can now be about money.

## Batch climax (Chapter 19)

Orrin reads a failing Green Span hospital pump correctly, with Aftershock, out loud, in front of people, having learned to do that in Chapter 9. The repair requires certification of a machine's local toll. A class-three maintainer's licence may certify a single civic machine and nothing else, and may not certify a district toll, and the hospital's district has no standing to ask for one, and the pumping ward cannot pay for a Reading because a Reading is charged to a district reserve and that is the thing that failed. So he is right, and being right changes nothing, and the only instrument that could make it change something is the one instrument whose use would suspend him in Chapters 44–49. He does not use it. **He must not commit the licence offence in this chapter.** The rule he wrote at the top of his ledger in Chapter 10 — blind margin and toll named in public, in a room, to somebody who can object — is satisfied and produces nothing, and the chapter says so without editorialising.

## Required character changes

- **Orrin:** from a man with a private certainty and one piece of paper to a man who understands that a private certainty plus a piece of paper is not an instrument. He ends the batch with a copy of the guild's labour ledger in circulation that he did not control and cannot recall, and with a rule of his own that has been obeyed and has failed anyway.
- **Mara Venn:** from an investigator holding one exhibit to an investigator with a copybook, a price schedule and an objection, and a decision to make about the law's own phrase — whether *calibration strain* can be challenged as an unrecorded toll without a court that will hear it. She gains standing and loses the comfort of being ignored.
- **Teo Arden:** from an auditor of Orrin to a man with a document and a plan for it, which is the beginning of the Loose Minutes as an organisation rather than a mood. He is not recruited, not softened, and in Chapter 18 his judgement is right and his action costs a tenant block.
- **Leda Vale:** from method to consequence. In Chapter 19 her ward is on the wrong side of a warning that was correct, and she is the one who has to say so in a corridor.
- **Gerren Hask:** the leash is felt in Chapter 12 without a threat being made. He is never asked about the Long Nine on the page, and his cover-up remains invisible to every character in the story.
- **Councilor Imani Rook:** files in Chapter 13 on arithmetic, in her own name, having blocked the demand three chapters earlier, and is explicit that she was shown a number and not a feeling. She is an obstacle and not a villain, and she appears in three more volumes after this one, not thirteen.
- **Tobias Rill:** the Continuity Office inspector who closes Hask's inquiry for the guild and is right to, in the sense that closing it is a decision a reasonable man makes with the paper he has. He is the mechanism by which an inspection becomes an inquiry and an inquiry becomes nothing.

## Required power changes

- **Aftershock, practised rather than arrived at.** Chapter 17 is practice: a dead pump, a hospital timer, and a bridge joint, with the cost visible, and with a rule the reader can state afterwards — narrow questions, and stopping before curiosity becomes compulsion. Ivo Pell appears at the bench at the end of Chapter 17 to be right and contemptuous once and offer no lesson.
- **A third keeper's toll, taken in Chapter 19 and the largest of the three**, because Chapter 19 is the first time Orrin reads something and then chooses not to act on it, and the body charges for wanting to. **As delivered, the permanent cost is the loss of the judgement and not the hearing: he can still hear a watch at thirty feet in a still room and can no longer tell by the sound alone whether a movement is running true. That is a precision loss, not a range loss, and it was chosen so that Volume 08's long-range injury and Volume 14's clean single-machine hearing both remain available. Chapters 17's two costs are entered in the ledger as *reading costs*, with the reason written under them, so that the count of three tolls is unambiguous on the page.**
- **What does not arrive:** no Choice Trace, no Loom access, no filed Reading, no certification, no ability to scale. He is the same man at the end of the batch as at the start, with a worse right ear and a document in circulation he cannot control.

## Required world reveals

Delivered in this order, each through an object, a job, or a conversation rather than an explanation:

1. **A Continuity Office hourline inspection is honest, thorough, and produces a finding that says there is nothing to find.** The forms are not the problem. The problem is that the district has no document for the officer to check.
2. **A district's own record is not a record**: Bevin Sarr's board and Wenna Pruitt's ledgers are real, kept, and inadmissible, because nothing countersigns them and no office is obliged to receive them.
3. **The guild's labour ledger can be copied**, and the copy is the first object in Morrowfen that contains more than one omission. Other districts appear in it. Northglass. The Sloughs. The practice is not local.
4. **The maintenance-deferred class is not only about people.** A bridge bracket on a hospital trestle was noted two years ago, deferred for cost, and signed by an inspector. The same form, the same price schedule, the same signature.
5. **The class-three licence's annual paperwork has a work-book requirement**, and the work book the guild wants is not the guild's property. The leash is a form and a conversation, and it does not need a threat.
6. **A councilor can file her own demand three chapters after blocking one, and will say in the room that she was shown arithmetic.**
7. **A copy of the ledger in the wrong hands is not a public record.** It is a rumour with page numbers, and in Chapter 18 it costs a tenant block something real.

## Rule and system guardrails for this batch

- **Keep the two kinds of toll apart in every line.** A clock toll is minutes and a paying district. A body toll is an interval of a living person's attention, coordination, or memory, and has no column anywhere. *Calibration strain* is the Directorate's euphemism for the second; *self-inflicted occupational strain* is the guild's printed phrase for an unfiled private reading. Both phrases entered the reader's vocabulary in Chapter 6 and are never used for each other again.
- **The renewal does not fall due inside this batch.** It is due about eight weeks after the 25th, on **Sunday the 19th of the third month**, which is outside this batch and inside Volume 01 — batch 0003 discharges it in Chapter 27, as a renewal and not a crisis. **The six weeks in this card was an estimate; the delivered date follows Chapter 10's finished prose, and the divergence is recorded here.** What arrives is the notice, the form, the work-book requirement, the fee receipt, and an interview nobody is obliged to hold. No chapter may describe the renewal as imminent, and no chapter may suspend him — the suspension is issued ahead of Hask's own clock in Chapters 44–49 and only for certifying a local toll he had no standing to certify.
- **Orrin commits no licence offence in this batch.** He certifies nothing at a district toll. In Chapter 19 he is right and cannot certify and does nothing, and the nothing is the point.
- **No certainty permit, no Council of Hours, no district time council, no Reading room, no local instrument, no Civic Time Keeper.** Volume 01 is a book about paper and these do not exist yet.
- **The Censor Gate, the return line, the Dark Reservoir, the First Hour, the binding plates and the command line are not named.** The volume may show that a practice has a form and a price. It may not name the machinery that benefits.
- **The Archive of Unlisted stays shut.** Its existence, its sealedness and its name are established. Nothing in this batch opens it, and no one gets closer to it than a signature on a routing card.
- **Anja Voss is not on the page in this batch.** She has had her one appearance, a signature in Chapter 4. Halden Rusk does not appear. Neither may appear a second time.
- **Nella Rook is not named.** Green Span is reached through Leda, the hospital pump, and the trestle.
- **The ninth beat and Aftershock are two different things and must never be fused.** The ninth beat is a sourceless pulse that counts one over under a machine, heard twice and only twice, in Chapters 1 and 3, and it is **not** in this batch. It shows no night, gives no picture and no word, and costs nothing. Aftershock is the load that answers a question Orrin asked in plain words about a named load, it costs a keeper's toll every time, and it is what he practises in Chapter 17 and overrules in Chapter 19. A batch that has Orrin "hear a night coming" again has broken the rule. It stays unexplained through Chapter 50.
- **The order of the keeper's tolls is fixed and it escalates.** First (Chapter 5) the minute. Second (Chapter 9) two days of fine motor control in the right hand, and the permanent ring above a given pitch. Third (Chapter 19) the largest, taken because he wanted to act and did not. No toll may be cheaper than the one before it, and the ear is a condition only: Volume 08 takes his long-range hearing and Volume 14 takes clean single-machine hearing, so neither may find this already banked.
- **Chapter 25's midpoint is batch 0003's job.** A second and larger public Reading on the Weirs' water schedule, properly filed and witnessed, that omits the same people. This batch must not stage it, preview it, or file a Reading about the water schedule.
- **One Reading notice maximum per chapter, and no notice at all unless the chapter is about one.** Nothing in this batch requires a notice. If one is used, it follows the four-line form exactly and never carries a paying district.
- **Twenty-three must not appear in any form.** Two hundred and twelve is the Long Nine, eighteen years back, and belongs to the guild's liability annex, which is a Volume 01 resolution item and not this batch's work.
- **No chapter ends mid-sentence or mid-action.**

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 11 — "The Honest Man With a Form"

**Type:** investigation or discovery.
**Immediate goal:** Get the Weir Two night crew into a document that an officer of the city is obliged to write down.
**Resistance:** Tobias Rill arrives on his routine quarterly hourline inspection of the Weirs, forty-four, unhurried, and he is the most reasonable man in the book. He measures the hourline at the pump house, finds it correct, and asks the question every inspector asks: who was in the building last night. There is no answer he can write. Bevin Sarr's board is offered and refused as a document, because it has no counter-signature and no office is obliged to receive it, and the refusal is not rudeness, it is procedure. The works' book says four, the board says five, and the discrepancy between a book and a board is not a discrepancy the form has a box for.
**Information revealed:** what a Continuity Office inspection actually is; that Rill's forms are honest and are still the mechanism by which an inspection becomes an inquiry and an inquiry becomes nothing; and the first hard fact about a district's own record, which is that keeping it well does not make it admissible.
**Action or decision:** Rill writes a finding: no shift record exists for the Weir Two night crew; nothing to certify; file closed. He tells Orrin, without unkindness, that if Orrin wants a record he needs a person who can sign one, and there is no such person in the district, and that is not a matter Rill can remedy from an office on the Rise.
**Emotional change:** the difference between an ally and an honest official. Orrin leaves understanding that the system's failures are performed by careful people.
**Power or resource change:** none. His sense is not used and is not admissibly relevant; the chapter is about the limits of documents.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Rill's finding is honest, complete, and useless. Rill closes the guild's portion of the inquiry later and is not a villain for it. The board is offered and refused, once, in front of witnesses.
**Ending type:** discovery, in the shape of a dead end.
**Next-chapter pull:** the licence renewal notice arrives at the bench with the guild's work-book requirement attached.

### Chapter 12 — "What the Form Wants"

**Type:** political or social pressure.
**Immediate goal:** Keep his class-three licence out of Hask's hands for the eight weeks before the expiry without a confrontation.
**Resistance:** not a threat. The renewal notice is a form; the fee is 4s 6d; the endorsement requires the master's signature; and the hall's standing request is for the holder's work book, hall form W-4, which is a request for a document that does not exist, because the work book that matters to Orrin is a school exercise book with a hard grey cover that is not the hall's property and has never been entered in anything. Hask asks for it courteously, in a warm office, and explains that a man who keeps a private book of faults is either an unusually honest operator or a man with a grievance, and that the hall would like to know which, and would prefer to know before the renewal rather than after.
**Information revealed:** what the leash is made of. The class-three licence is a fee, a signature and a conversation, and the conversation is scheduled at Hask's convenience and cannot be refused without giving him a reason. The renewal date is not moved and not threatened and does not arrive in this volume.
**Action or decision:** Orrin does not lie and does not hand over the book. He says the book is not the hall's and Hask says, pleasantly, that the hall agrees, and that the hall will therefore require a substitute, and that the substitute is a fresh fault book, and that the fresh fault book will be the hall's. Orrin realises he has been told the price of the next six weeks and has not been able to refuse it.
**Emotional change:** the first time a man with power has asked him for the thing he actually keeps, and the first time keeping it has a bill attached.
**Power or resource change:** none. Eight weeks of margin, visibly spent, and an optional interview on Tuesday the 28th that he cannot decline without giving a reason.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the renewal is not due inside Volume 01 and must never be described as imminent. The grey ledger is not the hall's property and is not entered in anything. Hask has still not been asked about the Long Nine.
**Ending type:** cost, quietly incurred, and a form that will be answered.
**Next-chapter pull:** a notice that a demand has been filed by somebody else, in a different name.

### Chapter 13 — "Arithmetic"

**Type:** political or social pressure.
**Immediate goal:** Find out who filed a toll-ledger demand in the Assembly and why a Green Span councilor is on the same side of a table as the Weirs. **As delivered she is the mover, in her own name, five days after blocking one.**
**Resistance:** Councilor Imani Rook files the demand herself, three chapters after blocking it, in her own name, having been shown arithmetic: the pumping district's own uncorrected hours over four months, produced by Wenna Pruitt out of a rent book and a chalk board, do not reconcile with the Quarter's, and the difference is a number large enough to require an explanation. She is entirely clear in the room that she was not moved by sentiment and that she will say so if asked. Her own committee resists, on the sentence Mara Venn taught the reader three chapters ago: who pays for the record. Rook pays for it out of her own ward's clerical allocation, which is a small, brutal, credible act, and it is the reason she becomes a real character rather than an obstacle.
**Information revealed:** how the Assembly actually works — debate, division, referred to committee, read twice, entered in the minutes, discharged — and that a demand is a thing with a number in it that a clerk can put in a bundle. Imani Rook's competence and her stake.
**Action or decision:** the demand is filed and read twice and entered in the minutes, with the paying-district clause, and with the name-and-duration clause referred again with a date. Orrin, who is not a member, is not permitted to speak and does not try.
**Emotional change:** Orrin watches an opponent do the thing he wanted done and understand, for the first time, that being right and being useful are different skills.
**Power or resource change:** none. Institutional access, not power: he is a man named in a footnote of somebody else's motion.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Imani Rook files on arithmetic, says so, and is not softened. The Assembly's kinship to Nella Rook is not mentioned in this volume. No certainty permit exists; nothing here resembles one.
**Ending type:** small victory with a bill attached.
**Next-chapter pull:** a form number, a fee, and a door that closes behind you.

### Chapter 14 — "Behind a Closed Door"

**Type:** investigation or discovery.
**Immediate goal:** Put the price schedule and the copybook in a room where somebody can act on them.
**Resistance:** Della Marsh, sixty-one, secretary of the guild hall, keeps the certification ledger, the shift forms and the form-number series in a drawer, and she has kept the hall's paper for thirty-one years and knows exactly which drawer the answers are in. She will look up a number for a fee and a closed door, and she is frightened of an inquiry rather than loyal to anybody, and she is not a conspirator: she is a woman who has kept other people's paper for three decades and has never once been thanked. The lookup gives what Chapter 8 implied and more: the copybook behind the form, and in the copybook, *non-certified standby* lines for the Lower Weirs, for a Northglass boardinghouse stair, for a pumping station in the Sloughs, and for a hospital generator that is not the hospital's business. The practice is not one crew. It is a district-shaped habit with a price schedule.
**Information revealed:** the omission is not local and not about one crew. Roughly nine districts appear in one copybook over four years, and the numbers are small enough that a hall can call it housekeeping.
**Action or decision:** the labour ledger is copied, and the copy has to be made by somebody who can reach the book. Wenna Pruitt and the Loose Minutes can reach it; Mara can requisition it lawfully and wait a year. The batch must be clear about which happened and who paid for it.
**Emotional change:** the first time Orrin is afraid of what he has found, because a fact that appears in four districts is a fact that can be used against four districts.
**Power or resource change:** none. A lead becomes a pattern, which is worth more and is not power.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Della Marsh is frightened, not corrupt, and looks up a number and is never thanked. The practice is proven not to be local without naming any new district that the bible does not have. The Archive of Unlisted remains shut.
**Ending type:** discovery that enlarges the problem and worsens the mood.
**Next-chapter pull:** Orrin has not slept properly since the trestle and his sister works nights, and there is a kitchen in Green Span with a lamp in it.

### Chapter 15 — "Supper, and the Rule"

**Type:** character and relationship work.
**Immediate goal:** Eat a hot meal, and find out whether the rule he wrote in his ledger survives contact with somebody who has to live in it.
**Resistance:** Leda is on nights and has the kitchen for an hour. He has come to tell her about the copybook and cannot tell her about the copybook, because telling her means telling her what hour-sickness is and he has started to understand that he has been telling her about the machines and not about himself. She is not fooled and does not let him get away with it, and the argument is about method and not character, and it is also, for the first time, about him. She makes him say the rule out loud and then tells him exactly what a rule is worth when the person who needs it is a patient and not a witness.
**Information revealed:** the first time a person Orrin loves says the thing the volume is about, in the correct words, and does not have to be argued into it. A small, strictly bounded beat about the Long Nine: Leda was nine and in a shelter and remembers the bells stopping; she does not know what her father did and has never once pretended to. No note, no message, no hidden timing tolerance is revealed — that is Volume 08's.
**Action or decision:** he gives her the copybook's existence, in a form she can hold: four districts, one form number, a price. She does something with it that he does not expect, which is to write the four districts on the back of a ward roster and post it in the nurses' room, where patients' families will see it.
**Emotional change:** the first unburdening in the batch, and the first time his sister is an ally of the work rather than an opponent of him.
**Power or resource change:** none. Emotional, and it is the chapter that makes Chapter 19 possible.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Leda is a working nurse with a real job and does not leave it. No Nella Rook. No Edwin revelation. The rules she is given are: say the number out loud, in a room, in front of the people whose hours they are.
**Ending type:** recovery beat with a cost in view.
**Next-chapter pull:** the hospital trestle's bracket has been in somebody's file for two years, and the file is a public record.

### Chapter 16 — "Deferred, for Cost"

**Type:** investigation or discovery.
**Immediate goal:** Find out whether the trestle's failing load was ever written down before Orrin put his hand on it in Chapter 9.
**Resistance:** the Continuity Office keeps a copy of every transfer, every deferral and every inspection because that is the Office's business, and its files are public on petition in the way a customs house is public: anybody may ask, everybody may read, and reading takes **eleven days**, not the fortnight this card first assumed. The trestle's conduit bracket was noted two years ago, almost to the month, in the maintenance-deferred class, priced, signed by an inspector, and deferred. The inspector is Rill. The deferral is legal, the deferral is correct, the deferral was a decision by a careful man about a sum of money, and the same form and the same schedule removed a night crew from a model and put a bridge bracket on a shelf for two years.
**Information revealed:** the omission is not a moral instrument only; it is a maintenance policy with a price schedule, and the volume's climax can therefore be about money and about a public argument over a bill.
**Action or decision:** they do not accuse Rill. Mara's view is that the deferral is the only document in this case that can be attacked without attacking a man, and Orrin's view is that it is the only one that matters, and they disagree about whether to use it, in one exchange, in a corridor, and do not resolve it.
**Power or resource change:** none. The evidence gets better and the argument gets worse.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the trestle's deferral is signed by an inspector and is not villainy. The hospital trestle is repaired, not destroyed, and is not the Northglass rail bridge. No Continuity Office command line, sealed chamber or hidden mechanism is named.
**Ending type:** discovery that moves the argument from blame to arithmetic.
**Next-chapter pull:** Orrin has three machines he can practise on and a body that has been paying for every question he has asked.

### Chapter 17 — "A Dead Pump, a Timer, and a Joint"

**Type:** repair, training, or controlled experimentation.
**Immediate goal:** Learn to ask a narrow question of one assembly without paying for it twice.
**Resistance:** the three machines are the three the craft actually gives him: a dead pump in a yard in Northglass where the answer is already known, a hospital timer where a woman is waiting for the result, and a bridge joint on a cold morning with no water and no consequence. The rule he is teaching himself is the one a practitioner needs and nobody teaches: narrow questions, and stopping before curiosity becomes compulsion. He pays a small keeper's toll on the first and none at all on the third, and the lesson is uncomfortable, because the third is the one he wants. Ivo Pell comes to the bench about an unrelated repair, watches him do it, and is right and contemptuous once: a bench man's ear is not an instrument, and a man who practises on a dead pump is behind his trade, and a man who practises on a live one is a public hazard. He offers no lesson and takes no interest.
**Information revealed:** Aftershock as a practice with a cost and a discipline, not a gift. The reader can now state what Orrin can and cannot do and what it takes out of him.
**Action or decision:** he stops himself at the moment of wanting to go further, once, deliberately, and the chapter says what that cost him in the specific currency of a lost interval and a right hand that will not close for two days.
**Power or resource change:** the ability becomes usable by him and useless to him, which is the volume's position and the next volume's problem. Pell's presence sets up Volume 02's teaching without spending a word of it.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** Ivo Pell is not a mentor here. Pell's contempt is for the trade he left and is aimed at a bench, not at a man. Orrin certifies nothing.
**Ending type:** training beat with an earned boundary.
**Next-chapter pull:** the copied labour ledger is in the Weirs, and it is being read by people it was not written for.

### Chapter 18 — "Rumour With Page Numbers"

**Type:** investigation or discovery.
**Immediate goal:** Get the Weirs' own hours and the guild's own ledger into the same room, in a form the room cannot dismiss.
**Resistance:** the copy of the labour ledger circulates. It is not a public record; it is a copy, and a copy has no stamp, and the Loose Minutes are right that a fact nobody can see is a rumour and wrong that a fact everybody can see is a document. In Chapter 18 it is both. A landlord and an employer use the Weirs' own hours — the counter-record — to argue that the pumping district's residents are not reliable tenants and not reliable employees, and a tenant block loses a rent increase, or a man's hours, or a family's standing, in a single afternoon, on the strength of eleven years of accurate bookkeeping.
**Information revealed:** the cost of the counter-record. The Weirs can produce their hours and cannot produce anybody's signature, and a district that can prove its own labour is a district that can be invoiced for it. This is the volume's argument turning on itself, and it is not a misunderstanding; it is the structural problem.
**Action or decision:** Teo was right to send it and is the one who has to stand in the room where it does damage. He does not apologise to Orrin, because Orrin did not send it, and that is the beginning of their actual working relationship: a disagreement about method with consequences on both sides.
**Power or resource change:** none. A capability the reader can see and an inability to aim it, which is Stage 6's problem forty volumes early and is here only a bruise.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the Loose Minutes are neither heroes nor a syndicate and disagree about disclosure. Nothing in this chapter is theft of a record that can be charged as theft; if a section cap or a page is taken, say which and let it cost something. Teo is not recruited and not softened.
**Ending type:** cost, and a friendship that survives it by accident.
**Next-chapter pull:** a Green Span hospital pump is going to fail on a schedule Orrin can feel and cannot certify.

### Chapter 19 — "The Pump He Could Not Certify"

**Type:** repair, training, or controlled experimentation. **The batch climax.**
**Immediate goal:** Get a failing hospital pump repaired before the ward's clocks lose their grip on a medicine schedule.
**Resistance:** he reads it correctly. ### Chapter 20 — "What the Ledger Says About Him"

**Type:** recovery, aftermath, or emotional payoff. **The batch's end.**
**Immediate goal:** Keep working while his right hand will not close and his ear will not stop.
**Resistance:** ordinary work, an ordinary day, four small jobs and a body that is not cooperating, and the discovery in his own handwriting that he has written four entries in the grey book this month and three of them are other people's faults and one of them is his own. The copybook is in hands he cannot count. A ward has had a morning. A tenant block has lost a rent increase. Sel Kade's diversion is public and reviewed by nobody. The Archive of Unlisted is as shut as it was a month ago. Nothing he has done has cost anybody's time in a place anybody can read.
**Information revealed:** the first reading-back of the volume's own failure to Orrin by a person who loves him and is not invested in his dignity, and the first moment he understands that a rule obeyed and a warning unused are the same failure wearing different clothes.
**Action or decision:** he writes a second rule under the first, in the same hand, and it is worse than the first: *no result of mine goes out of this room without a person named who can refuse it.* He goes to work. Teo arrives at the bench at the end of the day, uninvited, with a page of the copybook and a question about a name in a Northglass boardinghouse, which is the first of the work they will do together and is not an alliance.
**Emotional change:** from shame to method. He is not comforted and is not absolved; he has a job and a rule and a worse ear and a young man in the doorway.
**Power or resource change:** none. He is the same man with less and more paper.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the Archive of Unlisted stays shut. The renewal is still weeks out and is not threatened. No institution is created. Ada Vance is on her tram and is not in this chapter. The three minutes remain the only number the city published.
**Ending type:** recovery that is also a decision, and a forward pull into Chapter 21's organisation and Chapter 25's repetition.
