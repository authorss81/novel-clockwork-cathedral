# Chapter 48 — Four Lines

The document went up on the licensing floor of the Guild of Horologists on the Monday the twenty-eighth of the fifth month at about two in the afternoon, because paragraph eight of it said the licensing floor of this hall was one of four places, and nobody in that building had any power to stop a sheet of foolscap being put on their own wall, and by the Wednesday morning of the first of the sixth month it had been on the wall for two days and about four hundred people had walked past it and the hall's own master had read it twice and declined to sign it in front of twenty people in a pump house four miles away.

Gerren Hask did it on the Wednesday the first of the sixth month at ten in the morning, on the licensing floor, standing at the counter, in front of about thirty people, and he did not do it with a question.

A question may be put against a holder by a member, or by the hall on behalf of a member, upon a matter concerning that holder's returns. A question is not read to the holder. It is not to be discussed with the holder. It is answered in writing within twenty-one days. It does not suspend any right of a holder, and does not alter any term, and does not require an interview, and none will be offered.

That is notice nine, and it is on the board on the licensing floor along with eleven others about the use of the building, and it is what the hall went after in the fourth month about the Weir Two return, and it cost Orrin Vale nothing at all, because a question cannot suspend anything.

"A determination is a different instrument," said Gerren Hask, "and I am going to say why I have used it, in a room, before I read it, because in about eleven years somebody is going to find out which one I used and I would like it on the record that I chose it and why.

"A question is about returns. It asks a man what he has written down. It takes twenty-one days, it cannot stop him working, it cannot be discussed with him, and it costs him nothing, and it is the right instrument for a question about a book.

"A determination is about an act. It is made on a day and it takes effect on a day. And there is only one act in this city that can be got at with a determination, and it is a holder certifying a thing his class does not permit him to certify, and I did not have to look for it, and I want that said: I did not have to find a way round a rule, I used the rule, and the rule did exactly what it has been put there to do since before I was a master, and the fact that it worked on a good man in front of a good crowd at a cost to the good man is not a defect in the rule and I am not going to stand here and pretend that it is."

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

Then he read it out. He read it out himself, standing at the counter, in his own voice, in the guild's own type, twice, the way a notice is read in this city when it is a required line, and it is four lines and it is the whole of what happened.

> **DETERMINATION OF THE LICENSING FLOOR, GUILD OF HOROLOGISTS, CLOCKWORK QUARTER. First day of the sixth month.**
>
> *1. That on the thirtieth of the fifth month, in a pump house on the canal road in the Lower Weirs pumping district, the holder M. Vale, class three, did sign and certify a local toll upon the main of that district, in the figure of three hundred and sixty minutes, upon a document posted at four places in this city, one of which was the licensing floor of this hall, and did then cause work to be done in accordance with that document.*
>
> *2. That a local toll is a district toll. That a district toll may be certified only by the holder of a licence of the second class or above, or by the district that owns the reserve.*
>
> *3. That the holder named above holds a licence of the third class. That the Lower Weirs pumping district has no standing under the water act. That the master of this hall declined to certify the toll on the ground stated by him in the pump house on the thirtieth instant, as is entered in his own hand on the document itself, and that the holder knew both of the first two of these before he signed.*
>
> *4. That this hall does not find that the holder acted in bad faith; and it is determined that the licence of the holder be suspended in accordance with the standing orders of this hall; and that the term of the licence is not altered by this determination; and that the licence expires on the nineteenth of the third month next year.*

Nobody applauded and nobody cheered and about four people in that room did not breathe for a moment. The hall's oldest clerk put her hand flat on the counter. Orrin Vale heard line four twice and only understood the whole of it the second time, and what the whole of it was, is this: nobody had shortened his clock. His licence still ran until the nineteenth of the third month next year, four months off, and a master had moved before his own expiry by four months, on his own signature, in front of thirty people, in four true lines, and there was nothing in the world to appeal it to and no date anywhere in the year at which it might have been better for him.

"Thank you, Master," he said.

"Mr Vale," said Gerren Hask. "I have nothing to do with this and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I have read your W-4 of the nineteenth of the third month and I have read the answer you sent on the twenty-fourth of the fourth, and there are eleven words in that answer that I have thought about every working day since, and I am not going to say which eleven words they are in this room, because if I say them in this room then the hall has noticed them and the hall noticing them is a thing that will be in a file by Friday."

It was the same Della Marsh who had said *don't*, four times, since the first of the fifth month.

---

Then he read the second thing, and the second thing is the part of this chapter that the volume of the future is built on, and it took eleven minutes and it had three parts, and every part of it was the guild's own standing orders operating correctly on a guild master.

"Where a master of this hall declines a certification which the ordinance permits him to make, the master's class is subject to review, and the master's class is determined by the licensing floor, and the determination is published." He read it out of a book and not out of his own head, which is what a man does when he wants the room to be able to look it up. "I declined a certification on the thirtieth of the fifth month. I declined it for a reason I gave in a pump house and the reason is on a sheet of foolscap on the wall of this hall, and I stand by the reason and I am not going to pretend that I declined it for any other."

"1. The class of the master is under review from this date. Pending the review, the master certifies no shift alone. Every certificate issued by a master of this hall from this date is countersigned by a holder of the first class, and a list of them is published monthly, at the counter, in a book, on the wall."

"2. The right of a master of this hall to certify a shift in the maintenance-deferred class is withdrawn, and is not restored by the review, and the class itself is abolished by this hall within twelve months, and any site in it is to be entered in the ordinary class or not at all."

"3. The labour book of this hall, series 1 to 9, eighteen years, is to be published in full, at the counter and in the hall's own copy for sale at a price that covers paper, with an annex drawn by the hall and not by any other body, which shall state the date of the night of the Long Nine, the figure of two hundred and twelve, and the finding of this hall as to the hours of that night; and the figure of two hundred and twelve is the figure of that annex and is not an entry in any shift book of this hall and is not to be added to any other figure in it, and the master of this hall has instructed that this be printed at the head of the annex because a figure in a book with nine other figures in it is a figure somebody will add up."

The room did not make a sound at all.

Two hundred and twelve had been on a small notice on a wall case in that hall for as long as anybody could remember, and the figure was in the guild's own liability annex, and Della Marsh had refused it in the second month of the year to a woman who paid four shillings in coin with no receipt, and there it was now, in a determination read out at a counter, with the master instructing that it not be added up.

"That is the whole of the punishment," said Gerren Hask, "and I want to say the last thing about it myself, before anybody in this room does, and then I am going to go and do a job in Northglass and I am not going to be thanked for it.

"It is a punishment and it is the correct use of a well-run system and I am not going to be ironic about it in a room with thirty people in it. I declined a thing I was entitled to do. I was right to decline it. The rule that says a master who declines is a master whose class is reviewed does not know or care whether he was right, and I would still be a man with my class under review if I had signed a district's outage at two in the morning because a man I liked asked me to, and I would rather be a repairer under public oversight with my name on a monthly list than be a master who signs whatever is put in front of him at two in the morning, and I have not been asked about the Long Nine and I am not going to start now, and that is the end of what I have to say."

He put the sheet down and squared it, and did not shake anybody's hand, and went out into Verge Lane at about twenty past ten in the morning with his hat in his hand, and he is a repairer under public oversight for the rest of his life, and it is stated here without any irony at all, because that is what it is.

---

The other two things in the settlement were not the guild's and were not done on the Wednesday.

The district works' schedule of maintenance works for the Lower Weirs pumping district has nine items and an interval at item ten and it was adopted on the twenty-second of the fourth month for £4 202 10s 6d and raised to £4 613 18s 6d on the twenty-eighth of the fourth. Item one is *Weir Two, second stage: bearing renewal and re-alignment of the gate-handling line, £412 6s, programme year three.*

It was brought forward into the current programme year on the Wednesday the first of the sixth month, by a variation, in about nine minutes, on a sheet at a counter, by a man who is about thirty and has been at that counter for eleven years and who refused correctly and to whom nothing whatever was done, and who did not make a speech about it, and whose name is on the variation, and who was thanked by nobody including the person it was for.

The bearing was out of a box in the pump house, where the spare bearing has been since the station was built, and it was fitted on the Thursday the second and the Friday the third of the sixth month by a works' fitter with four of the district's own men holding the lantern, and it took two days and it cost four hundred and twelve pounds and six shillings, and it is the first work done out of that schedule, and it was done four days after a sheet of foolscap went up on a licensing floor and eleven days after a notice went up on a ward board.

And then the line, on the district's own sheet, in the works' own hand, in the maintenance column, which is a column four inches wide, and which is the first time in the history of that sheet that anything under it has been a person.

> *Weir Two, second stage. Maintained by: B. Sarr. A. Dench, known as Amos, to be entered as a full name and not as an initial. T. Arden, from the 30th ult., licensed: no. R. Morrow, night porter, Green Row, hours as returned.*
> *Vance, P. — 1st month, the seventeenth. Night shift 4. Carried on the line because the line is a line and the line does not begin where a man begins.*

The fifth name on a night board at Weir Two has been a boy who cannot be entered on a form for four months, and he is entered on this, in full, with the words *licensed: no* against his name, in a works' hand, on a published sheet, and that is a form for the first time in the district's history, and nobody in the works' office argued about it and the man who wrote it out said it was a maintenance line and a maintenance line carries names, and if anybody did not like it they could write to the works and the works would read it.

---

The toll ledger went into a case on the wall of the outer room of the pumping ward on the canal road on the Friday the third of the sixth month at about two in the afternoon, and the case has a glass in it and a lock and the key is on a string and the string is on the counter and anybody in the district may ask for the key, and there is a chair in that room for a person who wants to sit and read it, and there is a book of pencil and a book of ink on the ledge.

It is the Office of Public Reckoning's requirement, and the Office required it on the strength of its own audit and it needed no vote from anybody, because the Office audits tolls and a toll is a thing a city is obliged to have written down somewhere, and the Office's requirement is one line and it is the first line in this book that anybody has ever written down:

> *That every body toll recorded by any body in this city be recorded with the name of the person, and with the duration of the interval; and that where no instrument recorded the duration, the column shall be left empty and shall not be filled by inference.*

And the ledger has two sets of columns, and the first two are old, and the second two are new.

> | MINUTES | DISTRICT | | NAME | DURATION |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | 3 | Clockwork Quarter | | | |
> | 26 | Clockwork Quarter | | | |
> | | | | VANCE, P. | |

And the head of the book carries a headnote in the Office's own type, and it is printed on the inside cover and it is the sentence the whole of the last year of this book has been walking towards, and it is five lines long, and it is this:

> *The four columns at the head of this book are the city's toll ledger. The first two carry the minutes taken from a district's clocks and the district they were taken from, and have been kept in public for forty-three years, and the stair on Cathedral Rise carries them in chalk every morning of the world.*
>
> *The second two are new, and they carry, for every toll taken from a living person's attention, coordination or memory: a name, and a duration.*
>
> *Where no instrument in this city recorded the duration, the column is empty. An empty column in this book is a statement that the interval was not recorded. It is not an omission of this office. It is not a mystery, and it is not to be filled in by inference, and any person who fills one in by inference commits an offence against this office and against the person whose name is in the column beside it.*
>
> *The first entry under the new columns is on this page. It is the name of a man who went down a ladder on the seventeenth of the first month to close a hand valve that a model had already said was shut, and the pump that should have stopped did not stop, and there is no instrument anywhere in Morrowfen that timed that interval, and I will not put a number in that column and neither will anybody else, and his name is in the column next to it, which is the whole of the reform and the whole of what cannot be fixed.*

That is what is in it. There is no figure in the duration column. There is nothing in the duration column at all, and a man stood on the other side of that case on the Friday afternoon with a pencil in his hand and did not put one there, and there are about sixty-one calibration-strain entries in a year in one committee's files in this city with the same empty space opposite them, and there will be more, and the empty space is now a shape that the law recognises rather than a gap that the law has never noticed.

---

Orrin Vale shut the shop on the Friday the third of the sixth month at six, and he did not go up the hill, and he did not write it in the grey book until the Saturday, and the entry is four lines and it is the last thing he writes in that book this year.

> *Sixth month, the third. Suspended. Certifying a district toll with no standing to certify it, in a room, in daylight, on a sheet, with my name on it and the reason in my own hand on the same sheet.*
>
> *My licence runs to the nineteenth of the third month next year and nobody has moved that date and nobody has shortened my clock, and a master has done a thing four months before he had to, in four true lines, at a counter, in front of thirty people, and it is the correct use of a well-run system and I would not be able to explain to anybody why it is not.*
>
> *I cannot certify a civic machine. I can still clean a barrel and bush a wheel and beat a clock to a rate, and there is a lease on two benches in Verge Lane that runs to Michaelmas, and there is a woman of sixty-six who winds four clocks on a Monday, and a man with a bar in the Weirs who will not be thanked, and a copyist in a front room three doors down from me who is owed eleven shillings and has asked for a lease and a wage a month and has not been given either.*
>
> *Nothing of mine went out of that room without a person named who could refuse it. That is the only line I have got out of the whole of the year and I have got it the hard way and the price of it is on a sheet in four places, and I would do the hour again, at six in the morning, in front of the people it is done to.*

The man who could have written the number next to Vance's name is in that entry, and so is the man who could have written nothing, and those are the same man, and the book does not say which it was.
