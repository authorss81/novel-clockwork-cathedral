## What this phase is for, in one paragraph

Volume 01 is a book about paper, and it is over, and the danger to it is not that it was written badly but that the next writer opens `outline/volume-01.md`, reads a plan, and finds fifty chapters of delivered prose that do not match the plan in eleven specific places. Every one of those eleven places has been recorded in the state files by the batches that caused them, and this phase's first job is to **confirm that each record is on disk and is true of the page**, and its second is to **write a closing brief that states the ending as it was actually delivered, not as it was planned**, and its third is to check the seven concrete resolution items, the seven chapter types, the calendar, the toll ledger, the suspension mechanism, and the two toll columns against the finished chapters. If everything checks, this phase writes one file and stops.

## Task 1 — Verify the seven resolution items against the page

`outline/volume-01.md` §Concrete resolution requires all seven on the page in Chapters 48–50, distributed rather than queued. Check each and record the chapter, the line and the wording in the closing brief. They should be:

1. **A repaired pump and a public toll ledger with two kinds of column** — Ch 48. Weir Two's second stage: item one of the published schedule brought forward in about nine minutes on a variation, bearing out of a box in the pump house, fitted on the Thursday and Friday, **£412 6s**, the first work done out of that schedule. Maintained by name, in the works' own hand, in a four-inch column, **the first time anything under it has been a person**, including *Vance, P., 1st month, the seventeenth, night shift 4, carried on the line because the line is a line and the line does not begin where a man begins.* Ledger in a glass case in the pumping ward's outer room, key on a string, chair and pencil and ink on the ledge; MINUTES and DISTRICT forty-three years old; NAME and DURATION new; **first entry `VANCE, P.` and no figure at all in the duration column**; a five-line headnote in the Office's type saying an empty column is a statement that the interval was not recorded, is not an omission of that office, is not a mystery, is not to be filled in by inference, and that any person who fills one in by inference commits an offence against the office and against the person named beside it.
2. **A standing protection from silent shifts, temporary, and the reader told so in the same sentence** — Ch 49. No shift in the Lower Weirs pumping district certified in the maintenance-deferred class until the ledger and annex are published and received; expires at the first sitting of the committee after the report, not before; **the committee has not undertaken to renew it and has no power to promise that it will**; report due at the ninth month's sitting; if late, the twelfth. Worth about three months against an audit that takes about fourteen.
3. **Hask loses the right to certify silent shifts and publishes the labour ledger with a liability annex naming the night of the Long Nine** — Ch 48, in three standing orders read out of the guild's own book. **212 appears only in that annex and the master instructs that it be printed at the head of the annex because a figure in a book with nine other figures in it is a figure somebody will add up.** The master may not certify a shift alone; every certificate countersigned by a first-class holder with a monthly list published; the maintenance-deferred class withdrawn and to be abolished within twelve months; series 1 to 9, eighteen years, published in full at the counter and for sale at a price that covers paper.
4. **Sel Kade keeps her post and submits to a tenant review** — Ch 46. Four paragraphs on a board, found against her **in a sentence about a flood** and expressly not in a sentence about employment; the warden holds her office; the process stands; no person is thanked.
5. **Rook files the toll-ledger demand herself, on arithmetic rather than sentiment** — Ch 49. Carried nine to four, the four not the clerk, read twice, entered on the minutes, **and she thanked nobody, including the four.**
6. **Orrin's licence is suspended, removing his right to certify a machine's local toll; the bench stays open on a lease and a reputation** — Ch 48. **The term is not altered and the licence expires on the nineteenth of the third month next year.**
7. **The ninth tick remains unexplained** — Ch 50 alone, on the second step of the Cathedral at a quarter to twelve on the eighth of the sixth month, heard for the third and last time, costing nothing, giving no night and no face and no word, and explicitly distinguished from Aftershock in the text.

## Task 2 — Verify the ending as delivered, against the plan's shape

The plan called for Chapters 47, 48 and 50 to be the three to get right, and for the climax to be neither heroism nor punishment. The delivered version is: **a competent man doing a competent thing in public, on a piece of paper, with his name on a number he is not allowed to sign, while the people he is costing stand in the room and are entirely right to be furious** (Ch 47); **a four-line lawful instrument suspending him and a guild ordinance reviewing the master for saying no** (Ch 48); **a stair, some chalk, a stranger's question, a name, a blank, and eight** (Ch 50). Confirm the batch delivers that and not the other thing. If any of it reads as a triumph, say so and fix the prose locally; **do not restructure a chapter.**

## Task 3 — Verify the three mechanical invariants the series depends on

- **The calendar, absolute, both maps already in `state/continuity.md`.** The prompt for batch 0005 mislabelled the 11th and the 12th of the fifth month; the prose followed the day map. Confirm the prose and the state file agree and that nothing in Chapters 41–50 names a relative date. **Confirm no Assembly or committee sits on a Sunday anywhere in the volume.**
- **The two kinds of toll, never merged.** A clock toll is minutes and a paying district. A body toll is an interval of a living person's attention, coordination or memory and had no column in this city until the third of the sixth month. **Confirm the posted document keeps them apart on a public sheet (paragraph 6 is the ward, paragraph 7 is the toll) and that the blank in the ledger's duration column is a stated absence and not an omission.**
- **The count of the reader's carried quantities.** Nine **toll hearings**? No — **three readings of the ninth tick (Chapters 1, 3, 50) and no more; three keeper's tolls (Chapters 5, 9, 19); two hand corrections of his own; three minutes charged and five minutes found on Halloway Street; one ward's forty minutes; one tank; one reserve and two districts.**

## Task 5 — Write the closing brief

Create exactly one file: **`workspace/volume-02/VOLUME-02-BRIEF.md`**, and nothing else in that directory. It is the first thing a Volume 02 writer opens and it must be usable without re-reading fifty chapters. It should be compact and contain, in this order:

1. **The ending as delivered, in about eight hundred words, in the present tense of the page.** What a reader who has read all fifty chapters is holding on the morning of the eighth of the sixth month: a man with a lease, a bench, a sign, a repair he did in a flood, a bolt from a tram bogie, a piece of conduit four people have a length of, a suspended licence whose date nobody moved, and a piece of paper with his name under a number he had no standing to sign. A district with a protected maintenance line, a public ledger with two sets of columns and one entry, a four-paragraph finding in chalk, and a chandler who would like it minuted that he is a chandler. A master who is a repairer under public oversight and did not confess. A nurse with a card and a date on it and four names. A woman of sixty-six winding four clocks with a lamp. A copyist owed eleven shillings. **And a stair with a name in it and nothing in the column beside it, a stranger who asked how many more, and eight.**
2. **The seven resolution items with their chapter and their numbers**, copied from Task 1, so that Volume 02's writer can cite them without guessing.
3. **What Volume 02 inherits, and the eight things that are load-bearing and temporary.** Put at the top of the list: **the standing protection is temporary, runs to the first sitting after the guild's report, and the report is due in the ninth month and may be late, and the committee has not undertaken to renew it.** Then the ledger's empty column and the headnote's prohibition on filling it by inference; the eighteen-year ledger and the annex and the 212 that must never be added to anything; the maintenance-deferred class to be abolished within twelve months, which will cost a ward a rate base and the hall eleven shillings a head; the twelve-month inspection interval with no first inspection in it yet; the tell-tale nobody has agreed to make a line item of; the tank; and the two clocks on the Quarter hill.
4. **The open questions that are deliberately open, and must not be answered in Volume 02 unless the outline says so.** (i) **How many other people are missing from the civic model, and why does the Directorate keep a sealed room under the Cathedral full of the names of the people it stopped counting** — asked out loud on a stair in Chapter 50 and not answered, and Volume 01 leaves it open on purpose. (ii) The lived intervals taken by severe body tolls: some families know, some never will, and **the blank in the ledger is not a mystery to be solved.** (iii) The ninth tick: three hearings, no mechanism, no cost, no column, and Volume 04 owns the mechanism with the device kept live. (iv) The uncharged minutes: three charged, five found, two nobody's. (v) The Teo–Wenna disclosure argument, open since the ninth of the fourth month, and the book of initials with no key written down, still in a kitchen on Weir Lane.
5. **The canon a Volume 02 writer must not contradict.** One page. The three tolls and their order; the two hand corrections; the three rules and their dated texts and what each has cost him; the four districts, four lines, four numbers and one blank of Chapter 42; the two kinds of toll; the 212; the absence of twenty-three; the licence dates, both of them; **that the ninth tick was heard three times and never again inside Volume 01**; that the Water Users' Committee has nine members and a property qualification and has never had a night worker on it and there is no form by which one could be put on it; that the pumping district has no standing under the water act; that a district toll may be certified only above the third class or by the district that owns the reserve; that the Office of Public Reckoning has four staff, one window, a written right to be ignored, and a drawer of thirty-one four-line cards; that Hask was right and said no; that the settlement is the guild's, the Assembly's, the works' and the tenants' and **the Director's door is not in this volume and does not open for another eleven volumes.**
6. **A short list of the eleven card-versus-prose divergences, with the prose governing each.** They are recorded in `state/open-threads.md` §Review pass of batch 0005 and in `state/continuity.md`, and the brief should list them by number with one line each, because a writer who finds a plan and a chapter in conflict must know which one wins and it is always the chapter.
