# Chapter 74 — A Form in a Hall, and Who Signs It

The only room in four districts that can produce a document with a body at the bottom of it has a gate, a register, and a clerk who takes minutes, and the price of the whole thing is forty pounds a year out of a tin, and on the morning of the tenth of the ninth month a man of about thirty from the district works' counter stood in the rain outside that gate and put it on a table, and by the Wednesday it was on the table in a room with nine people in it, and nobody had closed the door and nobody had opened it either, and the door is still open.

---

**Saturday the tenth, about half past seven in the morning, in the rain, outside the Northglass gate, with about three hundred men on the road.**

**It is the tenth day and there is no way to tell anybody what day it is.** The early shift went out on the Thursday, the day shift followed on the Friday, the pattern shop followed on the Saturday morning at about ten, about a hundred and forty men of the fettling shop came back on the Sunday, and the district has been in and out since and nobody is keeping a list, because a list is what a strike has and this is not a strike. By seven on the Saturday morning there were about three hundred men on the road and a hundred and forty of the second shift were inside.

Ambrose came out of the hut at about half past seven with a sheet of paper in a stiff envelope and a works' coat over his own coat, and he is about thirty and he has been at a counter eleven years and he does not belong in a road.

"Mr Arden."

"You have got about four minutes before the shift goes in and I am not going to be responsible for four hundred men being late on a wet Thursday because I have been talking to you."

"I am not here about the men. **I have had a thing costed and I am going to say it in about ninety seconds and then I am going to go back inside, and if you say no I am going to say thank you and I am not going to come back with it.**"

"Go."

**Forty pounds.** That is what he said, and he said it in the rain, and three hundred men heard it.

"Four hundred heads at two shillings the head for a year is eight hundred shillings, which is forty pounds. Out of the gate fund. The tin at the front office with the wages bill in it. **And that is a standing withdrawal and reinstatement, series GH-9, lodged with the guild, and it carries a body, and a body is the first of the three requirements, and a form is not a body, and I have watched you say that sentence in three rooms this year.**"

"And the other two."

"The other two are the instrument, which is the same form, and the person, which is a station on the rail that a district standing fills. A company that lodges a standing fills it. **A company can be formed in a morning. I have the name of a man in the third floor of the register office who will do it for eleven pounds and I have not asked him and I am not going to.**"

Somebody at the back of the crowd said something. The second shift went in at seven forty-five.

"**No,**" said Teo Arden.

"You said that in August."

"I said that in August. **I am not going to say it again and I want you to understand that I am not saying it again, because a man who says the same thing twice in a month has stopped having an argument and started having a tune, and you will stand in a room in about four years and everybody in it will be humming.**"

"Then give me the other one."

And that is the whole of the moment, and three hundred men on a wet road heard it, and the man who had the argument in August was the man who would not have the argument in September, and the reason is in the next four sentences and he gave it standing in about fifteen inches of mud.

"**In August I said that a company that lodges the first instrument is the district's representative in a room where the district is not allowed to speak. That was true and you have written it down and it is not wrong and I am not withdrawing it.**"

"Then—"

"**The other reason is this, and it is the one I am going to use from now on, and I have thought about it for a month and it took me a month and the first one took me nine seconds.** A company is a body. A body is made out of people who agreed to be it. **You have four hundred men on this road and you have not asked one of them, and you cannot ask four hundred men anything on a Thursday morning, and I have watched you spend six months telling anybody who would listen that a form is not a room.**" He looked at the gate. "You are asking a body to be a room before anybody has been in it. I am not going to be the man who does that and I will not be the man who asks anybody else to."

Ambrose folded the envelope under his arm.

"Thank you," he said, exactly as he had said he would.

"Give it to somebody else."

"**I have given it to two other people and one of them has said no and I am not going to name him in a road.**"

He went back into the hut.

---

**Monday the twelfth, ten in the morning, in the works manager's office on the first floor over the gate, and the stoppage is on its sixth day and about a hundred and ten men are out.**

There were eleven people and a works' clerk of about thirty with a book. Idris Vane in his own chair. Ambrose at the end, standing, because there is one chair and it is the manager's. Ivy Pike, who signs for the Weir One gate and keeps a book of her own that she has never shown anybody. Wenna Pruitt, who has the council's books and no money and has been in eleven rooms in this district since the fourth of the fourth month and has not been thanked for any of them. **Bessie Crane, who is about sixty and has a cloth bag and a book of her own and has run nineteen years of laundry on Bellweather Road on a penny a sheet under the trade price.** Hanna Stroud, nine blocks. Teo Arden, who came and stood at the back. Orrin Vale, who had no business in the room and was in it.

"The report is in," said Ambrose, before anybody sat down. "**The guild's labour book, series one to nine, eighteen years, about a thousand pages, and the annex. Received at the committee's counter at about four o'clock yesterday afternoon, which is late, and the standing orders required it before the twelfth month's sitting and it is the twelfth.**"

"And the protection," said Wenna Pruitt.

"Expires at the first sitting of this committee after the report is received, and not before. **That is the first sitting, and it is tomorrow, and the committee has not undertaken to renew it and has no power to promise that it will.**" He put the sheet down. "I have not moved anything and nobody has moved anything and there is nobody in this city entitled to move anything. **Councilor Rook moved it in June, in about eleven minutes, on a show of hands, and she read the ninth word and I did not, and she has since said so in a pumping ward and I am not going to pretend to a chronology of my own negligence.**"

Nobody said anything about it for about four seconds, and then Bessie Crane said, without looking up:

"That is the third time somebody has told me a thing is temporary and then acted as though it was permanent."

"It is the third time it has been said to anybody at all," said Ambrose. "**I moved it myself in June, in eleven minutes, on a show of hands, and I did not read the ninth word.**"

---

**And then the price, which is what the room was for, and it is forty pounds, and it went on the table and stayed there.**

Ambrose put the envelope on the table and took out of it three sheets, and they were photocopied, and the top one was a printed form with a reference number in the corner, and it was not the guild's and it was not the works' and it was not a return.

"**That is what a limited company has to do to exist.** I got it at the register office on the Wednesday, and it cost one shilling to look at and I did not go on the Thursday or the Friday because I wanted to be certain of what I was putting on your table. Anybody may inspect the register of a company for a fee of one shilling, and anybody may inspect the memorandum, and the memorandum has a clause in it called the objects, and the objects are what the company is for, and they go in a public book in a public room."

He put it flat.

"**If this company is formed, the objects clause reads: *To represent the pumping district of the Lower Weirs in all questions concerning the water supply of that district, and to do all such things as a body corporate may lawfully do.*** And that sentence goes into a public register, in a book anybody may read for a shilling, and somebody has to sign it, and the man who signs it signs it in his own name, and his own name is on it for ever and in a public room."

The room did not say anything for about half a minute.

"That is the thing," said the works clerk, from the end of the table, in a flat voice. She had the form in front of her and she had a finger on a line and she had not said anything for an hour and a half. "**That is the only bit of it I did not know at eleven o'clock this morning and I have been taking minutes of this since June. Every other part of it is money and a wet stamp. That clause is the whole of the danger and it is not a matter of money and it is not a matter of a vote.**"

"Go on," said Ambrose.

"**A company's standing is a company's standing.** That is not a defect in it, that is what it is, and I would write it on the wall if there were a wall. **And the clause says the company exists to answer. It does not say the company exists to be asked. It says to represent, and a company represents, and everything a company answers is going to be true and is going to be about the company.**"

She read the line out loud again, the printed one, in a room with nine people in it.

"**In about four years there is going to be a room. It is not going to be this room. And that room is going to ask somebody about the water on this district, and the only party to the question that anybody can point at is going to be a company with an objects clause in a public register that says it exists to represent this district. And it will answer. And the answer will be correct. And the answer will be a figure off the tin at the front office and the works' own inspection programme and the works' own opinion of its own hazard, which is a thing a company is the only possible authority on and cannot be cross-examined about, because there is nobody in that room who can say the company is wrong, because the company is the only one there.**"

She shut the form.

"That is what I have been taking minutes of since June and I have not known how to say it and it took a printed form and a shilling."

---

**And then the other half, and it was said by four people, and no two of them said the same thing, and they were all right.**

**Bessie Crane's half**, and she had it ready and had clearly had it ready since June.

"You are asking me what I think about a door, and I have stood in one. **In the seventh month I put myself in a return for two shillings the head, and it cost me sixty-two shillings a year in coin out of this bag, and it bought me a place in a list and a name on a board and a rate that says I am in it.**" She put her hand flat on the cloth bag. "So I know what it is to be counted and I know what it costs and I would do it again, and I am telling you what I got for it, and I did not get a vote on anything. **I got counted.**"

"What would you get."

"**Standing.** In a question. **That is the thing and there is no other thing, and I have wanted it since the seventh of the seventh and I have wanted it for thirty-one women who are on a penny a sheet under the trade price because a class was invented to keep a landlord's water cheap.**" She took her hand off the bag. "**So it is worth something. I am not going to stand in this room and say it is worth nothing because I do not like the price.**"

**Ivy Pike's**, in one sentence, because she has been the person who signs for a gate for eleven years and has never been asked what a gate is for.

"**Forty pounds.** I have signed for that gate for eleven years and I have never once been asked what a gate is for, and if a company can stand in front of it and be asked, then somebody is finally going to ask me, and I do not know whether I am glad."

**Wenna Pruitt's half**, and she had been in the room for an hour and had not spoken, and she said it flat, with her hands on the table, which is a thing she does.

"I am going to say the other half and then I am going to stop, and Mr Vale is not going to like it, and I have said since June that neither of us is going to choose.

"**If nobody lodges anything then nothing happens, and that is not a threat, it is the schedule.**" She looked round. "For eighteen years the answer to every question about this district has been a schedule, and a schedule is a thing nobody decides, and I have watched a woman of forty-four put down a post on Thursday morning and I have watched three hundred men stand at a gate and I have watched a man with a bar in an override at half past two in the morning, and **not one of those three things was decided by anybody and all three of them happened.** A district that lodges nothing is a district that is administered. **I want something on the table, and I do not want it to be a company, and those two sentences are both true and I have said them both and I am not going to be asked which one I would put second.**"

"And you will not choose," said Teo Arden, from the back.

"No. **I said it on the nineteenth of the eighth and I said it in a works' office, and I am not going to stop saying it now that it is inconvenient.**"

---

**Hanna Stroud's was four sentences and it was about September.**

"Forty pounds is nothing. **I have nine blocks and I could find forty pounds in a drawer in this district and so could the chandler and so could Mr Vane and so could about forty other people who have each decided that the reason nothing has happened for eighteen years is that nobody has ever had to pay for anything.** Do not let the smallness of it make it sound safe. I said in August that a room is a place a person can be told no in, and I have not changed my mind, and nobody in this room can be told no in this room, because a thing is going to be decided about forty pounds and none of us has ever been asked what we want and all of us are going to be asked whether we agree."

"And the mutual?" said Orrin Vale.

"**You are not going to ask the mutual.** The mutual said no in four minutes on the twenty-ninth of the seventh and gave you twenty-six addresses instead, and it is not going to say yes in a fortnight, and it is not a body anybody can go back to twice in a fortnight, and if you ask them again this month that is the end of the only list in this district that the people it is about keep themselves." He put his hands in his pockets. "Do not ask them. Ask somebody else or ask nobody."

---

**And on Tuesday the thirteenth of the ninth month the standing protection lapsed, on a day and not before, and nobody extended it, and it lapsed while a stoppage was running.**

The committee received the report at about four in the afternoon. It is about a thousand pages, series one to nine, eighteen years, and an annex drawn by the hall stating the date of the night of the Long Nine, and the figure of two hundred and twelve, and the hall's finding as to the hours of that night, and the master's instruction that the figure be printed at the head of the annex because a figure in a book with nine other figures in it is a figure somebody will add up.

**Nobody added it up. There is no column anywhere in this city for the size of a thing nobody has counted, and the drawer of four-line cards in an office with four people in it holds the only instrument that could, and it audits tolls.**

The protection expired at that sitting. It is the first of the standing protection's conditions — the report received — and the first is the only one, and there is no discretion in it and there is no form on which anybody could have asked for one.

**And the finding that nobody made and that everybody in this district now has to work out is the one Councilor Rook made in a pumping ward on the fourth of the eighth, standing, before she asked for anything: the protection protected a certificate in a class that was abolished on the first of the seventh, and it did not cover one of the six names in the works' own maintenance line, and did not on the thirtieth of May either.**

**So nothing stopped on Tuesday the thirteenth except a piece of paper that had been protecting nothing for eleven weeks.** Nobody extended it because the committee has not undertaken to renew it and has no power to promise that it will, and nobody moved to have it renewed because there is no form on which anybody in this district may move anything, and the class is gone, and the six men in the works' line with *licensed: no* against two of them are exactly where they were in June.

Bevin Sarr read the notice on the ward board outside the Weir Two pump house on the Wednesday morning at about nine o'clock and copied the two operative lines into the night book in her own hand, with the date, and neither line has a number in it and neither line has a name in it except the clerk's.

---

**And on the Wednesday the fourteenth at about eleven in the morning they were back in the same room and the price was on the table and nobody had picked it up.**

Nothing had been decided. **The company had not been formed and had not been paid for and the eleven pounds had not been spent and the register office had not been approached, and the objects clause was still a piece of paper in an envelope, and the door was still open, and the man who had the argument in August was the man who had the other argument in September, and both of them are in this book because both of them are true.**

Ambrose put the envelope back in his coat.

"I am not going to close it," he said. "**I am going to put it in the works' file, under a minute, with a date on it, and the minute will say that it was proposed, and the price, and that a member of the district refused it and gave a reason, and that no other person has been asked, and that the file is open to anybody who comes to that counter with a reason.** And I am not going to minute why I am doing that, because I do not know, and a clerk does not minute why."

"Can a minute be produced in a court?" said Wenna Pruitt.

"**No.** I have been told so and I have believed it for three years and I have never tested it."

"Then it is worth doing," she said, "for the same reason it is worth doing and not for a better one. **A thing nobody can be asked about can also not be refused, and this one can be refused, and it costs nothing, and I am not going to say thank you for it and neither is anybody else in this room.**"

**And the price stayed on the table, and it was forty pounds, and it was in an envelope in a works' file with a minute on top of it that binds nobody, and at the end of the fourth day there were about ninety men out of that gate and nobody in this city could say on which day the last one of them would go in, or the first.**
