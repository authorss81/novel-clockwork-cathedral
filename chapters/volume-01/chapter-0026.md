# Chapter 26 — What the Second One Cost

The Toll Stair had twenty-six and CLOCKWORK QUARTER on it in wet chalk on the eighth and it had on the ninth and the tenth, and it would have it on the eleventh, and that was the whole of what anybody in Morrowfen could read about what the eighth had cost.

Everything else had to be asked for. That was the discovery, and it was made on the Friday morning of the tenth by four people who had never done it before and who got it wrong for two days before they got it right, and the getting-it-right took the better part of a week, and it produced a list with no column in it.

---

The method, when it arrived, was not clever. It was that a person went and asked somebody who was not in the room, and asked them a specific question, and wrote down the answer and the date and the name of the person who had given it, and then asked the next person the same question.

Mara Venn had nine years of requests that produced *received and noted*. Orrin had a bench and a hand that would not close. Wenna Pruitt had a kitchen and eleven years. Teo Arden had a list of eleven addresses and no index and the beginnings of an idea about what a list was for. And between them, in eight days, they put together the cost of a Reading that the city had recorded in two numbers and a district name.

The list is this. It is written in a hand that is not Orrin's and it is not on a form.

> *Cost of question 4 112, read 8th inst. Compiled the 10th to the 17th by persons going and asking. Nobody in this list was at the reading. Where a figure is somebody's own count, it is marked so.*
>
> **1. Minutes, off a named district.** Twenty-six, drawn from the Clockwork Quarter in proportion to reserve. On the stair in chalk from the eighth to the eleventh, in a district's own hand, and scrubbed at half past five each morning. Within the Quarter: the Quarter's public clock, which is the reference for every timetable in this city, was out by twenty-six minutes for about an hour from a little after eleven on the eighth, and was wound back. **The Quarter holds the most reserve in the city. The draw is a rounding error and the whole city was wrong for an hour and cannot prove it.** Nobody has a record that the hour happened. The Receipt is a bucket and a board and a man's memory.
>
> **2. An interval out of a named person. Mrs Enid Skellern, 61, of Leafmead Row, Green Span.** On the second-Wednesday rota. Sat the affected-district station at the rail on the eighth. Her district on the form was Green Span, and she is a resident of Green Span, and she was correct, and she was lawful, and she was the wrong person. **She has lost the afternoon of the eighth.** She says she went to the library at eleven and sat in a chair by the window until about two, and that she cannot account for the three hours, and that she is sixty-one, and that her mother's hip was like that at sixty-one. The Green Span district's own health file for that week carries one entry in the third column headed *calibration strain*: undated, no name, no duration. The rota carries her name for the second Wednesday. **Two documents, both of them real, and we are the first people to have put the name on the rota against the entry in the third column, and we cannot put a duration beside it, because nothing in Morrowfen recorded one, and I will not invent a number for a woman's lost afternoon.**
>
> **3. A shift worked late.** Weir One, night of the eighth. The district's own board says the crew came off at twenty past two. **The works' certified shift return for the same night says two o'clock.** The return is signed and the board is not and the board is the one that is right. The difference is twenty minutes, on the one night in four months when a Reading of district scale was run over that line, and the reason the district's own record is wrong is that we proved our hours.
>
> **4. A hospital.** The Green Span hospital's auxiliary rising main, third-floor landing, draws off the Lower Weirs pumping line. **The auxiliary was re-packed and re-seated in the second month, the packing is correct, it was correct when it was done, and it is holding. The sub-line that feeds that floor has been correcting out of something for eleven years. The Reading ran over it on the eighth and took eleven minutes out of a morning on the Thursday, and what went was not the pump and was not the packing.** The ward on the third floor lost a morning on the Thursday, the day after. **The sub-line has been correcting out of something for eleven years and this is the first week it gave any of it up.** Four minutes between two medicines. A girl of nineteen on nights did the ward clock's work by hand and was paid for it and did not know that any of it was connected to a question filed on the second.
>
> **5. A bridge.** Ferrywright Bridge, gate interlock, Lower Weirs approach. **The interlock is a fixed delay, released a fixed number of minutes after the last pump of a window. A Reading of this size over the main runs the window long; the interlock counts from the pump and does not care that anything else is going on, so it released the approach early, and the road came open before the water had.** R. Morrow, night porter, Green Row, on the night of the eighth, walked out from the pump house shift at ten past two and found the interlock had released the approach at twenty to ten instead of a quarter past eleven, and the bridge standing open on a water that was forty minutes from being where the model said it would be, and walked round. **He was brought back across at two in the morning by a man with a lamp.** There is no form for a lamp. There is no form for an hour and a half of a man's walk. He has told two people and one of them wrote it down.
>
> **6. A ferry timetable.** The Weirs slipway. **The printed sheet is dated eleven weeks ago and is the document about eleven hundred people are holding, and it was set from the Quarter's public clock, and the Quarter's public clock was wrong for an hour on the morning of the eighth, and the boat ran on the eighth out of it, and the sheet cannot be reprinted, and from the fifteenth, when the schedule begins, the boat will be between four and eleven minutes out on eleven nights, which is in the notice and is not yet a bill.** The company's clerk has written on the sheet, in pencil, in one hand, in eleven places: *as printed — see notice.* That is the whole of the city's response to eleven hundred people and it is by a man with a pencil and a proper hand.
>
> **7. A school signal.** The chapel school on Weir Lane. **Out twice since the eighth, and four to eleven minutes late on eleven nights from the fifteenth, which is in the notice.** The headmistress has written it in a book: *Fri 10th, 4 mins. Mon 13th, 7. — and the notice says eleven more.* Eighty-one children. She has written *no explanation given* on the first line, which is fair, because there is no explanation she has been given, and because the notice that gave it is on a board on Cathedral Rise and says the delay is expected and the delay is, in fact, expected, and the word *expected* is doing an enormous amount of work in it.

---

"It is a good list," said Mara Venn on the Friday the seventeenth, in a room on the second floor of a building on the Rise with the window open. "And I have been sitting here for two days trying to make it a record and I cannot, and I want to say exactly why, because the reason is the reason and it is worth more than the list.

"Any one of those seven could have been an objection on a toll receipt. One. The quarter of an hour the school lost, that is a claim by a school against a district and it goes on a form and a form is a form of receipt and I am very good at forms of receipt, and the objection goes in and is accepted for filing and nothing is repaid.

"Two. The name. I cannot put her name in a *calibration strain* line and I cannot do it because that column has never had a name in it in forty years and there is no form on which to write one, and a woman in an office in Green Span who has never spoken to me is not going to be persuaded out of a health form by an investigator from the wrong building. And I could try, and if I tried, and if she wrote a name in that column, then there would be a name in it with no duration beside it, and the first name in the public record of a lost attention in this city would be a woman of sixty-one from Leafmead Row who did a duty on a rota and went to the library afterwards, and she would never in her life have chosen to be that, and I am not going to do it to her without asking, and if I ask her she will say yes, because she is exactly the sort of woman who says yes, and that is the argument against asking her."

"Then the list is a list," said Orrin.

"The list is a list, and it is in a book in my own office, and there are four staff, and one of them is on the second floor, and I have had to tell him not to file it, because filing it would open an inquiry into a toll charged to the Clockwork Quarter, and an inquiry into a toll charged to the Clockwork Quarter is a thing my office does not have the authority to open and has never tried." She put her pen down. "That is the sentence. The one place in this city whose entire function is to know what a decision cost cannot find out what a decision cost, because knowing would require it to open a file against a district that pays for nothing, and four staff and a written right to be ignored is not a power, Orrin, it is a *description*."

---

She sent it anyway on the Tuesday, because she always sends it, and because in nine years she has never once not sent it, and because a note that nobody sends is worse than a note that is sent, and because the note that came back on the Friday morning was the single most instructive document her office produced that quarter.

> *Received and noted. This office has no standing in the Lower Weirs pumping district and no authority to receive a return compiled by persons not in its employment. A district may compile its own returns, and may print them, and may circulate them. It has not been the practice of this office to advise upon the effect of any Reading upon the works of any district. No further correspondence on this matter.*

Seven days, which was one day slower than the same office had managed the pumping ward's certified shift returns a month before, and nobody in the building could account for the difference in the other direction, and it had the words *no further correspondence* in the corner in small type, and there was no appeal against it, because an appeal lies against a refusal and this was a note, and a note is a piece of furniture.

Mara Venn read it twice and put it in the drawer with the others, and there were eleven in the drawer now, and four of them were about the same seven items on that list.

"It says they have no authority to receive a list of people going and asking," she said. "That is the most accurate sentence anybody in this city has written about me in nine years and I am going to have it framed by somebody who hates me."

---

The Water Users' Committee of the pumping district met on the Monday the thirteenth in a room over a chandler's shop, and there were nine members of it, because it is a body constituted under the water act with a property qualification, and it had voted nine to four on the second inst. and the four were in the minute.

There was one other thing about the room. Orrin was not a member of it, and had never been in it, and could not be: a water users' committee is made of people who own or lease property that draws on a common main, and he owned two benches in Verge Lane in the Clockwork Quarter, four miles away, on a different main. So the difference between a room he could be in and a room he could only be written about in was, in the pumping district, the difference between holding a building and serving a building, and it was not a difference anybody had ever thought to be sentimental about.

He was there because the clerk had written to Vale's Bench, on the Thursday, to ask a question. The ward on the third floor of a hospital had lost a morning. The ward clock, the corridor clock and the pharmacy timer disagreed. Somebody in the room wanted to know from a maintainer what had physically happened to the clocks, and had written a proper letter, and had paid a penny for the postage, and the question was four lines long and it was a good question.

He got four minutes in that room and it was a trap, and he knew it was a trap within about forty seconds, and he answered anyway because four minutes of being useful was four minutes more than any other man in the city.

"The corridor clock is on the sub-line and the sub-line has been correcting out of something for eleven years," he said. "The ward clock is on the same sub-line and is right at the moment of speaking and has not been right continuously since about the fourth. The pharmacy timer is a good instrument with its click set a quarter of an inch too deep and losing four and a half minutes a round." He put the gauge down on the table and kept his hand on it. "None of those three is a fault. There is no fault in that building for me to certify. What happened on the ninth is that a change was made four miles away on a main that feeds it, and the clocks in that hospital are arranged in a chain in which the *reference* is the one nobody uses, and I certified the reference four years ago, and the certificate is in a pigeonhole in a two-room office on this hill, and it says the reference is correct to a tenth of a second."

"And what would you have us do?"

"Nothing," said Orrin. "There is nothing in that building for a maintainer to do, and the only thing anybody in this room could do about the ninth is stop the water schedule, and that is your vote and not mine, and I am a clockmaker and I have no standing and no certificate that would help you with either."

The chair said, "Thank you. The clerk will note that the maintainer advises no action is available to his trade."

*The maintainer advises no action is available to his trade.* It went into the minute in ten words. It was true. It was the most useful thing he had said in a committee room in his life, and it got the Green Span ward nothing, and he walked out of the room and stood in Weir Lane and found that he had wanted to say the other thing so badly that his hand was shaking, and that the reason he could not say it was the same reason as always, and that a room which cannot be entered and can only be written about in is a room where you can be *completely honest* and get nothing.

---

The blind margin was read out in that room at about half past two on the Monday afternoon by a woman of twenty-seven in a nurse's coat with ink on the side of her right hand, who had come from Green Span because the auxiliary rising main on the third floor comes off the Lower Weirs pumping line and she was the only person in that hospital who could say what the Thursday after had done to a medicine round.

She was not a member of the committee. Nobody objected to her being in the room, because a person who can tell a room what happened to a ward clock is exactly the sort of person a room likes having in it, and because there were chairs at the back for anybody who came.

Her name is Leda Vale, and Orrin had asked her three days beforehand whether she would do it, and she had said yes in a way that had frightened him, and the thing she had said was: "You have been in rooms where you can talk for a month. I have never once been in one. Do not ask me to be careful."

She read it out from the printed notice, which she had copied out by hand, because there is no facility for obtaining a printed copy of a Question Hall notice and she had asked and been told so politely.

"BLIND MARGIN: unregistered district labour is not represented." She put the sheet down. "That is what it says. I am going to tell this room what it means, because I work in a building where that sentence is the whole of my trade, and I am going to tell it as a nurse and not as anything else.

"It means that when a calculation like that one is made, the hours of anybody whose work is not on a certified shift are not in it. Not wrong in it. Not estimated. Not in it.

"And the effect of that is not that the machine says something untrue. The effect of that is that the machine cannot feel us. The rows and the wards and the early shifts and the girls who do a ward clock by hand at two in the morning because a clock has gone out — we are not represented, and so the pressure we put on the system is not represented, and so the system does not slow down to ask about us, and so it does not know that it is asking us.

""Forty-three years ago somebody wrote a compact with four safeguards in it, and the second one is that the benefit and the cost are both shown, and this office is the cost. We are the cost. And the cost is not wrong; the cost is not a lie. It is simply not in."

Nobody in the room said anything for about a minute.

Then the chairman asked whether the committee would note that the margin had been read. And Orrin, standing at the back with his case in his hand, understood the whole shape of the next eighteen seconds about two seconds before it happened, and could not have stopped it and would not have wanted to.

"It will be noted," said the chairman. "And the committee has a schedule and the schedule is in force and a schedule is a schedule, and there is no mechanism under this act by which a committee that has voted may be re-voted by a reading of a document aloud, and I am not going to pretend otherwise to a young woman who has come four miles on a tram to read us a line of type." He put his pen down. "It will be entered in the minute: that the blind margin of question four thousand one hundred and twelve was read aloud in this room on the thirteenth inst. at the request of a member of the public, and that the request was granted without objection. It will say who read it. It will not say what the committee thought about it, because the committee has thought about it, and has a schedule, and will not be re-opening a voted item on the strength of a footer."

They wrote it down. It took ninety seconds. It is in the minute.

The Water Users' Committee of the Lower Weirs pumping district did not re-open the schedule. It did not do anything else at all. It went on to the next item, which was a boundary, and finished at four.

---

Leda caught him in Weir Lane at about ten past five, outside the chandler's, under the awning, out of the rain.

"Eleven minutes," she said. "On the Thursday after. Eleven minutes between the insulin off the corridor clock and the anticoagulant out of the ward's own drawer, on a Tuesday morning, in a ward with eleven patients, and the sister wrote *within* eleven times and cried twice in the linen store and did not apologise for it. That is on your committee's minute now, isn't it. Not my words. *Advice received from the hospital.*"

"Yes."

"Right." She had her bag over her shoulder and her hood down and the rain going into her hair and she did not seem to be aware of either. "Then here is the part I am not going to say to a room of nine men with a property qualification, and I am going to say it to you in the rain because you cannot minute it.

"Three weeks ago you stood on a third-floor landing in that hospital with your hand on that pump and said out loud that the repair needed a district toll and a class-three licence could not certify one, and that was correct, and the room was right, and the ward lost four minutes anyway. I told you in a corridor that your rule had worked and that working was the problem. And I was right about all of it.

"And now the same building has lost eleven minutes, and it has lost them because nine people with property qualifications voted nine to four on the second to suspend the pumping district's night draw so that the canal front would stop flooding in the spring tides — which is *a good decision* — and because their clerk posted a notice on a pump house door on the morning of the day because the ordinance says he must, and because a man at a brass indicator in the Question Hall asked one question of a machine and got a true answer and could not use it, and because the Compact in 1881 says that a machine which states what it does not know is a safe machine, and because the Compact is correct about that, and I have been a nurse for eight years and I would not put a ward clock in the hands of anything that did not know what it did not know."

"Leda—"

"The water is worth more than the bridge was," said Leda Vale. "Say it. I know you think it. I have thought it too, this afternoon, in a room with nine chairs at the back, and I could not argue with it, and I have been trying since a quarter past two and I have got nothing. Thirty-one houses went under in the spring and the district's own record of it is a chalk board, and a water users' committee with no night worker on it and no form to put one on has just done the best arithmetic anybody in this district has ever done with real numbers in a real room, and it is going to stop the flooding, and I have a ward that cannot find eleven minutes."

She was not crying. She had the same face she had in a corridor a month ago with a number on her clipboard.

"And here is what I want you to hear, and I am not asking you to do anything about it, and I have known since a quarter past two this afternoon and I have been walking around the block four times. **There is no answer.** I have looked. I have been a nurse for eight years and I have watched men try to answer for machines and I have watched them get it wrong with their hands, and this time it is not a machine, and there is nothing to be brave about, and there is no instrument anywhere in the country that will put eleven minutes back into a Tuesday morning, and nobody in that room did a wrong thing, and I am the one who has to say so, and I said it in the street, to a man with a bar."

She put her hood up.

"You are not to write that down as a lesson," she said. "You are not to put it in the book in the shop. You are not to work it out. I am telling you a thing that has happened so that you were in the street when it happened, and that is all I have ever wanted from you, and you have still not got it, and I am going home now."

She went off down Weir Lane at five past five with her bag over her shoulder and did not look back, and Orrin stood under the awning for about a minute and then walked up to the Quarter with the rain coming in.

---

The last piece came in on the Friday the seventeenth at about eleven in the morning and it was Rill's, and it was the only piece of the seven that anybody put in writing in a form that belonged to an office.

The Ferrywright Bridge gate interlock on the Lower Weirs approach is a mechanism about the size of a biscuit tin, and it releases the road a fixed number of minutes after the last pump of a window, and it is timed off the pump, not off a clock, and the whole point of it is that it does not depend on any instrument in the district.

Tobias Rill came out on the sixteenth, on a Thursday, with the case and the folding handle and the four-inch column, and read the line four times and wrote all four readings down, and the fourth line read *consistent*, and Orrin was not there and did not know until the Friday.

His finding was two paragraphs and the second paragraph was not a fault and had never been intended as one.

> *Ferrywright Bridge, Lower Weirs approach. Gate interlock, timed from the pump cycle. Read at 09.20, 10.02 and 11.44 hours. Readings consistent and within tolerance. The interlock is mechanical and holds.*
>
> *This Office certifies that a line agrees with its own record. The Office notes that the interlock's release delay is fixed at forty-one minutes from the last pump of a window, and that the window in question is now shorter by design, and that the delay has not been reviewed by any office since the mechanism was installed. The Office has no function in respect of a pumping schedule and none in respect of the schedule of a water users' committee.*

Four lines of true thing, in a printed column, in a file that went on a shelf. He read the fourth reading out twice before he signed it, because that was his practice.

A man with a lamp, at two in the morning, on the ninth, is still a man with a lamp. But on the Thursday of the seventeenth the Ferrywright approach has a sentence in it, in a public file, saying that a forty-one-minute delay has not been reviewed by anybody since the mechanism went in, and that sentence is true, and it was not there on the Wednesday, and there is a form in Morrowfen with a four-inch column in it and the sentence is in the column and nobody can take it out.

Mara Venn read it on the Friday morning with her tea in her hand and put it down.

"That is a sentence in a file," she said. "It is not a record. There is a file on the first floor of a building on the Rise that says a man with a lamp was not necessary on the ninth, and no form exists on which a man with a lamp can be necessary, and he was forty years old and walked round the Ferrywright approach in the dark because the road came open early, and the sentence that exists about him says that a delay has not been reviewed."

"Yes," said Orrin.

"It is better than nothing," said Mara Venn, "and I have spent nine years learning exactly how much better than nothing is, and it is a line of type in a column four inches wide, and it is the first time in this entire business that anybody has written down something *true* that was not there before. It is not going to get him his night back. It is going to mean that the next man who walks out at ten past two knows."

---

He went up to the guild hall at half past two on the Friday afternoon and stood on the licensing floor in front of the board for a while, reading the fifth notice again, which he had read four times on the twenty-seventh of last month and had not liked.

*A renewal is complete only when the fee, the endorsement, and the work book in hall form have all been received at this counter. The counter receives renewals on the day of expiry only, and between the hours of ten and twelve.*

The nineteenth of the third month was a Sunday. There were two days left.

He went to the counter and asked the man behind it for a blank work book, hall form W-4, current issue, and got the second one out of the rack under the counter without looking at a ledger, and it cost nothing, and nobody wrote a word about it, and the man on the counter said, "You want the second sheet as well, they'll not ask for it, but you'll want it," and handed that over too, and Orrin stood at the counter holding a two-page form with four things printed at the head of it and a ruled table of three columns beneath, and one of the three was headed *recommendation*, and it was going to be the widest column on the sheet and it was going to be empty all year.

"Twenty-two blanks is the whole stack," the man said. "There is no more coming this month. Somebody's coming in with one tomorrow and I shall have to give him the same one, and there is nothing anybody can do about that."
