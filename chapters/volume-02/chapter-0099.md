# Chapter 99 — Eighteen Years of Being the Person Who Had To Be Asked

On Tuesday the third of the first month a woman of forty-four refused in nine lines a post that nobody had power to give her and that she had already left on the first of September, and on the Saturday the seventh of the same month she sat down in a room over a chandler's shop for the first time in nineteen years as somebody who was going to be asked rather than as the person who answered, and she is not going to be thanked and said so on the Tuesday and did not say it again; and she is not redeemed and she is not softened and she is on nobody's side, and the argument she made for giving the whole thing up is the argument this district has been making since the fourteenth of the tenth, and she made it herself, in about nine minutes, and did not ask anybody in the room to agree with it.

---

**The letter came on the Tuesday the third of the first month at about eleven in the morning, by hand, in an envelope with the district works' address on it and nothing else, and it had been in an envelope for nine days.**

The district works accountancy offers the appointed warden post of the Lower Weirs pumping district to **S. Kade**, and it is a form of four lines with a reply required in seven days, and the seven days is the ordinary seven days' notice of this city and it has nothing to do with this letter.

> *1. That the said post be offered to S. Kade, late warden, the same terms as formerly, the works not having appointed and the works not being able to appoint, and the post being vacant since the first day of the ninth month.*
> *2. That the reserve, the gauge sheets, the day book, the store and the form on the lid of the box be transferred to the person accepting, and that the form be signed by that person in the manner in which it was signed on the first of the ninth.*
> *3. That the low-level alarm on the ward's reserve, inhibited since the second of the third month, be restored at the earliest convenience, no report being required and none having been made.*
> *4. That a reply be sent within seven days.*

She read it in the room over the chandler's shop, standing, at about eleven o'clock on the Tuesday, and she was not in a hurry about it, and she put it down on the table face up and said the first thing she was going to say that morning, which was that **she was not going to be thanked for refusing it and she was going to say that once and not again and if anybody in that room found themselves getting ready to say something on her account they could stop.**

Nobody said anything. There were nine people in the room and it is the tenant council's room and the room has eleven people in it on a Wednesday and nine on a Tuesday, and one of the nine was a man of about twenty and one of the nine was a woman of thirty-eight and one of the nine was a woman of fifty-four, and two of the nine had been in the same room since the fourth month and had not chosen between each other since the nineteenth of the August and were not going to choose now.

**Her reply is nine lines and it is dated the third of the first month and it went back by hand on the same morning and she wrote it at that table and four people read it before it went.**

> *1. No.*
> *2. The post is vacant and has been since the first of the ninth and the works did not appoint me and cannot, and I was asked to put a form on a box in a store and I did it in my own hand and I filled in the line that says where it is to be kept and I wrote the store, and the store is a room and a shelf is a piece of furniture, and I have said that four times in four weeks and I will not say it a fifth.*
> *3. The alarm goes back on when somebody restores it and not when a form of four lines asks a person to restore it in the first clause. **That it is in the second clause and that there is no report required and none made is the whole of what has happened to that tank since March and it is in a letter from a works and it has taken me nine days to read.**
> *4. I am not the person who ought to have a reserve. **A reserve with no margin in it and an alarm off since March is not a thing a person ought to have handed back to.** Whoever has that tank has been handed something that was not signed for and I signed for it for nineteen years and I am giving it back inside a box with a form on the lid and I would like it understood that I am not the person to ask whether it is all right.*

And then she said the thing out loud, which took about nine minutes, and it is the argument of a year of this and she made it and she did not ask anybody to agree with it and nobody agreed with her.

---

**"I have been the person who had to be asked for nineteen years, and it was never the job, and it is the whole of my life, and I am giving it up and I am not sorry about it and I want the room to hear the reason before I stop talking.**

"The job was water. A tank, a main, a gate, a rotation in a box, a column of levels with no heading on it, and a store. I could move the reserve. I could put nine thousand gallons a day into a street that was standing in water and hold a district's night crew in a standing and have ninety-one jobs in a works and sixty-one men resident in it, **and I did that in March and I would do it again, and the tenant review found against me on a flood and did not find against me on employment and not one word of that minute is about jobs.** That was the job. It was eleven years of the twentieth century's water and it was a machine you worked with your hands and a number you were frightened of in the winter.

"And the other thing was this. For nineteen years, if anything went wrong in this district and a person had to be asked anything, **they asked me.** Not the works — the works had a form and a reference and a counter. Not the committee — nine men with a property qualification who could not consult the district and said so on the foot of their own notices. **Me, in a room, on a Tuesday, with no notice at all, because I was the person and a person is not an office and an office is answerable and a person is available.** I have had about four hundred of those. Things said to me in a room with one other person in it, four hundred times, for nineteen years.

"And here is what none of them ever was. **Not one of the four hundred ever said no to me.** Not one. In nineteen years. And I have worked out since about the twentieth of November why, and it is because a person who can be asked is exactly as rare in this district as a person who can refuse, and they are the same rarity, and I was the only one of either, **and a person who is the only one anybody can be asked, and is never the one anybody says no to, is not a body and is not an office and is a woman in a room being extremely helpful, and every single thing that has been decided in this district for nineteen years has been decided by a woman in a room being extremely helpful.**"

She put her hand flat on the table. It is not a gesture; it is a hand on a surface and it has never meant anything except that.

"That is why I am not sorry. **I was the instrument. An instrument is the thing the safeguard is written to stop, and I have been it for nineteen years and nobody wrote a safeguard about me because there was no form for me and a person does not have to be counted to be used.** And the row of houses that got asked at twenty-two doors in December found a thing in nine days that I have not been able to do in nineteen years, and the thing they found was a column of households, **and I have had a column of levels with no heading on it since 1938 and it is a column of a tank and not a column of anybody, and there is not one name in it.**"

Nobody in that room said anything at all for about a minute, and then a man of about twenty at the back said one sentence, and it was not a joke and he did not smile, and it was the sixth time he had said it and this was the first time anybody had said it to her.

"You are the only person in this district who has ever found out that the thing you were doing was the thing the safeguard is about, and you have found it out at forty-four and on your own, and there is nobody I could name to thank you for it and you have told me not to thank you, and I am going to keep to that, and I am also going to say that a man who says *I am not a leader* in a room like this about forty times a month has been the loudest person in it every time and has never once been at the front of it."

"I know," said Sel Kade. "That is the only part of the last four months anybody has got right about me and you have got it right for the wrong reason and I am not going to correct you."

---

**And on the Friday the sixth of the first month, at about half past seven in the evening, in the same room, the council asked her to take a seat, and she took it, and she asked for one condition and got it.**

There was a vacancy. One of seven members resigned in the middle of November over a winter rotation he had not been able to produce and nobody had been able to produce, and there had been no replacement and there had been six members and a room and a stove and a bell that has not worked since 1949 and a minute book whose most recent volume is 1948.

"Sit with us," said Wenna Pruitt, who is thirty-eight and is the council's organiser and who has been in that room every week since the fourth month.

"I will sit, and I will tell you what I want before I do, and if you cannot do it I will not sit and I will not think about it again."

"Go on."

"**I want a seat and not an office, and I want nothing on this council that can be asked a question about water.**" She said it standing up, which is the way people in that room say things. "A tenant council can be asked about a room, about a rent, about a light on a stair, about a hearing in a back room over a shop with four clauses in it three of which are mine, about a notice, about a resolution, about a refusal. **A tenant council cannot be asked one question about the water of this district, because the water is a works' asset at a boundary and the bodies that can be asked about it are a committee of nine men with a property qualification who cannot consult the district, and the district itself, which is not a body and has no standing under the water act and has not had a standing since it was made a district.**"

"Then what is the point of you," said Hanna Stroud, who is fifty-four and has nine blocks and about two hundred and eleven households and who has been asked that question by about nine people since the fourteenth of the November.

"**The point of me is the column.**" She said it as though she had been carrying it since the Thursday. "This room has never had a column in it. It has had minute books to 1948 and a clerk's pencil and no clerk and a show of hands, and everything this district has decided for nineteen years has been decided in it and **not one line of any of it has ever been a line anybody could stand a person in.** There is a column in a book in the pumping ward's outer room about two hundred yards from this door and it has a name in it and nothing beside the name, and there is a column in a book in a nurses' room in Green Span with a date on it and four names under it, and there is a column on a sheet in a room in Green Span with three of them in a woman's hand and a fourth in a mother's, and **there is not one column in this room and there never has been, and a room without a column is a room in which nothing can be refused because nothing can be entered.**"

"Whose column," said Hanna Stroud.

"That is the question and I am not answering it, and neither is Wenna, and neither are you, and it is not going to be answered in a room over a shop by three people who have been refusing to choose since the nineteenth of August." Wenna Pruitt said it and sat down. "**Hanna will not choose and I will not choose and we have said so five months running and we are not choosing this either, and Mr Arden is going to enjoy telling us that we have still not chosen, and he may.**"

"Enjoy it," said Teo Arden.

---

**And the argument of a year of this, said by her, on the Saturday the seventh, at about eleven in the morning, to a man of thirty-two who had not asked her for it.**

He had come about a thing with a book in it, and she had read a page of it standing, and had said: "You have a sheet of paper in that book with two hundred and twenty-one days on it and a bill of four pounds two shillings under it and no date at the end of the hundred and fifty. **You have written a number with a bill under it and no day on it, for eleven months, and you have worked out since the twentieth of September that a form in a hall in the Clockwork Quarter runs out on the first of the fifth next year, and you have not written the day, and the reason you have not written the day is that the day is a day with nothing on it.**"

"How do you know that."

"Because it is what I did on the first of September and I did it in four minutes and I told a man in a shop the reason at the time and I have said the reason four times since and this is the fifth. **The form runs out and the forty-one heads fall out of the standing again as quietly as they came out in 1939 and there is no hearing and no vote and no notice and no clause, and a day you have written down is a day somebody can be asked about, and I had nobody to ask.**"

"You have me now."

"You have a row book with a column in it and a maintenance schedule with six names on it, and if you wrote the day down on the wall in that room then somebody in this district could be asked about it, and that is the first time in nineteen years that sentence has been true, **and it is true because a woman of thirty-three put a figure on a ward's return and it cost two hundred and eleven households a rate rise, and nobody thanked her, and now there is a room in this district with a number in it that somebody is answerable for.**" She put the book down. "So write the day down. And when somebody comes and asks you what the day is you will have to answer, and that is the whole of the difference between a number in a book and a date on a wall, and it is the only thing anybody in this district has been after all year, and it is not worth very much and it is the entire thing."

Then she said the last of it, and she said it standing, and it is the last of what she said that day and it is hers.

"**I was the person who had to be asked. From Saturday the seventh of this month I am one of seven people in a room that can be asked what it thinks, and it can be told no, and it will be, and the first thing it is told no about will be a number on a wall in a pumping ward, and I have wanted that for about four years and I could not have had it while I was the woman somebody rang.**"

---

**The rate went up in the twelfth month and the third of the first month was the day the notice of it went on the ward board, in Bevin Sarr's hand, and about nine people a day read it, and the two hundred and eleven households in nine blocks are in it and the landlord who asked for it in the name of all nine of them read it standing up in the street on the Tuesday, alone, with a hat on, and did not go inside for about ten minutes and nobody spoke to her.**

Nobody thanked anybody in any of it. The woman who refused a post she could not legally be given did not say so twice. The woman who asked a rate authority to make her own street cost more is not thanked and did not expect it and has said that she is not going to be thanked for it by anybody including the people who live in the blocks. The council's minute book is 1948 and there is a column in another book about two hundred yards away and the two have never been in the same case.
