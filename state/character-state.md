# Character State

State at the end of Volume 01, batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10). Read with `state/chapter-summaries.md` and `state/continuity.md`. A volume is roughly a year; ages are canon and must not be rounded.

## Orrin Vale, 32 — municipal clockmaker, line horologist

- **Wants, in the order he would say them if asked:** to find out what the ninth beat is; to have the Weir Two night roster in a document; to stop the guild's version of the night being the only version; and, further back and never said out loud, to find out what his father did in the storm eighteen years ago and whether the street photograph ever becomes a name.
- **Has:** a class-three maintainer's licence, two benches, a lease, a repair shop with a reputation, a private grey ledger nobody can read, a Long Nine regulator in a cloth on his shelf that is in no book anywhere, and a form number written on a clean page.
- **Has lost:** one minute of lived attention with clean edges, and any claim that what he heard was real. He can no longer say whether he read the Weir Two stage or invented the reading.
- **Power:** Stage 2, Aftershock, arrived involuntarily in Chapter 5 and used deliberately, out loud, in Chapter 9. He can feel the next short-term failure of a named load on one connected machine or one immediate assembly. He cannot see intent, cannot see a district, cannot see anything the network has routed away, and cannot file, cite or defend a word of it. No Choice Trace, no Loom access, no filed Reading.
- **The ninth beat is not a stage and not part of this line.** It is heard twice, in Chapters 1 and 3, and it is a sound with a count in it that leans on a place he cannot name or locate: no picture, no word, no cost, and no prophecy. It is the volume's hook and it stays unexplained through Chapter 50. **He must never treat it as a free Aftershock, and Chapter 5's "he had never done it about something that had not happened yet" is only true because the ninth beat shows him nothing.**
- **Cost of the two readings:** keeper's tolls, unfiled and unreimbursed, and the order is fixed by the page. The first, in Chapter 5, took the minute. The second, in Chapter 9, took two days of fine motor control in his right hand and left a ring in his right ear above a given pitch that dulls when he is tired. That is a condition, not the series' final cost: Volume 08 takes his long-range hearing and Volume 14 takes clean single-machine hearing for good, and both must still be available.
- **His leash:** the class-three licence may service and certify one civic machine. It may not file a district proposal, may not witness a Reading, may not certify a district toll. Its renewal is Hask's discretion and is due about eight weeks after the 15th. He thinks of it as a tax. He has committed no licence offence; the offence that costs him his trade is reserved for Chapters 44–49, and he certifies nothing in Chapter 9.
- **His rule, written at the top of the ledger in Chapter 10 and not yet tested:** *nothing of mine is used again without its blind margin and its toll named in public, in a room, to somebody who can object.* The volume tests it in Chapter 19, where he is right and cannot certify and nothing happens.
- **Flaw, in action rather than in summary:** he told Teo Arden he filed the question and kept back the ninth beat and the regulator; he answered Ada Vance's question through a tram window with *I'm sorry*; he has twice declined to write a defect note on Hask's reasonable account of the cost.

## Mara Venn, 33 — investigator, Office of Public Reckoning

- **Wants:** the Reading's charge made visible as a charge, then a public record that cannot be scrubbed off a slate at seven each morning. Not the destruction of the machine; the machine's answerability.
- **Has:** an objection on the toll receipt, accepted for filing, on the ground that the paying district was neither affected nor consenting. A measurement: the blind margin at eleven to the point against twenty-two for the trace line and fourteen for the signature, unchanged over two years. Forty requests for records held where she may not name them; four came back. Eleven years of an office with a written right to be ignored.
- **Does not have:** any way to read a machine, any authority over any other office, and any personal disclosure. Her mother is not mentioned and must not be until a volume the outline names. She quoted the rule about calibration strain once, in a tone with a room behind it, and refused to explain.
- **State with Orrin:** professional. She has identified his hour-sickness and looked frightened for him rather than at him, and has told him to bring her a name and not to do anything with it. He has not asked her for anything. Neither has told the other anything true about themselves.
- **Her stake in the argument:** she warned him that a published body-toll ledger would put unasked-for names in a public book, and that the sentence which will kill the demand in any committee room is *who pays for the record.*

## Teo Arden, 20 — unlicensed tuner, organizer, the Weirs

- Perry Vance's apprentice. Twenty years of a master's trade with no form behind it. Alive throughout batch 0001 and elsewhere in the district on the night of the death: he was two streets over, which is the fifth name on Bevin Sarr's board and the reason four looked like a miscount instead of a removal.
- **Wants:** the district kept live without permission, and proof the city can survive without waiting for Cathedral Hill. **Needs:** to learn that a sabotage can save one street and make another unlivable.
- **State at the end of the batch:** not recruited, not softened, not grateful, and not stupid. He gave Orrin a flat audit of his own evidence, called Orrin's statement of what he did not know the most useful thing he had heard in three days, and then said *it's not generosity, it's an audit, don't mistake it.* He has decided to leave Orrin alone until he knows what to do with him. He has watched Orrin refuse to lie to a district in public and cannot decide what to do about it.
- **Not yet on the page as:** a member of the Loose Minutes by name. He has their methods, their anger, and nine people who came when he called.

## Leda Vale, 27 — emergency nurse, Green Span

- Opposes Orrin's self-sacrifice and any plan that hides the cost. In Chapter 6 she used *calibration strain* at him in a corridor in front of a ward clerk as a deliberate provocation and then refused to explain. She showed him a health slip recording a tram driver's twenty-five-minute interval with no name, no duration and no date.
- **What changed:** at the end of the batch she has stopped arguing about his character and started arguing about method, and the condition she set is procedural: if her ward is going to be the number he spends, say it out loud in a room, in front of the people whose hours they are, before you spend it. She has a professional stake in the batch's climax, which is why Chapter 9 works.
- **Still owed her:** the volume's test of his rule, in which he is right and cannot certify and nothing happens (Chapter 19).

## Perry Vance, 41 — dead, the end of Chapter 5, the night of the 17th

- The man the Reading left out. Unlicensed, twenty years on the Weir Two night pump crew, red to the second joint, closes things by hand and complains about it. He was a competent unlisted wage-earner finishing a shift and would have been embarrassed by any reading of himself as a symbol. His last act was shutting a valve because it was open, on his own initiative, because it was his valve and his shift.
- He is not Teo. The two are never to be confused, and in batch 0001 the confusion is prevented on the page by the fifth name on the board.
- On the page: Chapter 3 alive, specific, and liked; Chapter 5 dead with the Directorate's notice calling the day a success; Chapter 6 in the private ledger as the first person in it, written as a man and not a shift, with a blank where a duration would go and the words *none recorded*.

## Sel Kade, 44 — pump warden, Lower Weirs

- Her name entered the evidentiary record in Chapter 8 as the countersignature on a batch of forty-one transferred entries eighteen years ago, when she was a deputy of twenty-six. She is a local ruler, not a villain, and her diverting of the pump reserve to Carrow Street in the spring — seventy-one people — is true and defensible and structurally unjust, and she has said so out loud on a public street and invited the committee to use it.
- Owed: a tenant review of the diverted reserve by about Chapter 46, which is a process she helps design, and the reason she is still a character in Volume 02.

## Gerren Hask, 55 — master of the Clockwork Quarter guild hall

- His crime is legible to the reader by Chapter 2 and invisible to every character for forty more chapters. The ledger line in a copying clerk's hand, the eighteen-year-old governor stopped mid-travel on purpose, the reasonable and unanswerable case against a defect note, and the offer to reduce the regulator to scrap without recording it. In Chapter 10 the hall's reply to the Office's request for a labour ledger is courteous and true: the hall keeps a record of machines and not a record of hours for work that is not part of a certified shift, there being no such hours, there being no such shifts.
- Owed: losing the right to certify silent shifts, and publishing the guild's labour ledger with a liability annex that names the night of the Long Nine as the night the hours stopped. He is not destroyed; he becomes a repairer under public oversight.
- **Not yet on the page:** Della Marsh, the guild secretary who keeps the certification ledger and the form-number series, and who can be made to look up a number for a fee and a closed door. Her number is the first item in the Volume 02 index.

## Secondary people now established on the page

- **Bevin Sarr, 33,** second on the Weir Two night pump crew, survived the night, keeps the crew's hours on a chalk board, refused twice to be thanked. Her board is the first counter-record to the guild's *non-certified standby* line.
- **Wenna Pruitt, 38,** Weirs tenant-council organiser, keeps the pumping district's shift book, eleven years and her aunt's forty before it. She said, before anyone else, that Orrin has the paper and does not have the person, and she asked the question he cannot answer: whose name goes on a form that says there was no crew.
- **Alma Festinger, 56,** Green Span resident on a public-health rota, signed the affected-district station on the witness rail because she was asked, asked what *unregistered* meant, said somebody should write it down, and said no when a clerk in a committee gallery asked whether she understood what she signed. She means it. It is written down. Nobody followed it up. Her name is also on the routing card of the batch that removed the crew, four removes away, and nobody has connected it.
- **Ivo Pell, 36,** veteran operator, contemptuous of the trade he left, correct about the difference between a model's conditions and a promise. He is competent and faintly insulting and has not yet taught Orrin anything.
- **Ivo's assistant Ostrand,** the record keeper at the rail in Chapter 4, copying notices into a ledger the size of a paving stone and not wishing to be asked a single question. Minor; do not promote him. **Ostrand is not Ostrow.**
- **Ostrow,** the Toll Stair chalker named by Mara in Chapter 10, who writes the district's daily figure on the board and scrubs it off at half past five each morning. One line, no scene, not the guild's man. **Ostrow is not Ostrand.** The two surnames sit four districts apart and must never be merged, cross-referenced, or renamed: if either recurs, keep the spell exactly as it is on the page.
- **Marta Dey,** Weirs woman who held the lamp at the top of the ladder on the night of the 17th and holds the light on the trestle; in Wenna Pruitt's kitchen every Thursday. Minor but load-bearing.
- **Sindri Lake, 55,** keeper of the public Archive room, who explains the difference between the room that is open to petition and the room that is not, and who will not hold testimony.
- **Tobias Rill, 44,** Continuity Office inspector of hourlines, named once, in person in no scene yet.
- **Anja Voss, 49,** and **Halden Rusk, 58,** have each appeared once, as a name on a document, and have taken no scene, no offer and no bargain. Rusk is not to meet Orrin before Volume 04.
- **Nella Rook** has not been named. Green Span is reached through Leda and the trestle.

## Ages, fixed

Ada Vance is 13: she is on the six o'clock tram in Chapter 6, asks one short question through a closed window, and is not answered. She is a child on the page in Volumes 01 and 02 and an adult in every appearance from Volume 08 on.
